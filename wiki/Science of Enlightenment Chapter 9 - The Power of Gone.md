Chapter 9 of Shinzen Young's 2016 *The Science of Enlightenment* is the book's dedicated Gone chapter. It makes detected vanishing a precise practice object and then pushes it into Source, death, fulfillment, service, Flow, dissolution, and deep body-emotion territory.

## Source Snapshot
- **Path**: `raw/Shinzen Sources/science-of-enlightenment/09-the-power-of-gone.md`
- **Origin**: Shinzen Young, *The Science of Enlightenment: How Meditation Works*, Sounds True.
- **Date**: 2016.
- **Format**: Book chapter converted to Markdown; pages 167-177 in the source markers.
- **Reliability**: Primary source for Shinzen's mature Gone definition, Just Note Gone practice, Gone/Flow relation, dark-night interventions, and primordial Feel account. Strong for his internal phenomenological distinctions and practice routing; weaker for Source metaphysics, world-mysticism synthesis, developmental claims about infancy, and claims about complete reparenting.
- **Scope**: Defines Gone as abrupt vanishing or diminishing, gives the practice axiom for clear/deep Gone, explains relief, rest, satisfaction, Source contact, bodhicitta, Flow/Gone figure-ground reversal, dissolution risks and interventions, and a speculative primordial Feel mechanism.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says humanity probably does not yet know the quickest path to enlightenment, but if he could teach only one focus technique as a thought experiment, he would choose Just Note Gone.
- **S2**: Ordinary attention notices sensory arisings more than passings, creating an unbalanced view of sensory experience and missing a route to Source.
- **S3**: Just Note Gone means clearly acknowledging the transition point when all or part of a sensory experience is present and then no longer present; if nothing vanishes, wait, or note the ending of the thought that nothing is ending.
- **S4**: With practice, a practitioner may progress from noticing events, to riding arising-passing rhythms, to fascination with continuous passing; Shinzen links this to the *Visuddhimagga*.
- **S5**: Gone means all or part of a sensory event abruptly disappears or diminishes; it need not be permanent, total, whole-object disappearance, and gradual fading counts only when it finally winks out.
- **S6**: In this chapter's broad change vocabulary, Flow is any change and Gone is any abrupt decrease, making Gone a special case of abrupt Flow.
- **S7**: Four factors deepen Gone: total momentary focus, total momentary equanimity, quick momentary focus, and quick momentary equanimity; the more quickly and completely arising is affirmed, the deeper and clearer its passing becomes.
- **S8**: Gone is not esoteric because ordinary life supplies constant examples, including two Gones in every breath.
- **S9**: Every Gone, however ordinary, points to the Uncreated or Source; the Source itself is not a sensory experience but may be indirectly contacted through vanishing, while the aftereffect of vanishing becomes sensorially clearer.
- **S10**: Gone can produce relief, tranquility, rest, microrelief that sums into macrorelief, satisfaction or fulfillment, and, counterintuitively, spontaneous love and service.
- **S11**: Subtle vibratory Flow and Gone can alternate and homogenize sensory spaces, weakening distinctions between visual, auditory, somatic, inner, outer, active, and restful categories.
- **S12**: Shinzen presents expansion-contraction as more fundamental than subtle vibratory Flow and says abrupt contractive Flow, or Gone, destroys time and space; intense Gone can become an abiding ground through figure-ground reversal.
- **S13**: Extreme Goneness, emptiness, and no-self can sometimes produce disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, or hopelessness, named through dark-night, pit-of-the-void, or dukkha-nana language.
- **S14**: The chapter gives three interventions for dissolution distress: accentuate subtle positive aspects, deconstruct the negative reaction into emotional body sensation, mental image, and mental talk, and affirm positive emotions, behaviors, and cognitions; extreme cases may need ongoing intensive teacher and practitioner support.
- **S15**: Usually Gone is restful, but sometimes it evokes emotional agitation that Shinzen interprets as powerful rather than as the dramatic dark night.
- **S16**: Primordial Feel is Shinzen's account of Gone contacting chaotic infant body emotion, the "second most primordial" human experience after Source/Zero.
- **S17**: The chapter claims primordial infantile freak-out underlies later emotional freak-outs and can be worked through by an adult meditator with techniques, "loving it to death" and "knowing it to death."
- **S18**: At the deepest speculative edge, Zero polarizes into expansion and contraction, creates a cleft of space, vibrates into pure heat of Feel, and can resolve back through expansion-contraction and Gone into reparented, enlightened adulthood.

## Summary
The chapter begins by elevating Just Note Gone without claiming that a universally fastest path is known. Shinzen says ordinary attention is biased toward arisings, while passings are rarely noticed. Just Note Gone corrects that imbalance by tracking the moment when all or part of a sensory event disappears, drops, or abruptly diminishes. The definition is deliberately subtle: Gone is not permanent disappearance, not necessarily the whole object, not necessarily total disappearance, and not mere gradual fading before the final wink-out.

The middle of the chapter turns Gone into a transformation route. Quick and total focus plus quick and total equanimity with arisings deepen the perception of passings. Ordinary breath endings, sounds stopping, thoughts ending, and partial image evaporation all count. Each Gone is said to point toward Source, not as a sensory object but as an indirectly contacted not-thing whose aftereffect becomes clearer. The claimed fruits include relief, tranquility, rest, satisfaction, "satisfying nothingness," and spontaneous love or service.

The final sections widen the frame. Flow and Gone can alternate until distinctions among sensory modalities, active/rest states, and subject/object begin to collapse. Goneness can become an object of high concentration, producing a figure-ground reversal where self and world become fleeting figures and Gone becomes abiding ground. The chapter then handles the risk side: intense Goneness, emptiness, and no-self can produce dark-night or pit-of-the-void reactions. It recommends accentuating subtle good aspects, deconstructing negative reactions into image/talk/body sensation, reconstructing positive affect/behavior/cognition, and using support. It closes with the speculative primordial Feel account, where Gone exposes chaotic infant body emotion and ultimately the polarity of Source as expansion, contraction, space, and pure heat of Feel.

## Source Frame
The source is a major practice-theory chapter with a strong contemplative agenda: it wants readers to take vanishings seriously as a direct path rather than as subtle side effects of meditation. Its reliable core is definitional and procedural. It clarifies what counts as Gone, how to practice, why ordinary Gones matter, how Gone relates to focus and equanimity, and what to do when emptiness reactions destabilize.

The chapter's evidence is phenomenological, pedagogical, classical-comparative, and interpretive. References to the *Visuddhimagga*, nirvana, bodhicitta, Tibetan emptiness-compassion, Goethe, Rinzai, dark night, and pit-of-the-void language help locate the practice in contemplative history, but they do not by themselves establish a unified world-mysticism mechanism.

The weakest zone is developmental and metaphysical. The primordial Feel section may be important for Shinzen's deep model, but it should be treated as Shinzen's speculative contemplative account, not as established infant psychology, neuroscience, trauma theory, cosmology, or clinical reparenting protocol.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that Just Note Gone would be Shinzen's best single-technique candidate is explicitly a thought experiment, not an empirical comparison of techniques.
- The Source, Uncreated, shared-womb, and Love Itself claims are Shinzen's contemplative interpretation of Gone, not established metaphysics.
- The claim that Gone may lead to spontaneous bodhicitta or service is important internally but not behaviorally verified by the chapter.
- The assertion that primordial infantile freak-out underlies all later emotional freak-out is a very broad developmental claim without clinical evidence in this source.
- The claim that repeated completion of primordial Feel can completely reparent the practitioner into enlightened adulthood is an advanced transformation claim, not a safety protocol or measured outcome.

## Important Omissions
- The chapter does not define how to distinguish precise Gone practice from dissociation, blankness, freeze, depression-related emptiness, depersonalization, or compulsive cessation-chasing.
- It does not give operational criteria for when dissolution distress requires stopping, grounding, therapy, medical care, or crisis support rather than continued practice.
- It does not specify teacher competence or community-support criteria for the extreme cases it says may need ongoing intensive support.
- It does not provide empirical or developmental evidence for the primordial Feel theory.
- It does not define behavioral verification for the claim that Gone leads to love, service, or reparented adulthood.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The chapter presents Gone as a possible quickest route, while also saying different techniques seem to work for different people and humanity may not know the quickest path.
- Gone is called a special case of Flow here, while the Basic Mindfulness manual often contrasts Flow as ongoing change with Gone as abrupt passing.
- Gone is framed as restful, satisfying, loving, and service-generating, yet the same territory can produce terror, paralysis, aversion, and hopelessness.
- The chapter asks practitioners to destroy distinctions through Flow and Gone after earlier Shinzen sources insist that healthy discrimination is essential.
- Primordial Feel is described as both not the dramatic dark night and still a deep chaotic emotional process with major safety implications.
- The final Source/polarity account shifts from practice phenomenology into cosmological and developmental claims that need stronger posture.
