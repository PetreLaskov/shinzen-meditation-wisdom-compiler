## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen frames happiness through related but distinct categories: objective circumstances, skillful action, service, relief, fulfillment, self-understanding, spontaneous output, and Source-side depth.
- **S2**: The day's central theme is the Source or Expansion-Contraction layer that, in Shinzen's frame, underlies relief, fulfillment, self-understanding, spontaneous action, and service.
- **S3**: The opening practice sequence trains Just See, Just Hear, Just Feel, and Note Everything; compact See/Hear/Feel labels are used as reminders, while the practitioner lets off-range experience remain in the background.
- **S4**: In Note Everything, the practitioner may label See, Hear, Feel, or Rest, where Rest can mean a spontaneous release of the tug toward inner or outer activity.
- **S5**: Shinzen teaches CCE through two responses to a sensory event: if it vanishes, detect the passing; if it remains, soak into it or get to know it, while equanimity lets foreground and background events come and go.
- **S6**: Arising and passing practice is taught as detection clarity rather than content analysis: notice beginnings and endings in visual, auditory, somatic, or all sensory domains, with patient waiting and some guessing allowed.
- **S7**: The yes/no/both/rest frame maps arising to yes, passing to no, simultaneous arising-passing to both, and relative or absolute absence of activity to rest; the labels are equanimity cues as well as detection cues.
- **S8**: Shinzen's Source explanation uses speculative analogies from Zen koans, evoked potentials, free energy, and pre-conscious sensory processing; these claims are teaching bridges, not established neuroscience or physics.
- **S9**: The retreat offers a combinatorial menu: see Source, hear Source, feel Source, or work broadly with all modalities through yes, no, both, and rest.
- **S10**: Continuous arising can present as effortless spatial spreading, continuous passing can present as effortless collapsing, and mature polarity practice may simplify into cycles of Source activity and rest.
- **S11**: Motion periods remain formal practice when the practitioner chooses a method such as Just See, Just Hear, Just Feel, Note Everything, or Source-facing yes/no practice rather than treating movement as an unstructured break.
- **S12**: Auto Speak or Auto Chant is presented as a motor-output analogue of Expansion-Contraction: the speech organs are allowed to self-organize while the practitioner listens and matches the leader or group rhythm.
- **S13**: The Om Mani Padme Hum segment gives only a brief chant gloss in this source; its durable new contribution is the Auto Chant frame, not a replacement for the earlier dedicated chant-practice source.
- **S14**: The closing restricts driving and heavy machinery to ordinary sensory task contact: see the drive, hear the drive, feel the drive.
- **S15**: Retreat effects need not evaporate after the bell; Shinzen recommends micro-hits and background practice as practitioners return to daily life.

- establish ordinary See/Hear/Feel contact before Source-facing practice;
- use Just See, Just Hear, Just Feel, and Note Everything as warmup for yes/no/both/rest;

## Summary
Shinzen opens by placing the retreat theme inside his broad happiness frame:
objective circumstances, skillful action, service, relief, fulfillment,
self-understanding, and spontaneous output are related but distinct. The day
focuses on the Source or Expansion-Contraction layer he says underlies those
dimensions, while his science language remains explicitly speculative.

The first half establishes the sensory on-ramp. Practitioners cycle through
Just See, Just Hear, Just Feel, and Note Everything. If an event vanishes,
detect the passing; if it remains, soak into it. CCE is the bridge from
ordinary sensory appreciation to later Source-facing work.

The middle turns arising and passing into a combinatorial practice. Visual,
auditory, somatic, and all-sensory fields can be explored through starts,
stops, both, or rest. Yes/no/both/rest cue yielding to the force pattern;
continuous arising may feel like spreading space, continuous passing like
collapsing space, and the deepest version like cycles of activity and rest.

The close returns Source expression to ordinary form. Auto Chant with Om Mani
Padme Hum becomes a speech-side analogue of Auto Move, but the final guardrail
is ordinary: cars have consequences, so driving meditation means safely seeing,
hearing, and feeling the drive, plus using micro-hits and background practice
after retreat.
