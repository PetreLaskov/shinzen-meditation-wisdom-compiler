## Practice Handles
- When a report uses "dense," "thick," "contracted," or "not spacious," ask whether the density is painful, resisted, rigid, frightening, or functionally impairing, or whether it feels pleasant, unifying, and liberating.
- If the density is painful, do not relabel it as progress by default. Check resistance, medical or somatic safety, trauma/dissociation risk, and ordinary support needs.
- If the density is pleasant and surrendered, it may be worked as contracted Flow rather than as a failure of spaciousness.
- Keep the global-Gone/trance language as a map caution: it may describe an advanced direction, but it does not authorize breath manipulation, immobility chasing, trance pursuit, or ignoring physiological safety.
