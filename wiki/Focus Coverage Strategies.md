Focus coverage strategy is the question of how attention covers the chosen field. Shinzen's local taxonomy distinguishes free-floating, systematic inventory, and even coverage; the practical rule is to match the coverage strategy to capacity, aim, and strain.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Method choice includes both a target range and a coverage strategy: let attention float, scan the range, hold the range broadly, or use a broader sequence as field clearing rather than shallow hopping.
- **Why this matters**: The same sensory territory can train different capacities depending on coverage: free-floating supports natural contact, inventory supports detail and completeness, and even coverage supports integration and expansive concentration.
- **Key tensions**: Broad coverage can strain or lose contact, inventory can become frustrating when little is detectable, and free-floating can be easy without necessarily being complete or well-routed.
- **Source posture**: Probable as a Shinzen-specific practice distinction from one focused YouTube talk, SHF zoom/range material, and retreat-stream body-location implementation; incomplete as a safety or developmental protocol.

## Model
[[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] gives the basic taxonomy:

- **Free-floating**: attention moves where it is pulled while the practitioner stays aware of where it goes. Shinzen's body example is noting body locations as sensations call attention ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S1-S2).
- **Systematic inventory**: attention scrolls through the possibilities, samples them, and may repeat the cycle. Body scanning, band-by-band scanning, limb-by-limb scanning, surface sweeping, through-body sweeping, and other continuous scans are examples ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S3).
- **Even coverage**: attention attempts to hold all relevant locations or sensory spaces together at once ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S4).

The strategies are not a hierarchy. Shinzen gives each a different affordance profile. Inventory ensures coverage and builds sensitization and detail, but it can become difficult when sensation is subtle or absent. Even coverage integrates and gives the big picture, but it is challenging because contact with parts can be lost. Free-floating is natural, easy, and often more enjoyable, though the talk's silence about disadvantages should not erase drift or avoidance risks ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S5-S7).

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] gives the most
extended body-location example in the current corpus. The practitioner stays
with body experience while attention is drawn to approximate anatomical
locations, whole-body regions, beyond-body space, repeated locations, or
local splash plus global spread; the labels support spatial resolution rather
than a fixed inventory route ([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant
Training]] S6). That confirms body-location noting as free-floating coverage
with possible zooming, not as body scanning by default.

[[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] gives the
retreat-stream Note Everything version of the same coverage problem. Broad
See/Hear/Feel tracking can initially feel too effortful or too busy, so
Shinzen routes the practitioner toward a narrower modality, re-noting one
event, or inclusive noting when co-occurrence is the actual field
([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] S4-S6). The
source also permits an advanced reframing of overwhelm as expansion, but only
after preserving the simplifying options ([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend
the Self Express the Source]] S5).

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] confirms that same coverage
logic and adds an even-coverage Space branch. Broad Note Everything may
free-float among See, Hear, and Feel, narrow to one modality, or re-note the
same event when flooding becomes too much. Later, whole-body coverage and
Zoom Beyond practice use expansive concentration: the field is held broadly,
and contact loss becomes either a reason to reestablish coverage or a Gone/
contraction window ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S2-S3,
S11-S12).

## Method Mappings
In Shinzen's own mapping, Focus In, Focus Out, and Focus on Rest are generally free-floating among their categories. Zooming out can create brief even-coverage moments inside those methods ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S8).

Focus on Flow can become the broadest even-coverage case when somatic, visual, and auditory flow are present and the practitioner zooms out to all sensory spaces at once ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S9).

Focus on Positive can work either way. A practitioner may free-float among positive image, talk, and body-feeling systems, or attempt even coverage across one, two, or all three inner spaces ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S10).

Existing SHF material supplies adjacent options. Focus range defines the set of experiences selected for a practice period, and zooming changes spatial scope inward, outward, beyond the object, or both inward and outward at once ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Starting Focus Ranges]] S1; [[See Hear Feel Introduction - Noting Nutshell and FAQ]] S7). The coverage taxonomy explains one reason those options matter: they change how much of the field attention tries to hold.

The Part 2 zooming talk sharpens the difference between coverage strategy and [[Zooming]]. Coverage strategy is the overall way attention covers a selected range. Zooming is the spatial-scope option during the focus phase after an event is acknowledged. Its default is non-control; its optional forms are narrowing to a small part, widening to the whole event, or holding the most intense point while spreading through the body ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S2-S5).

[[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]] adds a developmental relation between parts and whole. Shinzen says skillful work with individual parts can later support global awareness that covers a vast field without much effort, because the clarity momentum of local efforting remains present while Do Nothing contributes minimum effort ([[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]] S2-S7). This does not replace the free-floating, inventory, and even-coverage taxonomy; it explains one way broad coverage may become less effortful without losing precision.

[[Digging Holes]] adds a strategy metaphor adjacent to coverage. Shinzen
rejects the automatic dismissal of broad practice as many shallow holes and
offers a backhoe image: the practitioner can clear the whole field to one
level, then another, until the bottom is contacted everywhere ([[Digging
Holes]] S4-S7). This is not a fourth attentional micro-technique; it is a
way to understand broad or multi-method work as systematic field clearing
when it remains clear, productive, and integrated.

## Routing Rule
The talk's fallback rule is divide-and-conquer. If a practitioner is trying to work with a large piece of experience and cannot, they should work with something smaller. If even coverage fails, Shinzen suggests free-floating or, often better, systematic inventory, then building back toward coverage ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S11).

This makes coverage strategy a regulation variable:

- Use **free-floating** when the field needs ease, responsiveness, or immediate contact.
- Use **inventory** when the field needs systematic sampling, detail, or sensitization.
- Use **even coverage** when the field needs integration, broad contact, or expansive concentration.
- Use **backhoe-like field clearing** when broad practice is genuinely
  clearing levels of the field rather than avoiding depth in any one route.
- Use **divide-and-conquer** when broad coverage strains, collapses, or loses too much contact.

## Boundaries
Even coverage should not be treated as more advanced in a simple linear sense. Shinzen's own instruction is to hold the field as broadly as possible without straining, and he explicitly names contact loss as a difficulty ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S6).

Free-floating should not be treated as vague letting-go. It is still a mindful coverage strategy; attention can be allowed to move naturally while the practitioner knows where it is going.

Inventory should not become forced sensation production. When little is detectable, the practitioner may use rest or absence only when that absence is actually part of the focus range and can be detected as presence.

This page is not a clinical or safety protocol. It does not decide when body scanning, broad awareness, or strong attention is appropriate for trauma, panic, dissociation, medical pain, fatigue, obsessive checking, or destabilizing practice effects.

Zooming inherits that boundary. Local-global zooming can be useful with intensity in Shinzen's frame, but the Part 2 talk also says it may initially feel overwhelming or like loss of control, which requires support criteria not supplied by the coverage taxonomy ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S8-S10).

Parts-to-whole momentum inherits the same caution. A large field with little effort is useful only if contact with parts remains clear enough. Otherwise "whole" can mean lost differentiation, dullness, dissociation-like spacing out, or an aesthetic preference for openness.

## Implications
Practice routing should ask one more question after choosing a focus range:

- Should attention float, scan, or hold broadly?
- Is the current strategy producing CCE, or producing strain, dullness, avoidance, or loss of contact?
- Would zooming out help integrate the field, or would narrowing restore workable contact?
- Is the current issue coverage of the range, or zooming within one acknowledged event?
- Is systematic inventory needed to build detail before broad coverage becomes viable?
- Is broad practice functioning like field clearing, or like novelty-driven
  method hopping?
- Is broad, low-effort awareness carrying clarity momentum from parts work, or losing the parts?
- Is "as broadly as possible without straining" actually being honored?
