Short subtle instruction in response to a practitioner-report pattern.

## Why This Talk Matters
The wiki already had a field-level route for subtle Image/Talk/Feel
activation in [[Taking Temperature of Subconscious Processing]]. This talk
adds the even subtler threshold before activation becomes obvious: the
practitioner may know only that image space, talk space, or emotional-feeling
space is pulling attention. That pull counts as enough sensory information
to note and meet the system without needing to know the content.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: In Focus In, especially with inclusive noting, a practitioner may
  report that the field is not All Rest while also being unable to identify
  explicit See In, Hear In, Feel In, or a combination of them.
- **S2**: Shinzen frames this report not as a failure but as the beginning of
  a subtle practice object: the inner spaces are not restful even though
  explicit content is unavailable.
- **S3**: He identifies the relevant layer as subtle or subconscious
  processing: by definition the practitioner cannot observe subconscious
  visual, auditory, or somatic content the same way conscious content is
  observed, but cues of its activation can still be detected.
- **S4**: One detectable cue is subtle activity or flow: shimmering in image
  space, undercurrent-like talk, or emotional-body idling without a named
  emotion.
- **S5**: A still subtler cue is no explicit activity and no obvious flow,
  only a tug toward image space, talk space, emotional-feeling space, or all
  three.
- **S6**: Shinzen says the tug is why locating the inner spaces matters: if
  Focus In is not All Rest, the tug toward one or more spaces indicates that
  subconscious activation has begun there.
- **S7**: The practice move is to note or acknowledge the relevant inner
  space even without knowing what is being acknowledged; he treats that
  content-free acknowledgement as sufficient contact with subliminal flux.
- **S8**: Shinzen extends the practice interpretation toward no-self,
  suggesting that the subjective system may need only minimal thingness when
  subliminal activation is met at this level.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The talk protects a subtle report from being mislabeled as a
  problem. "Not All Rest, but not explicit activity" becomes a legitimate
  practice fork.
- **S3-S6**: The core discrimination is between content, activity, and
  spatial pull. A practitioner does not need recoverable image, words, or
  emotion before the inner system becomes workable.
- **S6**: The talk makes spatial location more than taxonomy: knowing image
  space, talk space, and emotional-feeling space lets a practitioner detect
  activation by the direction of pull.
- **S7**: The note does not claim content knowledge. It simply acknowledges
  the space where the tug is occurring.
- **S8**: The no-self language should be preserved as Shinzen's
  interpretation, not as an independent proof that ego has dissolved.

## Practice Handles
- During Focus In, distinguish three cases: explicit Image/Talk/Feel,
  subtle activity or flow in an inner space, and only a tug toward an inner
  space.
- If nothing is active and the field is genuinely restful, note All Rest or
  the relevant rest flavor.
- If the field is not restful but no explicit activity is present, check
  whether image space, talk space, emotional-feeling space, or multiple
  spaces feel as if they are pulling attention.
- Note the tug according to the active range: See In, Hear In, Feel In, a
  combination, or All In when appropriate. The note acknowledges the space,
  not a story about what is hidden there.
- Do not search for hidden content, memories, or diagnoses. The handle is
  content-free contact with a subtle sensory cue.
- If the cue is unavailable, do not manufacture it. Return to the actual
  object, Rest, or a simpler range.

## Summary
Shinzen describes a Focus In scenario in which a practitioner using inclusive
noting cannot truthfully report All Rest, but also cannot say whether Image,
Talk, emotional Feel, or a combination is active. He treats this as an
opening into subtle subconscious processing rather than as a practice
failure.

He first names more recognizable cues: a shimmer in image space, an
undercurrent of inner talk, or emotional-body idling without an explicit
emotion. Then he moves to the subtler version. The practitioner may not be
aware of any activity at all, yet may sense that image space, talk space, or
emotional-feeling space is tugging attention.

The instruction is to use the location of those inner spaces as the cue. If
the field is not All Rest, the pull toward one or more spaces can be noted as
the subtlest level of interactivity. Shinzen says this is enough: the
subjective system may only need to be acknowledged at the level of
subliminal flux, without content recovery.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen says the subconscious cannot be observed like conscious content,
  yet he also says activation cues can be monitored in real time. The
  distinction is useful only if the cue is not overread as content access.
- The report is both "not All Rest" and "no explicit activity"; the talk's
  value is preserving that middle case without collapsing it into blankness
  or analysis.
- The practice asks the student to note something without knowing what is
  being noted. That protects content-free contact but can become vague if
  the practitioner lacks spatial clarity.
- The talk is framed as beginner-relevant yet described by Shinzen as highly
  subtle, so guidance should normalize not detecting this cue.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not treat the tug as proof of hidden memory, trauma release, diagnosis,
  or a specific unconscious meaning.
- Do not manufacture tugs or shame a practitioner who only finds Rest,
  explicit activity, or no clear object.
- Do not use "subconscious processing" to bypass ordinary therapy,
  grounding, relational repair, or medical care when those are needed.
- Do not turn content-free noting into dissociative blankness, vague
  spacing out, or avoidance of painful content that is actually explicit.
- Do not treat the no-self language as proof that ego has become harmless or
  behaviorally transformed.
