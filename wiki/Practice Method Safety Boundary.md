This page owns technique-level safety and fit. It asks when a Shinzen method or option is improving CCE, and when the same instruction is producing strain, spaciness, avoidance, shame, compulsion, or overload.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Method choice is a fit hypothesis, not a hierarchy; the right method restores workable concentration, clarity, equanimity, support, and ordinary functioning.
- **Why this matters**: Gate 1 made the method menu richer, but every option needs criteria for simplifying, switching, softening, stopping, or adding support.
- **Key tensions**: The sources preserve flexible options but rarely define dosage, clinical thresholds, trauma criteria, or teacher authority limits.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's practice-routing map; incomplete as a safety protocol.

## Decision Matrix

## Continue, Simplify, Pause, Refer, Stop

Continue the method when it is green: CCE and ordinary functioning are
improving, the person can change methods without identity threat, and the
chosen technique is serving a live aim rather than a hierarchy.

Simplify when the case is yellow. Use fewer labels, a slower pace, a smaller
focus range, a gentler object, a shorter sit, a single technique, Rest,
grounding, ordinary sleep recovery, or competent guidance before adding more
complexity.

Switch methods when the racy/spacey or bear-down/ease-up diagnostic is clear:
racy Noting may need Do Nothing or Rest; spacey Do Nothing may need labels,
structure, or a more concrete object; overchoice may need a one-technique
session.

Pause practice optimization when method effects cannot be separated from
sleep loss, panic, trauma activation, medication or substance factors,
medical symptoms, ordinary-life impairment, teacher pressure, or altered-state
fixation.

Refer or stop first when the case is red. Medical care, mental-health
support, sleep support, recovery support, protection from coercive guidance,
or emergency help outranks adding another Shinzen technique.

## What We Know
[[Practice Entry and Method Choice]] begins with the broadest rule: the best path is what works for a given person at a given time, and comparison should name strengths and weaknesses as tendencies rather than absolutes ([[The Best Path]] S1, S5-S7).

[[See Hear Feel]] lowers performance pressure through the Four Okays: missing, guessing, lateness, averaging, and repeated notes can still be valid when there is a quality moment with sensory experience. It also requires comfortable label pace, corresponding words, and an equanimous voice rather than mechanical mantra ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Four Okays and Required vs Allowed]] S1-S9).

[[Noting]] and [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] give label gears. No-label tracking is ideal only when clarity and momentum are present; mental, spoken, strong, and forced spoken labels can restore traction when practice goes vague or steep. The boundary is that hard work, shame, social exposure, compulsion, panic, trauma activation, and retreat pressure may require softening, switching, stopping, or support rather than stronger labels ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S7-S12; [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S1-S8).

[[Zooming]] adds spatial control as an option, not a requirement. Default focus does not manipulate spatial attention; zooming in, zooming out, and local-global zooming can help with intense body sensation, but local-global practice may feel overwhelming or like loss of control and needs stop/support criteria ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S2-S10).

[[Do Nothing]] carries the non-effort boundary. Do Nothing is not continuous intention-monitoring, and racy Noting can route to Do Nothing while spacey Do Nothing can route back to Noting. Shinzen also says there is no reason to continue Do Nothing at that time if it is horribly agitating or uncomfortable ([[Do Nothing Meditation]] S5-S7).

[[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] adds a sole-method safeguard. Do Nothing may be the only workable technique for a practitioner with attention difficulty, but Shinzen says competent input should occur at least once or twice a year and later capacity to use systematic techniques is one sign that practice is working ([[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] S4-S6).

[[Effort Regulation]] gives the cross-test. Bearing down can mature into clarity; easing up can mature into release. One-sidedness is the risk: effort becomes drivenness, and no-goal language becomes aversion to systematic practice ([[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]] S5-S8).

[[Focus Coverage Strategies]] and [[Practice Cycles]] widen method safety into session design. Free-floating, systematic inventory, and even coverage can each help or strain; one-technique simplicity, workout sequencing, looping-and-branching, micro-hits, and challenge sequences all need load and context criteria ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S6, S11; [[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S1-S7; [[Intermediate FAQ Practice In Daily Life Micro-Hits & Challenge Sequences]] S1-S9).

[[Organizing Your Practice]] adds a trigger-practice edge. Shinzen says
formal stillness can deliberately include sights, sounds, or sensations that
evoke daily-life reactions, but the practice is safe only as a graded
training hypothesis: stimulus type, intensity, duration, timing, and
frequency must be controlled, and the practitioner should work near a
manageable edge rather than overburdening the system. The source gives no
clinical contraindications or trauma protocol ([[Organizing Your Practice]]
S5-S6).

[[Mindfulness with Sickness]] adds an illness-method edge. Shinzen says
sickness may become the retreat when it is the actual condition, but he
explicitly contrasts modern leniency with no-mercy temple austerity: sleep,
rest, and breaks are allowed. Method choice can alternate between turning
toward the illness sensory challenge and turning away with background
equanimity; the unsafe drift is converting sickness-as-retreat into pressure
to keep formal practice going through needed rest, symptom care, or medical
support ([[Mindfulness with Sickness]] S2-S8).

[[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] adds a
specific broad-practice safety edge. Shinzen motivates broad See/Hear/Feel
practice partly by its life portability, including task examples such as
driving, but the safe reading is sensory contact with the task, not inward
absorption, breath fixation, or altered-state practice. The same source gives
simplifying options when broad practice strains: narrow the modality, re-note
one event, use inclusive noting, alternate methods, or choose another
technique ([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]]
S4-S6).

[[Mindfulness Strategies when Interacting with Others]] adds the
conversation-method edge. Shinzen says some substantive conversations need
all available resources, while lighter interactions can leave enough room for
Focus Out, body-based lovingkindness, or reaction-noting. The method-safety
rule is therefore demand-sensitive: explicit technique during interaction is
green only when ordinary listening and response remain intact ([[Mindfulness
Strategies when Interacting with Others]] S1-S7).

[[Retroactive Meditation]] adds the demanding-work edge. Shinzen says that
intellectual work, writing, and figuring things out may not allow formal
technique while the work is happening. The practice move is to stop on a dime
when stopping is safe, start a technique immediately, and work with the
See/Hear/Feel activation left by the task. This sharpens task safety: some
life activity is better preserved first and processed afterward than diluted
by divided attention ([[Retroactive Meditation]] S1-S6).

[[There's No Need to Leave Samadhi]] adds the complementary state-carryover
edge. If a deep state is present and action is needed, Shinzen says one need
not leave samadhi in order to function; action can arise from the state, and
fear, disorientation, or inner-system reactivation can be equanimized and
observed as self-forming. The safety boundary is that this brief talk does
not define which actions are appropriate from such a state, so its "function
from samadhi" handle must be checked against task contact, ordinary
responsiveness, grounding, and altered-state differentials ([[There's No
Need to Leave Samadhi]] S3-S6).

[[Guided Meditation of Expansion and Contraction]] adds a subtle-detection
edge. Shinzen permits guessing and groping while practitioners look for
surface Expansion-Contraction and possible deep polarity, but he also tells
them to return to surface patterns when the deep version is unavailable. The
method-safety test is whether uncertainty remains light, reversible, and
clarity-oriented, or becomes strained subtle-experience chasing, pressure
spiritualization, or decentering overreach ([[Guided Meditation of Expansion
and Contraction]] S1-S7).

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] adds a long-retreat
method edge. Shinzen allows method switching when body-location practice
mostly produces discomfort, gives equanimity supports if the practitioner
stays with it, explicitly warns against body damage, and closes by limiting
driving practice to sensory contact with driving rather than altered or
inward practice ([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]]
S8, S12).

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] adds the Auto Move edge. Auto
Move is a legitimate motion practice when movement happens in a safe context
and its automatic quality is detected with CCE; the same source limits
driving and heavy machinery to sensory driving practice, so Auto Move and
Source-expression should not be used in safety-critical tasks ([[Four Ways
Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S5-S6, S14).

[[Auto Output Practice Family]] extends the same method-safety logic beyond
movement. Shinzen's channel shorts define Auto output across movement,
speech, thought, and combined practice: Auto Walk tastes reflexive movement,
Auto Chant lets speech organs self-organize, Auto Think lets thought
self-organize after Image/Talk/Both/Rest and mind-space work, and Auto
Everything combines branches only after individual elements are workable
([[Auto Walk Demonstrated by Shinzen Young]] S1-S3; [[Auto Chant or Auto
Speak]] S1-S4; [[Auto Think]] S1-S4; [[Auto Everything]] S1-S4). The safety
test broadens accordingly: automatic output does not certify safe movement,
truthful speech, reliable insight, social consent, professional competence,
or wise conduct.

[[Shinzen Young Retreat at the Monastic Academy 05.27.2017]] repeats and
extends that boundary. Auto Chant can be a speech-output practice in a retreat
container, but the close says driving meditation is limited to seeing,
hearing, and feeling the drive because cars and other forms have ordinary
consequences ([[Shinzen Young Retreat at the Monastic Academy 05.27.2017]]
S12-S15). This keeps Source-facing and automatic-output practices out of
safety-critical tasks.

[[Sleep Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] adds a night-method edge. Shinzen
recommends continuous technique during sleep disturbance so consciousness can
rest, but the same instruction should not become hard concentration,
self-surveillance, or a mandate to stay conscious all night ([[Sleep
Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] S5-S8).

[[Late Night Sitting Can Change Your Life]] adds the intentional Yaza edge.
Unlike the good-night's-rest source, this talk describes all-night sitting as
a supported retreat practice where sleepiness itself may be tracked as
Feel/See/Hear Rest, absorption, Image availability, and visual Flow. Its
safety value is that Shinzen also names next-day function, intense discomfort,
and the breakdown/abuse risk of sleep deprivation, so Yaza cannot be routed as
ordinary sleep advice, toughness proof, or a method for unsupervised intensity
([[Late Night Sitting Can Change Your Life]] S1-S8).

[[Penetrating Sleepiness]] adds a shorter ordinary-sitting edge. Shinzen says
to repeatedly straighten the spine and open the eyes while enjoying pleasant
sleepiness waves, equanimizing discomfort, and penetrating sleepiness as a
sensory event. This is useful only as a bounded sitting-practice correction;
the clip does not license pushing through chronic sleep loss, blackouts,
medical fatigue, medication or substance effects, unsafe posture collapse, or
dangerous drowsiness ([[Penetrating Sleepiness]] S2-S5).

[[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] adds a dream-method
edge. Shinzen says lucid dream practice can use the same Five Ways and
Sensory Grid labels as waking practice, with dream events labeled by how they
present and dream melting or permeability treated as Flow. The source does
not define induction, dosage, nightmare, trauma, sleep, dissociation, or
clinical criteria, so dream practice remains optional and bounded rather
than a method everyone should train ([[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways
Mindfulness Meditation]] S2-S8).

[[Tea, Coffee and Meditation]] adds a stimulant-method edge. Shinzen says
there is no automatic problem with a stimulant when it does not make the
practitioner jangled, because meditation includes clarity and alertness as
well as tranquility. The method-safety question is whether caffeine is
supporting workable CCE or producing racy effort, agitation, insomnia,
compulsion, medical concerns, or ordinary-functioning cost ([[Tea, Coffee and
Meditation]] S2-S4).

[[Consciously Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem Mutata Resurgo]] adds an
active-object dropout edge. Shinzen says a practitioner working with sight or
touch can briefly disconnect from that system as reverie, sleepiness, or
another pull begins; the resulting sudden Rest can be a tell that attention
has decoupled. The method-safety question is whether the practitioner can use
that Rest cue to return or deliberately switch branches, rather than turning
dullness, microsleep, avoidance, or dissociation into an advanced claim
([[Consciously Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem Mutata Resurgo]] S1-S4).

## Boundary Questions
- Is the technique increasing CCE, or just increasing performance pressure?
- Would fewer options, weaker labels, a narrower field, a different technique, rest, grounding, or guidance be wiser now?
- Is Do Nothing releasing control, or worsening spaciness, confusion, passivity, panic, or dissociative drift?
- Is broad awareness retaining parts-level clarity, or becoming vague openness?
- Is broad life practice preserving the task itself, especially in safety-
  critical activity, or turning into divided attention, inward withdrawal, or
  altered-state seeking?
- Is conversation practice leaving enough attention for real listening,
  consent, repair, and role competence, or has hidden technique become
  relational unresponsiveness?
- If sickness is the context, is the method respecting rest, sleep, breaks,
  symptom care, and medical support, or is illness-as-retreat becoming
  austerity pressure?
- Is Auto output training safe automaticity detection, or becoming impulsive
  movement, performative spontaneity, compulsive motion or speech, unsafe
  task attention, unreviewed "insight," or Source-authorized conduct?
- Is subtle detection using light guessing to improve clarity, or chasing an advanced experience that is not currently available?
- Is discomfort-heavy body practice producing workable purification taste,
  or should the practitioner switch methods, protect the body, or stop
  optimizing meditation first?
- Are micro-hits and challenge sequences improving ordinary responsiveness, or interrupting the task that needs doing?
- Is retroactive meditation preserving demanding work first and practicing
  after a safe stop, or becoming divided attention, procrastination, or
  unsafe interruption?
- Is samadhi-to-function practice preserving task contact and ordinary
  responsiveness, or turning fear, disorientation, or state protection into
  unsafe action?
- Is trigger practice graded by type, intensity, duration, timing, and
  frequency, or has it become flooding, outrage training, self-punishment, or
  unsupervised trauma exposure?
- Is nighttime technique restful and simple, or is it reinforcing insomnia worry, sleep deprivation, or compulsive monitoring?
- If ordinary sitting sleepiness is present, can posture and eyes-open alertness be restored while sleepiness remains a clear sensory object, or is the person drifting, forcing, blacking out, or needing sleep/support?
- If Yaza or all-night sitting is proposed, is it a supported, optional retreat practice with recovery and function checks, or is sleep deprivation becoming coercion, endurance pressure, altered-state chasing, or breakdown risk?
- Is dream-state practice using lucidity that is already present, or is it creating sleep disruption, dream-control chasing, or altered-state fixation?
- Is caffeine supporting alert clarity, or is it being mistaken for clarity while producing jangling, strain, sleep loss, compulsion, or health risk?
- Is sudden Rest during active-object practice a clear decoupling cue, or is it sleepiness, reverie, dullness, dissociation, avoidance, or loss of the intended object?

## Evidence Needed
- Labeling-intensity criteria for no labels, mental labels, spoken labels, strong labels, forced labels, equanimity voice, repeated notes, and repeat-until-Gone.
- Do Nothing routing for spaciness, agitation, sleepiness, confusion, ADD, panic, dissociation, trauma, and clinical or medication factors.
- Zooming criteria for intense sensation, overwhelm, loss-of-control impressions, and somatic activation.
- Method-fit, coverage, session-setup, micro-hit, trigger-practice, and challenge-sequence criteria for simplifying, switching, stopping, grounding, or seeking guidance.
- Conversation-practice criteria for distinguishing useful Focus Out,
  body-based lovingkindness, and reaction-noting from divided attention,
  social performance, relational avoidance, or task-incompatible practice.
- Sleep-disturbance and sinking criteria for when continuous technique or posture/eyes-open correction supports rest and clarity versus when it becomes effortful monitoring, passive dullness, unsafe fatigue, or needs medical, clinical, or ordinary sleep support.
- Lucid-dream method criteria for induction, dosage, nightmare or trauma content, parasomnias, dissociation, mania/psychosis risk, sleep disruption, and daytime impairment.
- Caffeine/stimulant criteria for timing, dose, tolerance, dependence, withdrawal, sleep impact, anxiety or mania-like activation, medication interactions, medical risk, and ordinary functioning.
- Decoupling/dropout criteria for distinguishing useful Rest cues from dullness, microsleep, reverie, dissociation, avoidance, fatigue, and premature Source interpretation.
- Body-location and driving-transition criteria for distinguishing workable
  discomfort practice from body-risk endurance, and sensory driving from
  altered-state or inward practice.
- Auto output criteria for safe setting, stopping, social exposure, injury
  risk, vocal strain, interpersonal speech, thought-output review,
  dissociation, compulsive movement or speech, and task incompatibility.
