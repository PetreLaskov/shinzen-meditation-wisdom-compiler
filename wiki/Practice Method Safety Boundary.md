This page owns technique-level safety and fit. It asks when a Shinzen method or option is improving CCE, and when the same instruction is producing strain, spaciness, avoidance, shame, compulsion, or overload.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Method choice is a fit hypothesis, not a hierarchy; the right method restores workable concentration, clarity, equanimity, support, and ordinary functioning.
- **Why this matters**: Gate 1 made the method menu richer, but every option needs criteria for simplifying, switching, softening, stopping, or adding support.
- **Key tensions**: The sources preserve flexible options but rarely define dosage, clinical thresholds, trauma criteria, or teacher authority limits.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's practice-routing map; incomplete as a safety protocol.

## What We Know
[[Practice Entry and Method Choice]] begins with the broadest rule: the best path is what works for a given person at a given time, and comparison should name strengths and weaknesses as tendencies rather than absolutes ([[The Best Path]] S1, S5-S7).

[[See Hear Feel]] lowers performance pressure through the Four Okays: missing, guessing, lateness, averaging, and repeated notes can still be valid when there is a quality moment with sensory experience. It also requires comfortable label pace, corresponding words, and an equanimous voice rather than mechanical mantra ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Four Okays and Required vs Allowed]] S1-S9).

[[Noting]] and [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] give label gears. No-label tracking is ideal only when clarity and momentum are present; mental, spoken, strong, and forced spoken labels can restore traction when practice goes vague or steep. The boundary is that hard work, shame, social exposure, compulsion, panic, trauma activation, and retreat pressure may require softening, switching, stopping, or support rather than stronger labels ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S7-S12; [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S1-S8).

[[Zooming]] adds spatial control as an option, not a requirement. Default focus does not manipulate spatial attention; zooming in, zooming out, and local-global zooming can help with intense body sensation, but local-global practice may feel overwhelming or like loss of control and needs stop/support criteria ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S2-S10).

[[Do Nothing]] carries the non-effort boundary. Do Nothing is not continuous intention-monitoring, and racy Noting can route to Do Nothing while spacey Do Nothing can route back to Noting. Shinzen also says there is no reason to continue Do Nothing at that time if it is horribly agitating or uncomfortable ([[Do Nothing Meditation]] S5-S7).

[[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] adds a sole-method safeguard. Do Nothing may be the only workable technique for a practitioner with attention difficulty, but Shinzen says competent input should occur at least once or twice a year and later capacity to use systematic techniques is one sign that practice is working ([[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] S4-S6).

[[Effort Regulation]] gives the cross-test. Bearing down can mature into clarity; easing up can mature into release. One-sidedness is the risk: effort becomes drivenness, and no-goal language becomes aversion to systematic practice ([[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]] S5-S8).

[[Focus Coverage Strategies]] and [[Practice Cycles]] widen method safety into session design. Free-floating, systematic inventory, and even coverage can each help or strain; one-technique simplicity, workout sequencing, looping-and-branching, micro-hits, and challenge sequences all need load and context criteria ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S6, S11; [[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S1-S7; [[Intermediate FAQ Practice In Daily Life Micro-Hits & Challenge Sequences]] S1-S9).

## Boundary Questions
- Is the technique increasing CCE, or just increasing performance pressure?
- Would fewer options, weaker labels, a narrower field, a different technique, rest, grounding, or guidance be wiser now?
- Is Do Nothing releasing control, or worsening spaciness, confusion, passivity, panic, or dissociative drift?
- Is broad awareness retaining parts-level clarity, or becoming vague openness?
- Are micro-hits and challenge sequences improving ordinary responsiveness, or interrupting the task that needs doing?

## Evidence Needed
- Labeling-intensity criteria for no labels, mental labels, spoken labels, strong labels, forced labels, equanimity voice, repeated notes, and repeat-until-Gone.
- Do Nothing routing for spaciness, agitation, sleepiness, confusion, ADD, panic, dissociation, trauma, and clinical or medication factors.
- Zooming criteria for intense sensation, overwhelm, loss-of-control impressions, and somatic activation.
- Method-fit, coverage, session-setup, micro-hit, and challenge-sequence criteria for simplifying, switching, stopping, grounding, or seeking guidance.
