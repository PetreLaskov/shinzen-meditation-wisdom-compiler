## Practice Handles
- Treat parenting practice as immediate application, not retreat afterglow: feed, hold, respond, clean, protect, and coordinate care while remaining available to touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, and Talk.
- Let contact with the baby be sensory before it becomes story: look, listen, touch, and notice expansion, contraction, aliveness, distress, rest, and responsiveness.
- Use infant distress to train compassion only if it increases ordinary care and responsiveness; do not use Source language to ignore crying, medical needs, parental exhaustion, or support needs.
- When raw fear appears in the parent, parse it first as Feel, then notice whether Image or Talk is added to explain its magnitude.
- Keep pediatric care, postpartum mental health, trauma support, sleep, family labor, consent, and safety outside the meditation teacher's authority.

## Summary
Shinzen completes the mindful-birth frame by turning to Zen parenting. Birth may blast the practitioner into Source-like intensity, but parenting immediately demands function. He compares this with Zen sanzen or dokusan: one contacts the teacher's dynamic nothingness, then must take it back into cold, difficult meditation-hall conditions. The point is not to worship a guru-zap but to become strong enough to hold and apply what was received.

He then applies the metaphor to the baby. The baby becomes the parent's "little Roshi," still linked to Source and showing dynamic nothingness through touch, sight, and sound. The parent can perceive expansion, contraction, and Zero in the baby and use that contact in the challenges of care.

In Q&A, a student relates a prior experience of huge raw fear in Feel space that later recruited an image to represent its size. Shinzen asks clarifying phenomenological questions and frames it as chaotic primitive Feel. The student says this gives compassion for a baby's distress: instead of merely reacting to crying, she can sense the infant's primordial freak-out. Shinzen affirms the compassion effect.

## Source Frame
The source is contemplative parenting instruction by metaphor, not parenting science. Its reliable Shinzen contribution is the applied practice frame: transmission, Source contact, and raw infant-like Feel are validated by practical functioning and compassion, not by devotional passivity, metaphysical certainty, or role inflation.

- The student's raw-fear report is one experiential analogy to infant distress, not evidence that adult primordial Feel accurately models what babies experience.
- The transcript gives no criteria for distinguishing compassion training from projection, anxiety, postpartum distress, trauma activation, or intrusive imagery.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk treats the baby as a Source-linked Roshi while ordinary ethics require the baby to remain a dependent child whose needs govern the parent's action.
- The source values intense transmission but criticizes passive guru-zap consumption; practice is validated by application under difficulty.
- The raw-fear analogy can increase compassion, but it can also project adult contemplative experience onto infant distress without developmental evidence.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not use "baby as Roshi" to make a child responsible for a parent's spiritual growth.
- Do not treat infant crying as only a meditation object; check ordinary care, safety, hunger, pain, illness, sleep, and support.
- Do not use Source or dynamic-nothingness language to bypass postpartum depression, anxiety, trauma, intrusive thoughts, rage, psychosis risk, or caregiver exhaustion.
- Do not make raw fear into a developmental diagnosis or trauma explanation without qualified support.
- Do not turn immediate functioning into pressure to ignore the parent's recovery, consent, sleep, or need for help.
