## Teaching Register
Short advanced instruction with idiolect. Shinzen is not giving a full Rest, Do Nothing, sleep, or Source teaching; he is preserving a subtle diagnostic sign inside object-based practice.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk adds a micro-router for a common practice edge. When attention is working with active sights, touch, or object spaces, a sudden restful blanking or thinning can mean attention is being pulled from the object. The useful response is often to notice the decoupling and return, not to moralize it as failure or overread it as attainment.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: In an active sight or touch practice, attention can briefly disconnect from the chosen sensory system when reverie, sleepiness, or another pull begins.
- **S2**: That disconnection may produce a brief restful state; the Rest is a "tell" that the practitioner is being pulled away from the meditation object.
- **S3**: If the practitioner recognizes the one- or two-second Rest shift, the recognition can bring attention back to the chosen object.
- **S4**: Shinzen distinguishes ordinary decoupling from possible deep dropping out: what would be "not out" for a beginner may, after long practice, be a dropout from time-space and a moment with Source.
- **S5**: The phrase "eadem mutata resurgo" is used as a poetic return marker: one drops back into the world "the same but changed."

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: Rest is not only a chosen object. It can also be a diagnostic sign that active-object contact has thinned because attention is leaving the object field.
- **S4**: The same surface event, loss of object contact, needs capacity-sensitive interpretation. For a beginner it may be sleepiness or reverie; for a mature practitioner it may sometimes be a deeper dropout.
- **S5**: The resurrection phrase preserves the return-to-world framing: the relevant endpoint is dropping back into ordinary life changed, not lingering in a blank.

## Practice Handles
- During an active sight/touch practice, watch for a sudden shift from active contact to Rest, blankness, defocusing, thinning, or eyes sinking.
- Treat the shift first as information: attention may be decoupling from the object because sleepiness, reverie, or another object is taking over.
- If the intent is active-object practice, use the noticed Rest as a cue to return to the object rather than as a reason to chase Rest.
- If decoupling repeatedly opens deep Rest, consider a deliberate Focus on Rest branch only when it remains clear, embodied, and not merely dullness, sleepiness, or avoidance.

## Summary
Shinzen says that even with eyes open and the physical world present, attention can disconnect from an active sensory system such as sight or touch. That disconnect may create a brief restful state: the practitioner is no longer fully looking or sensing the object, and attention is going elsewhere. He calls this "decoupling" and treats the brief Rest as a tell that the practitioner is being pulled away.

The practical instruction is to learn the tell. If the practitioner catches the one- or two-second restful shift before eyes sink, awareness thins, or reverie takes over, the recognition itself can return attention to the chosen object. Shinzen then adds an advanced caveat: when decoupling drops into very deep Rest, it may be a Focus on Rest situation rather than a simple failure. What a beginner would call "not out" may, after long practice, be a dropout from the time-space continuum, followed by returning "the same but changed."

## Source Frame
The source is trying to make an ephemeral transition detectable. It does not argue that all blanking, drifting, or loss of object contact is good practice. The oral frame is subtle and practice-specific: the same surface sign can be distraction, sleepiness, a Rest object, or an advanced dropout depending on capacity, clarity, and context.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The same brief Rest event is treated both as a warning cue for losing the object and as a possible doorway into deep Rest. Future guidance must ask what practice branch is intended before evaluating it.
- Deep dropout language could invite attainment comparison, but the talk's practical move is modest: notice the tell and return, unless the practitioner has enough context and maturity to work consciously with Rest.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Dropping out" should not be used to sanctify spacing out, dissociation, sleep deprivation, or loss of ordinary functioning.
- "Moment with Source" should not become objective-reality proof, attainment certification, or a reason to ignore sleepiness, safety, or guidance.
- Decoupling is not evidence that active-object practice is failing; it is a cue that can be folded back into practice.
