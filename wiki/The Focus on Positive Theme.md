This short instruction places Focus on Positive inside live method choice. Shinzen says unusually positive Image, Talk, or Feel may arise spontaneously or be deliberately created, and the practitioner can either deconstruct it, formally hold it as Focus on Positive, or temporarily think and write in the ordinary way when it functions as an insight window.

## Teaching Register
This is conceptual instruction with method-choice guidance. Shinzen is not giving a guided meditation; he is telling practitioners how to interpret positive inner events that may arise during ordinary observation practice.

## Why This Talk Matters
The durable value is the routing rule. Positive content is not automatically a sign to chase, suppress, or privilege. It can be treated matter-of-factly as sensory material, converted into a formal positive-cultivation practice, or occasionally explored through ordinary thought when it appears to open an insight window.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Focus on Positive can begin either when positive Image, Talk, or Feel arises spontaneously during observation practice or when the practitioner intentionally creates it.
- **S2**: A powerful positive Image may be noted in ordinary Focus In, held intentionally as a positive Image, or used as a meaningful directional image.
- **S3**: A positive wisdom word, phrase, or sentence in Talk may be observed matter-of-factly or intentionally held as Focus on Positive, and such phrases can also be deliberately created.
- **S4**: Positive Feel includes usually pleasant emotional body sensations, compassion-like sad-warm tones, positive behavioral or aspirational direction, concentration-related pleasure, Flow or Rest-related pleasantness, and intrinsic joy without a reason.
- **S5**: When unusually positive subjective content arises, Shinzen gives three good choices: deconstruct it as Feel/Image/Talk, formally hold or repeat it as Focus on Positive, or suspend formal practice briefly for ordinary discursive thought and note-taking when insight seems to be opening.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The source adds a spontaneous-positive branch to the previous create-hold-radiate definition; Focus on Positive is not only deliberate manufacturing.
- **S2-S3**: Image and Talk can shift from observation objects into intentional cultivation objects when their practical significance is clear.
- **S4**: Positive Feel is broader than cheerfulness: it can include compassion, concentration pleasure, Flow, Rest, and causeless joy, while Shinzen admits some classification fuzziness.
- **S5**: The three-choice rule prevents both positivity chasing and rigid anti-thinking. Matter-of-fact deconstruction, formal positive cultivation, and occasional ordinary thinking can all be appropriate.

## Practice Handles
- If a positive Image, phrase, or body-feeling arises, first notice that a choice is available; do not assume the event must be privileged or ignored.
- Choose deconstruction when the practice aim is to remain matter-of-fact: it is still just Feel, Image, and Talk.
- Choose Focus on Positive when the positive content has enough significance to hold, repeat, maintain, or combine across Talk, Image, and Feel.
- Choose ordinary discursive thought and writing only occasionally, when the event seems to open an insight window; know that formal meditation is being suspended.
- Avoid turning a meditation session into only discursive thinking.

## Phenomenological Distinctions
- **Spontaneous versus intentional positive**: Positive Image, Talk, or Feel can show up without being manufactured, or it can be deliberately created and held.
- **Matter-of-fact versus significant**: The same positive event can be treated as ordinary sensory material or as personally meaningful enough to cultivate.
- **Positive Feel range**: Pleasantness, compassion-like sadness, aspiration, concentration pleasure, Flow/Rest pleasure, and causeless joy all appear inside the positive Feel family, though Shinzen flags classification uncertainty.
- **Insight window versus formal practice**: Discursive thought can be a deliberate temporary choice, not merely distraction, when it is knowingly used to pursue an insight.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by contrasting simple observation of the subjective Feel/Image/Talk system with intentionally finding, creating, holding, and radiating positive states. He says positive images may arise spontaneously, including deep directional images, or may be intentionally created; either way, the practitioner can simply note them or intentionally hold them as Focus on Positive. The same applies to wisdom words, phrases, and sentences in Talk.

He then broadens positive Feel beyond simple pleasant emotion. It usually means pleasant emotional body sensation, but can include compassion-like sadness, aspirations for one's own behavior or others' welfare, concentration-related pleasure, the pleasantness of Flow or Rest, and intrinsic joy without a reason.

The main instruction is a three-way routing rule. When unusually positive visual, auditory, or somatic subjective content arises, the practitioner can deconstruct it matter-of-factly as Feel/Image/Talk, formalize it as Focus on Positive by holding or repeating it, or occasionally suspend formal practice to think and write in the ordinary way when nature seems to have opened an insight window. Shinzen explicitly says all three choices are good, while cautioning that meditation should not become only discursive thought.

## Weakest Claims
- The discussion of Native fasting and vision quest is anecdotal and compressed; it should not be used as a reliable anthropology source.
- The category boundary for compassion-like sad positive Feel is explicitly uncertain in the talk.
- "Intrinsic joy for no reason" and concentration/Flow/Rest-related pleasantness are teaching classifications, not independent evidence of a universal affect taxonomy.
- The three choices are all described as good, but the talk does not give detailed criteria for which is best in difficult, compulsive, dissociative, manic, or trauma-related cases.

## Important Omissions
- No explicit safety criteria for positive-content chasing, spiritual inflation, mania-like enthusiasm, trauma bypass, or inability to feel positive.
- No dosage guidance for how long to hold positive content or how often to suspend practice for discursive thinking.
- No behavior-verification standard for positive aspiration, behavioral direction, or insight that arises in Talk/Image/Feel.
- No explanation of how to distinguish an authentic insight window from distraction, avoidance, or novelty seeking.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Focus on Positive intentionally holds content, while Focus In deconstructs the same content; the talk resolves this as choice rather than contradiction.
- Discursive thought is allowed occasionally, but ordinary meditation should not become only discursive thought.
- Positive spiritual imagery can be meaningful, but Shinzen also uses the Zen-gods story to warn that even exalted content may need to be treated as ordinary sensory material.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "All choices are good" could be misread as no need for discernment, support, or consequences.
- Directional images and wisdom phrases could be inflated into unquestioned guidance or spiritual authority.
- Discursive insight work could become rumination, planning, or avoidance while being labeled meditation.
- Holding positive content could become suppression of grief, fear, anger, repair needs, or clinical symptoms.
- Causeless joy, Flow, and Rest pleasure could become state-chasing rather than trainable sensory clarity and equanimity.
