## Teaching Register
Brief advanced instruction and series conclusion. Shinzen uses Source, womb-of-Zero, and enlightenment language, but the operational teaching is a practice dialectic: experience oneness without becoming averse to distinctions, and make distinctions without losing the unitive frame.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk prevents the series from being read as "drop analysis and stay in unity." Shinzen explicitly says tracking Feel, Image, Talk, touch, sight, and sound is not a regression into self and materiality; it is the kind of empowering distinction that untangles experience.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Self and world, or I and thou, can be understood as coming from and returning to the same "womb of zero" moment by moment.
- **S2**: Shinzen contrasts ordinary vulnerable thing-self with enlightened self-understanding as "I just came from the source."
- **S3**: The path should move dialectically between healthy oneness and healthy discrimination rather than become one-sided on either side.
- **S4**: Refusing to track Feel/Image/Talk or touch/sight/sound because those distinctions seem selfing or materialistic is a mistake; those distinctions can be empowering.
- **S5**: Discrimination turns multiplicative overwhelm into additive manageability by teasing tangled experience apart.
- **S6**: Shinzen treats the sensory category list as containing the most important healthy distinctions for psychological personal growth and spiritual transpersonal growth.
- **S7**: The category list distinguishes somatic, visual, and auditory experience; subjective Feel/Image/Talk from objective touch/sight/sound; and six corresponding restful states.
- **S8**: Breaking thought into visual and auditory components makes thought tangible, spatial, trackable, and therefore tractable; with training, the depths of the being can keep track automatically.
- **S9**: Repeated movement between healthy distinctions and healthy oneness marks the path to what Shinzen calls psycho-spiritual super adulthood.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S3-S4**: The series completion is dialectical. Oneness does not cancel ordinary sensory tracking; sensory tracking remains a liberating move.
- **S5**: "Multiplicative overwhelm" versus "additive manageability" gives future practice reasoning a compact rule for why decomposition can reduce suffering.
- **S7-S8**: The sensory taxonomy is justified by use: it makes thought, emotion, and self-world experience observable enough to work with.

## Practice Handles
- If oneness, Flow, or emptiness language creates aversion to ordinary labels, return to Feel/Image/Talk and touch/sight/sound as empowering distinctions.
- When experience is overwhelming, tease apart the sensory strands rather than treating the whole tangle as one fused problem.
- Break thought into visual Image and auditory Talk so it becomes spatially tangible and easier to track.
- Treat restful states as discriminable practice objects, not as a vague calm background.
- Let practice alternate: distinguish components, unify through Flow/Source/oneness when available, then distinguish again without shame or regression anxiety.

## Summary
Shinzen begins from the previous talk's I-Thou frame. Self and world are separate but never completely separate because both arise from and return to Zero moment by moment. He names ordinary vulnerability as thinking "I am a thing," while enlightenment is framed as recognizing that one has just come from Source.

The main correction is that unification should not become one-sided. Shinzen says healthy oneness and healthy discrimination are empowering contrasts, and the path moves back and forth between them. A practitioner should not refuse to track Feel, Image, Talk, touch, sight, and sound out of fear that distinctions will mire them in self and materiality. Those distinctions are precisely what let experience untangle and become manageable.

He then compresses the category system. Somatic, visual, and auditory experience can be divided into subjective Feel/Image/Talk and objective touch/sight/sound; smell and taste are folded into touch; six corresponding restful states matter because they lead to tranquil experience. Breaking thinking into visual and auditory components makes thought tangible and spatial, hence trackable and tractable. The repeated alternation of healthy distinction and healthy oneness is Shinzen's closing marker for psycho-spiritual maturity.

## Source Frame
The talk mixes deep metaphysical rhetoric with practical category design. Its Source and enlightenment claims are Shinzen's contemplative interpretation. The strongest operational claim is narrower: sensory distinctions are useful because they reduce overwhelm, make thought and emotion trackable, and keep unitive practice from becoming an anti-discrimination stance.

## Weakest Claims
- "Enlightenment" is described briefly as thinking "I just came from the source"; this is evocative teaching language, not an operational definition or verification criterion.
- The claim that the category list contains "all and only" the most important healthy distinctions is a strong system-design claim, not an independently established taxonomy.
- "Psycho-spiritual super adulthood" is not defined with behavioral or developmental criteria in this short talk.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not define when discrimination becomes over-analysis, obsessive tracking, or strain.
- It does not give safety criteria for Source, I-Thou, invulnerability, or oneness language.
- It does not explain how to verify automatic tracking by the "depths of the being."
- It does not compare the category list against other psychological, clinical, or contemplative taxonomies.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen says the category list contains all and only the most important healthy distinctions, while existing pages preserve the grid as pragmatic and partly conventional, especially around smell/taste and Feel In/Feel Out.
- The talk praises invulnerability through Source recognition, but safety pages must preserve ordinary vulnerability, consent, harm, medical limits, and support needs.
- Oneness and discrimination are both desirable, but the transcript gives no concrete dosage rule for when to switch from one to the other.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "I just came from the source" should not become spiritual invulnerability, risk denial, or bypass of ordinary human limits.
- "All and only" should not turn the sensory grid into rigid ontology or a reason to ignore context, therapy, ethics, or behavior.
- "Automatic tracking" should not become a claim that practice no longer needs feedback, guidance, or ordinary accountability.
