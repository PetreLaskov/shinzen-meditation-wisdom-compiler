## Practice Handles
For a practitioner, the talk supports precise reporting to a teacher: what happened after the technique matters because it determines the next branch.

For a guide, the talk preserves a coaching loop:

- Set up a technique.
- Return after a short interval and ask what occurred.
- Interpret the reported result as one of several possible branches.
- Keep the same guidance, slightly modify it, give information, offer options, or suggest a different move.
- Treat student feedback as data that may improve the method itself.

For an interpreter, the talk adds a vocabulary rule: preserve Shinzen's attempt to describe the surroundings of deep experience precisely, while not turning precision into a claim that the wordless core itself is captured by concepts.

## Summary
Shinzen answers a question about his teaching specialties by naming "taking the mist out of mysticism" first. He distinguishes occult or power-related uses of mysticism from the scholarly and contemplative use that concerns deep states and what lies beyond them. He accepts the traditional point that the core of direct Source-contact has no words, but argues that the surrounding territory can be described with increasing precision, perhaps eventually with a rigor comparable to science and mathematics.

He then names interactive personal coaching as another specialty. In retreat or phone practice, he describes giving a technique, leaving the student to practice briefly, returning to ask what happened, and branching the instruction according to the answer. The exercise-station analogy makes the Five Ways a training environment that can be optimized by a nearby coach.

Finally, Shinzen says the system was not developed alone. He describes three decades of intense collaboration with students and claims that many innovations came from student discoveries. This makes the system, in his telling, a distributed collaborative endeavor rather than only a top-down teaching invention.
