## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen defines the Focus Out range in this clip as external sound, external sight, and physical body sensation, using the labels Sound, Sight, and Touch.
- **S2**: He instructs the student to note out loud whatever is available; if more than one object is available, choose one, focus intently for a few seconds, then note again.
- **S3**: He starts eyes closed for settlement, then adds eyes-open sight, clarifying that Sight means what is seen with the eyes.
- **S4**: He says what pulls attention away from Touch/Sight/Sound is Feel/Image/Talk: reactions to outer events or independent memory, planning, and fantasy.
- **S5**: He frames the practice as expansive concentration: when inward thought and emotion contract attention, the practitioner works outward into Touch/Sight/Sound.
- **S6**: After spoken labels stabilize, he has the student switch to mental labels.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: The range is defined before the technique begins. Shinzen does not just say "be present"; he specifies which sensory categories count for this practice and verifies the student's understanding.
- **S2, S6**: Spoken labels function as live quality control before mental labels. The instruction to choose one available object keeps the beginner from trying to hold the whole outer field at once.
- **S4-S5**: Feel/Image/Talk is not treated as bad experience. It is the inward pull that becomes background when the selected training range is Touch/Sight/Sound.
- **S7**: Transfer is introduced early but cautiously: formal labels can mature toward unlabeled awareness during ordinary activity, but the clip does not make that a beginner requirement.

## Practice Handles
For a practitioner or guide:

- Define the Focus Out range explicitly: Touch is non-emotional physical body sensation, Sight is eyes-open seeing, and Sound is external sound.
- Start with one or two outer modalities if three are too much; then add Sight after the student is settled.
- Use spoken labels first when the guide needs to hear pace, tone, and object selection.
- If multiple outer events are present, choose one rather than trying to label everything.
- When Feel/Image/Talk pulls inward, let that range go and return to Touch/Sight/Sound if Focus Out remains the chosen practice.
- Shift from spoken to mental labels only after the student can maintain contact.
- Treat conversation or motion transfer as a later carryover test, not as a reason to reduce ordinary task or relational responsiveness.
