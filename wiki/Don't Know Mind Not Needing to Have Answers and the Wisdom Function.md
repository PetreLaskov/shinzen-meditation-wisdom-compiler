## Teaching Register
Technical explanatory teaching with analogy, comparative references, and light humor. Shinzen uses compulsive eating as the opening analogy: working through compulsion does not stop eating; similarly, working through the need for answers does not stop knowing.

## Why This Talk Matters
The earlier Total Happiness source already makes Don't Know Mind the mind-side practice bridge. This talk gives the missing micro-mechanism: the pressure to find meaning is not just cognitive content; it has Image/Talk patterning and Feel reward/punishment loops that can be trained with equanimity.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen compares answer-hunger to compulsive eating: when the compulsion is worked through, the basic function continues in a healthier and more fulfilling way.
- **S2**: The drive to find meaning involves Image/Talk patterning plus Feel rewards when meaning is found and Feel discomforts such as fear, sadness, impatience, and agitation when meaning is absent.
- **S3**: Don't Know Mind can be systematically trained by working through the Feel/Image/Talk forces that drive the need for meaning.
- **S4**: Humans inevitably face not-knowing because new knowledge creates new questions, theoretical limits to knowledge exist, and aging brings decline in mental capacities.
- **S5**: Working through the need to know does not end answer-making; it changes knowing into a more fulfilling mode Shinzen calls wisdom function, insight, wisdom, or, at the extreme, enlightenment.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The compulsive-eating analogy preserves a boundary: the practice is not anti-intellectual blankness, because the ordinary knowing function remains.
- **S2**: The Feel/Image/Talk analysis makes answer-hunger a workable inner sensory object rather than only a philosophical stance.
- **S3**: The aging example makes Don't Know practical, not merely mystical: equanimity with cognitive decline is part of the practice frame, while not replacing ordinary care.
- **S5**: "Wisdom function" is useful idiolect for knowing after answer-compulsion relaxes, but the source does not define a separate faculty with diagnostic criteria.

## Practice Handles
- When answer-pressure is active, track not only the question content but also Image/Talk patterning and the body rewards or penalties around meaning.
- Treat confusion, muddle, spinning, fear, sadness, impatience, and agitation as the sensory forces driving the need to know.
- Work toward equanimity with the need for meaning rather than suppressing thought or refusing ordinary investigation.
- Preserve ordinary inquiry and care: Don't Know practice does not replace problem solving, factual learning, cognitive support, medical care, or decisions that need available evidence.

## Summary
Shinzen begins with the analogy of a compulsive eater who overcomes compulsion without stopping eating. He applies the same structure to the mind: the drive to find meaning has a sensory construction, including Image/Talk patterning and Feel reinforcement. Finding meaning may bring pleasant body affect; failing to find meaning may bring fear, sadness, impatience, agitation, and spinning.

He then restates Don't Know Mind as systematic training. Medieval Christian learned ignorance, ancient Greek suspension, and Zen Don't Know Mind are used as parallels, but the practice point is that one can work through the inner sensory forces that demand answers. Shinzen gives both theoretical and practical reasons: human knowledge always expands not-knowing, some scientific and mathematical frames point to limits of knowing, and aging makes mental decline unavoidable.

The closing move returns to the opening analogy. Just as worked-through eating compulsion allows a healthier relationship to food, working through answer-compulsion allows a new way of knowing. Shinzen names that new mode wisdom function, insight, wisdom, and in its strongest form enlightenment.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not say when not-knowing should be accepted versus investigated, researched, treated, or acted on.
- It does not distinguish ordinary confusion from cognitive impairment, dementia, delirium, dissociation, psychosis, intoxication, neurological illness, or acute decision risk.
- It does not give a practice sequence beyond systematic work with Feel/Image/Talk forces behind the need to know.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The practice suspends the need for answers while preserving ordinary knowing; future use must keep both sides active.
- Equanimity with aging and "impending stupidity" can reduce fear, but it can also be misread as minimizing cognitive-health support.
- Wisdom function language can sound like a new faculty; the source only supports it as Shinzen's name for transformed knowing after answer-compulsion relaxes.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not use Don't Know Mind to reject facts, avoid decisions, or undermine careful reasoning.
- Do not use equanimity with cognitive decline to discourage medical evaluation, cognitive support, planning, or care.
- Do not turn Shinzen's cross-traditional references into proof that Greek skepticism, Christian learned ignorance, Zen koan practice, mathematics, physics, and theology are saying the same thing.
