This short companion video is a Shinzen-hosted exchange in which Soryu recounts a similar keisaku arc. Its durable contribution is the second-person confirmation that the stick can expose pride collapse, body threat, and resistance directly, while Soryu explicitly refuses to make the keisaku a heroic device.

## Teaching Register
Dialogue, autobiographical practice story, and public demonstration with humor. Shinzen frames the exchange playfully as public chastisement, then invites Soryu to tell his keisaku story; Soryu's account carries the practice content and the strongest safety caveat.

## Why This Talk Matters
Part 1 showed Shinzen's own primitive body fear when conceptual safety did not reach the body. Part 2 adds a companion route: meditation pride collapses into insecurity, pain, threat, and resistance, and the release is not a clever strategy but a point where resisting becomes impossible. That makes primal Feel less like a private Shinzen anecdote and more like a recurring body-level pattern under strong retreat conditions.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Soryu initially experienced the keisaku and tough monastery context as "kind of fun" because he was young, tough, and identified with being good at meditation.
- **S2**: The turning point came when he noticed he was deeply distracted, including being distracted by thoughts about being good at meditation; he then felt he could not keep concentration on anything even for one second.
- **S3**: After that pride collapse, the keisaku became horrifying and the carrier was interpreted through negative self-talk as a violent threat to the monastery.
- **S4**: Harada Roshi slowly carrying the keisaku felt to Soryu like a powerful wave, sun, comet, or penetrating force that especially entered his painfully held legs.
- **S5**: Soryu says resistance to pain and to the approaching keisaku made the pain much worse; when he finally could not resist anymore, the force came through, flowed, and became uplifting or floating.
- **S6**: Soryu explicitly cautions that the story should not make the keisaku seem like a heroic device or war story; it is only a tool, not a sign that a system has everything right, and not using it is also fine.
- **S7**: Shinzen's playful frame around public chastisement, Zen purity, and demonstration makes the source transmission-sensitive: humor lowers solemnity, but it can also obscure the authority and consent risks around being struck in a practice setting.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: The talk links primitive Feel with egoic concentration pride: threat appears after the practitioner can see through the self-image of being a strong meditator.
- **S4-S5**: The practice hinge is direct somatic resistance, not doctrine. The same external stimulus hurts more under resistance and becomes flowing/uplifting when resistance collapses.
- **S6**: The anti-heroic caveat is essential. The source is usable as evidence for primal Feel and equanimity under intensity, but not as an endorsement of keisaku practice.
- **S7**: Shinzen's humor and staged demonstration should be read as oral teaching style, not as a safety protocol.

## Practice Handles
- Watch for meditation pride as a cover for distraction: "I am good at this" may itself be the distraction that hides weak concentration.
- When threat or pain intensifies around an anticipated event, separate the physical sensation, emotional Feel, Image, and self-talk about the other person.
- Treat resistance as a sensory target only when the setting is safe, consented, supported, and within capacity; the point is not to endure more but to detect how resistance compounds suffering.
- If an intense ritual tool is being narrated as heroic, traditional, purifying, or proof of toughness, stop and re-check consent, teacher authority, trauma history, opt-out, ordinary body safety, and whether CCE is actually increasing.

## Summary
Shinzen opens with joking language about Soryu chastising him for perverting true Zen by mixing it with vipassana and for making arrogant claims about teachers. Soryu then says he had a similar keisaku experience. At first the tough setting and being struck were fun because they matched his youthful toughness and meditation self-image. Later he noticed how distracted he was, including the distraction of thinking he was good at practice, and he fell into insecurity when he judged that he could not sustain concentration even briefly.

At that point, the keisaku changed from exciting to horrifying. Soryu describes negative self-talk toward the person carrying it and then a more embodied phase with Harada Roshi slowly carrying the stick through the zendo. The approaching presence felt like a penetrating wave or comet entering his body and especially his painful full-lotus legs. Resistance to the pain and to the coming strike made the experience much worse.

The release came when he could not keep resisting. Soryu stresses that it was not a clever decision to stop resisting; it was more like finally letting go when the pressure was too much. Then the force came through, flowed, and became uplifting. He immediately adds that the story should not make the keisaku into a heroic device or war story: it is just a tool, not proof that the system using it has everything right, and not using it is also fine.

## Source Frame
The source frames keisaku as a vivid object lesson in body resistance and primal Feel, not as a portable technique. Soryu's account is practice-phenomenological: pride, distraction, insecurity, negative self-talk, pain, resistance, nonresistance, Flow, and uplift. The source omits the institutional, consent, trauma, and safety details that would be required before using such a tool with others.

## Weakest Claims
- Speaker attribution is mostly inferential from the title and transcript flow; Soryu appears to carry the central story, while Shinzen frames and reacts.
- The full-lotus pain and keisaku release are anecdotal; the source does not establish that similar intensity is advisable, safe, or necessary.
- The claim that resistance collapse produces Flow and uplift is practice-specific and lacks criteria for distinguishing skillful release from dissociation, shutdown, compliance, or injury.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk uses humor and a live demonstration while also warning that keisaku stories should not become heroic war stories.
- Soryu describes a powerful transformation through nonresistance, but also says the keisaku is just a tool and that a system using it does not thereby have everything right.
- The source validates body-level release through intensity while leaving ordinary safety and authority safeguards almost entirely unspecified.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Resistance made it worse" should not be used to blame practitioners for pain, fear, trauma activation, injury, or destabilization.
- "I finally could not resist" should not be romanticized as a goal; it may also resemble overwhelm, shutdown, compliance, or dissociation without additional criteria.
- The keisaku should not be treated as a badge of toughness, lineage authenticity, or advanced practice.
- Shinzen's joking frame should not be copied as permission to stage public humiliation, surprise, or coercive demonstration.
