- **S2**: After Rest, the dynamic aspect may become available, allowing practice with Flow and vanishing.
- **S3**: Vanishing can point toward what Shinzen calls that which is outside time and space, beyond the human, Zero, nothingness, or Source.
- **S4**: When a human being touches that nothingness, Shinzen says joy, love, compassion, and a call to service naturally arise.
- **S5**: The sequence can complete with intentionally created positive Feel/Image/Talk, functioning like a psycho-spiritual workout that exercises the major "muscle groups."

## Practice Handles
- Use this as a sequence template, not a mandatory ladder: ordinary sensory contact, Rest, Flow/Gone, and positive Feel/Image/Talk.
- Treat Focus on Positive as the reconstructive close of a deconstructive or Source-facing sequence.
- Check whether Rest actually clarifies experience and whether Flow or Gone is naturally available; do not manufacture vanishing or Source language to complete the circuit.
- Keep service claims behaviorally accountable: felt joy or compassion after practice is not itself proof of competent service.

## Summary
Shinzen presents a compact workout routine. A practitioner can first work with ordinary sensory experience, then shift to restful states, which he describes as a thinned or attenuated version of ordinary experience. From there, dynamic aspects may become present, opening practice with Flow and vanishing.

He then gives the Source-facing interpretation: vanishing points toward what is outside time and space, beyond the human, Zero, nothingness, or Source. When a human being touches that nothingness, he says joy, love, compassion, and a call to service naturally arise. The final phase intentionally creates positive Feel, Image, and Talk, completing the practice cycle.

The closing metaphor is a psycho-spiritual Nautilus workout. Just as exercise equipment can work the main muscle groups, this sequence touches ordinary experience, Rest, dynamic Flow/Gone, and positive reconstruction.

## Source Frame

The Source and service claims are Shinzen's contemplative interpretation of vanishing and return; they should be routed through existing Source, Total Happiness, and service boundaries rather than treated as verified metaphysics or conduct guarantees.
