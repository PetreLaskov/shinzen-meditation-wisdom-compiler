This four-minute `@ShinzenVideos` short is a method-strategy calibration.
Shinzen counters the pejorative "many shallow holes" critique by saying a
single focus route can work for some practitioners, but a broad
backhoe-like clearing of the whole field can also be a coherent path.

## Teaching Register

## Why This Talk Matters
The wiki already has one-good-technique, modular practice, coverage strategy,
and working-smarter pages. This talk adds a clean transmission handle: a
practitioner need not equate many methods with shallowness if the broad work
is clearing the field level by level, and a single-method path still has to
metabolize the rest of experience through that chosen route.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says that, in theory, a practitioner could take one focus
  option deeply enough that complete experience through that option draws the
  larger field toward the formless; the exact opening label is transcript-
  garbled and should not be over-specified.
- **S2**: He says any "atom," modality, or theme could in principle function
  this way because mindful appreciation means experiencing with
  concentration, clarity, and equanimity.
- **S3**: Appreciation taken to its limit becomes transcendence or points
  toward the formless.
- **S4**: The "dig one deep hole" strategy is valid for some people, but it
  is not inevitable or universally superior.
- **S5**: Even when one technique is used, the rest of the field still has to
  be dealt with; in the one-hole metaphor it is dealt with indirectly by
  being drawn through that technique.
- **S6**: Shinzen proposes a non-pejorative alternative to "many shallow
  holes": a backhoe clearing the whole field layer by layer until the bottom
  is contacted everywhere.
- **S7**: The practical strategy criterion is what is fun, interesting, and
  productive for the practitioner.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: The source treats any well-chosen focus route as potentially
  complete when CCE is strong enough; no category is privileged merely by
  its title.
- **S4-S6**: The one-deep-hole and backhoe images preserve two legitimate
  strategies: concentrate depth through one route or cultivate broad field
  coverage without reducing it to shallow hopping.
- **S5**: Single-method practice is not a license to bypass the rest of the
  field; it claims the rest is metabolized through the one route.
- **S7**: Strategy choice remains pragmatic and fit-dependent rather than
  status-dependent.

## Practice Handles
- If one method is alive, clear, and productive, it can be treated as a deep
  hole rather than as an incomplete path merely because other options exist.
- If a broad menu is alive, clear, and productive, it can be treated as
  backhoe-like field clearing rather than as shallow method hopping.
- When using one deep route, keep checking whether the rest of experience is
  actually being metabolized or merely ignored.
- When using a broad route, keep checking whether the field is being cleared
  level by level or whether novelty, avoidance, or choice confusion is
  driving the switching.
- Use fun, interest, and productivity as live fit signals, but not as the
  only safety or outcome criteria.

## Summary
Shinzen begins with a theoretical single-route example. The transcript
appears to garble the exact focus label, but the intended point is clear:
one chosen focus option can be practiced so completely that the whole field
is drawn through it. He generalizes this across "atoms," modalities, and
themes because mindful appreciation means experience with concentration,
clarity, and equanimity; taken far enough, appreciation becomes
transcendence.

He then takes up the familiar advice not to dig many shallow holes. He
accepts that one deep hole is right for some people, but rejects the idea
that this must be the only valid strategy. Even the one-hole route still has
to deal with everything else, although it does so through that one technique.

The alternative metaphor is a backhoe. Instead of digging many shallow holes,
the practitioner may clear the whole field to one level, then another, until
the whole surface contacts the bottom. Shinzen closes with the pragmatic
criterion: use the strategy that is fun, interesting, and productive.

## Source Frame
This is a compact method-strategy talk, not a comparative proof. Its
strongest evidence is for Shinzen's own teaching posture: he resists
shaming broad practice as shallow while preserving one-method depth as a
valid route.

The metaphysical phrases are oral metaphors. "Nirvanic black hole,"
"time-space continuum," "formless," and "fold up the board" should be used
as Shinzen's way of pointing from formed sensory experience toward
formlessness, not as scientific or ontological claims.

## Weakest Claims
- The theoretical claim that any focus option can open toward the formless
  is a Shinzen teaching claim, not demonstrated by the clip.
- The "nirvanic black hole" and time-space language are metaphorical and
  should not be promoted as physics, cosmology, or objective metaphysics.
- "Fun, interesting, and productive" is too thin by itself as a safety,
  ethics, or long-term outcome criterion.
- The quality-B auto captions garble the opening example and may also
  flatten technical distinctions around "atoms," modalities, and themes.

## Important Omissions
- No concrete procedure for deciding when to stay with one method versus
  broaden the field.
- No stop criteria for method hopping, overchoice, strain, avoidance,
  dissociation, clinical risk, or practice worsening.
- No explanation of what evidence shows that the rest of the field is
  actually being dealt with through one technique.
- No teacher, peer, or behavior feedback criterion beyond the practitioner's
  sense of fun, interest, and productivity.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk validates one deep hole while also rejecting the pejorative frame
  that broad practice is necessarily many shallow holes.
- It says the rest of the field must be dealt with, but one strategy deals
  with it indirectly through a single technique.
- The "game over for a while" metaphor points toward cessation or
  formlessness while also saying the game comes out again, so it should not
  be read as a simple final-state claim.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Broad practice can be misused to rationalize novelty seeking, avoidance,
  or never developing enough depth in any route.
- One-method practice can be misused to ignore material that is not actually
  being metabolized through that method.
- The formless and "nirvanic black hole" language can be over-literalized or
  turned into status rhetoric.
- Fun and interest can be mistaken for sufficient evidence that a strategy is
  safe, complete, or behaviorally transformative.
