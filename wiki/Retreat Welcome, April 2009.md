This short April 2009 retreat welcome opens an intensive container by naming retreat as one more level of human learning. Its durable value is the entry-side frame: participants are not merely taking a break or chasing states; they are learning concepts, techniques, and core skills for Total Happiness.

## Summary
Shinzen welcomes participants by saying they have come a long way to come to the practice. He says the retreat will involve talking about the practice, the concepts needed for the practice, and opportunities to do techniques that develop core practice skills.

He then frames retreat as a stage of human learning beyond ordinary education and adult establishment. Some people realize that another level of learning is available: a "last lesson" designed to bring Total Happiness. He defines that aim in compact form as happiness dependent on conditions and happiness independent of conditions, for oneself and others.

The talk ends with Shinzen's facilitator posture and enthusiasm for the week. It does not teach a technique directly; it orients why the retreat exists and what kind of learning participants are entering.

## Source Frame
The source is strongest as Shinzen's own retreat-entry rhetoric and weakest as a general account of human maturity. It uses praise and motivational elevation to help participants commit to the retreat container. Claims about "few people" reaching this readiness and the "last lesson" should be read as retreat exhortation, not as an empirical ranking of practitioners or humanity.
