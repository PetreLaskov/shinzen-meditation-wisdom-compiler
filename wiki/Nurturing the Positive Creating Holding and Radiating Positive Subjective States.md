This compact teaching defines the positive-practice operation at the sensory-carrier level: create, hold, and radiate positive states in Feel, Image, Talk. Its value is not a new map but a crisp oral description of how [[Nurture Positive]] uses partial voluntary control over the inner sensory system.

## Teaching Register
This is brief conceptual instruction. Shinzen is naming the final positive theme inside the inner Feel/Image/Talk system and giving practical examples rather than offering live guidance or a full method sequence.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk sharpens the micro-mechanics of Focus on Positive. It confirms that the positive route is not merely "think happy thoughts"; it can use Talk, Image, Feel, any pair, or all three, with a clear difficulty gradient and distinct theme types.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The final theme is to intentionally create, hold, and radiate positive states in Feel, Image, and Talk; Shinzen calls this approach Focus on Positive.
- **S2**: Feel/Image/Talk is presented as the thought-and-emotion system over which people have partial voluntary control.
- **S3**: Positive Talk is usually easiest: a person can choose a phrase and repeat it, making the same operation resemble mantra practice or cognitive therapy when the phrase is a rational or appropriate antidote to irrational or inappropriate cognition.
- **S4**: Positive Image can include rational images, desired behavior change, performance improvement, desired outcomes, spiritual symbols or archetypes, and people, places, or objects that activate pleasant emotional Feel.
- **S5**: Positive Feel is often harder to create than Talk or Image, but Shinzen says people can systematically learn to turn on pleasant Feel such as interest, joy, love, gratitude, and compassion, then work in one, two, or all three inner systems.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: "Create, hold, radiate" is the concise operative verb chain for Focus on Positive and should route future practice advice toward actual cultivation, not vague affirmation.
- **S2**: Partial voluntary control keeps the practice trainable without implying total command over emotion.
- **S3**: The mantra/CBT comparison preserves Shinzen's translational style: the same sensory operation can appear in contemplative and clinical languages, while the wiki should not turn the talk into clinical protocol.
- **S4**: The Image examples show that positive practice includes behavior, performance, outcome, devotional/symbolic, and simple pleasant-memory objects.
- **S5**: The difficulty gradient matters for guidance. A practitioner unable to create positive Feel may still work with Talk or Image, while positive Feel can remain a trainable but non-forced capacity.

## Practice Handles
- Use positive Talk first when a practitioner can choose and repeat a rational, appropriate, or wholesome phrase more easily than they can evoke Image or Feel.
- Use positive Image when behavior change, performance, desired outcome, spiritual ideal, or simple pleasant association is the natural carrier.
- Treat positive Feel as learnable but often harder; route difficulty or absence of Feel to patient training, another carrier, or [[Finding Feel Good in Emotional Body Space]] rather than shame or forcing.
- Combine one, a pair, or all three inner systems only when the combination helps hold the positive theme.

## Phenomenological Distinctions
- **Theme versus carrier**: The positive content may be rationality, behavior change, performance, outcome, ideal, or pleasant association; the carrier is Talk, Image, Feel, a pair, or all three.
- **Positive as appropriate**: In the Talk branch, "positive" can mean rational or appropriate, not only cheerful.
- **Voluntary control gradient**: Talk is framed as easiest, Image as harder but doable, and Feel as harder still but trainable.
- **Radiation versus creation**: The same positive state can be created internally, held with concentration, and then radiated beyond a private mood frame.

## Summary
Shinzen defines Focus on Positive as intentionally creating, holding, and radiating positive states in Feel, Image, and Talk. He locates this in the thought-and-emotion system and says people have some internal control there, especially over Talk.

He then gives examples by carrier. Positive Talk may be a repeated phrase, which resembles mantra, or a rational and appropriate cognition used as an antidote to irrational or inappropriate word-based cognition. Positive Image may represent behavior change, performance improvement, desired outcomes, spiritual goals, or ordinary people, places, and objects that evoke pleasant emotional Feel. Positive Feel is harder for many people, but he says it can be systematically learned, naming interest, joy, love, gratitude, and compassion.

The closing definition is the important compression: intentionally creating, holding, and radiating positive states in one, two, or all three parts of the Feel/Image/Talk system is Focus on Positive.

## Weakest Claims
- The talk says people have partial internal control over Feel/Image/Talk but does not specify how much control, for whom, or under what emotional, clinical, or trauma conditions.
- The claim that positive Image can function as an antidote to depression, fear, and related uncomfortable emotions is under-specified and should not be treated as clinical advice.
- The statement that positive Feel can be systematically learned is plausible within Shinzen's system but not operationalized here.
- "Radiating" positive states remains Shinzen's practice language; this source does not establish external interpersonal effects.

## Important Omissions
- No guided sequence, timing, repetition count, progression, or troubleshooting criteria.
- No criteria for when positive practice should give way to direct contact with negative material, ordinary action, therapy, medical care, or accountability.
- No safety discussion for mania, compulsive positivity, trauma, depression, shame around inability to feel positive, or spiritual bypass.
- No behavioral verification standard for behavior-change or performance-improvement imagery.
- No explanation of how radiating positive states should be understood phenomenologically versus interpersonally.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Focus on Positive actively creates content, while much of Shinzen's deconstructive practice observes what is already present; future guidance must preserve both stances.
- Positive content can be an antidote to uncomfortable emotion, but using it to avoid grief, fear, repair, or clinical support would violate the broader completion and behavior-accountability model.
- The talk links contemplative mantra and cognitive therapy by operation, but those contexts have different training, consent, and scope standards.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Cognitive therapy" could be misread as permission to self-treat serious depression, anxiety, trauma, or harmful behavior with phrase repetition alone.
- "Antidote" could become suppression if the practitioner uses positive Talk or Image to deny present pain.
- "Behavior changes" and "performance improvements" in Image could become fantasy rehearsal without external action, feedback, or skill development.
- "Turn on positive Feel" could become coercive self-pressure when positive Feel is absent, numb, or clinically inaccessible.
- "Radiating" could be inflated into proof of maturity, power, interpersonal benefit, or consent-free service.
