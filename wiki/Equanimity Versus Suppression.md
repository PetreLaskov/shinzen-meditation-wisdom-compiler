Equanimity versus suppression owns the safety differential inside Shinzen's
noninterference language. The practical question is not whether the person
can say "I am equanimous"; it is whether experience is available, behavior
is responsive, and support remains reachable.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Equanimity gives radical permission for sensory events to
  arise and pass; suppression, numbness, passivity, and dissociation reduce
  contact, responsiveness, or support.
- **Why this matters**: Many risky states can imitate calm from the outside.
- **Key tensions**: Shinzen gives strong conceptual boundaries, but the wiki
  still needs ordinary clinical, medical, relational, and teacher-conduct
  checks for live cases.
- **Source posture**: Strong for the internal Shinzen distinction; limited
  as a diagnostic protocol.

## Differential

| Presentation | More likely equanimity when... | More concerning when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm with pain or emotion | Sensation remains trackable and expression can respond to context | Sensation is blank, distant, numbed, or inaccessible |
| Turning away from challenge | The challenge is permitted in the background and can be turned toward at times | The person avoids knowing the challenge or needs it to disappear |
| Gentle label voice | Tone supports contact while feeling and expression remain possible | Tone becomes performance, shame-control, or teacher-pleasing |
| No-self or no-reactivity | Selfing can arise and be clarified without hostility | Personality, needs, boundaries, or ordinary repair are rejected |
| Power, bliss, visions, or unusual states | Fear and craving are included as sensory events | Vividness becomes authority, status, or consent bypass |
| Strong effort or discomfort | Learning, clarity, and responsiveness improve | Endurance, compliance, self-punishment, or medical neglect grows |

## Source Boundaries

[[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] is the cleanest source. Suppression stuffs
states down; identification fixes on them. Equanimity is the balanced third
possibility that allows sensory states to arise, spread, and pass without
self-interference (S10). The same source explicitly says equanimity is not
apathy or suppression: apathy is indifference to objective outcomes, while
equanimity frees energy for external response and gives radical permission to
feel (S14).

[[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] gives the checklist:
equanimity is not indifference, passivity, suppression, lack of thoughts and
sensations, or necessarily stopping external expression (S8). That means a
person can cry, shake, speak, move, set limits, seek help, or repair behavior
without losing equanimity.

[[What Is Equanimity]] gives the short public-action version of the same
boundary. Shinzen distinguishes hands-off noninterference with sensory flow
from hands-off passivity toward world events; the person may still act
proactively or forcefully to change circumstances ([[What Is Equanimity]]
S1-S4). In safety routing, reduced action is therefore not evidence of
equanimity by itself.

[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 4 - Calming and Clarifying]] adds the
state-preference boundary. Longing for absorptive calm can create comparison
and frustration; the task is to work through craving, aversion, and
unconsciousness, not to collect or defend calm states (S11).

[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] adds the no-self
version. Shinzen says attachment to no-self states can become subtle
resistance to the natural arising of self. The correction is to let the self
storm arise, clarify it, and learn from self-arising as well as self-cessation
(S14-S15).

[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] adds the altered
phenomena version. Fear, craving, comparison, status-seeking, and authority
claims around visions, bliss, heat, entities, powers, or unusual content show
that the relationship to experience has become the problem (S6-S8, S11-S13).

## Method-Specific Risks

Background equanimity is valid only when the difficult material is permitted.
[[Using Turn Away and Background Equanimity with Sensory Challenges]] says
turning away from physical discomfort can count as mindfulness when the
eruption is allowed in the background, there is no agenda to make it vanish,
and the practitioner is sometimes willing to turn toward it (S5-S8).

Equanimity voice has a similar limit. [[How to do Labeling and Noting During
Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] says a gentle, matter-of-fact label tone can
support equanimity even during intense expression (S8-S9). The cue does not
prove that the person is not suppressing, dissociating, or complying.

Forced labels intensify the work-side risk. [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] treats
impatience, agitation, shame, and resistance as newly trackable practice
material (S1-S4), but hard work and discomfort are not evidence by
themselves. If social pressure, retreat authority, shame, or agitation reduce
clarity and responsiveness, load [[Guidance Scope and Accountability
Boundary]] and [[Practice Method Safety Boundary]].

## Stop Or Refer Triggers

Stop optimizing equanimity technique and route to safety/support when there
is medical pain or injury risk, severe sleep disruption, panic, traumatic
flooding, dissociation, DPDR-like flattening, mania or psychosis risk,
self-harm or harm risk, coercive teacher pressure, inability to function, or
practice language being used to avoid necessary care or repair.

When the issue is teacher pressure, role confusion, consent, applied-life
scope, behavior mismatch, or protection, load [[Guidance Scope and
Accountability Boundary]] before giving more technique.
