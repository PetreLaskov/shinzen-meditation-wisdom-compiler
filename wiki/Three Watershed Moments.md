## Summary
Shinzen begins by saying there is an instant when something starts: the
primordial perfection of Source acting. He associates that with the world of
infancy and says that practice over years has given him vague recollections
of that time.

He names three remembered watersheds. First, there was expanding-contracting
effortless space rather than objectified self and world. Second, he
discovered that crying could bring attention, which he presents as a major
early agency discovery. Third, worry, thought, and fear appeared, frightening
him because that mode had not been present before. In retrospect, he reads
that onset as the inner system arising as a self.

The talk closes with the reconstructive claim. Adults can return with adult
competency and have the best of both worlds because the first world is not
really lost. It is available now as complete simplicity.

## Source Frame
The source is strong evidence for Shinzen's oral framing of self-arising,
Source, and early-life memory. It is weak evidence for objective infancy
phenomenology, recovered-memory accuracy, or developmental sequence. The
teaching wants to make a practice distinction available: selfing can be
understood as an inner-system event that comes online, while the underlying
simplicity is not gone.

The Source language should stay calibrated. "Primordial perfection," "first
world," and "complete simplicity" are Shinzen's contemplative idiolect and
retrospective interpretation, not established theology, metaphysics, or
neuroscience.
