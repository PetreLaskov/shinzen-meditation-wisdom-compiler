It then abstracts those contrasts into Expansion, Contraction, Zero, and spaciousness, while preserving Gone as the special vanishing case.

## Teaching Register
Brief advanced instruction and lineage translation. Shinzen begins with practical Flow taxonomy, abstracts it into polarity language, then uses Dzogchen and Sasaki Roshi references to name the unification side as space-ness rather than a thing called space.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk prevents Flow from becoming mystical or amorphous. It says Flow can be noticed in ordinary intensifying, fading, speeding, slowing, spreading, collapsing, pushing, pulling, scattering, and gripping; those contrasts can then reveal spaciousness when complete experience makes events rich but transparent.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen uses Flow for any change other than abrupt vanishing; vanishing is a special case because it briefly turns attention toward the unborn Source.
- **S2**: Flow can be demystified as ordinary intensifying, fading, speeding, slowing, spreading, collapsing, inward pressure, outward pressure, scattering, or gripping.
- **S3**: These Flow flavors can be abstracted into two principles: increase as Expansion and decrease as Contraction, with Zero as their balance point.
- **S4**: When expansion and contraction flavors are tracked closely, they may reveal a sense that space or time-space is inflating and collapsing.
- **S5**: Tuning into that spaciousness is, in Shinzen's interpretation, tuning into the spacious nature of consciousness.
- **S6**: Complete experience is "utterly rich but utterly transparent," presenting itself as space itself; Sasaki Roshi's "space is laughing/crying" points to this experiential shift.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: Flow is made practice-level by ordinary examples before any Source or spaciousness language appears.
- **S3-S4**: Discrimination of many concrete Flow contrasts supports unification into a simpler polarity model.
- **S5-S6**: Spaciousness is bounded as space-nature or space-ness, not a pre-existing solid stage called space.

## Practice Handles
- When "energy" or Flow is vague, test for concrete change: intensity, frequency, spread, collapse, pressure, scattering, or gripping.
- Treat scattering as possible expansive Flow and gripping or obsessive holding as possible contractive Flow, while not erasing ordinary problem-solving or support needs.
- If expansion/contraction begins to feel spatial, attend to the space-making or space-collapsing quality without turning "space" into a fixed object.
- Keep Gone distinct from continuous Flow: vanishing is a special Source-facing interruption, not just another wavy movement.

## Summary
Shinzen first defines Flow broadly as change other than abrupt vanishing. Vanishing has special significance because the moment something disappears, attention is briefly turned toward what he calls the unborn Source, the place outside place and time from which the next moment of being arises.

He then demystifies Flow. Flow may be intensity increasing or decreasing, thought or sensation speeding up or slowing down, sensation spreading or collapsing, pressure moving inward or outward, awareness scattering, or attention gripping and obsessing. These ordinary examples matter because they show how apparent problems can be reinterpreted as force-patterns when CCE is available.

The talk then abstracts the examples into Expansion and Contraction, with Zero as a balance point. As practitioners notice those flavors, they may sense space or time-space inflating and collapsing. Shinzen links that to Dzogchen-style spaciousness but immediately corrects a possible reification: spaciousness should not mean a solid stage called space. In complete experience, the event is rich and transparent enough that it presents as space-ness itself.

## Source Frame
The strongest material is practice taxonomy and phenomenological routing. Dzogchen spaciousness, unborn Source, and Sasaki Roshi's "space is laughing" phrase are lineage translations that extend the practice into a deep interpretive frame. The operational claim is narrower: when Flow is discriminated as ordinary change and polarity, the event may unify as transparent spaciousness rather than remain a solid object.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that vanishing turns attention toward the unborn Source is Shinzen's contemplative interpretation, not independent metaphysical evidence.
- "Time-space gets inflated" is useful phenomenological language but should not be promoted into physics.
- The Dzogchen and Sasaki Roshi references are translation bridges, not proof that those traditions use identical models.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not specify how to distinguish helpful reinterpretation of scattering or gripping from bypassing ordinary attentional, behavioral, clinical, or relational problems.
- It gives no safety criteria for spaciousness, Source-facing vanishings, or intense contraction.
- It does not explain how practitioners should proceed when Flow is not detectable or when spaciousness language creates blankness, dissociation, or comparison mind.
- It is still only part 2 of a four-part sequence.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says scattering and gripping can be reinterpreted as Flow, but those same experiences may still require practical correction, rest, therapy, behavior change, or external support.
- Spaciousness is offered as the reward of sustained practice, yet the transcript also warns implicitly against reifying spaciousness as a solid stage. Future synthesis must preserve that distinction.
- Gone points toward Source, but Source is described in place-language. This should be governed by [[Source And Polarities]] and its afterglow/not-a-sensory-object boundary.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Scattering is expansive Flow" should not become permission to indulge distraction or ignore functional impairment.
- "Gripping is contractive Flow" should not become permission to stay in obsession, compulsion, or harmful attachment without support.
- "Space is laughing/crying" should not become a dissociative instruction to erase the person, body, or social context.
- "Unborn Source" should not be treated as an owned metaphysical insight.
