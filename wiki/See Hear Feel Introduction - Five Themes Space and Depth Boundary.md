This unit covers pages 52-59 of Shinzen Young's *See Hear Feel: An Introduction*, using a mechanical Markdown extraction from the source PDF. It adds Space/Spaciousness to the modern SHF Grid and answers the objection that See Hear Feel can initially feel too busy for practitioners accustomed to tranquil depth.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The Unified Mindfulness Grid cross-classifies sensory experience by three modalities, See/Hear/Feel, and five themes: In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Space.
- **S2**: Space means Spaciousness: the openness around and/or thinness within what one sees, hears, or feels, yielding See Space, Hear Space, and Feel Space.
- **S3**: Each theme has a family of range options; for example, the In theme can be explored through individual elements such as See In, Hear In, and Feel In, or through Focus In as all three inner elements.
- **S4**: Shinzen presents the columns as a natural progression: In and Out appreciate Self and World, while Rest, Flow, and Space progressively integrate and transcend Self and World.
- **S5**: The five family effect slogans are: Focus In untangles self, Focus Out merges with surrounding scene, Focus on Rest attenuates self and world into rest states, Focus on Flow dissolves them into energy, and Focus on Space shows the perfection already there.
- **S6**: Mindfulness can include deep tranquil states because visual, auditory, and somatic rest states can be objects of mindful observation.
- **S7**: Attending to rest states makes them clearer, creating a positive feedback loop that can deepen tranquility.
- **S8**: Practitioners coming from calming forms may initially experience SHF, even unlabeled noting, as too busy and less like meditation.
- **S9**: Shinzen frames that loss of familiar depth as a learning curve: revving up clarity through noting may temporarily reduce access to deep states, but the intended mature integration is to go deep while staying clear, including during ordinary activity.

## Summary
The first half of the unit presents the modern SHF Grid as a five-theme table. The modality rows remain See, Hear, and Feel. The theme columns are In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Space. The new column names visual, auditory, and somatic spaciousness, defined as openness around or thinness within sensory experience. The unit also groups each column into a "family" of focus options and gives each family a practice effect, moving from Self and World through rest, energy, and spacious perfection.

The second half addresses a common mismatch between calming meditation expectations and SHF training. Shinzen says mindfulness can be practiced in stillness and ordinary activities, and deep tranquil states are included when visual, auditory, and somatic rest become objects of observation. However, beginners with a calming background may find noting busy, mental, or even opposite to meditation. The source frames this as a temporary tradeoff: clarity is being strengthened before it can integrate with depth.

The claimed mature goal is not shallow tracking instead of depth. It is going deep while staying clear. Because that integration is trained through ordinary sensory experiences rather than only through special quiet states, Shinzen says it can become portable into daily activity.

## Source Frame
This is a taxonomy-and-reassurance unit. It wants the reader to see Unified Mindfulness as both wider than the older four-column Basic Mindfulness grid and friendlier to deep tranquility than noting may first appear.

The strongest material is classificatory and pedagogical: three modalities, five themes, the Space definition, the five focus families, rest states as mindful objects, and the explicit shamatha-vipashyana teaching frame. These claims clarify how the later SHF interface should be routed inside the compiled model.

The weaker material is outcome- and depth-oriented. "Shows you the perfection that was always there," "ordinary becomes utterly extraordinary," and abiding at the Stillpoint throughout ordinary comings and goings are Shinzen's interpretive and aspirational claims. The unit does not supply criteria, counterexamples, duration guidance, teacher-assessment rules, or safety boundaries for those outcomes.

## Weakest Claims
- The Focus on Space slogan that it shows "the perfection that was always there" should remain source-attributed; the unit gives no evidence or operational criteria for perfection, realization, or stable spaciousness.
- The column progression from Self and World to integration and transcendence is a useful teaching map, but the unit does not show that it is universal, necessary, sequential, or safe for all practitioners.
- The claim that patience will eventually integrate clarity and depth is not supported here by timing, prerequisites, failure modes, or teacher-assessment criteria.
- The ordinary-activity examples for mindfulness do not establish safety or appropriateness while driving, working, conversing, eating, or in intimate contexts.
- The periodic-table analogy clarifies the Grid's intended elegance, but it should not be treated as proof that the table is exhaustive or scientifically validated.

## Important Omissions
- The unit does not give detailed instructions for Focus on Space beyond the definition of Spaciousness and the family-effect slogan.
- It does not distinguish spaciousness from visual defocus, auditory quiet, somatic relaxation, dissociation, depersonalization, shutdown, or blankness.
- It does not specify how long the clarity-before-depth learning curve may last, how to tell productive busyness from strain, or when to switch practices or seek coaching.
- It does not address clinical, trauma, medical, sleep, driving, workplace, or relationship boundaries for maintaining mindful awareness during ordinary activity.
- It does not explain how Space relates to Gone, Source, no-self, Flow, or the older Basic Mindfulness symbolic grid beyond the new table itself.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The older compiled Basic Mindfulness grid is built around In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Gone, while this SHF unit presents In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Space. This is best preserved as an old/new interface difference, not forced into one flat ontology.
- The source says rest states can deepen through observation, yet also says noting may initially prevent calming practitioners from going deep. The proposed resolution is a learning curve, but the unit does not give evidence or diagnostics.
- Space is introduced as a sensory theme, but the "perfection always there" slogan turns it toward a metaphysical or realization claim faster than the procedural instruction supports.
- The claim that ordinary activity can become a place of deep clear mindfulness is central to Shinzen's system, but this unit does not separate portable practice from divided attention, imprudent risk, or social/ethical context.
