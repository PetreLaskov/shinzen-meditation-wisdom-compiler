## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S2-S3**: The instruction does not make sleepiness either an enemy or a pure Rest object. It pairs alertness corrections with sensory acceptance.
- **S3**: The posture and eyes-open moves keep Rest practice from sliding into passivity, while the pleasant-rest and discomfort instructions keep alertness effort from becoming aversion.
- **S4**: Sleepiness is made into an object of clarity: the practitioner penetrates the sensory event before expecting changes in posture, consciousness, or noise.
- **S5**: The long-term encouragement belongs in practice-transmission context. It should not become a promise that every sleep or fatigue problem can be solved meditatively.

## Practice Handles
- When sleepiness appears during sitting, first name the branch as sinking: haziness, lurching, snoring, posture collapse, or loss of alertness.
- Repeatedly restore alertness supports: straighten the spine and open the eyes.
- Do not only fight the state. Notice and enjoy the pleasant restful waves that sleepiness brings.
- Do not only indulge the state. Equanimize the discomfort and dullness of sleepiness without collapsing into it.
- Treat sleepiness as a sensory event to clarify and penetrate before assuming its objective effects should clear.
- Keep ordinary sleep, medical, neurological, medication, substance, and safety concerns outside the scope of this clip; the talk does not override body care or dangerous fatigue.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by pointing to his wider "From Fuzz to Buzz" writing on
sinking. He then defines sinking broadly, from mild haziness through obvious
sleepiness behaviors such as lurching, snoring, and loss of alertness during
practice.

His practical instruction is repetitive: straighten the spine, force the eyes
open, enjoy the pleasant restful waves of sleepiness, and equanimize the
discomfort. The important combination is alertness plus equanimity. The
practitioner neither treats sleepiness as mere failure nor passively surrenders
to unconsciousness.

The clip's strongest claim is that sleepiness itself can eventually be
penetrated as sensory experience. Shinzen says that when the sensory event of
sleepiness is penetrated, its objective effects on consciousness, posture, and
sleep-noise behavior can clear. He closes by recalling early retreat advice
that pain may break up but sleepiness can remain hard, then says that after
decades of practice he has seen sleepiness break up too.
