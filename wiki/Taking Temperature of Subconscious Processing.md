## Summary
Shinzen opens with a neon-bulb analogy. A neon bulb can contain very high
particle activity while transferring little heat because its density is low.
He then compares that image to Freud's pressure model and shifts to
present-time sensory practice: when mental images and inner talk become
explicit, they may disappear, but their spaces can still show subtle
undercurrents.

Those undercurrents become the talk's main practice object. Image space,
talk space, and emotional body space may have a general level of activation
even when specific pictures, words, or emotions are not conscious. Shinzen
calls this "taking the temperature" of the subconscious: monitoring the
activation field without needing to know the content.

He then gives two routes. If the activation is steady, cover the whole
subjective space and soak awareness into it, which he says can make the
system less viscous and more fluid. If the activation bursts and then
subsides, follow the rhythm of arising and passing, detect Gone, and look
briefly into the silence after the subsiding. He closes by extending the
metaphor into scalar-field language and by saying the inner See/Hear/Feel
self becomes creative and liberated when it flows.
