## Editorial Target

The target is a curated practitioner atlas: part field guide, part reference
work, part hypertext book.

It should sit between two extremes:

- too dense: the internal wiki's agent-optimized, source-heavy reasoning
  pages;
- too thin: a brochure-like summary that loses Shinzen's practice handles.

The right middle is readable, source-calibrated, safety-aware, and rich enough
to help practice reasoning.

## Compiler-To-Public Loop

The internal wiki is the power source. The public atlas should exploit its
routing intelligence, source posture, safety boundaries, contradictions,
practice handles, and owner-page syntheses, but should not expose the
machinery.

For each serious atlas edit:

1. Start from the public reader job: what question, confusion, practice
   choice, or safety boundary should this page help with?
2. Load the relevant internal owner page, then only the cited source pages or
   neighbors needed to strengthen the public page.
3. Extract the best durable distinctions: practice handles, method-choice
   cues, source tier, safety/scope limit, and behavior/service test.
4. Rewrite in public prose: human sequence first, internal provenance second,
   no frontmatter machinery, claim IDs, model deltas, or agent routing
   language.
5. Add only links that improve the reader's next move.

The win condition is not a simpler wiki. It is an unusually useful public
map made possible by an unusually dense agent memory layer.

before any voice, style, or page edit. It defines the register as behavior (not
the adjective list that used to sit here), the four-question page probe, the tic

exemplars - the-one-move, noting, and equanimity-versus-suppression - are
complete and live as the anchor. The register now carries a deepened section,
"Carry the movement through to the cut": cut safety language to the failure mode
that bites, but preserve reference and decision density through progressive
disclosure (thesis and cut first, the exhaustive table or taxonomy beneath for
the careful practitioner), and aim for a direct pointing-out only at the rare
pages where the material opens. Next step: the spine, meaning index.md and the
ten pillar pages.

## Boundaries

- The atlas should not be registered in `wiki/index.md` as Shinzen domain
  content.
- `public-atlas/` pages should use ordinary Markdown links, not Obsidian
  wikilinks.
- Keep minimal public frontmatter only: `title`, `atlas_type`, `tier`,
  `status`.
- Do not expose internal source-page machinery such as Model Delta or
  Integration Notes.
- Do not publish raw-source text or long quotations until rights and quote
  policy are settled.
- Keep claim tiers visible: Shinzen says, compiled synthesis, editorial
  inference, speculative extension, and not established here.
- Safety, behavior, consent, teacher accountability, clinical/medical scope,
  and service boundaries should remain first-class public features.
