Primordial Feel names the deep body-emotion account at the end of Shinzen's Gone chapter and the later primal Feel examples. It should be read as a speculative practice model for safety-sensitive, layered emotional body states, not as established developmental psychology.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Gone, primitive threat cues, resisted pain, and sustained emotional-body practice can sometimes expose chaotic, infant-like body emotion rather than tranquility; Shinzen treats that emotional agitation as a layered deep self-pattern that can be known, loved, and completed.
- **Why this matters**: It gives future agents a place to route deep Gone-related emotional agitation without flattening it into ordinary dark night, ordinary Feel In, or Source metaphysics.
- **Key tensions**: The sources treat primordial Feel as powerful and purgatorial, but they do not give clinical criteria for trauma, panic, dissociation, depression, induced fear, or developmental claims about infancy.
- **Source posture**: Primary evidence for Shinzen's own model; speculative outside his contemplative phenomenology.

## Model
Chapter 9 says intense dark-night problems are not common, and that repeated vanishing is usually restful and tranquil. In some cases, however, Gone can produce emotional agitation. Shinzen distinguishes this agitation from the dramatic dark night of Goneness, emptiness, and no-self, while still treating it as challenging and powerful ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S15).

The model has two levels. The most primordial level is not personal: Gone points to Source or Zero, a "groundless grounding" that is deeper than human identity. The second most primordial level is human: chaotic infant body emotion. Shinzen says each vanishing is like a little death and the return after it is like being born again as an infant with chaotic body emotion ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S15-S16).

That body emotion is the main practice target. The chapter claims this infantile freak-out is the early pattern that separated the person from Source and underlies later emotional freak-outs. An adult meditator has techniques and can work through it by knowing it and loving it until the pattern completes ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S17).

The deepest layer uses [[Source And Polarities]]. Zero breaks into Expansion and Contraction; the gap between them creates pure space; that space vibrates into the pure heat of Feel. This pure heat of body emotion is the first personal expression after impersonal Source. If detected and completed, it resolves back into expansion, contraction, mutual cancellation, and Gone ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S18).

[[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] gives the clearest oral layer
model. Shinzen says emotional-body practice has definite long-term trends:
uncomfortable sensations become less problematic and pleasant emotional
sensations become more fulfilling. Locally, however, emotional Feel may move
in unpredictable directions: more mellow or more intense, appropriate or
inappropriate, sometimes with tiny triggers releasing huge somatic
emotionality ([[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] S2-S4, S7). His
practice explanation is that a concentrated, clear, equanimous meditator
self, witness, no-self, or higher self shines attention on a freaked-out
infant-animal layer; as one layer is integrated, its influence shrinks but a
more primitive stratum may be exposed ([[Working Through the Primal Feel
Strata]] S5-S6).

## Practice Interpretation
Primordial Feel belongs near the intersection of [[Gone]], [[Inner Sensory System]], and [[Deconstruction-Reconstruction Balance]]. The body-emotion component matters because Shinzen is not describing a purely cognitive belief, a purely energetic Flow state, or a mere blankness. He is describing Feel as the first personal heat of self.

The practical route should therefore stay concrete. When this territory becomes workable, future agents should think in Shinzen's ordinary skill language: detect the body emotion, clarify it, allow it to move, deconstruct image/talk/body reactions, and reconstruct positive emotion, behavior, and cognition when needed ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S14-S18).

The "reparenting" claim is best treated as the chapter's transformation metaphor unless later sources add operational detail. It may describe a felt shift in self-organization, but this source does not establish a clinical reparenting method.

The strata talk adds a practical reassurance rule: local weirdness is not the
same as global deterioration. The first move is to "recycle the reactions,"
including fear, shame, and the self-diagnostic thought that something is
wrong, while still checking whether the activation remains safe, functional,
and supported ([[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] S3-S4).

The reparenting talk adds a pacing rule: even if Shinzen interprets the
agitating Gone/Feel process as good practice, the practitioner can nap or sit
and should take ego-death-like work in manageable doses. This makes pacing
part of the primordial Feel handle rather than an external afterthought
([[Reparenting Our Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure
Feeling]] S6).

[[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] adds a small but useful
applied bridge. A student reports a huge raw fear in Feel space before she
grasped for an image to explain its size; Shinzen checks the report as
chaotic primitive Feel, and the student says it increases compassion for a
crying baby because she can sense an infant-like "primordial freaked out"
state ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] S6-S9). This
supports primordial Feel as a compassion handle, not only a destabilizing
Gone-side frontier.

[[Primal Feel and the Zen Keisaku - 1 of 2]] adds a sharper oral example.
Shinzen says years of practice had quieted Image/Talk enough that, when the
keisaku carrier approached, conceptual safety did not remove the body's
primitive terror; "what was left" was a frightened infant-like body. The
practice move was not rationalization but training the raw body fear directly
until the fear wave became Flow, merger with the carrier and room, and
cathartic bliss ([[Primal Feel and the Zen Keisaku - 1 of 2]] S3-S6). This
supports primordial Feel as body-level separateness training while raising
stronger consent and induced-intensity boundaries.

[[Primal Feel and the Zen Keisaku - 2 of 2]] adds a companion account from
Soryu. In that source, the keisaku becomes terrifying after meditation pride
collapses into noticing distraction and weak concentration; resistance to the
approaching stick and full-lotus pain makes the pain worse until resistance
can no longer continue and the force flows through as uplift ([[Primal Feel
and the Zen Keisaku - 2 of 2]] S1-S6). This sharpens primordial Feel as
pride collapse plus resisted body threat, not only Shinzen's private
autobiographical example.

[[Reparenting Our Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure
Feeling]] gives the clearest live Gone-to-primal-Feel router. A practitioner
reports that vanishings, previously soothing around breath and sound endings,
have become global and agitating. Shinzen first checks correlation, then
isolates the agitation as primarily Feel, and only then says the technique
was done well: vanishing has exposed the second-most-primitive human layer,
chaotic infant-like Feel. The practice response is to keep noting vanishings
while including the Feel vanishings, with the adult meditator functioning as
a "super adult" able to work at the level of the freaked-out infant
([[Reparenting Our Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure
Feeling]] S1-S4, S7).

## Boundaries
Primordial Feel is not the same as the dramatic dark night, though the two can be adjacent. The dark-night section names disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, and hopelessness. Primordial Feel names emotional agitation and chaotic infant body emotion that Shinzen sees as purgatorial and workable ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S17).

It is also not a license to push through destabilization. The source's own dissolution section says extreme cases may require ongoing intensive support from teachers and other practitioners. This page should route high-risk cases to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] rather than treating agitation as automatically good practice.

The developmental language needs caution. Claims about the first hours, days, and weeks of life, the limbic undercurrent, and all later freak-out being rooted in infantile freak-out are not established by this chapter. They should remain source-attributed and should not substitute for trauma-informed, clinical, relational, or medical assessment.

The strata talk strengthens that caution. Shinzen's limbic-system hypothesis
and infant-animal/super-adult model are useful practice images, but the talk
does not establish neuroscience, infant development, or a clinical theory of
trauma and emotional dysregulation. Its reassurance that chaotic strata may
be a progress sign should not be used to override panic, dissociation,
rage behavior, loss of function, medical factors, or ordinary support needs
([[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] S1-S7).

The reparenting talk strengthens the same caution through a different route.
Shinzen's "cosmic parents" and pure-heat-of-Feel explanation belongs to his
Source/polarity model, while "reparenting" is not presented as a clinical
attachment or trauma protocol. Future agents should preserve the operational
sequence - correlation, sensory channel, Feel vanishings, manageable doses -
without treating the metaphysical explanation as a safety differential
([[Reparenting Our Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure
Feeling]] S1-S7).

The parenting source sharpens that caution. Adult raw Feel may help a parent
respond with tenderness to infant distress, but it does not prove what the
infant is experiencing, diagnose the baby's state, or replace pediatric,
postpartum, trauma, sleep, or family support ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen
Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] S8-S9).

The keisaku sources add another boundary: primitive Feel can be exposed by a
ritualized threat cue, resisted pain, and the collapse of self-image, but
Shinzen explicitly says his own keisaku use seemed to foster fear and then
craving or addiction, and Soryu explicitly warns against turning the keisaku
into a heroic device or war story. These sources therefore support
body-level fear practice only under safety, consent, support, and
capacity-check conditions; they do not authorize startling, striking,
shaming, heroic endurance, or coercing practitioners ([[Primal Feel and the
Zen Keisaku - 1 of 2]] S1-S2; [[Primal Feel and the Zen Keisaku - 2 of 2]]
S5-S7).

Finally, the Source/polarity description is speculative. It may be useful for interpreting Shinzen's advanced language around Zero, expansion, contraction, pure space, and Gone, but it should not be loaded as physics, neuroscience, or a universal metaphysics of self.

## Implications
This concept adds a middle option between two simpler readings of Gone. Gone is not only peaceful cessation, and Gone-related distress is not only catastrophic dark night. There may also be an emotionally hot, body-based layer that Shinzen interprets as deep purification and reconstruction material.

It also sharpens the reconstruction side of practice. If deconstructive Gone exposes primordial body emotion, then positive affect, behavior, cognition, lovingkindness, and support become structural complements rather than optional mood repair.
