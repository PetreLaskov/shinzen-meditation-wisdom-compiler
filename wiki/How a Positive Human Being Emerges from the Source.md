This five-minute `@ShinzenVideos` Q&A answers where positive practice belongs
relative to Both-Gone or Source. Shinzen's central move is to deny that
positivity is intrinsic to the impersonal Source, then require the human
return from Source to manifest love, compassion, and ordinary helpfulness.

## Why This Talk Matters
The existing wiki already had Source-love and service pages. This talk adds
the negative qualifier they need: Both-Gone or Source is not itself positive,
negative, love, hate, ethics, or law. The positive side belongs to the human
being who is changed by Source contact and returns as a more loving,
compassionate, ordinary, and helpful person.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Positive cultivation or positive identification is not "within"
  Both-Gone; Shinzen says the point is no positive and no negative, as close
  to the nonhuman as one can get.
- **S2**: Source is not intrinsically love or hate, but when the impersonal
  or nonhuman contacts a human being, it tends to trigger deep human love.
- **S3**: Shinzen frames the central mystery as human contact with the
  nonhuman nurturing and improving the person by way of reaction, so that
  ripples of love remain on return.
- **S4**: The authentic person is imaged as both puppeteer and puppet: an
  invisible hand or power source animates the person, but the puppet's surface
  should manifest love and compassion in the world.
- **S5**: Shinzen uses a radio/TV analogy: Source is like wattage or pure
  energy, while the human surface carries the modulated or encoded message.
- **S6**: He contrasts classical or "Class C" enlightenment with crazy-wisdom
  specialness: the surface should be approachable, admirable, helpful, and
  ordinary.
- **S7**: Shinzen explicitly treats the puppet, broadcasting, alternating,
  and spatial images as metaphors; he prefers a spatial metaphor of inner
  space animating the person, but leaves metaphor choice flexible.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: The talk blocks positivity inflation at the Source level.
  Positive practice and human love are real in Shinzen's system, but not
  because Source is itself a positive emotional content.
- **S4-S6**: The return-to-life test is concrete. A Source-touched person
  should become more loving, compassionate, ordinary, approachable, admirable,
  and helpful, not more exempt, theatrical, or special.
- **S5-S7**: The analogies clarify the relation between Source and expression
  while staying analogical. Energy-source language should not be upgraded into
  physics, metaphysics, or conduct proof.

## Practice Handles
- If a practitioner asks whether positive states are found inside Both-Gone,
  route the answer through polarity: Both-Gone/Source is not positive content;
  human return from it may produce love and compassion.
- If Source contact is claimed, ask about the surface: does the person become
  more approachable, admirable, helpful, ordinary, and compassionate?
- If someone uses "crazy wisdom" or specialness to excuse odd presentation,
  use this talk as a boundary: Shinzen's preferred mature surface is ordinary
  helpful humanness.
- Keep Nurture Positive distinct from Source metaphysics. Positive
  Feel/Image/Talk remains a constructive human-surface practice, not proof of
  direct access to Source.

## Summary
The student asks where cultivating or identifying positive belongs within
Both-Gone. Shinzen answers that it does not: Both-Gone is no positive and no
negative. He then clarifies the paradox. Source or the nonhuman is not
intrinsically love or hate, but when a human being touches it, the human
being is changed. On return, love and compassion may appear as ripples of
that contact.

Shinzen then turns to expression. The authentic person is both the invisible
animating hand and the human puppet. The inner power source is not the same
as the message carried at the surface, so he uses a broadcasting analogy:
wattage is pure energy, while modulation carries music or meaning. In the
same way, Source may animate the person, but the human surface should carry a
message of love, compassion, and helpful ordinary presence.

The final boundary is explicit. Shinzen says this is what he calls classical
or Class C enlightenment, as opposed to crazy wisdom or other forms where the
surface is not necessarily an approachable, admirable, helpful, ordinary
human being. He leaves the metaphors flexible, preferring a spatial image of
inner space animating the person.

## Source Frame
The source is trying to preserve both sides of Shinzen's model: the
impersonal or nonhuman depth is not sentimental positivity, and the human
return from that depth should still look loving and useful. It is strong
evidence for Shinzen's teaching distinction and weak evidence for objective
metaphysics or reliable conduct outcomes.

The talk's most important calibration is that positive human expression is a
return effect, not a property of Source itself. That prevents Source-love
language from being flattened into either blank impersonality or emotional
idealism.

## Weakest Claims
- The causal claim that Source contact nurtures and improves the human being
  is Shinzen's contemplative interpretation, not independent evidence that
  realized people reliably behave well.
- "Classical" or "Class C" enlightenment is local oral terminology here and
  should not be treated as a universal Buddhist taxonomy.
- The radio, puppet, alternating, and spatial metaphors clarify the teaching
  but do not prove metaphysics, physics, psychology, or conduct outcomes.

## Important Omissions

- No ethics, consent, feedback, repair, or teacher-conduct protocol for

- No Nurture Positive instructions despite the opening question about
  positive cultivation.
- No discussion of cases where Source contact, emptiness, or nonhuman

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says Source is not love or hate, while several other sources speak
  of Source-love. The reconciliation is source-level: love belongs to human
  contact with Source and the return ripples, not to Source as emotional
  content.
- "Authentic person" is both puppeteer and puppet, which can help express
  non-doership but can also blur responsibility if separated from the
  ordinary-surface test.
- Classical/Class C enlightenment is used here as ordinary helpful surface,
  while other pages record different Shinzen uses of "classical
  enlightenment." Future use must route by source context.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not use impersonal Source language to excuse coldness, lack of repair,
  ethical indifference, or unapproachable teacher behavior.
- Do not use the puppet metaphor to evade accountability for effects,
  consent, harm, or role responsibility.
- Do not use crazy-wisdom language to override the talk's ordinary-surface
  criterion.
- Do not treat positive charisma, warmth, or helping impulse as proof of
  realization.
