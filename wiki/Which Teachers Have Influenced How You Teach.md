This short Q&A answers a biographical question by giving a transmission map. Shinzen presents his teaching as standing on two sets of shoulders: contemplative masters and prehistoric explorers of consciousness on one side, and Western mathematical, empirical, rigorous, skeptical, pragmatic science on the other.

## Why This Talk Matters
The durable value is a compact map of what Shinzen says he extracted from several sources: Sasaki Roshi's Expansion-Contraction paradigm, U Ba Khin-style body sensation emphasis, Mahasi-style noting as modified by Shinzen, Vajrayana/Shingon body-speech-mind attention to visual, auditory, and somatic experience, prehistoric shamanic concentration/equanimity, and Western science's mathematical-empirical-skeptical-pragmatic rigor.

## Summary
Shinzen opens with Newton's "shoulders of giants" image. He then says he does not know whether he has seen farther than other teachers, but he has certainly been privileged to stand on giants' shoulders.

The spiritual influences are organized by practice functions. Sasaki Roshi is the strongest influence and supplied a consciousness paradigm based on Expansion and Contraction, which Shinzen reads as a vast generalization of early Buddhist impermanence. Burmese vipassana supplied body-sensation emphasis through the U Ba Khin lineage and noting through the Mahasi lineage, though Shinzen says he changed noting. Vajrayana/Shingon supplied attention to visual, auditory, and somatic dimensions of subjective and objective experience. Behind those named lineages, he places unnamed prehistoric shamanic explorers whose rituals, ceremonies, and ordeals cultivated concentration and equanimity.

The second shoulder-set is Western science and mathematics. Shinzen names Newton, Euclid, Bacon, Feynman, Einstein, Maxwell, and the broader mathematical, empirical, rigorous, skeptical, pragmatic tradition. He then summarizes his teaching as an attempt to create a contemporary, modern, as-close-to-science-as-possible version of what the contemplative and shamanic inheritance developed.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen separates himself from teaching within Sasaki's Zen lineage while saying Sasaki's paradigm deeply shaped his model.
- He presents a contemporary scientific-style synthesis while grounding it in inherited contemplative and shamanic practice worlds whose evidence standards differ.
- Gratitude to lineages can support humility, but it can also be misread as authority by association unless bounded by practice results and explicit evidence tiers.

## Easily Misused Claims
