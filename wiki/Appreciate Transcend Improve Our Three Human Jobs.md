This 2014 `@ShinzenVideos` talk is a service and aim-structure teaching. It
gives Shinzen's three human jobs in one oral arc: appreciate self and world
as sensory form, transcend form through Source/Zero contact, and improve
self and world through character, behavior, ordinary service, and teaching or
supporting mindfulness.

## Teaching Register
The register is broad map teaching with practice instruction, comparative
translation, and service framing. Shinzen speaks informally to practitioners:
he uses flower, pain, thought-emotion eruption, Zero, mountain/no-mountain,
ABC behavior change, and service examples to make the three-job map usable
rather than merely taxonomic.

## Why This Talk Matters
The wiki already had the three jobs from the See Hear Feel introduction and
Total Happiness pages. This talk adds a direct oral source that links each
job to a concrete practice route: appreciate through complete sensory
experience of outer and inner form, transcend through contact with rich
nothingness and the two polar movements of Source, and improve through
affect, behavior, cognition, support structures, material service, and
Dharma service.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen frames the human condition as having three jobs:
  appreciate, transcend, and improve self/world or form.
- **S2**: Appreciating the world means appreciating both outer sensory form
  and inner reactive or proactive form; either outer form can expand while
  inner selfing contracts, or the whole outer-inner sensory event can be
  experienced mindfully.
- **S3**: Thought and emotion are not the enemy of meditation; the enemy is
  unconsciousness, coagulation, and scatteredness around thought and emotion.
- **S4**: Transcendence means going beyond form through contact with Source:
  a rich nothing, Zero, or one nothing that breaks into the two movements of
  Expansion and Contraction while ordinary self/world continues to arise.
- **S5**: Source contact and enlightenment incline improvement but do not
  guarantee it; after "no mountain" there remains a large ordinary-life
  mountain of becoming an admirable person.
- **S6**: Improving self can be organized through affect, behavior, and
  cognition, using Nurture Positive, antidote practice, deconstruction, and,
  when needed, external accountability and support.
- **S7**: Improving the world includes material service, family service,
  social change, teaching mindfulness, and supporting people who teach
  mindfulness.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The three jobs keep Shinzen's aim from collapsing into either
  state attainment, self-improvement, or altruism alone.
- **S2-S3**: Appreciation is not only enjoying outer sights and sounds. It
  includes self-reaction, memory, planning, fantasy, and emotional eruption
  when these are met with CCE rather than suppression.
- **S4**: The transcend branch should be routed through Source and
  Expansion-Contraction owner pages, not through generic "escape the world"
  language.
- **S5**: The talk explicitly blocks the conduct-guarantee mistake:
  realization may make improvement easier and more attractive, but it does
  not automatically make a person admirable.
- **S6-S7**: Improvement is a mixed route. Meditation contributes through
  positive construction and deconstruction, while ordinary support,
  accountability, family, material service, teaching, and support for
  teachers remain part of the job.

## Practice Handles
- When appreciation is the task, include both outer See/Hear/Feel and inner
  Image/Talk/Feel. A flower, pain, memory, fantasy, or emotional eruption can
  be appreciated when enough concentration, clarity, and equanimity are
  present.
- When thoughts and emotions feel like obstacles, distinguish the content
  from unconsciousness, coagulation, and scatteredness around the content.
  The route is completion, not blanket suppression.
- When transcendence appears, ask whether the practitioner is actually in
  contact with Gone, Zero, Expansion-Contraction, or Source-like
  disappearance/repolarization, rather than merely holding a philosophy.
- When a practitioner claims realization, keep the "big mountain" of ordinary
  character, behavior, support, and service visible.
- For behavior change, combine Nurture Positive and deconstruction with
  external support when the behavior does not shift.
- For service, keep both ordinary material service and Dharma service visible:
  teaching mindfulness and supporting teachers are service branches, not the
  whole of service.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by defining three human jobs: appreciate, transcend, and
improve self/world. Appreciation is sensory. The outer world is physical
See, Hear, and Feel; the inner world is mental Image, mental Talk, and
emotional Feel, including subtle or subliminal activation. A satisfying
experience can happen when outer form expands and inner selfing contracts,
or when the entire outer-inner event is experienced with mindfulness. Even
memory, planning, fantasy, and intense thought-emotion eruption become
workable when they are not unconscious, coagulated, or scattered.

The transcend section gives Shinzen's Source-polarity language. He describes
what is beyond form as Source, destination, rich nothingness, Zero, the one
nothing, and the two movements of Expansion and Contraction. He maps this
through Buddhist, Hindu, Taoist, Christian, Jewish, and Islamic vocabulary,
but the practice point is sensory: one may function in ordinary form while
not fully losing contact with Source or its two polar movements.

The improve section blocks a common overclaim. Shinzen says Source contact
and enlightenment make improvement easier and create a proclivity toward it,
but they do not guarantee character or behavior. The post-transcend task is
the large ordinary-life mountain of becoming admirable. He organizes personal
improvement through affect, behavior, and cognition; uses Nurture Positive
to cultivate positive habits; uses antidote practice and deconstruction for
negative patterns; and names external accountability such as twelve-step
support when meditation is not enough. Improving the world includes material
service, family, social change, teaching mindfulness, and supporting
mindfulness teachers.

## Source Frame
The source wants the listener to treat the three jobs as practical life
architecture. Appreciate, transcend, and improve are not three abstract
values; each names a way to work with sensory experience, Source contact,
ordinary behavior, and service. The talk's strongest evidence is for
Shinzen's own map and teaching moves. Its comparative-religion, math, and
universal-mysticism claims are oral teaching bridges and should remain
calibrated rather than treated as independent scholarship.

## Weakest Claims
- The comparative vocabulary around apophatic theology, Jewish `ayin`,
  Taoist wuji/taiji, Buddhist shunyata/nirodha, Hindu yoga, and Islamic
  fana is broad oral translation, not scholarship.
- The set-theory riff about different sizes of zero is a teaching analogy
  and should not be promoted as mathematical argument.
- The claim that all sufficiently attained contemplatives talk about the
  same special nothing is a strong perennialist claim that requires
  calibration.
- The service value of teaching/supporting mindfulness is clear inside
  Shinzen's system but not evidence that any particular teacher, method, or
  institution is beneficial.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not give safety criteria for using intense thought-emotion
  eruption as catharsis rather than stopping, grounding, or seeking support.
- It does not define clinical, legal, or social-service boundaries for
  behavior change, addiction-adjacent patterns, or family/social service.
- It does not provide independent evidence for behavior-change outcomes,
  comparative-mysticism claims, or the effectiveness of mindfulness
  teaching.
- It does not specify teacher competence, consent, feedback, or
  accountability criteria for Dharma service.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says transcendence makes improvement easier and gives a
  proclivity toward improvement, while explicitly denying that it guarantees
  admirable behavior. Future reasoning should preserve the weaker, behavior-
  accountable version.
- Shinzen says thought and emotion are not the enemy, but intense emotional
  eruption still needs the wiki's safety boundaries when overwhelm,
  dissociation, medical risk, or behavior worsening appears.
- The comparative Source language is useful as teaching translation but can
  overstate cross-tradition sameness if detached from source-frame caution.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Thought and emotion are not the enemy" should not become permission to
  intensify unsupported overwhelm or ignore clinical care.
- "Everyone experiences no mountain" should not flatten practice
  differences or become a status claim.
- "Improve the world" should not collapse service into teaching mindfulness
  only, nor should teaching/supporting teachers bypass competence and
  accountability checks.
- "Source contact makes improvement easier" should not be used to
  self-certify conduct or pressure others to accept a realized person's
  behavior.
