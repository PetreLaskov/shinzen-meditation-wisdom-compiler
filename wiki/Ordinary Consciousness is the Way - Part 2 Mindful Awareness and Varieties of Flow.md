This edited YouTube transcript is the second short instructional talk in
Shinzen's "Ordinary Consciousness is the Way" sequence. Its central move is
to distribute Flow and Gone across inner visual, auditory, and body spaces,
then warn that unusually vivid imagery should be routed through the movement
of the medium rather than captured by message or power content.

## Teaching Register
Instructional oral teaching with altered-phenomena warning. Shinzen moves
from ordinary blank-screen practice to rare vivid imagery, then returns to
ordinary talk and body sensations; this structure keeps the power-phenomena
branch subordinate to the general Flow/Gone method.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk makes the Way of Flow more operational across the sensory grid. It
adds concrete handles for visual Rest, image morphing, subtle talk-space
vibration, sentence vanishings, whole-body puffing/pulling, and pressure as
momentum or force Flow, while preserving the boundary that visionary content
is less liberating than the movement of the medium.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The darkness/brightness behind closed eyes can function as a blank
  mental screen; if stable it may support kasina-like stabilization, and if
  swirling or vibrating it is Flow.
- **S2**: Mental image space can show Flow through fade-in/fade-out,
  melting, morphing, expansion, contraction, and animation; images also
  vanish, which routes them through Gone.
- **S3**: When imagery becomes realistic or spirit-like, Shinzen allows that
  its message may be honored but says the preferred liberation route is to
  focus on the movement of the medium rather than the content of the powers
  world.
- **S4**: Subtle auditory thinking can contain a background vibratory current
  beneath explicit words; explicit talk may expand, grip, and abruptly vanish.
- **S5**: Internal talk is easy to initiate, hard to stop, and often one of
  the easiest sensory phenomena in which to notice abrupt vanishing.
- **S6**: Whole-body awareness often reveals puffing, pulling, tingling,
  vibration, undulation, and pressure as Flow rather than stable body content.
- **S7**: Pressure can be investigated as inward, outward, or bidirectional
  force Flow; the practice move is to yield to the force pattern instead of
  making pressure into self-blame or resistance.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2** make visual Rest and visual thinking practice-ready without
  requiring special visual effects: stability, Flow, and Gone are all valid
  branches.
- **S3** gives a compact altered-phenomena router: if "spirits" appear, do
  not let message, fear, or fascination displace CCE with the Flow that makes
  the image seem alive.
- **S4-S5** sharpen Hear In practice by distinguishing subtle background
  processing, explicit talk expansion/contraction, and easy-to-detect talk
  vanishings.
- **S6-S7** turn body pressure from a problem story into a force-pattern
  object, with inward, outward, and bidirectional pressure as Expansion,
  Contraction, or Both.

## Practice Handles
- With eyes closed, check whether the visual blank is stable, restful,
  swirling, vibrating, fading, or morphing; do not force movement if it is
  stable.
- If an image appears, watch how it fades, melts, morphs, expands,
  contracts, animates, or vanishes before interpreting its content.
- If internal talk is present, notice whether there is a subtle buzz beneath
  words, whether words boom or grip, and whether acknowledging a word makes
  it vanish.
- When the whole body is included, look for puffing, pulling, tingling,
  vibration, undulation, and pressure patterns rather than only local
  sensations.
- When pressure is salient, ask whether the force is outward, inward, or
  bidirectional, then yield to that force pattern while preserving ordinary
  safety and medical/common-sense boundaries.

## Summary
Shinzen begins in visual Rest. The darkness or brightness behind closed eyes
may stabilize as a kasina-like support, but it may also swirl or vibrate; in
that case it is Flow. Visual thinking then adds active image phenomena:
images arise, fade, vanish, melt, morph, and sometimes appear unusually
alive or three-dimensional.

The rare vivid-image branch receives the talk's strongest warning. If a
person sees something spirit-like, Shinzen says the message can be honored,
but his preferred liberation path is to release preoccupation with the
content and study the underlying Flow that animates the medium. In his idiom,
being close to "spirits" can become close to "Great Spirit" only when the
practitioner shifts from power content to formless movement.

The talk then returns to ordinary inner sound and body. Internal talk can
contain a subtle background current, explicit words can boom or grip, and
the ending of words is often an unusually easy place to notice Gone. Whole
body awareness may show puffing, pulling, tingling, vibration, undulation,
and pressure. Pressure becomes a force-flow object: outward, inward, or
bidirectional momentum rather than evidence that the practitioner is doing
something wrong.

## Source Frame
The source is trying to make Flow and Gone usable in several sensory spaces,
not to promote visionary content, force experiences, or metaphysical claims.
Its "spirits" and "Great Spirit" language should be treated as Shinzen's
oral altered-phenomena router: the liberating object is the movement of
experience, while objective claims about entities remain outside the talk's
evidence. The Einstein/momentum-flux pressure image is a teaching analogy for
force detection, not a physics claim that validates a contemplative model.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that animated realistic imagery can lead to Source by focusing
  on expansion and contraction is a source-level Shinzen practice claim, not
  evidence that spirit-like images are objectively real or universally safe.
- The kasina reference is brief and does not supply a full absorption
  protocol, readiness criteria, or jhana map.
- The pressure-as-momentum analogy helps reframe sensation but should not be
  treated as medical, biomechanical, or physics validation.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not give clinical criteria for hallucination-like imagery,
  mania, psychosis, trauma activation, sleep deprivation, substance effects,
  neurological symptoms, or destabilizing fear.
- It does not specify when pressure should be treated as injury, illness,
  posture, medical concern, or ordinary stress rather than only practice
  material.
- It does not define dosage or stop rules for practitioners who become
  frightened by vivid images, internal talk disappearance, whole-body Flow,
  or pressure intensification.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen says spirit-like content can be honored as message while also
  saying the preferred route is not to be preoccupied with the message. The
  tension should be preserved as a hierarchy: content may matter, but
  liberation practice privileges medium Flow.
- The talk presents stable visual blankness as good and moving visual
  blankness as Flow. Future guidance should not imply stability is inferior
  or that visual Rest must be made to move.
- Pressure may become more intense briefly when yielded to. This supports
  the force-Flow frame but also increases the need for safety boundaries
  when pain, panic, trauma, or medical risk is present.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Seeing spirits" can be misused to inflate status, validate entities, or
  bypass clinical assessment; the talk's practice move is sensory Flow, not
  ontological certainty.
- "Pressure is Flow" can be misused to endure unsafe pain, ignore medical
  symptoms, or blame insufficient surrender.
- Internal-talk vanishings can be misread as suppressing thought; Shinzen's
  claim is that naturally noticed disappearance is insight into
  impermanence, not that thought must be stopped.
