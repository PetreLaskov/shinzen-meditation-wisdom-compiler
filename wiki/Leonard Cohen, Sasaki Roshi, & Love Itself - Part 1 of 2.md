## Summary
Shinzen begins with Leonard Cohen's *Ten New Songs*, noting its dedication to Joshu Sasaki Roshi and identifying "Love Itself" as Cohen's poetic version of Sasaki Roshi's teisho. He then summarizes what he calls Sasaki's one talk: Zero contains positive and negative, breaks into Expansion and Contraction, creates a vibrating cleft of space, and matures into a feeling-thinking self that either knows or forgets its origin.

The middle of the talk translates this through Shinzen's Flow vocabulary. Expansion and Contraction are named as the fundamental Flow flavors underneath wavy Flow and vibratory Flow. Shinzen describes a blissful, misty, bubbly dissolution of body, mind, and world, but immediately warns that its sweetness can become an attachment. If not clung to, that vibratory field can flatten into Zero, or Gone.

The closing teaching is return and love. After Gone, there is nowhere to go but back to self and world, now seen differently. Repeating that cycle until Source and ordinary experience are on a continuum is Shinzen's living nirvana frame. He then identifies Sasaki Roshi's true love with Zero, meaning a release deeper than even celestial love or "spirit massage."
