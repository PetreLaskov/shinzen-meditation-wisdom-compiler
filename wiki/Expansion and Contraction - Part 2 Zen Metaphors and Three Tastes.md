Advanced map and lineage translation. The talk uses Sasaki Roshi's provocative

## Why This Talk Matters
The durable value is the bridge between poetry and operational practice:
Expansion-Contraction is not only a metaphysical polarity list. In this talk,
the practitioner is asked to notice how each momentary self and scene is born
between push-out and pull-in forces, and how surrendering both sides can
deconstruct thing-like self into three tastes: positive, negative, and Zero.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen presents Sasaki Roshi's positive/negative list as a polarity teaching where life/death, being/not-being, future/past, father/mother, outside/inside, God/devil, success/failure, praise/blame, and similar pairs point to the same abstract pattern rather than literal moral ranking.
- **S2**: Shinzen contrasts Zen and vipassana teaching style: Zen is a powerful poem that may say too little, while vipassana should be a carefully crafted essay but can say too much.
- **S3**: The "child" born between father and mother is not only a physical baby; it can be this moment of consciousness, the self of the moment, or any scale of self born between polar forces.
- **S4**: If Zero coming apart into Expansion-Contraction is seen as it molds Feel/Image/Talk self and Touch/Sight/Sound scene, the momentary self would arise as an enlightened self that knows its job.
- **S5**: Allowing every push-out and every pull-in in sensory experience to occur simultaneously can collect the pushes into effortless expansion of pure space and the pulls into effortless contraction of pure space.
- **S6**: Shinzen's electrolysis analogy says the many ordinary sensory "tastes" disappear into three tastes: positive, negative, and Zero, with Zero pervading both polarities and surrounding the whole field.

## Summary
Shinzen begins with Sasaki Roshi's list of positive and negative pairings:
life and death, being and not-being, future and past, father and mother,
objective and subjective, God and devil, success and failure, praise and
blame. He immediately warns that literal reading misses the teaching. The
point is not that mother is devil or negative is bad; the point is that Zen
poetry is asking for abstraction strong enough to see one repeated polarity
pattern.

He then widens the phrase "born in between." Humans are born between sky and
earth, East Asian flower arranging includes sky, earth, and human principles,
and each moment is born between fundamental Flow. The child can be a physical
baby, but in this teaching it is also the moment of consciousness or any scale
of self. If the practitioner could see Zero breaking apart and vibrating into
the Feel/Image/Talk self and Touch/Sight/Sound scene, that momentary self
would arise as enlightened.

The final section gives the vipassana explanation of the poem. All sensory
affirmations and negations in the present moment can be analyzed into tangled
affirming and negating parts. If everything pushing out is allowed to push out
and everything pulling in is allowed to pull in, the pushes gather as pure
expansion of space and the pulls as pure contraction of space. Shinzen likens
this to electrolysis: many different tastes disappear, leaving the taste of
positive, the taste of negative, and the taste of Zero, with Zero pervading
and surrounding the polarities.
