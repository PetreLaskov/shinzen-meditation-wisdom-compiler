This short Shinzen YouTube talk gives a concrete spatial image for
simultaneous Expansion-Contraction and then offers a deliberately uncertain
brain-anatomy analogy. Its value is mainly instructional: it helps future
agents describe the shape without turning the image into neuroscience.

## Teaching Register
Advanced-map instruction with a speculative analogy. Shinzen is answering
what "shape" simultaneous Expansion-Contraction would have, then explicitly
marks the brain-anatomy comparison as something he does not know to be
connected.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk preserves a useful correction to flat arising-and-passing imagery:
simultaneous Expansion-Contraction is not merely up/down or out/in along one
axis, but a center-periphery, all-directions, spherical gush-and-gather
pattern. It also reinforces the wiki's Source-and-science boundary because
Shinzen names the corona-radiata analogy as intriguing but uncertain.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: If simultaneous Expansion-Contraction needs a shape, Shinzen gives
  "concentric spheres spreading out and concentric spheres coming back" as the
  image.
- **S2**: He also describes it as a fountain that gushes from the center and
  gathers to the center in all directions at once, not merely upward and
  downward.
- **S3**: Shinzen says the large-scale sensory-input and motor-output
  pathways through the spinal cord, cortex, and corona radiata visually
  resemble the same gushing-and-gathering pattern, but he explicitly says he
  does not know whether the anatomy has anything to do with the contemplative
  pattern.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The concentric-spheres image gives future practice reasoning a
  compact non-flat spatial handle for simultaneous Expansion-Contraction.
- **S2**: The three-dimensional fountain image corrects a too-simple vertical
  or two-dimensional reading by emphasizing center-to-periphery and
  periphery-to-center simultaneity.
- **S3**: The brain analogy is valuable mostly as a boundary marker:
  Shinzen's visual resemblance claim is not evidence that the contemplative
  pattern is produced by, or proves, a neural mechanism.

## Practice Handles
When Expansion-Contraction is already salient, a practitioner or guide can
test whether the perceived movement is merely alternating or whether it
resembles all-directional spreading and gathering around a center. The handle
should not be used to force a visualized shape, chase a brain map, or require
an advanced perception.

## Summary
Shinzen says the shape of simultaneous Expansion-Contraction would be
concentric spheres spreading outward and returning inward, or a fountain
gushing and gathering at the same time in every direction. The point is not a
new technique so much as a clarifying spatial image for the spherical
observer-including frame developed in nearby Gate 4 talks.

He then notes that large-scale brain anatomy looks intriguingly similar:
sensory input travels upward and inward, processing moves across cortex, and
motor output travels back downward and outward through the corona radiata and
spinal cord. He says this is grossly oversimplified and explicitly uncertain,
so the analogy should remain illustrative rather than evidential.

## Source Frame
The source is trying to make an advanced Flow image more imaginable. Its
practice claim is spatial and phenomenological; its neuroscience comment is
an analogy of visual resemblance. The transcript gives no clinical,
scientific, or mechanistic evidence, and Shinzen's own caveat should govern
future use.
