This page owns unusual-state and deep-map safety. It covers the boundary between Shinzen's practice interpretation of altered phenomena and the clinical, ethical, metaphysical, or status risks that can cluster around them.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Unusual states should be routed as sensory practice material before being treated as attainment, objective ontology, power, or authority.
- **Why this matters**: Flow, Gone, no-self, powers, bhanga, Source afterglow, and primordial Feel are high-leverage in Shinzen's system and high-risk when overread.
- **Key tensions**: The sources provide strong anti-status and source-posture cautions, but not full clinical, emergency, sleep/substance/medication, trauma, or teacher-competence differentials.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's altered-phenomena routing frame; speculative outside that frame.

## What We Know
[[Intermediate Realm]] gives the broad altered-phenomena rule. Visions, energies, bliss, light, entities, out-of-body impressions, mind-reading impressions, past-life impressions, heat, shaking, and hypersensitivity can occur, but they are not required for progress. Subjective vividness is not evidence of objective existence, and Shinzen is skeptical or agnostic about powers and entities ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S1-S17).

The same source gives the practice route: decompose altered phenomena into physical body sensation, emotional body sensation, mental image, internal talk, Flow, and Gone, then meet them with CCE rather than fear, craving, status, message fixation, or Diversion. It also names illness, trauma, sleep deprivation, drugs, fasting, and chance as possible routes into intermediate-realm descent without giving differential criteria ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S4, S15-S17).

[[No-Self And Personality]] gives the no-self boundary. No-self means no thing-self, not no self-experience or no personality. The positive criterion is that self as image, talk, and emotional body sensation becomes more trackable and fluid, while self-arising is allowed and clarified rather than rejected ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S1-S18).

[[Gone]] and [[What Is Mindfulness]] give the dark-night boundary. Intense Goneness, Emptiness, and No-Self can produce fear, loss, confusion, disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, hopelessness, or difficult adjustment to an attenuated self. Shinzen's internal interventions are to accentuate subtle positive aspects of the void, deconstruct negative reactions, reconstruct positive affect/behavior/cognition, and add sustained teacher/practitioner support when needed ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S9; [[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S15-S18; [[What Is Mindfulness]] S10-S11).

[[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] now makes DPDR
the clearest explicit clinical neighbor for no-self and emptiness. Shinzen
calls DPDR enlightenment's evil twin because no-thingness of self and
paper-thin world may appear in both, while the outcome is opposite:
empowering freedom in enlightenment and nihilistic dysfunction in DPDR. He
also says no one knows why possibly similar nothingness has opposite effects
([[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] S1-S3).

[[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]] adds that dark-night-like distress
usually should not be assumed to be fully practice-caused. Shinzen says
perennial dark-night presentations often include ordinary depression,
anxiety, or clinical biochemical factors, and he encourages professional
assessment and openness to standard treatment while keeping medication
decisions outside the meditation teacher's role ([[Classic Dark Night or
Clinical Issues]] S1-S7).

[[How We Evolve and Integrate]] adds a spectrum and an overlabeling warning.
Shinzen reserves dark night for difficulty integrating emptiness or
nothingness, not every difficult meditation experience. He also places
flatline between easy void integration and rare DPDR-like severity, where old
agitation has faded but motivation, bounce, and human expressiveness have not
yet been rebuilt ([[How We Evolve and Integrate]] S6-S8).

[[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] gives the compact practice-only
response when the issue is advanced-student pit-of-emptiness distress rather
than a clinical mixed case. Shinzen names loss of humanity, edge, and dynamic
motivation as warning signs, then says to see the freak-out as empty and
balance the void by intentionally creating a new positive self through
positive Feel/Image/Talk ([[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] S1-S5).

[[The Spring of the Void]] adds the positive-void teaching idiom without
changing the safety rule. Shinzen says emptiness is not only bleak or vast but
can be directly experienced as bounciness and springiness, "the spring of the
void" ([[The Spring of the Void]] S1-S2). Use this as a possible balancing
image for cold or nihilistic emptiness, but not as a substitute for clinical,
functional, support, or positive-reconstruction checks when void distress is
active.

[[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]] gives
the shorter recursive version of the same altered-state router. Fear after a
deeper or no-self-like state, and flatness around the dark night or pit of
the void, are treated as reactions caused by practice; Shinzen's internal
move is to practice with the reaction itself. The source is important because
it keeps fear and bleakness inside CCE when workable, but it gives no
clinical or support differential for severe fear, relational withdrawal, loss
of humanity, depression, DPDR, trauma activation, or functional decline
([[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]]
S3-S5).

[[Primordial Feel]] marks an especially speculative Gone-side frontier.
Shinzen interprets some Gone-triggered chaotic body-emotion as infant-like,
purgatorial, and reparentable, but those are source-attributed contemplative
claims rather than established developmental or trauma criteria ([[Science of
Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S15-S18). [[Primal Feel and
the Zen Keisaku - 1 of 2]] extends the frontier beyond Gone: a ritualized
threat cue can expose primitive body terror when Image/Talk quiets, and that
terror may turn into Flow, merger, and bliss, but the same source also warns
that keisaku use can foster fear and craving ([[Primal Feel and the Zen
Keisaku - 1 of 2]] S2-S6). [[Primal Feel and the Zen Keisaku - 2 of 2]]
adds Soryu's companion pattern: meditation pride collapses, resisted pain
and threat intensify, and nonresistance becomes Flow and uplift, while the
talk explicitly rejects heroic keisaku mythology ([[Primal Feel and the Zen
Keisaku - 2 of 2]] S1-S7).

[[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] adds the layer-by-layer version of
the same frontier. Shinzen says the concentrated, clear, equanimous self,
witness, no-self, or higher self can integrate a freaked-out infant-animal
layer, but as that layer loses influence it may expose a more primitive and
chaotic stratum of body emotion. This makes primal Feel a non-linear
purification model, not merely a single fear event; it also increases the
need to distinguish workable emotional-body practice from destabilization
or clinical overread ([[Working Through the Primal Feel Strata]] S3-S7).

[[Reparenting Our Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure
Feeling]] adds the most explicit Gone-triggered live router for this frontier.
When vanishings become global and agitating, Shinzen first checks correlation
and sensory category; only when the agitation is primarily Feel does he read
it as chaotic infant-like Feel exposed by well-done Gone practice. The same
source is safety-relevant because it frames ego death as something to take in
manageable doses and allows either sitting or napping ([[Reparenting Our
Freaked Out Infant - Noting All Vanishings & Gone in Pure Feeling]] S1-S7).

[[Dissolution]] and [[Source Afterglow Boundary]] add the Source-side boundary. Source/Zero is not directly experienced at the moment of cancellation; what returns is an after-representation or afterglow made of image, talk, feeling, fulfillment, safety, peace, and love. Bhanga may include blissful vibratory Flow or harsh chaos, fear, loss of center, disorientation, and loss of borders, but the sources do not define clinical differentials or stop rules ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S12-S19).

[[Reality & Sensory Experience]] adds the direct epistemic version of the
same boundary. Shinzen explicitly says he avoids claims about objective
reality behind sensory experience and treats his everything-at-once causal
nexus idea as wild conjecture. That makes Source, suchness, and
everything-language safety-sensitive when they start functioning as proof,
status, or reality certainty rather than sensory-practice framing
([[Reality & Sensory Experience]] S1-S5).

[[Shinzen's Weird Meditation]] adds an advanced visual no-objectification
case. Shinzen reports paper-thin color/form, a breath-like need to objectify
self/world, psychedelic-like sacred-space merging, and incandescent terror
moving as Flow. The same source explicitly warns against copying the practice,
so these signs should be routed through CCE, support, ordinary functioning,
and DPDR/substance/sleep/clinical differentials rather than treated as
attainment instructions ([[Shinzen's Weird Meditation]] S1-S6).

[[Non-Dual Awareness]] adds a non-duality terminology boundary. Shinzen
distinguishes simple object-side oneness without Feel/Image/Talk reaction,
formless subject-object disappearance into Zero, and his preferred stronger
use: habitual Zero-polarization-Zero cycling in daily life. That distinction
helps prevent a single non-dual event or open-awareness state from being
treated as mature integration, while still requiring the same DPDR,
dissociation, support, and behavior checks as other no-self and Source
claims ([[Non-Dual Awareness]] S1-S5).

[[Dissolution (Bhanga), and T.S. Eliot]] sharpens that boundary with a
four-way router: no dissolution, heavenly dissolution, purgatorial
dissolution, or a mixture. It also gives a useful but risky distinction:
harsh dissolution may be purgatorial, meaning uncomfortable but purifying,
rather than hellish, meaning pointless suffering. Because the transcript
does not define the differential, future agents should treat that distinction
as a source-attributed practice frame, not as permission to intensify
frightening or destabilizing states ([[Dissolution (Bhanga), and T.S.
Eliot]] S3-S5).

[[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 1 of 3]] makes the
differential more urgent by giving a severe supported case. The student
reports post-retreat onset, childhood terrors, fear of going crazy,
unfamiliarity of surroundings, hiding at home, collapsed sight-space, shame,
guilt, and animal-death imagery. Shinzen's response includes specific
questions, sensory isolation, and harsh Flow routing, but the transcript does
not define when such features require grounding, stopping, therapy, medical
assessment, or emergency support ([[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga)
Process - 1 of 3]] S1-S5).

[[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 2 of 3]] adds the
disclosure and spiritual-emergence boundary. Shinzen says naming bhanga can
help practitioners understand what is happening and what to do, but it can
also create fear, craving, and comparison. The student's concern about
psychiatric misclassification strengthens the need for clinicians and guides
who can distinguish unusual spiritual reports from pathology without
dismissing genuine clinical risk ([[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga)
Process - 2 of 3]] S2-S4).

[[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 3 of 3]] adds the
pleasant altered-phenomena boundary. Shinzen coaches broad pleasant Flow,
eyes-open light, and eye-contact transfer, but the source does not define
how to distinguish stable integration from dissociation, visual-effect
chasing, teacher performance, or social/trauma activation ([[Experiences of
the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 3 of 3]] S3-S6).

[[Flow]] provides an anti-energy-fixation rule. Flow is ordinary experience under CCE, not a separate spiritual energy world, and wanting only pleasant Flow while avoiding remaining solidity self-limits practice ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 4 - The Way of Flow]] S13-S14; [[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 6 - The Many Faces of Impermanence]] S14-S16).

[[Shaktipat or Energy Transmission in Buddhism]] adds the transmission and
Kundalini version of that same boundary. Shinzen allows energy-cultivation
systems as distinct practice paradigms, but his own translation reads Shakti
or Kundalini as Flow when ordinary sensory experience reveals its wave nature
through CCE. The talk also creates a safety flag around transmission claims:
teacher contact should be tested by independent practice under difficult
conditions, not by dependency on repeated energetic charge ([[Shaktipat or
Energy Transmission in Buddhism]] S2-S7).

[[Mysticism As Concentration]] gives the comparative boundary. Cross-tradition parallels, shamanic ordeal, secular samadhi, and spontaneous awakening are useful in Shinzen's frame, but they do not prove one universal structure or erase cultural, doctrinal, clinical, consent, or safety differences ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 3 - Mysticism in World Culture]] S4-S12).

[[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] adds the clearest current boundary
for deity and archetypal identity practice. Shinzen says the sane,
non-possessive way to "become a deity" is to deliberately replace ordinary
internal talk, self-image, human feelings, and touch with mantra, deity image,
deity feel, and mudra or other ritual touch, while knowing the alternate
identity is constructed. The insight is into the arbitrary nature of
self-identification, not proof of possession, external entity contact, or
special authority ([[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] S3-S8).

[[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] adds a mild
altered-state boundary for dreams. Shinzen says lucid dream objects should
be handled with ordinary practice labels by how they present, and dream
plasticity can be treated as Flow when objects melt, soften, or become
permeable. This routes dream vividness and control into sensory practice
rather than power, ontology, or attainment, while leaving sleep-health,
nightmare, trauma, parasomnia, dissociation, and clinical criteria undefined
([[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] S2-S8).

[[Consciously Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem Mutata Resurgo]] adds a small
dropout boundary. Shinzen says brief decoupling from an active sight or touch
object may create sudden Rest, and that after long practice a dropout that
would be "not out" for a beginner may be interpreted as a moment with Source.
The source is useful precisely because it keeps the same surface sign
ambiguous: distraction, reverie, sleepiness, Rest, and deep dropout require
different routing rather than automatic Source attribution ([[Consciously
Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem Mutata Resurgo]] S1-S4).

[[Late Night Sitting Can Change Your Life]] adds the longer sleep-transition
version of that ambiguity. In Yaza, Shinzen says sleepiness waves may become
Rest and absorption, late-night dreamscape may expose mental images, and tired
vision may make See Out Flow easier to inspect. The same source is a safety
boundary because it explicitly links sleep deprivation with discomfort and
breakdown/abuse risk; sleep-transition phenomena should be routed as sensory
practice only when ordinary function, consent, and sleep-health checks remain
intact ([[Late Night Sitting Can Change Your Life]] S1-S8).

[[Density that is Unifying and Liberating]] adds a compact but high-risk
global-Gone edge. Shinzen distinguishes painful resisted density from
pleasant surrendered contracted Flow, then says the pleasant version is
partway toward global Gone and uses physiological trance language. The source
is useful for practice discrimination, but the breath and immobility material
is too brief and auto-caption-limited to function as guidance or attainment
criteria ([[Density that is Unifying and Liberating]] S4-S5).

## Boundary Questions
- Is the phenomenon becoming more sensory-decomposable and less self-special, or more message-fixated, status-laden, or fear/craving-driven?
- Is no-self freeing personality, or becoming self-aversion, blankness preference, dissociation, nihilism, or relational withdrawal?
- Is emptiness/no-self empowering and functional, or DPDR-like: derealizing, depersonalizing, nihilistic, meaning-stripping, and disabling?
- Is apparent dark night fully practice-based, or are depression, anxiety, burnout, biochemical factors, medication/substance effects, trauma, or other clinical conditions part of the case?
- Is "dark night" being used precisely for emptiness-integration distress, or
  loosely for any gnarly meditation or life difficulty?
- Is the void-side issue loss of humanity, edge, or motivation that needs positive self-reconstruction rather than more emptiness-only practice?
- Is no-self spontaneity becoming more responsive and able to learn from
  mistakes, or being used to excuse inappropriate behavior?
- Is "recycle the reaction" increasing sensory clarity and ordinary
  responsiveness, or is it being used to continue unsupported fear, bleakness,
  social withdrawal, or functional decline?
- Is Gone precise vanishing practice, or blankness, collapse, dissociation, nihilism, freeze, or cessation chasing?
- Is Flow ordinary impermanence under CCE, or energy fixation, grandiosity, kundalini-style activation, or avoidance of solidity?
- Is a claimed energy transmission increasing independent CCE and
  hard-condition practice, or increasing teacher dependence, specialness,
  charge-seeking, coercion, or energy fixation?
- Is primitive terror turning into Flow and merger through supported CCE, or
  is fear induction, catharsis craving, coercion, trauma activation, or
  dissociation being spiritualized?
- Is a newly exposed primitive stratum becoming more sensory-decomposable and
  less behaviorally binding, or is "progress" language masking
  destabilization, panic, rage dysregulation, or loss of function?
- When vanishings become global and agitating, is the agitation actually
  correlated with Gone practice and primarily Feel, or are sleep, stimulants,
  life stress, panic, trauma activation, dissociation, mania-like activation,
  or other factors being bypassed by a Source interpretation?
- Is nonresistance functioning as embodied equanimity, or as overwhelm,
  shutdown, compliance, humiliation, or injury tolerance being overread as
  spiritual release?
- Is Source language preserving afterglow boundaries, or claiming possession of the unconditioned, special authority, or metaphysical proof?
- Is reality or suchness language being marked as conjecture, or is it being
  treated as settled ontology that outranks sensory evidence and ordinary
  functioning?
- Is visual no-objectification making color/form, self/world, and fear more
  sensory-decomposable, or is it becoming derealization, panic, imitation of a
  teacher's advanced practice, substance-like state chasing, or functional
  impairment?
- Is non-dual awareness being used for a simple no-observer event, a
  formless disappearance, or stable daily-life Zero cycling; and is the claim
  being checked against function, support, embodiment, and behavior?
- Is deity or archetypal identity practice held as conscious
  Touch/Feel/Image/Talk construction, or is it becoming possession language,
  grandiosity, reality-testing loss, religious coercion, or status claim?
- Is lucid-dream vividness or control being used as ordinary sensory
  practice, or becoming power/status display, metaphysical proof, sleep
  disruption, or clinical-risk material?
- Is a brief dropout being recognized as a Rest cue or deliberately worked as
  advanced practice, or is sleepiness, reverie, microsleep, dullness,
  dissociation, fatigue, or avoidance being spiritualized as Source?
- Is sleepiness in night sitting becoming clear Rest, Image, or visual Flow
  practice, or are sleep deprivation, microsleep, coercion, mania-like
  activation, dissociation, trauma flooding, or next-day impairment being
  spiritualized?
- When global-Gone or trance physiology language appears, is the report
  safely differentiated from breath manipulation, fainting, freeze,
  catatonia-like immobility, dissociation, sleep, medication/substance
  effects, neurological issues, or medical risk?

## Evidence Needed
- Altered-phenomena differentials for psychosis, mania, trauma activation, dissociation, neurological issues, substance effects, medication effects, sleep deprivation, fasting, medical crisis, and unsafe retreat effects.
- Gone, no-self, Dark Night, DPDR, primordial Feel, and bhanga criteria for practice, grounding, stopping, teacher support, therapy, or emergency care.
- Criteria for primal-Feel fear turning into Flow, merger, or uplift: consent,
  trauma/dissociation screening, teacher-mediated threat boundaries,
  catharsis-craving checks, anti-heroic framing, nonresistance versus
  shutdown/compliance, grounding, and post-practice functioning.
- Criteria for primal-Feel strata practice: how to distinguish local chaotic
  emotionality that is being integrated from clinical destabilization,
  dissociation, trauma activation, manic or panic escalation, rage behavior,
  or unsupported exposure.
- Criteria for agitating Gone: how to distinguish a workable global-vanishing
  and pure-Feel process from panic, trauma activation, dissociation,
  sleep/stimulant activation, destabilizing ego-death framing, or unsupported
  exposure, and how to set manageable doses.
- Depression/anxiety and medication-referral criteria for cases that look like dark night but are not clearly practice-only.
- Reaction-recycling criteria for no-self fear and void flatness: when direct
  CCE is appropriate, when positive reconstruction is needed, and when to
  ground, stop, reduce practice, contact a teacher, seek therapy, or seek
  medical/clinical support.
- Flatline criteria for void integration: how to distinguish workable loss of
  old bounce from depression, dissociation, DPDR, burnout, trauma shutdown,
  medication/substance effects, or ordinary life overload.
- Behavior criteria for no-self output: how to tell spontaneous, dynamic
  behavior from impulsivity, indifference, dissociation, or conduct bypass.
- Clinical criteria and current treatment evidence for depersonalization-derealization disorder, especially where meditation-linked and spontaneous presentations differ.
- Criteria for distinguishing Shinzen's purgatorial dissolution from
  pointless suffering, harm, illness, panic, trauma activation, dissociation,
  mania, psychosis risk, or unsafe retreat effects.
- Criteria for routing post-retreat onset, derealization-like unfamiliarity,
  collapsed sight-space, hiding at home, death imagery, shame/guilt spirals,
  and fear of going crazy when Shinzen's bhanga frame may also apply.
- Disclosure criteria for advanced altered-phenomena maps: when naming
  bhanga reduces confusion versus when it creates fear, craving, comparison,
  or status pressure.
- Pleasant-bhanga criteria for when broad Flow, sight-to-light perception,
  and eye-contact transfer are stabilizing integration versus visual chasing,
  dissociation, social performance, or teacher pressure.
- Competence criteria for severe dissolution support, including how lived
  passage through comparable territory interacts with clinical training,
  referral capacity, and accountability.
- Power-service ethics for consent, verification, supervision, feedback, grievance, referral, and accountability.
- Transmission-claim criteria for distinguishing useful influence,
  teacher-student attunement, and practice-context support from dependency,
  charismatic pressure, coercion, payment/loyalty pressure, energy chasing,
  or unsupported Kundalini-style destabilization.
- Source afterglow criteria that distinguish useful wisdom representation from inflation, grandiosity, overclaim, or dissociative absorption.
- Criteria for keeping everything-at-once, suchness, causal-nexus, and
  objective-reality language in Shinzen's explicitly conjectural tier rather
  than using it as guidance authority.
- Non-dual-awareness criteria for distinguishing temporary no-observer or
  formless states from durable ordinary-life integration.
- Cross-tradition evidence criteria for when Shinzen's translations guide practice versus flatten traditions.
- Deity/archetype practice criteria for cultural context, consent, clinical
  risk, de-roling, reality testing, teacher supervision, and behavior
  verification.
- Lucid-dream practice criteria for induction, sleep disruption, nightmares,
  trauma-linked dreams, parasomnias, dissociation, mania/psychosis risk,
  medication/substance effects, and daytime functioning.
- Decoupling and dropout criteria for distinguishing active-object loss,
  sleep-transition phenomena, dissociation, dullness, and mature Rest or
  Source-side practice.
- Yaza/night-sitting criteria for distinguishing sleepiness as workable Rest,
  Image, or visual Flow from sleep deprivation, microsleep, breakdown,
  coercion, mania-like activation, dissociation, trauma activation, and
  impaired function.
- Global-Gone/trance criteria for distinguishing surrendered contracted Flow
  from unsafe breath change, immobility, shutdown, syncope, dissociation,
  catatonia-like presentation, sleep phenomena, medication/substance effects,
  or neurological/medical risk.
