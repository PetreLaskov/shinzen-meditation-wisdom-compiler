## Key Claims
- **S1**: Different descriptions of a subtle practice direction can work for different people at different times; trying several languages may help, but it can also confuse.
- **S2**: Shinzen distinguishes a technique's focus range and instruction set from the experiences a person may have while doing it; likely experiences can be named as sensitization, not as required stages.
- **S3**: In Turn Back practice, people often begin with confusion about what is being asked, may pass through false learning that expects a verbal answer, and may then discover a spatially located witness or observer.
- **S4**: The fixed witness can bring equanimity, but because it is fixated in space, the practitioner must turn back to it, from it, and through a sequence beyond it.
- **S5**: Shinzen describes the mature direction as being unfixated in space while the eyes are open and one is taking care of business; he calls that true non-dual awareness between formless Source and embodied daily-life forms.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The talk makes language itself part of the method. A teacher can vary language, but should not confuse the technique with the experiences that may arise from it.
- **S2-S3**: The likely-experience sequence is expectation calibration, not attainment pressure. Confusion and false answers become predictable process data rather than signs of failure.
- **S3-S4**: The observer is treated as a transitional construction. Its equanimity value is preserved, but its spatial fixation marks it as incomplete.
- **S4-S5**: The practice does not stop by strengthening a witness; it keeps turning back from the observer until awareness is no longer organized around a back-here standpoint during eyes-open life.
- **S5**: The non-dual awareness claim should be held as Shinzen's advanced practice map, not as independent evidence of metaphysical Source or behavioral completion.

## Practice Handles
- When teaching Turn Back, separate the instruction from the experiences students may report.
- Present common experiences as tendencies: initial "what are you asking me to do?", possible false verbal answers, then a fixed witness with equanimity.
- If a student reports the observer back here, do not treat that as final. Ask them to turn back toward and from the observer itself.
- Preserve the value of equanimity in the witness while naming its limitation: it is still spatially fixed.
- Keep the endpoint ordinary-life capable: eyes open, taking care of business, not a private closed-eye state.
- Watch for overreach. The transcript does not give readiness criteria, destabilization criteria, or a clinical differential for self-inquiry.

## Summary
Stephanie opens by comparing Turn Back with Gone and by recalling how a
previous instruction exposed a separated observer. In her report, when the
observer was inspected as sensory activity rather than accepted as a self, it
lost solidity and became a more abiding empty awareness. Her question is how
to language that move for students who first land in a back-here observer and
cannot simply be told what Shinzen once told her.

Shinzen first broadens the issue. Subtle practice directions can be described
in different ways, and different descriptions land for different people at
different times. He then gives a cleaner teaching distinction: the technique
has a focus range and instruction set, while the experiences people have when
doing the technique vary. A teacher may sensitize students to common
experiences without saying they must happen or must happen in a fixed order.

For Turn Back, the common sequence begins with confusion: the student does
not know what is being asked. False learning may follow, where the student
expects to bring the teacher a clever verbal answer. Eventually the student
may get the hang of the direction and find a witness back here. Shinzen says
that witness has equanimity, but it is fixated in space. The next move is to
turn back to and from that observer through a sequence until awareness is
unfixated in space, eyes open, and ordinary life is continuing.
