## Teaching Register
Terminology clarification and advanced phenomenological map. Shinzen is not giving a guided practice or theological definition; he is preventing a poetic phrase from being mistaken for doctrine while preserving its usefulness as an oral teaching handle.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk gives future agents a clean way to handle Shinzen's "soul" language. It belongs inside the inner sensory system: the soul is not an extra entity but the deeper, finer, broader strata of Feel, Image, and Talk that become available as surface activity clarifies.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen sometimes uses "soul" to mean one's subjective world at its deepest layers, specifically Feel, Image, and Talk considered beneath their explicit surface forms.
- **S2**: In Focus In, the surface subjective world can be tracked as explicit mental pictures, internal talk, and emotional body sensations; when emotional-type body sensations are present, Feel is active.
- **S3**: Beneath explicit surface Feel/Image/Talk are subtler and broader layers of similar activity: subtle image-space, talk-space, and emotional-center activity that may feel like rumbling, snow, idling circuits, or associations processing in the background.
- **S4**: Untangling Feel/Image/Talk is challenging because the system operates across multiple depth scales, but Shinzen says he does not think it is literally an infinite fractal; he guesses the layers are finite while admitting uncertainty and mentioning Yogacara-style layer counts only as a possible reference.
- **S5**: At the base of this inner "iceberg," Shinzen poetically says it floats in pure spirit, and when tangling unwinds the true beauty of the soul is manifested.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The talk routes soul-language back into Focus In and the sensory taxonomy, preventing a new metaphysical primitive from being introduced.
- **S3**: The subtle-layer description sharpens practice discrimination: quiet or explicit rest is not the only inner state; practitioners may notice broad, fine undercurrents in image, talk, and emotional body space.
- **S4**: The finite-not-infinite caveat prevents obsessive depth excavation. There may be subtler layers, but practice does not require treating the inner system as endless.
- **S5**: The pure-spirit and true-beauty language should be preserved as Shinzen's poetic Source-side transmission while remaining source-attributed.

## Practice Handles
- Start with ordinary Focus In: explicit Image, Talk, and emotional Feel, including where they seem centered or localized.
- When surface activity plays itself out, check gently for subtler background activity in image space, talk space, and emotional body centers.
- Do not force a subtle object. The practice handle is sensitivity to what is actually available, not a requirement to manufacture rumbling, snow, or hidden content.
- Treat "soul" as a poetic shorthand for deep subjective processing, not as a theological claim or a separate entity to find.
- Use the finite-layer boundary to avoid endless analysis. The point is untangling and simplification, not proving how many layers exist.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by saying that when he uses the word soul, he means the subjective world at its deepest levels. He then translates that into his standard inner taxonomy. Focus In tracks surface subjective experience as explicit mental images, internal talk, and emotional body sensations. The emotional body component is active when emotional-type body sensations are present.

As surface Feel/Image/Talk plays out, subtler layers of similar activity can become noticeable. Shinzen describes fine, broad image-space, talk-space, and emotional-body undercurrents: background rumbling, mental-screen snow, and circuits idling even when no explicit thought or emotion seems to be moving anywhere. These are the layers he sometimes poetically calls soul.

He then adds two boundaries. First, the layered inner system is not literally an infinite fractal. He does not know how many layers there are, and any count is conjectural until a future neuroanatomical model exists. Second, at the base of the inner iceberg he uses pure-spirit language: as tangling unwinds, the true beauty of the soul manifests. The wiki should preserve that as Shinzen's poetic Source-side frame, not as independent metaphysics.

## Source Frame
The strongest evidence value is for Shinzen's own vocabulary and practice map. The talk makes "soul" operational by mapping it onto deep Feel/Image/Talk and Focus In. It also gives a useful depth caution: subtle levels matter, but they are not to be treated as endless or scientifically mapped.

The wider claims are interpretive and poetic. Pure spirit, the base of the iceberg, and the true beauty of the soul are Shinzen's contemplative idiom.
