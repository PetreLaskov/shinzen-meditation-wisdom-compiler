Chapter 8 of the Basic Mindfulness manual adds five focus options to the grid. Its central contribution is the dedicated practice of Just Note Gone, which turns noticed vanishings into an impermanence, relief, Source, death, service, and safety frontier.

- **Date**: undated in the raw Markdown.

- **Scope**: Explains drill-down/back-up, row focus options, Focus on Everything, Just Note Gone, Gone rewards, dark-night risks, interventions, and the formal definition of vanishing.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Practitioners can alternate drilling down into individual sensory atoms with backing up into broad floating among the atoms of a column, training both individual circuits and the integrated system.
- **S2**: The grid can also be practiced by row through Focus on See, Focus on Hear, and Focus on Feel, each broadly floating across In, Out, Rest, and Flow within one modality and noting Gone when events drop away.
- **S3**: Focus on Everything broadly floats within all experience; Shinzen describes it as 12 sensory objects plus Gone, or 13 objects to note.
- **S4**: Some people call noting all experience "Choiceless Awareness," while others apply that term to Do Nothing, creating a terminology ambiguity inside the manual.
- **S5**: Gone means the noticed moment when all or part of a sensory event drops away or drops off; partial vanishing and sudden decreases in intensity count, unnoticed absence does not, and continued absence is Rest rather than Gone.
- **S6**: Just Note Gone develops clarity, intimacy, and familiarity with Goneness; with concentration and equanimity, micro-endings can become a significant source of macro-relief.
- **S7**: Noting Gone can create rewards of safety, tranquility, fulfillment, satisfying nothingness, Source, union, love, and service, but the Source and metaphysical claims remain Shinzen's interpretation.
- **S8**: When Gone becomes frequent enough, it can become an object of high concentration and produce a figure-ground reversal in which Gone is ground while self and world are fleeting figures.
- **S9**: Intense Goneness, Emptiness, and No-Self can occasionally create fear, loss, confusion, and disorientation; Shinzen recommends deconstructing the negative reactions with Noting, reconstructing self with Nurture Positive, riding Flow or Expansion-Contraction, and using intensive teacher/practitioner support in extreme cases.
- **S10**: Just Note Gone and self-inquiry are presented as different ways to approach the same question of what is behind or prior to self-arising.

## Summary
The chapter begins by generalizing the drill-down/back-up pattern already implicit in the first four Ways. A practitioner can work with one cell or float broadly through a column. Shinzen then adds row practice: Focus on See, Focus on Hear, and Focus on Feel each explore an entire modality across inner, outer, restful, and flowing expressions.

The fourth new option is Focus on Everything, which notes all 12 grid categories plus Gone. This is the manual's most global Noting option. The fifth is Just Note Gone. Gone receives the most detail: it is not absence, not permanent disappearance, and not merely discovering that something is gone after attention returned. It is the detected moment of vanishing or sudden drop-off.

The chapter then argues for Gone's transformative importance. Since attention usually favors arisings over passings, Just Note Gone balances perception. Micro-endings can provide relief, tranquility, satisfaction, death preparation, and a path toward Source or service in Shinzen's interpretive frame. The same territory also has a dark-night edge: emptiness and no-self can become fear, loss, confusion, or disorientation, requiring Noting, Nurture Positive, Flow, and sometimes intensive support.

## Source Frame
The source is both technical and mythic. Its technical core is precise: row options, all-experience noting, and the formal definition of Gone. Its interpretive layer is expansive: Gone becomes refuge under extreme duress, the Still Point, Source, satisfying nothingness, death preparation, and love for all creation.

The source's strongest safety contribution is that it explicitly names negative reactions to Goneness and gives interventions. Its weakest safety move is the extreme-duress example, which could be misread as instruction for handling trauma or torture without enough clinical, relational, or ethical context.

## Weakest Claims
- The waterboarding/extreme-duress scenario is not a sufficient guide for trauma, torture, medical crisis, or emergency response.
- The claim that each arising causes a passing somewhere else is plausible inside the finite-awareness frame but not independently established in this file.
- The Source, Nirvana, "Unborn Source," and shared-womb interpretations are contemplative claims rather than established metaphysics.
- The claim that Gone practice will transform one's relation to death is meaningful as a practice claim but unsupported as a general outcome.

## Important Omissions
- The chapter does not give screening criteria for trauma, depersonalization, derealization, panic, depression, mania, psychosis, or intense dissociation.
- It does not specify when to stop Just Note Gone, when to ground, or when to seek medical or clinical care.
- It does not define what qualifies someone to provide the intensive teacher/practitioner support it recommends.
- It does not operationalize how to tell productive emptiness practice from collapse, nihilism, freeze, or avoidance.
- It does not give behavioral tests for love and service effects after Gone practice.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Gone is framed as refuge and satisfaction, yet the same family of experiences can produce fear, loss, confusion, and disorientation.
- Focus on Everything may be called choiceless awareness, but the manual also associates that term with Do Nothing, so the term cannot reliably identify one technique.
- Gone is related to Absolute Rest, but the chapter sharply distinguishes the moment of Gone from continued Rest.
- The chapter uses Source language strongly while also giving a concrete sensory definition that does not require metaphysics.
- Micro-ending relief may help in distress, but it should not be used to bypass ordinary safety, action, or support.
