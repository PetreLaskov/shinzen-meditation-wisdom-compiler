## Teaching Register
Brief explanatory encouragement. Shinzen uses a mathematical metaphor to help practitioners interpret the discouraging early phase where growth feels real but too slow to matter.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk makes explicit why purification taste is not just a pleasant side effect. When a practitioner can feel that present CCE is cleaning up past conditioning and improving future moments, practice becomes self-reinforcing; that feedback can change perseverance, motivation, and growth rate.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Some, but not all, practitioners experience growth on the liberation path as a hockey-stick or exponential curve: early growth may feel linear and unimpressive, then later accelerate dramatically.
- **S2**: Shinzen cannot specify when the acceleration occurs; for some it may be early, while in extreme cases it may take 10 or 15 years.
- **S3**: One important cause of the upsurge is reaching the "flavor of purification": the tangible sense that mindful awareness of an unpleasant experience is purifying consciousness.
- **S4**: That taste includes the sense that the way this moment is experienced will make future moments more fulfilling and less painful, while clearing stored pain from previous moments.
- **S5**: Once that taste is present, CCE, especially equanimity with present experience, becomes a positive feedback loop: growth increases the rate of further growth.
- **S6**: Shinzen offers the metaphor as encouragement for practitioners in the slow-looking phase, not as a universal schedule or guarantee.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The curve is framed as common but not universal, preserving calibration against timeline promises or attainment prediction.
- **S3-S5**: Purification taste becomes a mechanism of motivation and acceleration, not merely an interpretation after the fact.
- **S6**: The teaching function is encouragement during slow progress; the metaphor should support perseverance without overriding safety, feedback, or ordinary outcome checks.

## Practice Handles
- When practice feels too slow, do not extrapolate a linear timeline from the early phase.
- Treat the first stable taste of purification as a motivational watershed: present equanimity can begin to feel directly rewarding because it is sensed as reducing future suffering and digesting past material.
- Keep the "especially equanimity" emphasis: the growth loop depends on how the present experience is met, not on intensity, drama, or belief in the metaphor.
- Use the metaphor for encouragement, not for self-diagnosing a breakpoint, guaranteeing acceleration, or comparing one's timeline with others.

## Summary
Shinzen says many practitioners experience liberation practice as a hockey-stick or exponential curve. Early growth may be real but slow-looking, making the path seem impossibly long. Later, growth may accelerate in a way that makes classic results feel feasible, though the timing varies and the pattern is not universal.

He identifies one cause of that acceleration as the taste of purification. A practitioner begins to sense that bringing mindful awareness to unpleasant experience is cleaning up past pain and making future moments more fulfilling. Once this is tangible, practice becomes a positive feedback loop: more CCE, especially equanimity, supports more purification, which supports more motivation and further growth.

## Source Frame
The source's practical aim is encouragement and motivation calibration. The exponential-curve language is a metaphor borrowed from mathematics; the load-bearing Shinzen claim is phenomenological and pedagogical: a recognizable purification taste can make practice self-reinforcing. It does not establish a measured growth law, timeline, or universal developmental stage.

## Weakest Claims
- The exponential-curve analogy is not operationalized with measurement, criteria, or data.
- The claim that present practice improves every future moment and clears stored past pain is a contemplative interpretation within Shinzen's system, not an independently verified outcome claim.
- The talk does not separate motivational confidence from possible overconfidence, comparison, or pressure to have a dramatic breakpoint.

## Important Omissions
- No guidance is given for practitioners who never taste purification, lose the taste, or become discouraged by comparison.
- No safety criteria are given for unpleasant experience, purification intensity, trauma activation, or clinical distress.
- No teacher, retreat, dosage, or support variables are specified despite the long timeline range.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk encourages hope for dramatic acceleration while explicitly saying the pattern is not universal.
- It treats purification taste as a cause of faster growth, but does not establish whether the taste is cause, marker, interpretation, or all three.
- It uses mathematical language for a phenomenological and pedagogical claim; future pages should not literalize the metaphor into a law of practice development.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Hockey-stick growth" should not become an attainment expectation, sales promise, or reason to ignore stalled practice.
- "Taste of purification" should not be used to spiritualize unsafe endurance, untreated trauma, medical symptoms, or coercive retreat pressure.
- "Positive feedback loop" should not replace behavior, relationship, clinical, or teacher-feedback checks.
