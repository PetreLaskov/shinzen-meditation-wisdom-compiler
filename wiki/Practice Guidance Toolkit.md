The Practice Guidance Toolkit is a routing page, not a source digest. It helps a future agent decide what to load next and what provisional guidance branch fits a live practice report.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen-style guidance is branch-and-loop: understand the person's sensory and objective world, choose a provisional strategy, tune the method, ask what happened, and update the branch.
- **Why this matters**: The same symptom can route differently depending on active focus range, capacity, venue, safety signals, and whether the issue is inside the chosen method or calls for a different method.
- **Key tensions**: The sources supply rich practice routing but not full clinical, medical, trauma, consent, teacher-competence, privacy, or emergency protocols.
- **Source posture**: Strong as a compiled map of Shinzen's guidance moves; incomplete as a safety or professional-scope standard.

## Agent Use Contract
Use this page to decide where to route, not to answer high-risk questions from memory. When a concrete branch matters, load the linked owner or source page and cite it.

Every guidance answer should preserve four checks:

- **Context**: What technique, focus range, venue, and life situation are active?
- **Branch**: Is the next move contact, support, Flow/change, Rest/positive, Do Nothing, method simplification, or outside help?
- **Quality**: Does the branch increase concentration, sensory clarity, equanimity, ordinary responsiveness, or behavior change?
- **Loop**: What report, behavior commitment, or follow-up would confirm or revise the branch?

## Minimum Inputs
| Input | Why It Routes |

| Practice aim | Reduced suffering, fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and service are different success surfaces ([[Why Meditate]] S1-S5). |

| Live report after the last instruction | Shinzen's oral coaching loop depends on giving a technique, checking what happened, and modifying or redirecting from there ([[What Are Your Specialties as a Teacher]] S4). |
| CCE capacity | Low contact, low discrimination, or low equanimity imply different tuning moves inside the same method. |
| Venue | Stillness, motion, and life practice have different load levels and task-safety constraints ([[Practice Cycles]]). |
| Risk and support | Pain, trauma activation, dissociation, medical signals, teacher pressure, or behavior harm can override meditation optimization. |
| Follow-up signal | Guidance is incomplete unless there is a behavior, support, or next-report check; when behavior does not change through mindfulness alone, Shinzen explicitly routes to outside accountability ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S10-S13; [[Mindfulness and Behavioural Change]] S6-S7). |

[[Chart of Human Happiness]] gives the concise visual-chart version of the
same minimum-input logic: when helping someone, Shinzen says to consider the
person's sensory challenges, needed behavior changes, objective situation,
and possible focus options together; for behavior change, mindful awareness
may need therapy, twelve-step, or another accountability structure
([[Chart of Human Happiness]] S1, S5).

## Fast Routing Algorithm
1. **Scope gate first**: if the report involves emergency risk, medical danger, self-harm, psychosis/mania-like instability, abuse, coercion, severe dissociation, or ordinary protection needs, route to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] and ordinary qualified support before technique optimization.
2. **Classify the live problem**: path choice, session setup, focus coverage, effort regulation, Noting option, Do Nothing/Rest issue, sensory challenge, emotional challenge, positive reconstruction, life transfer, teacher/accountability issue, or crisis-scale Wall/Window.
3. **Choose the branch family**: turn toward, turn away with background equanimity, focus on Flow/change, stabilize in Rest or positive material, release effort through Do Nothing, simplify the method, add guidance/support, or shift to behavior/accountability work.
4. **Tune the controls**: range, coverage strategy, label strength, label pace, zooming, emphasis, re-noting, effort level, venue load, sequence, or follow-up.
5. **Close the loop**: ask what changed in sensory contact, equanimity, behavior, support, and life functioning. If the report worsens or becomes ambiguous, downgrade confidence and load the safety frontier.

## Branch Families
**Turn toward** brings CCE directly to the challenge. Load [[Turn Toward and Turn Away]] first, then a physical, emotional, agitation, evocation, or Flow source as needed. In health crisis, the trained focus-on branch divides the sensory challenge into physical body, emotional body, visual thought, and auditory thought, then uses concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity to reduce multiplicative overwhelm and resistance; it is not a two-minute self-instruction for an untrained person in crisis ([[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 3 of 3]] S1-S9). In ordinary sickness or retreat illness, [[Mindfulness with Sickness]] adds a lighter version of the same branch: local physical discomfort, global physical discomfort, Image, Talk, and body emotion can be contacted directly only when rest, care, and capacity remain intact (S2, S5-S7).

When the challenge was produced by practice itself, load [[Recycle The
Reaction]] before treating the report as either success or failure. [[Recycle
the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]] gives four
useful cases: the practitioner pops back into ordinary consciousness after a
state, becomes afraid after a deeper or no-self-like state, becomes alienated
from ordinary people, or encounters void flatness. The provisional branch is
to identify the reaction as Feel/Image/Talk or another sensory object and
apply CCE; the safety branch remains active when the reaction is severe,
disabling, clinically mixed, or unsupported ([[Recycle the Reaction -
Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]] S2-S5).

In childbirth, direct contact is rhythm-based rather than a generic pain mandate. [[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 1 of 2]] says a practitioner who already knows uncomfortable Touch, Feel, Flow, and equanimity may use early contraction peaks to learn the rhythm: let Touch expand at the peak while Image, Talk, and emotional selfing contract into Rest, then relax during the valley ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 1 of 2]] S1-S9). This belongs under birth-care and consent boundaries, not under meditation-teacher authority over obstetric decisions.

In parenting, practice is validated by immediate function. [[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] frames the baby as a Roshi-like source of "dynamic nothingness" contacted through sight, sound, and touch, but the actual guidance branch is ordinary care: use sensory contact and raw-Feel recognition to increase compassion and responsiveness, not to replace pediatric care, postpartum support, sleep protection, or family help ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] S1-S9).

In sleep disruption, body rest is the first constraint. [[Sleep Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] says practice-linked sleep disturbance can happen, but the instruction is to lie still so the body rests and maintain a continuous technique so consciousness rests; the framing shifts from demanding sleep to getting rest, while severe or impairing insomnia stays outside meditation-only guidance ([[Sleep Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] S1-S8).

For tea, coffee, and caffeine, the first question is effect, not ideology.
[[Tea, Coffee and Meditation]] says meditation includes a clarity-alertness
component as well as tranquility, so a stimulant is not automatically a
practice contradiction. The stop signal is jangling or ordinary cost:
agitation, insomnia, compulsion, health or medication concerns, or degraded
function route away from technique optimization ([[Tea, Coffee and
Meditation]] S2-S4).

In lucid dreams, the route is continuity rather than novelty. [[Lucid
Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] says a lucid dream can be
treated like waking life for practical labels: external-seeming dream sights
are sight, dream contact is Touch, the thought "I am dreaming" is Talk, and
melting or permeable dream objects can be Flow. The aim is meditation
mileage, not dream-control entertainment, and the source gives no sleep,
nightmare, trauma, parasomnia, dissociation, or clinical criteria ([[Lucid
Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] S2-S8).

In sexuality, consent and ordinary ethics govern before technique. [[Do You
Think Sex is Dirty]] says sex itself is not dirt; the "dirt" is craving and
unconsciousness obscuring sexual pleasure. Lovemaking can become pleasure
meditation only when enough concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity
already carry into the situation, so pleasure is not tightened around as it
arises or grasped as it passes. The same source gives no consent, trauma,
relationship, sexual-health, or compulsion criteria, so any guidance stays
low-confidence unless those ordinary conditions are clear ([[Do You Think Sex
is Dirty]] S1-S9).

**Turn away** stabilizes attention elsewhere while the challenge is genuinely allowed in the background. The core criteria are permission, no disappearance agenda, and some willingness to turn toward later ([[Using Turn Away and Background Equanimity with Sensory Challenges]] S5-S8). In health crisis, the chosen elsewhere may be Rest, positive Feel/Image/Talk, music, or pleasant sights and sounds, but the symptoms, fear, images, and talk remain permitted as the background sensory challenge ([[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 2 of 3]] S5-S9). [[Mindfulness with Sickness]] gives the anti-trap version: if turn-away is dismissed as denial and turn-toward is dismissed as reinforcement, the practitioner has created a no-place-to-turn problem rather than a wise safety boundary (S7-S8).

**Focus on change** uses Flow or vanishing when change is actually detectable. It is a third branch, not a status marker; when the Flow branch is absent, drop it rather than manufacture it ([[Turn Towards Flow (Change) Using the Labeling Gears & Options]] S1-S9; [[Hear-In to Mental Talk Space, Feel Flow in Body Space]] S1-S5).

When Flow is broad, pleasant, and stable, the branch can widen rather than
intensify: spoken or optional Flow labels, zoom-out coverage through
body-mind-world, eyes-open sight or light if it naturally appears, and
carefully paced eye-contact transfer in a consented live-coaching setting
([[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 3 of 3]] S2-S6).

**Rest or positive support** stabilizes with rest factors, positive Feel/Image/Talk, or Nurture Positive. This is useful support when it preserves CCE and ordinary responsiveness, but it can become shutdown or positivity bypass. [[Simultaneous Zooming In and Out During Positive and Restful States]] adds a gentle local-global support branch: positive Feel can be allowed to spread, and out-breath core relaxation can be noticed while awareness widens through the whole body ([[Simultaneous Zooming In and Out During Positive and Restful States]] S1-S4).

During active-object practice, Rest can also appear as a diagnostic rather
than a selected support. [[Consciously Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem
Mutata Resurgo]] says a sudden brief Rest shift may indicate that attention
has decoupled from sight, touch, or another active object and is being pulled
toward reverie or sleepiness. The guidance branch is to notice the tell and
return, unless the practitioner is deliberately and safely shifting to Focus
on Rest ([[Consciously Decoupling, Dropping Out & Eadem Mutata Resurgo]]
S1-S4).

**Do Nothing** releases the choosing and controlling function when choice-confusion or racy technique is active; it routes back to Noting when it becomes spacey ([[Do Nothing Meditation]] S3, S7).

When Do Nothing is blocked by wandering mind, [[The Happy Wanderer]] adds a
small positive-support loop: if the wandering has a detectable
pleasant-interest flavor, make that flavor conscious as Focus on Positive,
then return or continue according to workability ([[The Happy Wanderer]]
S2-S5).

**Outside support and accountability** enter when practice is not changing behavior, when the issue is clinical/medical/relational, or when teacher authority itself becomes part of the distortion. Shinzen's behavior-change talk names counseling, therapy, twelve-step programs, sponsors, friends, or other external structures with manageable assignments as the branch when sensory deconstruction is insufficient ([[Mindfulness and Behavioural Change]] S6-S7; [[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S6-S10). [[Mindfulness & Psychotherapy]] adds a scale check: therapy may be the better gross-anatomy picture for personality and behavior structure, while mindfulness may be the better microstructure picture for second-by-second sensory holdings and all-self insight ([[Mindfulness & Psychotherapy]] S5-S8).

When the relational issue is loss of self-contact, the microstructure branch
may support ego strength rather than ego dissolution. The weak-ego source
says a person who loses themselves around another can keep noting Feel,
Image, and Talk as the other's sight, sound, or touch looms. That is a
Shinzen-style self-boundary practice, but it remains clinical-adjacent and
does not replace therapy, diagnosis, trauma support, or ordinary safety
checks ([[Strengthening a Weak Ego Structure Through Mindfulness]] S1-S5).

## Safety Gates
Do not continue algorithmic meditation routing as the only response when these are active:

- Medical red flags, injury risk, faintness, severe pain, neurological symptoms, or medication/substance concerns.
- Self-harm, harm to others, abuse, violence, coercion, emergency instability, or ordinary protection needs.
- Severe dissociation, panic, mania/psychosis-like states, trauma flooding, or inability to function.
- Teacher pressure, shame, privacy exposure, group contagion, retaliation risk, or lack of consent.
- Source, science, AI, ultrasound, energy, or metaphysical proof claims being used to override evidence, consent, care, or role limits.
- Practice language replacing therapy, medical care, legal protection, behavior repair, or accountability.
- Repeated worsening without clear CCE gain, behavior improvement, or competent support.

When any gate is active, load [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] and
answer with lower confidence. If the issue involves teacher/coach authority,
referral, consent, behavior accountability, power, service, ritual, medical
