Its durable contribution is a precise oral definition of [[Complete Experience]] and an early Gate 3 bridge from ordinary Feel/Image/Talk practice into Source, polarization, purification, and the ordinary/extraordinary inversion that separates liberation from power-chasing.

## Teaching Register
Advanced explanatory teaching. Shinzen moves from comparative mystical language into technical practice definition, then returns to an advanced map. The oral style is deliberately paradoxical: ordinary experience becomes extraordinary when completed, while extraordinary visionary experience becomes ordinary when completed.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk sharpens the central transformation hinge. Complete Experience is not mere strong sensation, peak experience, or staying with something until it ends; it means concentration, clarity, and equanimity have reached a critical mass through the whole event.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Zero and One are two major contemplative path paradigms: oneness in some traditions and zero, emptiness, or shunyata in others.
- **S2**: Shinzen interprets large, overwhelming experiences as large polarizations from Source; if such a large polarization is completely experienced, its return or neutralization yields deeper purification than a small challenge.
- **S3**: Nature can be viewed as creating and wiping away distinctions, and consciousness reflects this when the practitioner fully gives themselves to the forces of division so nothing blocks neutralization.
- **S4**: Emotional experience can be analyzed as mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation; Shinzen uses "Feel" as a technical noun for emotional body sensation because "feeling" is too broad.
- **S5**: A Complete Experience is ordinary experience met by an extraordinary critical mass of concentration, clarity, and equanimity from beginning to end.
- **S6**: Complete Experience makes ordinary experience paradoxically extraordinary: sensorially rich, transparent, vacuous, fulfilling, and "no longer there" as a single experience.
- **S7**: When extraordinary experiences, such as a fully manifesting deity, are met with extraordinary CCE, they become utterly ordinary; Shinzen calls this the path of liberation rather than the path of powers.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: Complete Experience is explicitly not defined as intensity and not merely as staying with an experience until its end; it is the threshold of CCE present throughout the event.
- **S2**: Shinzen ties purification depth to the size of the polarization that can be fully completed, which raises both motivational and safety stakes around intensity.
- **S3**: The phrase "Feel, not feeling" preserves a technical-language move: emotional body sensation is being named as a sensory class, not as generic emotion or opinion.
- **S4**: The liberation-versus-powers distinction is phenomenological: CCE with extraordinary phenomena makes them ordinary rather than status-making.

## Practice Handles
For practice, the handle is threshold-oriented: ask whether concentration, clarity, and equanimity are all present enough to know the event clearly, allow it, and remain with its full arc. Do not infer completion from intensity, drama, or endurance.

For guidance, the handle is to keep ordinary inner sensory events central. Image, Talk, and emotional Feel are already large enough practice territory; extraordinary content should be evaluated by whether it becomes transparent and nonbinding, not by how impressive it is.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by naming Zero and One as two historic paradigms for the contemplative path. He links Sanskrit shunya or shunyata to both zero and emptiness, then interprets experience as polarization from Source. The larger or more overwhelming the experience, the larger the polarization; if the practitioner can have a complete experience of it, the return or reuniting yields deeper purification.

He then shifts to the experience of oneness as healing the alienation between thought and feeling. Thought appears as mental image, mental talk, or both. Emotionality appears as body sensation with distinct flavors, and Shinzen uses "Feel" as a technical noun for those emotional body sensations. The body can be trained into a high-resolution emotional screen.

The core definition follows: a Complete Experience occurs when concentration, clarity, and equanimity come together at a critical mass from beginning to end. Under that condition, ordinary experience becomes extraordinary because it is sensorially rich and also transparent, vacuous, fulfilling, and no longer there. Conversely, extraordinary phenomena become ordinary under the same CCE, which Shinzen identifies as liberation rather than powers.

## Source Frame
The talk tries to make advanced mystical language operational by tying it back to the skill triad and the inner sensory system. Its strongest evidence value is for Shinzen's own vocabulary and practice model. Its weaker frame is the implied Source/nature account in which polarization and neutralization are fundamental activities; the wiki should treat that as Shinzen's contemplative interpretation, not as established metaphysics or science.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk both values large overwhelming polarizations for deeper purification and insists that completion is not intensity; this tension must be preserved because it governs Gate 3 safety.
