Its main contribution is the branch logic: after body and visual Flow have been explored, Shinzen checks whether mental talk space has subtle Flow, does not force it when it is mostly absent, returns to body Flow, and then raises the challenge level through eyes-open and eye-contact practice.

## Teaching Register

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk prevents two overgeneralizations. First, it shows that after Flow is found in one domain, a guide may check other domains without requiring Flow to be present everywhere. Second, it makes daily-life transfer more concrete by moving from eyes closed to eyes open and then to eye contact, a stronger social challenge than soft visual openness alone.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide can use this sequence as a branching checklist:

- After body or image Flow appears, ask whether mental talk space has explicit chatter, quiet, or subtle preconscious vibratory movement.
- If subtle Hear In Flow is absent, do not force it; return to the sensory domain where Flow is actually present.
- If body Flow remains available, raise challenge gradually: eyes closed, eyes open, then eye contact or another ordinary-life social demand.
- If multiple domains are flowing, consider zooming out into a whole mind-body Flow field; if they are not, stay with the workable field.

## Summary
The student reports that image-space Flow continued but was more neutral than soothing. Shinzen then moves attention into the head, naming it talk space, and asks whether there is inner chatter or a subtler energetic rumbling beneath conscious talk. The student reports some tune-like or explicit talk but not much subtle vibratory Flow, and Shinzen accepts that result.

He returns the student to body space, where Flow remains available. He then tests whether the student can maintain contact with body Flow with eyes open and then while making eye contact with him. The student can, and Shinzen frames this as a significant ordinary-life transfer step.

In the debrief, Shinzen explains that he had been doing exploratory coaching: checking local Flow, checking whether it spread through the body, checking whether mind space also flowed, and changing guidance based on the answers. If mind-space Flow had been present, the next branch would have been whole mind-body Flow. Since it was not, body Flow was enough.

## Source Frame
The source is not a lecture on the whole Sensory Grid; it is a live demonstration of adaptive guidance. Shinzen's practice aim is to help the student find and stabilize Flow where it is actually presenting, then test whether that contact survives increasingly ordinary conditions. The talk's purification language is phenomenological and teaching-internal; it should not be treated as medical evidence or as a guarantee that Flow practice resolves all physical discomfort.
