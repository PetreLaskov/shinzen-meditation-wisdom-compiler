## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen frames practice as "touching the heart" in two linked senses: emotional access to unconditional love and literal contact with the heart of the matter, which he identifies with consciousness or sensory experience.
- **S2**: Direct contact with the nature of See/Hear/Feel is said to dissolve the felt brick wall between inside and outside and disclose a shared "one taste" across all sensory experience.
- **S3**: He distinguishes relative present-centeredness from the absolute now: relative presence may mean not being lost in thought, while absolute presence means instant-by-instant contact so fine that past/future thought can still occur without fixation into duration or space.
- **S4**: The talk says ordinary judgments, plans, preferences, or ignoring may still occur, but they are preceded by contact and love when practice has matured.
- **S5**: Shinzen describes one version of the absolute now as a cycle of absolute activity and absolute rest: continuous arisings spread as space, continuous passings collapse space, and surface self/world experience appears like foam between Expansion and Contraction.
- **S6**: The neuroscience and evoked-potential discussion presents temporal "just before" language as a science-facing way to describe sensory processing, but it remains an analogy inside the talk rather than empirical proof of Source.
- **S7**: The talk says practice can recover a primordial perfection while preserving adult competence, making aging, sickness, and death less troubling to the extent identity shifts toward Source activity.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The "touching the heart" phrase links practice mechanics to service without making love a mere mood. The first touch is direct sensory contact; the second is emotional warmth and service.
- **S3**: The relative/absolute present distinction prevents "be present" from being flattened into not thinking about past or future.
- **S4**: Love is placed before judgment, not instead of judgment. This keeps the ordinary action layer available.
- **S5**: Absolute activity/rest gives a vivid Source-polarity anchor for Expansion-Contraction without requiring the practitioner to adopt it as the only valid description.
- **S6**: The neuroscience passage is useful because it shows Shinzen trying to translate Source language into temporal processing language; it also needs the science-analogy boundary.
- **S7**: The mortality-facing claim belongs at the Source/service boundary. It can support liberation language but does not prove metaphysical eternity or remove ordinary care needs.

## Practice Handles
- Distinguish relative presence from absolute-now contact. A practitioner may be "in the present" behaviorally while still not contacting the micro-arising of sensory experience.
- When Source-love language appears, ask whether ordinary judgment, planning, correction, and protection remain available after the initial contact and love.
- Use "love deeply, act effectively" as a sequence, not a mood test: sensory contact, nonseparate love, then appropriate action.
- If Expansion-Contraction, absolute rest, or Source identity becomes intense, route through the usual Source afterglow, science analogy, and safety boundaries before treating it as realization or guidance authority.
- Treat neuroscience, Chinese/Japanese heart-mind language, St. John of the Cross, and infant-memory material as Shinzen's teaching bridges unless separately audited.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by saying practice can be understood as touching the heart. In
one sense, this means love deeply. In another, it means literally touching
the heart of the matter: consciousness, or sensory experience itself. He uses
Chinese/Japanese heart-mind language to bridge physical heart,
consciousness, and contact, then says every inner or outer heaven or hell
comes through sensory experience.

The central practice distinction is relative versus absolute
present-centeredness. Relative presence begins with not being lost in thought
and knowing ordinary activity as it happens. Absolute presence is subtler:
instant-by-instant contact with the arising of image, talk, body sensation,
sight, sound, and touch before they fixate into duration, space, or the
inside/outside split. Shinzen says this first contact has one taste across
surface differences and naturally gives rise to love for what had been
experienced as other.

The middle and late sections give one favored description of the absolute
now: Expansion-Contraction and absolute rest/activity. Continuous arisings
spread as space, continuous passings collapse space, and surface experience
is born in the gap between those forces. Shinzen then translates the same
route into temporal and neuroscience language, using auditory evoked
potentials and preconscious processing as a way to talk about "just before"
ordinary perception. The talk closes by returning to service and mortality:
touching the heart as liberation and as loving service is presented as the
reason for retreat practice.
