## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen defines contemplative practice as developing attentional skills and applying them to broad and deep happiness, with CCE central to all forms of happiness.
- **S2**: Equanimity is framed as a relationship to sensory experience rather than objective circumstances; ordinary circumstances may still need action, correction, or support.
- **S3**: The self-understanding ladder moves from personal/biographical, to archetypal/deep-psyche, to self as sensory system, to the Source-side question of what inner and outer sensory experience is made of.
- **S4**: The retreat's main theme is spaciousness, presented as one way to describe deep self/world nature while acknowledging many other, sometimes contradictory, traditional descriptions.
- **S5**: Somatic spaciousness has at least two approachable aspects: thinness or lightness within the felt body, and openness around the felt body.
- **S6**: Physical relaxation, especially settling into posture and out-breath relaxation, can be intentionally created and used as a subtle pleasant focus object for the thinness side of Feel Space.
- **S7**: Surrounding openness is contacted by spreading attention to the felt body boundary and then beyond sensation in one or more directions; broad or groping effort is allowed.
- **S8**: During this specific spaciousness technique, intense body or emotional material may remain permitted in the background while concentration and clarity foreground lightness or openness; other techniques may instead turn directly toward the intensity.
- **S9**: Mental images, talk, or emotional representations of the focus object are natural and may guide attention, but they are not identical to the direct sensory target unless deliberately included in the focus range.
- **S10**: Visual and auditory spaciousness are taught by analogy with body space: active and restful seeing/hearing are spatial, so one may attend to thinness within or openness around visual and auditory fields.
- **S11**: Stable openness supports absorption-style practice, while dynamic outward spreading or inward collapsing links spaciousness to Expansion-Contraction and arising-passing.
- **S12**: If fear follows no-self, emptiness, cessation, or deep spaciousness, Shinzen treats fear as a form of self-rearising to honor, untangle, and permit rather than as proof that the original opening failed.
- **S13**: Self-rearising has at least three strategies: keep foreground attention on the original no-self or spaciousness target, switch to complete experience of the self/fear, or alternate deliberately.
- **S14**: Practice alone is insufficient for avoiding spiritual-community problems; Shinzen names ethical guidelines, open feedback channels, behaviorally oriented accountability/support structures, and realistic role models as needed structures.
- **S15**: The closing asks practitioners to convert retreat momentum into life practice through micro-hits, background practice, and a driving-specific sensory anchor.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide could use this source to:

- begin spaciousness through ordinary posture relaxation rather than waiting for dramatic vastness;
- distinguish thinness within body from openness around body, then combine or alternate them;
- let intense or uncomfortable material remain permitted in the background when the chosen method is Space, while remembering other methods may turn directly toward it;
- treat mental images of body, space, or flow as natural representations that may help guide attention but need not become the intentional focus range;
- route unavailable or stubborn areas by using what is available, by shifting to surrounding openness, or by switching to a different technique such as direct body sweeping or Expansion-Contraction;
- recycle fear after no-self or cessation as a returning self made of image, talk, and emotional body sensation;
- carry retreat effects into life through micro-hits, background practice, and sensory-only driving practice.

## Summary
The day opens by placing spaciousness inside Shinzen's broad happiness and
self-understanding map. CCE supports surface psychological self-knowledge,
archetypal or deep-psyche material, self as sensory components, and the
deeper Source-side inquiry into what inner and outer sensory events are made
of. Spaciousness is introduced as one practice vocabulary among many, not as
the only legitimate description of liberation.

The main practice begins with the body. Practitioners intentionally relax
into posture and out-breath, then attend to the subtle pleasant quality of
lightness or thinness in the felt body. They then locate the felt body
boundary and spread attention beyond it to surrounding openness. The
instructions allow partial success and groping; available thinness or
openness is enough. The Q&A clarifies that mental images of body or space are
normal representations and can guide attention, but the technique's target
must remain explicit.

The later teaching expands the same logic into visual and auditory fields.
See and Hear are spatial because sights, images, sounds, talk, silence, and
quiet have some width, depth, and height. Practicing Space means attending to
thinness within or openness around those fields. If the openness or thinness
becomes dynamic, it may link to Expansion-Contraction: outward spreading,
inward collapsing, arising-passing, or absolute rest.

The final Q&A matters because it keeps advanced practice accountable. Fear
after no-self is treated as self-rearising, not as a failure: the practitioner
may ignore it and stay with the target, switch to complete experience of the
fear/self, or alternate. Questions about meditation communities lead Shinzen
to name structures beyond meditation itself: ethical guidelines, feedback
channels, accountability/support for behavior, and realistic role models. The
retreat closes with micro-hits, background practice, and explicit driving
safety.
