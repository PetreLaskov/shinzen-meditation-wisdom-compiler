Mastery without guru inflation is the Gate 0 accountability handle for reading Shinzen and other teachers. It preserves the possibility of real contemplative mastery while rejecting the move from depth in some components to perfection in judgment, behavior, map authority, social power, or community design.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen treats enlightenment and mastery as a vector of partly independent components, not a scalar perfection package; liberation signs do not prove teaching competence, existing maps remain provisional, powerful influence should not become personal credit, real transmission should produce independent peers rather than teacher dependents, and even real no-self or stream-entry-level classical enlightenment can coexist with public screw-ups and still needs senior, peer, student, family, non-practitioner, and environmental feedback.
- **Why this matters**: Future agents should not infer safety, ethics, behavior, institutional competence, or owned authority from depth, charisma, love, service rhetoric, powerful teaching, or technical precision.
- **Key tensions**: Anti-guru posture can mature discernment or collapse into cynicism; feedback can correct power or become vague community pressure; outside support can complement practice or be invoked without real criteria; non-doership and counterfactual improvement can reduce status projection or be misused to evade responsibility; "most criticism is student stuff" can preserve discernment or become defensive dismissal.
- **Source posture**: Strong for Shinzen's own oral accountability and map-humility frame; incomplete as an ethics policy, clinical referral model, research program, or independent study of teacher misconduct.

## Model
The core distinction is component independence. In the balanced-enlightenment talk, Shinzen says one can be a "master's master" in certain components and be hugely imbalanced in others; enlightenment is a vector rather than a scalar ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S4). His private phrase "classical enlightenment" means balanced enlightenment, where knowledge and behavior are both present ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S5).

This blocks a common inference: obvious contemplative depth does not guarantee good judgment. Shinzen explicitly says deep practice is no guarantee against seriously bad decisions, and that emptied ego, loving each arising, and unswerving service can coexist with horrible judgments and consequences ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S1-S2).

The teacher relation should therefore be deflated without being despised. Shinzen's local phrase is that a teacher is "just an old student," ideally serving pure communication rather than becoming a central object of projection ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S3). This does not deny expertise; it denies perfection projection.

The accountability ecology has several layers:

- **Guidelines**: sila or precepts create behavioral reference points.
- **Deconstruction**: negative urges can be worked with through focus techniques.
- **Reconstruction**: positive directions can be built through [[Nurture Positive]].
- **Feedback**: students, peers, teachers, and ordinary community members should be able to say when behavior is off.
- **Outsourcing**: psychotherapy, 12-step programs, and other accountability structures may be needed when Buddhist practice is not enough ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S6-S7).

Shinzen also gives a failure map. The obvious guru potholes are sex, power, finances, and addiction; he later adds co-dependent relationships as a subtler way teacher authority can be captured or distorted ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S8). His own example is intentionally humbling: he reports becoming high-handed and manipulative under such an influence, and needing months of student and peer feedback before exiting the pattern ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S9).

This page also inherits the style-calibration side of Gate 0. The self-critique clip shows that Shinzen can name real teaching liabilities in his own complexity, cold sharpness, and irreverence ([[Three Reasons Why Shinzen Young Is a Lousy Teacher]] S1-S5). The balanced-enlightenment talk adds the deeper accountability point: even real strengths and real depth need correction mechanisms.

[[Enlightenment Maps and Models]] extends the same deflation from conduct to knowledge. Shinzen says a best map may exist but has not been found, that all current maps are poor, and that even deeply enlightened teachers know relatively little about enlightenment compared with what future inquiry may discover ([[Enlightenment Maps and Models]] S1-S4). This does not erase teacher skill. It blocks the extra projection that a realized teacher's map, lineage vocabulary, or stage model must be complete.

[[After Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The Point]] adds Shinzen's most
personal Gate 6A version of the same deflation. He reports having a sudden
no-self experience, then spending his life refining its implications,
recognizing unresolved patterns, and later seeing a psychiatrist for 18
months for behavioral issues he felt needed something besides meditation
([[After Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The Point]] S2-S4). This is not
only teacher modesty; it is a model-level warning that realization and
ordinary behavioral work remain distinct components.

[[Enlightenment Downsides]] adds a credit-specific deflation. Shinzen says
enlightenment may bring influence and powerful teaching, but the person does
not get to take credit because they do not know what they are doing, what
they are saying, or even who they are ([[Enlightenment Downsides]] S5). The
teaching image is "just have to occur," like a leaf falling from a tree
([[Enlightenment Downsides]] S6). This supports anti-guru routing only if it
is paired with the existing accountability model: reduced self-credit is not
reduced responsibility for conduct, roles, consent, or effects.

[[Six Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] adds the most direct
realization-specific version. Shinzen says classical enlightenment may be
real and permanent, yet the person can still think they know more than they
do or begin teaching from that inflated place. The antidote is courage to
take feedback from vastly senior teachers and from the environment ([[Six
Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] S6-S7).

[[Classical Enlightenment Healing the World and Screw-ups]] adds the
misconduct-specific version. Shinzen says people who have seen no-self can
still have major public screw-ups, including people he regards as
stream-entry-level, and frames many teacher failures as ordinary human
patterns such as sexuality, biology, and boss-like misconduct ([[Classical
Enlightenment Healing the World and Screw-ups]] S7-S8). That supports
anti-perfection only when paired with accountability: ordinary human failure
does not become harmless because the person has realization.

[[Meditation Teacher's Qualifications and Liberation Experiences]] adds the
teacher-qualification version. Shinzen says having a liberation experience
does not necessarily qualify someone to teach, and not having one does not
necessarily exclude good teaching. He informally reads liberation through
body language and personal behavior, but treats both as uncertain and says
body language can mislead badly when no-self-like presentation appears
without integrated liberation or functional conduct ([[Meditation Teacher's
Qualifications and Liberation Experiences]] S1-S3). His stronger teacher
criterion is student results: the virtue of teaching is the effect of the
activity of teaching, and some deeply enlightened people may teach poorly
while less deeply realized people may help students effectively
([[Meditation Teacher's Qualifications and Liberation Experiences]] S4-S5).

[[Spiritual Teachers' Behaviour Feedback and Ethics]] adds the feedback-loop
version. Shinzen says uneven teacher development often comes from a
combination of inability and unwillingness to get feedback on behavior:
students may pedestalize the teacher, senior-teacher oversight may be
abandoned after decades of practice, and family or non-meditator feedback
may be dismissed as spiritually uninformed ([[Spiritual Teachers' Behaviour
Feedback and Ethics]] S2-S3). His corrective is structural and dispositional:
channels should exist, teachers should listen to everyone, and consensus
patterns should be allowed to emerge even when much individual criticism is
projection or "student stuff" ([[Spiritual Teachers' Behaviour Feedback and
Ethics]] S4-S6).

[[Teacher's or Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] adds the
student-dependence version. Shinzen says dependence on the teacher is the
main issue to watch, whether teacher-induced or student-generated. A
competent liberation teacher wants colleagues, peers, and independent people,
not dependent students, and Shinzen's own preferred response to gratitude is
to tell students to forget him and run with the principles and skill set
([[Teacher's or Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S1-S4). The
Rinzai sanzen example preserves transmission while requiring the student to
return to practice conditions and make the "hit" their own ([[Teacher's or
Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S5-S6).

## Boundaries
This concept is not an ethics policy. It does not specify grievance procedures, reporting requirements, consent rules, financial safeguards, clinical referral thresholds, power analysis, or organizational governance.

It should not be used to flatten all teachers. Some people may have rare depth, skill, and service capacity. The point is that those components do not remove the need for feedback, peers, outside support, and behavior verification.

It should also not be used to excuse harm. "Vector, not scalar" means imbalance is possible, not that imbalance is harmless. Serious misconduct still requires accountability, repair, protection, and often outside processes.

Non-ownership is not an accountability loophole. The fact that powerful
teaching may occur without self-credit does not remove ordinary responsibility
for teacher roles, community effects, consent, repair, or feedback
([[Enlightenment Downsides]] S5-S6).

Post-realization integration is not optional. The after-enlightenment talk
says seeing no-self is like seeing the ox; one still has to ride the ox in
daily life ([[After Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The Point]] S4). That
metaphor should block claims that a dramatic realization completes behavior,
relationships, or teacher competence.

Realization-specific feedback is not optional. The traps talk explicitly
routes the enlightenment trap through senior-teacher and environmental
feedback, so "real enlightenment" is not a reason to stop being corrected
([[Six Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] S6-S7).

Counterfactual improvement is not an accountability substitute. The
classical-enlightenment talk asks how badly realized people might have
screwed up without realization, but that question does not replace repair,
protection, consent, or role accountability after actual harm ([[Classical
Enlightenment Healing the World and Screw-ups]] S2, S7-S8).

Body language is not qualification. Shinzen explicitly treats no-self-like
voice, gaze, movement, or empty-energy presentation as insufficient without
behavior and results, and he presents a pseudo-enlightenment anecdote to show
how misleading appearance can be ([[Meditation Teacher's Qualifications and
Liberation Experiences]] S2-S4).

Student results are not a misconduct waiver. The talk makes results central,
but it does not define how to count adverse outcomes, harmed students,
dependency, power imbalance, or teacher behavior; those remain guidance and
accountability questions ([[Meditation Teacher's Qualifications and
Liberation Experiences]] S4-S5).

Feedback openness is not a full misconduct process. The feedback-and-ethics
talk makes openness to all human feedback central, but it does not define
anti-retaliation structures, legal reporting, student protection, grievance
procedures, or how to act when the consensus reveals serious harm
([[Spiritual Teachers' Behaviour Feedback and Ethics]] S4-S6).

Anti-dependency is not anti-guidance. The teacher-dependence talk still
values transmission and teacher contact; the boundary is whether contact
becomes student-owned capacity or dependence on the teacher-person
([[Teacher's or Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S1-S6).

"Classical enlightenment" is Shinzen's private language in this source. Do not use it as a universal Buddhist definition until later sources support that role.

Map humility is not teacher cynicism. Shinzen's claim that teachers know little about enlightenment is a pressure toward improvement, testing, and reduced perfection projection, not a reason to ignore competent guidance or flatten all teaching skill ([[Enlightenment Maps and Models]] S3, S7).

## Implications
When interpreting later talks, future agents should ask:

- Which component is actually being evidenced: concentration, clarity, equanimity, insight, love, service, behavior, teaching skill, or organizational competence?
- Is the teacher offering a useful provisional map, or being treated as if their map is final?
- What ordinary feedback would detect if this component were being overgeneralized?
- Does the teacher's system allow students and peers to say "you are off base" without retaliation or pious deflection?
- Does the teacher stay under some senior, peer, student, family, or ordinary-world feedback channel after decades of practice?
- Is outside help available when meditation practice does not change behavior?
- Are sex, power, finances, addiction, and co-dependent authority capture actively watched?
- Does the teacher's communication invite testing and correction, or centralize the teacher as a special object?
- Are teacher evaluation claims based on aura/body language, or on ordinary behavior and student outcomes over time?
- Is criticism being assessed for consensus patterns over time, or dismissed because some individual complaints are projections?
- Is the teacher-student relationship producing dependence on the teacher, or colleagues and peers who can apply the principles independently?
- Is non-doership reducing personal status, or is it being used to avoid ordinary responsibility?
- Is the "they would have been worse without practice" defense being used as context, or as an excuse not to repair concrete harm?

This keeps transmission fidelity and safety together. A future compiler should preserve Shinzen's mastery claims where sources support them, but route teacher authority through component analysis and feedback rather than reverence.
