This synthesis depends on the four Discrimination and Unification talks plus existing owner pages such as [[Sensory Clarity]], [[Sensory Grid]], [[Complete Experience]], [[Flow]], [[Expansion And Contraction]], [[Source And Polarities]], [[Insight and Purification]], and [[No-Self And Personality]]. The series' governing shape is dialectical: discriminate, unify, and then keep the freedom to discriminate again.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: In Shinzen's teaching, discrimination is not the enemy of unity; it is the way coarse fixation breaks into workable Flow, polarity, transparent space-ness, and a less thing-like self/world relation, while healthy unity must not create aversion to later distinctions.
- **Why this matters**: Future agents can answer "is this analytical or unitive practice?" by showing the sequence: distinguish components, complete them with CCE, let them digest, note Flow or polarity when present, and allow natural integration.
- **Key tensions**: The sources use Source, karma, Dzogchen, Sasaki Roshi, invulnerability, and enlightenment language; those frames should stay source-attributed and safety-bounded rather than becoming proof of metaphysics or behavior.
- **Source posture**: Probable as a synthesis of four primary Shinzen talks; bounded by short transcript length and the lack of concrete dosage or safety criteria.

## Landscape
- **Digestion** ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 1 of 4]]) - sensory discrimination breaks experience from coarse fixation into fine-grained components so useful value can assimilate and residue can release.
- **Flow flavors** ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 2 of 4]], [[Flow]]) - change becomes concrete as intensity shifts, speed shifts, spread, collapse, pressure, scattering, gripping, and all continuous movement other than abrupt vanishing.
- **Polarity simplification** ([[Expansion And Contraction]]) - many Flow flavors can be abstracted as increase and decrease, or Expansion and Contraction, with Zero as the balance point.
- **Spacious unification** ([[Source And Polarities]]) - complete experience can present as rich transparency or space-ness, while Gone and Source language remain bounded by not-a-sensory-object and afterglow cautions.
- **Self/world unification** ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 3 of 4]], [[No-Self And Personality]]) - stable experience remains a valid CCE object; when change is salient, Flow can be refined into Expansion, Contraction, or Both, and enough self/world wave integration may undermine the I-it paradigm while ordinary separate-self appearance still recurs.
- **Empowering contrasts** ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 4 of 4]], [[Sensory Grid]]) - the series closes by saying oneness and discrimination must alternate; tracking Feel/Image/Talk, touch/sight/sound, and restful states is not regression but the way tangled experience becomes manageable.
- **Image-space discrimination** ([[Working with Images and Image Space]], [[Inner Sensory System]]) - a later Gate 5 talk applies the same dialectic inside See In: body/self image, ambient place image, and central memory-plan-fantasy imagery can be distinguished, completed, and integrated into Flow.
- **Generalized contrast maturity** ([[A Mindfulness Path Arising Between Empowering Contrasts]]) - a later Gate 7 talk abstracts the same principle beyond this series: discrimination and destroying discrimination are one example of the larger path skill of working complementary contrasts without fixation.

## Through-Line
The emerging series through-line is that Shinzen's clarity practice is dialectical. First, experience is broken down. A moment that appears as a thing becomes Feel, Image, Talk, touch, sight, sound, intensity, pressure, vibration, spread, collapse, or vanishing. That discrimination is not mere analysis; it prevents fixation.

Second, CCE lets the broken-down event move. Part 1 calls this digestion: the event becomes Flow and can assimilate useful value while releasing residue. Part 2 calls the same precision ordinary Flow taxonomy: intensifying, fading, speeding, slowing, spreading, collapsing, scattering, and gripping can all become workable change.

Third, the differentiated strands may simplify. Many flavors become Expansion and Contraction; many sensory components can share one dynamic nature; complete experience becomes rich but transparent. Part 3 adds an important routing correction: if change is not salient, the practitioner should not force the advanced map, but should bring CCE to the stability of experience. If change is salient and analyzable, Expansion, Contraction, and Both become a refined Focus on Change option.

Fourth, the same discrimination can soften the self/world split. Shinzen contrasts an I over here at the mercy of an it over there with two rivers that automatically add into one flow. The unification is therefore not achieved by skipping distinctions. It follows from distinctions becoming clear enough that their common dynamic and spacious character is obvious.

Part 4 completes the sequence by preventing that unification from becoming an anti-discrimination stance. Shinzen calls healthy oneness and healthy discrimination "empowering contrasts" and says practice moves back and forth between them. Refusing to track Feel, Image, Talk, touch, sight, or sound because analysis seems selfing or materialistic is a mistake. Those distinctions turn "multiplicative overwhelm" into "additive manageability" by teasing apart the tangle ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 4 of 4]] S3-S5).

The series therefore does not end in a static unity. It ends in a dialectic: distinguish components, let CCE digest them into Flow, refine Flow into polarity when available, recognize shared Source or I-Thou relation when that is present, and remain willing to make ordinary sensory distinctions again. That final willingness is what keeps unification from becoming vague, inflated, or dissociated.

Gate 7 item 9 confirms that this dialectic is not a local exception. In
[[A Mindfulness Path Arising Between Empowering Contrasts]], Shinzen names
"making discriminations" and "destroying discriminations" as one of several
empowering contrasts. Maturity is not picking analysis or oneness; it is
learning what to do at what time and seeing the two sides as
complementarities rather than contradictions ([[A Mindfulness Path Arising
Between Empowering Contrasts]] S1-S3).

The third Ordinary Consciousness talk gives a later Flow-specific restatement
of the same dialectic. Shinzen says the path has a place for making
empowering distinctions among touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, and Talk, and
also a place for those distinctions disappearing. The empowering route for
disappearance is Flow/Gone unification: larger pieces of sensory experience
become one Flow field, and external sight/sound plus internal image/talk can
flow together until the inside/outside boundary drops
([[Ordinary Consciousness is the Way - Part 3 Mindful Awareness and Varieties
of Flow]] S7).

Gate 5 item 18 adds a finer example of the same principle within image space.
Shinzen distinguishes body/self image, ambient place image, and central
memory-plan-fantasy imagery. The point is not to multiply labels for their
own sake, but to make image-based self and world construction precise enough
that body image and body sensation, or ambient image and incoming sound, can
be completed and integrated into Flow ([[Working with Images and Image
Space]] S1-S6).
