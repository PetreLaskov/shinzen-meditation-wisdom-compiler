Equanimity and purification taste owns the transformation branch of
noninterference. Its central boundary is simple: in Shinzen's frame,
equanimity can purify experience, but pain level, intensity, drama, or
endurance do not prove purification.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Noninterference is the active variable in Shinzen's
  purification language, not whether the object is unpleasant, pleasant,
  neutral, mild, intense, ordinary, or altered.
- **Why this matters**: It keeps purification practice from turning into
  austerity romanticism, bliss chasing, or catharsis proof.
- **Key tensions**: Purification taste can motivate practice, but it is not
  independent evidence that past material has objectively cleared or future
  conduct will improve.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's practice model; limited as outcome
  proof or safety protocol.

## Transformation Claim

[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 5 - Insight and Purification]] places
equanimity inside the local theorem: concentration, sensory clarity,
equanimity, and time yield insight and purification (S10). Equanimity's role
is allowing sensory components to expand, contract, or remain still without
interference so clarity and concentration can percolate into deeper patterns
(S9, S12-S14).

The first Equanimity and Purification talk gives the anti-ascetic correction.
Shinzen says the Buddha refined asceticism by replacing "the more it hurts,
the more it purifies" with "the more equanimity is brought to experience,
the more purification occurs" ([[Equanimity and the Taste of Purification -
Part 1 of 2]] S2). It defines equanimity as sensory circuits not fighting
their own productions as sensations arise and not holding them
inappropriately as sensations pass (S3).

The second talk adds the bliss-side correction. Pleasant or blissful altered
states can also purify consciousness when met with equanimity, while
uncomfortable practices are optional and invalid when they harm the body or
wipe the practitioner out so badly that practice is no longer actually
happening ([[Equanimity and the Taste of Purification - Part 2 of 2]] S1-S5).

## Resistance Formulas

[[Purification and Fulfilment Four Formulas]] gives the compact valence map:
pleasure plus resistance becomes frustration, pleasure plus equanimity
becomes fulfillment, discomfort plus resistance becomes suffering, and
discomfort plus equanimity becomes purification (S1, S4-S7). These formulas
are teaching handles, not measured equations.

This formula makes pleasure and pain symmetrical as practice material. The
question is not "is this pleasant?" or "is this painful?" but "is there
resistance, grasping, suppression, identification, or noninterference?"

## Reward Taste

[[Growth and Tastes of Concentration, Sensory Clarity and Equanimity]] says
equanimity can taste like knowing that the open way the present moment is
being experienced will make future moments more fulfilling, involve less
suffering, and release past material as the present sensory event equalizes
(S4). This is a practice-recognition cue, not a guarantee.

[[The Hockey Stick Metaphor and Exponential Growth on the Spiritual Path]]
turns that taste into a motivation model: once the taste of purification is
tangible, bringing CCE, especially equanimity, to unpleasant experience can
create a positive feedback loop for further growth (S3-S6). Treat this as
source-attributed motivation, not a demand that every sit feel meaningful.

## Intensity Branches

Strong Determination is the clearest high-intensity case. [[Zero and One -
Part 3 Strong Determination Challenges, Benefits, and Tasting Purification]]
says the learning criterion is not duration or pain level but whether touch,
Feel, Image, and Talk stop fighting each other. Shinzen explicitly corrects
the macho reading: mild clear learning outranks severe endurance (S3-S7).

Kriyas supply the loss-of-control case. [[Kriyas & Complete Experiences]]
warns that spontaneous movements can trigger fear in some practitioners and
craving in others; the practice task is not to suppress or amplify them as
identity, but to experiment with allowing and not allowing while completing
detectable sensations (S3-S5, S9).

## Boundaries

Do not infer purification from intensity, tears, shaking, pain tolerance,
bliss, kriyas, visions, heat, social compliance, teacher approval, or the
felt certainty that something cleared. The test is whether CCE, function,
behavior, support, and ordinary responsiveness improve over time.

If purification taste makes someone less willing to change posture, seek
medical care, sleep, leave a coercive setting, repair behavior, or ask for
support, load [[Equanimity Versus Suppression]] and [[Complete Experience
Safety Boundary]].

Pleasure also needs a boundary. Fulfillment is not pleasure chasing; pleasant
absorption, calm, Flow, and bliss should be met with the same nonclinging
that applies to discomfort.
