## Practice Handles
For a practitioner: choose a brief positive Image or Talk that plausibly evokes warmth, joy, love, gratitude, or pleasantness. Use it only long enough to start body resonance. Then drop the Image/Talk and attend to the pleasant Feel in the body. If the Feel fades, re-strike briefly and return to the body.

For a guide: distinguish three reports. If pleasant Feel is already present, use the find-and-cover branch. If it fades, add brief triggering Image/Talk. If only calm or emotional rest appears, do not force it to count as positive Feel.

For scope: ask the practitioner to spread attention only as broadly as the pleasant Feel is actually present. Whole-body and beyond-body radiation are advanced possibilities, not immediate performance requirements.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by naming the student's previous report: positive Feel could be found for a while, then it disappeared. He says this is common and clarifies that finding positive Feel is only one route. Another route is to create it by briefly making a positive thought.

The thought can be mental image, internal talk, or both. Its job is not to become the meditation object. Its job is to strike the emotional body so that pleasant resonance appears or continues. Once the body is resonating, the practitioner drops the thought and attends to the pleasant Feel. If the resonance stops, the practitioner can think the same or a different positive thought again, just enough to refresh the body resonance.

Shinzen then demonstrates the metaphor with an external bell. Thought is like striking the bell; the emotional body is the bell's resonance. This lets the practitioner understand why frequent retriggering may be needed for some people and not for others. Some bodies may resonate for seconds; others may continue for minutes.

The focus remains body sensation. Shinzen describes the practice as strengthening positive emotional circuitry, like physical therapy for an underused muscle. He then answers the student's question about where to aim attention: spread attention as broadly as the pleasant Feel is present, without forcing. With practice, strong positive Feel may become whole-body, then seem larger than the body and radiate outward in all six directions.

## Phenomenological Distinctions
- **Trigger versus object**: positive Image/Talk can be the controllable trigger, but the intended object is positive body Feel.
- **Resonance versus thought stream**: the useful aftereffect is the lingering body vibration after the thought has been dropped.
- **Breadth versus force**: attention can stretch over the available pleasant Feel, but the practitioner should not manufacture whole-body coverage by pressure.
- **Positive Feel versus Rest**: this talk presupposes the earlier distinction that calm or emotional peace is not the same object as pleasant emotional body sensation.

## Easily Misused Claims
