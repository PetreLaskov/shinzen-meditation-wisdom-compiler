This five-minute `@ShinzenVideos` short is a terminology and map-calibration
teaching. Shinzen uses "the agony of jargon" to warn that practice language
does not translate reliably word by word, then gives one concrete bridge:
what some contemporary teachers call AP or rising-passing should be read in
his vocabulary as Both-Gone.

## Teaching Register
Short map-translation and expectation-calibration answer. The register is
not formal scholarship; it is Shinzen trying to reduce a student's confusion
by giving one crisp translation while preserving the larger warning that
jargon is unstable across teachers.

## Why This Talk Matters
The wiki already had map humility and lineage-translation boundaries. This
talk adds a sharper operational rule: when map terms collide, future agents
should ask what experience or practice function the term names in the
speaker's system before treating the words as equivalent or opposed.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Different teachers may use different language for identical
  phenomena, or identical words for entirely different phenomena; Shinzen
  says this is confusing and currently unavoidable.
- **S2**: Shinzen says it can take decades of practice to develop the
  discrimination needed to tell when terms are same-phenomenon/different-word
  or same-word/different-phenomenon.
- **S3**: He contrasts older teacher domains and debate-style encounters with
  a hoped-for future of more public, peer-to-peer, science-like, collegial
  dialogue among teachers.
- **S4**: As a simplifying translation, he says that when Daniel or Kenneth
  say AP, the listener should read that as Shinzen's Both-Gone.
- **S5**: He describes the relevant AP/rising-passing phenomenon as a deep
  experience of space spreading and collapsing as soon as it arises, while
  noting that the exact Pali and contemporary-map terms require caution in
  this auto-caption transcript.
- **S6**: Shinzen names several progress metrics that do not cleanly
  correspond: early Buddhist craving/aversion/unconsciousness criteria, the
  ten ox-herding pictures, and how long or through what intensity one can
  sustain Both-Gone or rising-passing.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The core move is not "learn more jargon"; it is discriminate the
  phenomenon or practice function behind the jargon.
- **S3**: Shinzen's future-science optimism is being used here as a dialogue
  and translation hope, not as evidence that a final map already exists.
- **S4-S5**: AP-to-Both-Gone gives a concrete local bridge between another
  map language and Shinzen's Expansion-Contraction/Gone vocabulary.
- **S6**: The talk protects map humility by showing that progress metrics can
  be real and useful without mapping neatly onto one another.

## Practice Handles
- When a practitioner imports another teacher's term, ask what sensory event,
  attentional skill, transformation criterion, or map function the term names
  before translating it into Shinzen's system.
- Treat AP/rising-passing reports as possible Both-Gone or
  Expansion-Contraction/Gone territory when the report includes space,
  spreading, collapsing, arising, and passing together.
- Do not convert AP, Both-Gone, ox-herding, stream-entry, or
  craving/aversion criteria into a single status ladder without checking the
  underlying metric.
- Use jargon confusion as a reason for humility and dialogue, not as a reason
  to collapse all systems into sameness or dismiss all maps.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by naming the basic problem: meditation teachers inherit or
invent different vocabularies, and those vocabularies do not line up cleanly.
Sometimes different words name the same phenomenon; sometimes the same word
names different phenomena. He treats this as a real burden on practitioners,
especially those without decades of practice.

He then places the problem historically. In his telling, older teachers often
worked inside their own domains and public encounters tended toward debate
rather than collaborative comparison. He expects modern teacher dialogue,
shaped by a more science-like and collegial spirit, to reduce this confusion
over time. That claim is aspirational, not a completed state of the field.

The concrete translation is AP. Shinzen says to read AP, as used by Daniel or
Kenneth, as Both-Gone in his vocabulary: space spreads and collapses as soon
as it arises. He closes by saying progress can be measured through several
different map systems, including craving/aversion/unconsciousness reduction,
ox-herding images, or duration and intensity of Both-Gone/rising-passing
experience, but those metrics do not obviously correspond.

## Source Frame
The source is a local translation aid. Shinzen is not proving that all map
systems are equivalent; he is giving one bridge and warning that such bridges
require high discrimination. The strongest evidence is his own teaching
posture toward jargon, AP, Both-Gone, and map incommensurability.

The weakest frame is external taxonomy. The transcript is quality B and
contains obvious caption artifacts around historical and technical terms.
Use this source for Shinzen's own translation posture and AP/Both-Gone
bridge, not for exact Pali spelling, Buddhist textual history, or the
positions of other named teachers beyond the short statement he makes.

## Weakest Claims
- The hope that collegial dialogue will substantially reduce jargon agony in
  future decades or centuries is aspirational and should not be treated as an
  established trend.
- The claim that it takes 30 or 40 years to develop cross-jargon sense is a
  teaching emphasis, not a fixed prerequisite.
- The AP-to-Both-Gone bridge is Shinzen's local translation judgment; it does
  not establish that all teachers use AP identically or that all
  rising-passing reports are the same phenomenology.
- The transcript quality limits exact Pali, historical, and teacher-name
  citation.

## Important Omissions

- No method for deciding when two terms are genuinely same-phenomenon versus

- No safety guidance for intense AP, Both-Gone, dissolution, fear,

- No behavioral, ethical, or clinical criteria for evaluating progress

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen gives a simple AP = Both-Gone translation while also saying jargon
  translation is intrinsically difficult and experience-dependent.
- He values future science-like dialogue while the current clip itself
  relies on informal oral shorthand and a noisy auto-caption transcript.
- He offers several ways to measure progress while also saying those maps do
  not seem to correspond cleanly.

## Easily Misused Claims
- AP = Both-Gone can be overused as a universal equivalence rather than a
  local translation handle.
- Duration or intensity of Both-Gone can become a status metric if separated
  from CCE, daily-life effects, behavior, and map humility.
- The science/collegiality hope can become premature confidence that a final
  contemplative taxonomy is near.
- Jargon confusion can become cynicism if the practical response is not
  careful translation, direct experience, and dialogue.
