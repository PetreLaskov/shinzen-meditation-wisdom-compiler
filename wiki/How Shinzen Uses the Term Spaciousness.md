This short dialogue with Har-Prakash Khalsa is a terminology and lineage-translation clarification. Shinzen explains why he made Spaciousness its own SHF dimension: it can name openness or thinness in sensory spaces even when Flow is absent, while still allowing Flow and Expansion-Contraction to appear inside that spacious field.

## Teaching Register
This is Q&A clarification rather than guided practice. Har-Prakash presses whether Spaciousness is just Expansion-Contraction under another name, and Shinzen answers by separating taxonomy, lineage translation, and optional dynamic features.

## Why This Talk Matters
The SHF introduction defines Space but gives little operational detail. This talk supplies the missing design rationale: Shinzen wanted a dimension that could integrate early Buddhist formless absorption language, Tibetan spacious-awareness language, and Sasaki-style Expansion-Contraction without forcing all spacious experience to be dynamic.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Spaciousness is provisionally defined as openness around sensory experience, thinness within it, or both, presenting in sensory spaces.
- **S2**: Shinzen says the term was introduced partly to move practitioners toward the four formless absorptions, though not as a one-to-one correspondence.
- **S3**: Tibetan spacious-awareness language and Sasaki Roshi's space/polarity formulation also influenced the term's role in Shinzen's integration.
- **S4**: Spaciousness is kept as an independent dimension because formless absorption descriptions do not necessarily mention energy, Flow, or impermanence.
- **S5**: If Flow is present within spaciousness, Shinzen frames that as insight absorption; if Expansion-Contraction Flow is present, the vastness/thinness can move outward or contract inward in a way close to Sasaki Roshi's Source formulation.
- **S6**: Shinzen treats "spacious awareness" as likely synonymous with his Spaciousness usage, while noting he would need to check the official manual vocabulary.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: Preserves the operational definition from the SHF source while making clear that Spaciousness can appear in any sensory space.
- **S2-S4**: Prevents future agents from reducing Space to either Rest, Flow, or Expansion-Contraction; the category exists partly because those mappings are useful but not exhaustive.
- **S5**: Gives the conditional branch: when Flow is absent, Spaciousness can simply stabilize; when Flow is present, it becomes an insight/Source-adjacent route.
- **S6**: Keeps "spacious awareness" available as Shinzen-compatible vocabulary without turning a Tibetan phrase into doctrinal equivalence.

## Practice Handles
- In Focus on Space, look for openness around or thinness within sight, sound, body sensation, image, talk, or emotional body sensation.
- Do not force spaciousness to move. If it is stable, treat it as a valid Space object rather than a failed Flow object.
- If Flow appears inside the spacious field, note the dynamic quality; if the spacious field expands, contracts, or does both, route through [[Expansion And Contraction]].
- Treat spaciousness as a translation bridge, not as proof that formless jhana, Tibetan spacious awareness, and Sasaki's Source formulation are the same thing.

## Summary
Har-Prakash begins by asking whether Spaciousness is just a special case of Expansion-Contraction: openness resembles Expansion and thinness resembles Contraction. Shinzen agrees that the term points in that direction but says it is not a one-to-one correspondence. He names several source frames he wanted to integrate: the early Buddhist formless absorptions, Tibetan space-language around spacious awareness, and Sasaki Roshi's use of space and polarity.

Shinzen then gives the main design reason. The formless absorptions classically emphasize boundless space, boundless consciousness, nothingness, and the border of consciousness without necessarily mentioning energy, Flow, or impermanence. Some absorption teachers distinguish pure shamatha from vipassana jhana, where impermanence is present. Shinzen therefore keeps Spaciousness as its own dimension so it can be stable when no dynamics are present.

The dynamic branch remains important. If Flow in general is present within spaciousness, Shinzen treats that as insight absorption. If Expansion-Contraction is present, the vastness does not merely sit there: it moves outward as force, and thinness can become the basis for effortless contraction. Shinzen says that branch approaches Sasaki Roshi's formulation of Source.

## Source Frame
The source is trying to explain a vocabulary decision inside Shinzen's system. Its strongest evidence is Shinzen's own account of why the Space/Spaciousness category exists and how it should be distinguished from Flow and Expansion-Contraction.

The comparative material should be kept as Shinzen's lineage-translation frame. The talk does not establish what early Buddhist, Tibetan, or Sasaki-lineage sources objectively mean; it shows how Shinzen uses them to build a dimensionally clean practice interface.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not treat Focus on Space as a shortcut to formless jhana, Tibetan realization, or Sasaki-style Source.

- Do not use "spacious awareness" language to bypass concrete sensory clarity, ordinary functioning, clinical support, or relational accountability.
