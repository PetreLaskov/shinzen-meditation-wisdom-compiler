Nurture Positive is the constructive technique in Basic Mindfulness. It intentionally uses inner sensory systems to cultivate wholesome patterns rather than only observe what is already arising; Shinzen explicitly does not posit a parallel technique that nurtures negative content. When the positive carrier is body emotion, live guidance begins by finding already-present pleasant Feel without an agenda before creating it.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Nurture Positive follows a choose-check-choose-hold algorithm over positive themes and inner sensory systems, with the older manual using six ABCISO themes and the later SHF appendix simplifying them to ABCD.
- **Why this matters**: It gives Shinzen's system an explicit reconstruction technique alongside Noting and Do Nothing, and chapter 8 uses it as one intervention for emptiness/no-self distress.
- **Key tensions**: Deliberate positive construction can support behavior and service, but it can also bypass pain or substitute imagery for action if not tested.
- **Source posture**: Strong as manual instruction; outcome and interpersonal-radiation claims remain source-attributed.

## Model
Nurture Positive uses mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation, the same elements used by Focus In. The difference is that Focus In observes what is present, while Nurture Positive actively creates positive images and talk that may evoke pleasant emotional body sensations ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S3-S4).

The practice then uses concentration to spread the positive feeling through the whole body and radiate it beyond the body to people and objects around the practitioner ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S5). The radiating-service claim belongs to Shinzen's source frame; the durable mechanism is constructive training of image, talk, and body affect.

[[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human Positivity]] gives the same
move a vivid oral form. Shinzen identifies lovingkindness as a traditional
version of Focus on Positive Feel, then says advanced practice can radiate
positive energy beyond the body "in all six directions" like a high-wattage
broadcast of human positivity ([[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human
Positivity]] S3-S4). The phrase is useful as idiolect; the interpersonal
effect remains source-attributed rather than verified.

## Choose-Check-Choose-Hold
All forms of Nurture Positive follow the same pattern ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S10-S12).

First, choose a specific positive theme. The six main categories are Positive Affect, Positive Behavior, Positive Cognition, Positive Ideals, Positive Situations, and Other Positives.

Second, check the theme in two common systems. Repeat a word or phrase that represents the theme while tuning into related pleasant emotional body sensation. Then create a mental image that represents the theme while focusing on that image and any related pleasant body sensation.

Third, choose the subjective system that seems most natural for this theme at this time:

- Image only.
- Talk only.
- Emotional Body only.
- Image and Talk.
- Image and Emotional Body.
- Talk and Emotional Body.
- Whole subjective system: Image, Talk, and Emotional Body together.

Fourth, hold the theme in the chosen system for a set period of time.

## Theme Space
The chapter's ABCISO mnemonic separates content from sensory location. Positive Affect includes joy, friendliness, enthusiasm, compassion, love, gratitude, and forgiveness. Positive Behavior includes eliminating negative behaviors, initiating desired behaviors, and strengthening current positive behaviors. Positive Cognition includes creating rational, productive thought patterns and replacing inappropriate ones. Positive Ideals, Positive Situations, and Other Positives cover symbolic, imagined, outcome, and uncategorized goodness themes ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S7-S9).

This is important because Nurture Positive is not one emotion practice. It is a general constructive interface: what to cultivate can vary, and where to cultivate it can vary.

[[Finding Feel Good in Emotional Body Space]] adds a live body-Feel branch. Shinzen first asks the student to look for already-present pleasant emotional body sensation such as interest, enthusiasm, joy, smiliness, humor, or pleasant excitement. If it is present, the instruction is to cover it broadly with awareness; if it evaporates, that is acceptable. If the pleasant Feel settles into calmness, he classifies the calm as emotional relaxation or Rest rather than positive Feel ([[Finding Feel Good in Emotional Body Space]] S1-S5).

Chapter 12 places those choices inside guidance. The "what" options remain A-B-C-I-S-O: Affect, Behavior, Cognition, Ideal, Situations, and Other positives. The "where" options are one inner space, two inner spaces, or all three: image, talk, and emotional body sensation ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S13). This makes Nurture Positive teachable as a set of adjustable parameters.

SHF Unit 08 records a later simplification. The current formulation reduces the six old themes to the four Shinzen says he has found most useful: Affect, Behavior, Cognition, and Ideal. The source suggests ABCD as the newer acronym, with D standing for Ideal or Vajrayana-style deity yoga ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S12). Future agents should preserve both interfaces: ABCISO is the older Basic Mindfulness manual and guidance-map version, while ABCD is the later Unified Mindfulness simplification.

## Difference From Noting
Nurture Positive is not [[Noting]]. Noting acknowledges and focuses on sensory events, with labels as optional support. Nurture Positive intentionally creates content and holds it in a chosen inner system. The Basic Mindfulness introduction already named Nurture Positive as the technique used by the Way of Human Goodness, while four other Ways use Noting ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S8; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S3-S4).

[[Evoking and Working Through Challenging Material]] adds the negative-side
contrast. Shinzen says Focus on Positive intentionally holds and radiates
positive content, but there is no corresponding practice that holds or
nurtures negative content. When negative material is intentionally evoked, it
is only struck like a body-mind bell so the remaining resonance can be
deconstructed through Focus In ([[Evoking and Working Through Challenging
Material]] S2-S5).

The two techniques can complement each other. If positive construction evokes resistance, grief, shame, numbness, or aversion, those reactions may become Noting objects. If Noting or [[See Hear Feel]] deconstructs self so thoroughly that warmth, aspiration, or behavior are underdeveloped, Nurture Positive becomes a balancing route.

Chapter 8 makes that balancing role explicit in a safety context. When intense Goneness, Emptiness, or No-Self produces fear, loss, confusion, or disorientation, Shinzen recommends reconstructing the self with positive content regarding affect, behavior, and cognition using Nurture Positive ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S9). Basic Mindfulness chapter 9 then confirms Nurture Positive as one of the three techniques that may be learned separately or cycled with Noting and Do Nothing ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 9 - The Full Grid]] S5).

The session-setup talk gives a simpler sequencing image. Shinzen compares ending a deconstructive workout with Focus on Positive to entering a jacuzzi after physical exercise: a pleasant support that helps the reconstructive direction after self/world deconstruction ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S4-S5). The metaphor strengthens Nurture Positive as a closing or balancing move, not as a replacement for contact, repair, or behavior.

Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 repeats the same safety logic in stronger dark-night language. When Goneness, emptiness, and no-self produce disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, or hopelessness, one intervention is to affirm positive emotions, behaviors, and cognitions in a sustained way, gradually reconstructing a new habitual self through lovingkindness-related practice ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S14).

[[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] gives the same
reconstructive move a sharper clinical-neighbor context. When meditation
practice begins moving toward pit-of-the-void distress, Shinzen says one
branch is to use concentration skills to create positive Feel/Image/Talk
again and again, like physical therapy for atrophied capacity, until the
person can build a new and better functional self ([[Enlightenment DPDR and
Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] S6).

[[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]] repeats that branch for the rare
case Shinzen judges to be fully practice-based dark night or pit-of-the-void
distress. It also adds the limit: when depression, anxiety, or clinical
factors may be involved, Focus on Positive is not a substitute for competent
mental-health evaluation. If medication is used and side effects appear,
those effects can be met as touch, Feel, Image, and Talk, while medication
decisions remain outside the meditation teacher's scope ([[Classic Dark Night
or Clinical Issues]] S1-S7).

[[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] gives the most compressed formulation:
emptiness is only half the picture. When advanced students lose humanity,
edge, or dynamic motivation after emptiness, Shinzen says to balance the void
by intentionally holding, manifesting, and radiating positive Feel/Image/Talk
as a consistent practice, thereby creating a new and better conditioned self
from emptiness ([[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] S1-S5).

## Boundaries
Nurture Positive should not be reduced to forced optimism. The chapter includes Positive Behavior, which means the practice should be answerable to external action and feedback, not only pleasant inner states.

The ABCD simplification should not erase the older Situation and Other categories when reading Basic Mindfulness sources. It is a later interface choice, not proof that those older categories were mistaken or unusable.

It also should not erase negative experience. The manual's broader system includes direct tracking of thoughts, emotions, physical sensations, rest, and Flow. Constructive cultivation becomes bypass when it replaces necessary contact, repair, grief, anger, medical response, therapy, or boundary setting.

The Feel Good guidance sharpens the boundary on the pleasant side. Pleasant
emotional sensation should not be forced merely because the practice is
positive; absence, calm, or emotional relaxation are valid reports and may
route to Rest or another positive-practice branch ([[Finding Feel Good in
Emotional Body Space]] S3-S5). The evocation contrast sharpens the same
boundary in the other direction: negative experience may be contacted or
occasionally evoked for deconstructive practice, but it is not intentionally
held, amplified, or radiated as a cultivation object.

The chapter does not give clinical or safety criteria for mania, compulsive positivity, depression, trauma, or shame around inability to feel positive. Those route to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].

The DPDR talk does not change that boundary. Positive reconstruction in
void distress is not a substitute for clinical assessment, referral,
ordinary functioning, or emergency support when those are needed
([[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] S4-S6).
The dark-night/clinical talk strengthens the same boundary: if the distress
is not clearly practice-only, Shinzen's recommendation is professional
assessment and openness to standard treatment, not more positivity practice
alone ([[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]] S5-S7).

## Implications
For practice routing, Nurture Positive is useful when the practitioner needs to strengthen wholesome motivation, affective warmth, behavioral intention, rational cognition, or service orientation.

For Shinzen-system interpretation, it proves that Basic Mindfulness is not purely deconstructive. The system explicitly trains the human personality as well as its dissolution.
