## Teaching Register
Advanced map, speculative analogy, and service teaching. Shinzen begins from a traditional insight-map reference, translates it into his Flow vocabulary, tests it against physics and cosmology analogies, then returns to pragmatic happiness and teaching.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk gives Gate 4 an opening router: impermanence can be read temporally as passing as soon as arising and spatially as simultaneous spreading and collapsing. It also clarifies that Source realization should not remain private; it should show up as care, behavior, coherent description, or explicit teaching.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says a stage near stream entry involves not merely arising then passing, but detecting passing as soon as something arises; he treats sensitivity to passing as a deep training.
- **S2**: Because every sensory event has spatial volume, passing-as-arising can be experienced as space collapsing while it spreads, yielding simultaneous Expansion-Contraction in Feel, Image, Talk, sight, sound, and touch.
- **S3**: When self-side Feel/Image/Talk and object-side sight, sound, or touch share the same simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, the subject/object and inside/outside split can momentarily collapse into one Source activity.
- **S4**: Shinzen compares Expansion-Contraction to stars, galaxies, atoms, cosmology, and quantum-scale questions, but explicitly says the relation to nature may be projection and is not why the practice is done.
- **S5**: Regular contact with shared Source makes service natural because others are experienced as part of a larger identity; service may occur through affect, visible behavior change, coherent description of practice, or explicit instruction.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The talk converts impermanence from generic change into a refined temporal discrimination: passing can become as salient as arising.
- **S2**: The spatial translation is load-bearing because it explains why Expansion and Contraction are not merely two alternating forces; a single event can spread and collapse at the same time.
- **S3**: The self/object unification claim gives a practice bridge from inner Feel/Image/Talk training to external perception and nondual Source language.
- **S4**: The nature analogies are teaching images, not evidence; Shinzen's own caveat protects the wiki from turning contemplative phenomenology into physics.
- **S5**: The service section turns Source contact into a behavioral and transmission test: care should appear in how a person affects others, changes over time, explains practice, and eventually teaches when competent.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner can train the passing side of impermanence rather than only the arising side. If sensory events have spatial volume, they can also test whether an event's space is spreading, collapsing, or both at once.

A guide can distinguish four service levels in this talk: affective presence, changed behavior over time, coherent description of what meditation does, and explicit prescription of a technique with reporting and follow-up. The third level is deliberately modest: a practitioner can teach descriptively without taking on the role of meditation instructor.

## Summary
Shinzen opens with the Visuddhimagga's seven purifications and warns that maps can distract from practice. He then highlights a near-stream-entry stage in which passing is detected as soon as arising occurs. Since sensory experience has spatial volume, he translates this temporal insight into a spatial one: as an event arises, space spreads; as it passes while arising, space collapses. This becomes simultaneous Expansion-Contraction.

He applies the same pattern first to inner Feel/Image/Talk and then to external sight, sound, and touch. When self-side and object-side expansion-contraction cannot be held apart, the subject/object and inside/outside split is momentarily annihilated into one activity of Source. He adds Dzogchen-adjacent language of spaciousness and effortless spontaneity, while defining spaciousness as the elastic capacity to spread and collapse rather than as a static large container.

The middle of the talk uses stars, supernovae, galaxies, atoms, cosmology, and quantum-scale questions as Expansion-Contraction analogies. Shinzen then marks the caveat: perhaps he is projecting from his own contemplative experience, and practice does not need scientific validation because its point is deep and broad happiness. The closing service section says shared Source contact makes others part of one's larger identity and naturally supports service through affect, behavior, coherent description, and explicit teaching.

## Source Frame
The source wants practitioners to see advanced impermanence as both temporally precise and spatially embodied. It also wants advanced Source language to return to pragmatic happiness and service rather than metaphysical fascination.

The nature analogies are explicitly speculative. The talk is strong evidence for how Shinzen teaches the relationship among impermanence, Expansion-Contraction, Source, and service; it is weak evidence for any objective scientific claim about stars, galaxies, atoms, cosmology, or physics.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen warns that path maps can distract from practice, then uses a path-map stage as the talk's starting point.
- The talk is pragmatic about happiness while also offering broad cosmological analogies that could easily seduce attention away from practice.
- Service is framed as a spontaneous consequence of shared Source, while the existing wiki model requires behavior verification and accountability.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Born between Expansion and Contraction" could be mistaken for a physics theory rather than a phenomenological practice frame.
- "Responding to larger identity" could be used to justify intrusive service, teaching without consent, or bypass of ordinary relational repair.
- Coherent description should not be confused with qualification to prescribe practice.
