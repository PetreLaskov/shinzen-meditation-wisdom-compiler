Chapter 11 of the Basic Mindfulness manual extends the grid from sensory classification into the human condition. It makes life practice depend on the relationship among sensory experience, objective behavior, objective situations, skill development, and service.

- **Path**: `raw/Shinzen Sources/five-ways/11-life.md`
- **Origin**: Shinzen Young, Basic Mindfulness manual.
- **Date**: No chapter-specific date in the raw Markdown.
- **Format**: Practice-manual chapter converted to Markdown, with diagram text partly flattened.
- **Reliability**: Primary source for Basic Mindfulness life-practice framing. Strong for Shinzen's own practice architecture; weaker for karma, behavior-change, and total-happiness claims as empirical generalizations.
- **Scope**: Defines the sensory and objective aspects of life, summarizes the keys to total happiness, introduces daily/yearly/fun practice cycles, gives the fourfold aim of total happiness, and adds Source/polarity plus history symbols that chapter 12 expands.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Human life has a sensory aspect and an objective aspect; the objective aspect includes actions, meaning what one says and does, and situations, meaning the circumstances one finds oneself in.
- **S2**: Total human happiness depends on understanding the relation among sensory experience, actions, and situations; understanding mindfulness; understanding how mindfulness affects sensory experience and therefore behavior and situations; and knowing how to maintain systematic practice.
- **S3**: Actions are classified as good, bad, or neutral; situations are classified as desired, undesired, or neutral; the chapter frames "what goes around comes around" as a general and average law of karma.
- **S4**: Practice has long-term effects in the three core skills, while the immediate goal is to establish daily and yearly cycles and make practice enjoyable.
- **S5**: The daily cycle includes practice in stillness, practice in motion, and practice in life; the yearly cycle includes maintaining the daily cycle, periodic retreat, and periodic teacher check-in.
- **S6**: Practice becomes fun by finding something interesting, enjoyable, or productive to focus on and by learning the distinct reward flavors of concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity.
- **S7**: Total happiness has four quadrants: achieve surface happiness for oneself, achieve deep happiness for oneself, help others achieve surface happiness, and help others achieve deep happiness.
- **S8**: Deep happiness for oneself is framed through formulas and transformations: fulfillment equals pleasure multiplied by mindful awareness, suffering equals discomfort divided by mindful awareness, "Don't Know" can become empowered by mindful awareness, and behavior can change because mindfulness gives a handle on thoughts and body sensations.
- **S9**: Helping others achieve deep happiness means teaching a path such as mindfulness or something with similar effects, or supporting those who teach such a path.
- **S10**: The chapter adds a Source/polarity frame in which one source or zero has two sides, Expansion and Contraction, and all experience arises in the gap created by their spread and collapse.
- **S11**: The historical frame moves from prehistoric shamanic ceremony, to formal meditation techniques in literate civilizations, to current contemplative-science collaboration, to the possible dawn of a new age.

## Summary
The chapter's main move is from sensory taxonomy to life model. Human experience is no longer only See/Hear/Feel and special categories such as Rest, Flow, and Positive States. It is also action and situation: what a person puts into the world and what the world presents back.

This lets Shinzen state a life-practice theory. Mindfulness develops concentration, clarity, and equanimity; those skills change sensory experience; changed sensory experience can change behavior; changed behavior can influence situations. The chapter packages this through practice cycles, enjoyment, and total happiness.

The fourfold happiness model is the chapter's major durable contribution. Surface happiness concerns pleasure, comfort, mundane answers, and desired situations. Deep happiness adds mindful fulfillment, reduced suffering, empowered not-knowing, self-knowledge, behavior change, and service through teaching or supporting a path.

## Source Frame
This source wants the reader to treat Basic Mindfulness as a complete life system, not only a set of formal meditation objects. The diagrams are meant to show that sensory experience, behavior, situations, skill training, and service belong to one map.

The chapter is strongest as a statement of Shinzen's own applied architecture. Its weaker claims are broad: karma is reduced to a general average feedback principle, total happiness is presented schematically, and behavior change is asserted through sensory leverage without specifying the many mediating conditions that can affect action.

## Weakest Claims
- The "law of karma" formulation is broad and qualified only as average and general.
- The formulas for fulfillment, suffering, and Don't Know are practice heuristics, not measured equations.
- The claim that mindfulness affects behavior and therefore situations is plausible inside Shinzen's model but under-specified as causal psychology.
- The possible "dawn of a new age" from contemplative-science collaboration is aspirational.

## Important Omissions
- The chapter gives no criteria for teacher competence, retreat dosage, or when retreat/check-in might be contraindicated.
- It does not specify how to verify positive behavior change beyond the assertion that mindfulness gives a handle on thoughts and body sensations.
- It does not address trauma, illness, severe mental health risk, power dynamics in teaching a path, or when ordinary social, medical, or therapeutic response should take priority.
- The visual diagrams are only partly recoverable from the Markdown conversion.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Total happiness includes surface happiness, so the chapter does not reject ordinary goals; yet its deep-happiness formulas can sound as if mindful awareness mathematically solves discomfort, confusion, and behavior.
- The chapter values teacher check-ins and retreat, but earlier modularity says one or two options may suffice if they work.
- Teaching a path is framed as deep service, but the chapter does not address authority, consent, competence, or harm risks in teaching.
- The Source and history material widens the frame beyond practice evidence and needs stronger source posture.
