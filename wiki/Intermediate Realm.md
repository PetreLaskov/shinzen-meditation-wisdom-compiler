The intermediate realm is Shinzen's name for the depth zone between ordinary surface consciousness and Source. It can present as blockage, frightening imagery, bliss, energy, entities, psychic-seeming powers, or nothing unusual at all; the durable practice rule is to meet whatever appears with concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Power phenomena are not required, not proof, and not the goal; when they arise, they become useful only if decomposed into sensory events, Flow, and Gone under CCE.
- **Why this matters**: This page gives future agents a Shinzen-specific route for altered phenomena without making a generic occult, clinical, or Buddhist encyclopedia page.
- **Key tensions**: Shinzen is skeptical about objective powers yet treats them as deep-mind contact and possible service tools; he also treats hallucination-like content as workable practice without giving clinical differentials.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's own model of altered practice phenomena; speculative or unsupported as ontology, clinical guidance, ethnography, or power-use ethics.

## Model
Chapter 7 uses a three-layer map. Surface consciousness experiences self and objects as separate things in rigid space and linear time. Source is the deepest layer, named through God, Brahman, Tao, Unborn, Zero, Emptiness, and similar terms, but also described as formless activity rather than a thing. Between them lies the intermediate layer ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S1-S4).

That intermediate layer has two faces. As blockage, it is the subconscious territory of conditionings, fixations, unresolved material, samskaras, and impediments. As power, it is the realm of archetypes, entities, spirits, psychic-seeming abilities, out-of-body impressions, past-life impressions, bright lights, bliss, heat, shaking, and visionary content ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S4, S16).

[[Kriyas & Complete Experiences]] gives one focused shaking-and-movement
case. Shinzen treats spontaneous rocking, shaking, grimacing, and sounds as
recognized meditation phenomena that may relate to sankhara purification, but
he immediately blocks both fear and craving: kriyas are not by themselves a
barrier to progress or a requirement for progress ([[Kriyas & Complete
Experiences]] S1-S4).

[[Kriyas & the Cloud of Unknowing]] adds the status-inflation version of the
same boundary. Shinzen says the Cloud author saw practitioners treating
strange movements and idiosyncratic gestures as signs of spiritual progress
or divine indwelling, and rejected that interpretation. In intermediate-realm
terms, unusual form is not the test; the relation to it is ([[Kriyas & the
Cloud of Unknowing]] S6-S8).

[[Six Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] gives the compact Gate 6A
warning. Shinzen says archetypes, powers, out-of-body experiences, past
lives, and similar phenomena can feel like movement toward formless Source
while actually turning sideways through the realm of powers. The antidote is
to treat even spirits and powers as banal sensory events and apply CCE
([[Six Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] S4-S5).

Shinzen's epistemic stance is deliberately mixed. He says he does not know the objective status of powers, spirits, or entities, and is generally skeptical. Vivid subjective sensory reality is not objective existence. The practical question is not "what does this prove?" but "how can this be used for growth?" ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S5).

The anti-status boundary is equally important. Extraordinary phenomena are not required for extraordinary growth. A practitioner who meets ordinary breath, clothing contact, aches, sights, sounds, and emotions with CCE and whose daily life improves may be traversing the whole path without any dramatic content ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S6).

## Practice Routing
When unusual phenomena arise, Shinzen routes them through familiar primitives: physical body sensation, emotional body sensation, mental image, and internal talk. In his own vision example, he also tracks attention movement across images, emotional body impact, expansion-contraction, fading, and vanishing. This ties the intermediate realm directly to [[Sensory Clarity]], [[Flow]], and [[Gone]] rather than to message interpretation ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S7-S10).

The maturity test is relation, not content. The same attitude applies to ordinary nothing-special practice, frightening phenomena, blissful phenomena, and empowering phenomena: bring concentration, clarity, and equanimity. Craving powers, fearing content, comparing experiences, or worrying that nothing special has happened are all signs that the content is capturing the practice frame ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S8).

The traps talk states the same test in path-direction language: altered
phenomena can become a right-angle trajectory away from Source if they are
explored as special content rather than deconstructed as ordinary sensory
events ([[Six Common Traps on the Path to Enlightenment]] S4-S5).

The chapter names three extreme vectors:

- **Freak-Out**: unusual phenomena frighten the practitioner back toward ordinary surface normalcy.
- **Diversion**: fascinating powers, entities, visions, lights, past lives, or status draw the practitioner horizontally through the realm of power.
- **Plumb Line**: the practitioner keeps moving vertically by meeting all content with CCE until Source is contacted ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S11-S12).

Oblique paths mix power exploration and liberation. Shinzen places classical shamanism on this spectrum and distinguishes having power from seeing deeply into consciousness. In this frame, liberation and power are dimensionally independent: one can have power without liberation, liberation without power, or both ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S13-S14).

## Upaya
The strongest constructive claim is that power phenomena can be upaya. Instead of treating them mainly as messages rising from the depths, Shinzen recommends treating them as conduits that carry clarity and equanimity down into the depths. This turns altered phenomena into platforms for deep [[Insight and Purification]] ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S15).

The routes are specific:

- Heat or nectar-like energy should be tracked as contour, vibration, and undulation, not as content.
- Out-of-body impressions should be used as a detached witness platform for deconstructive observation, not as entertainment about moving outside the body.
- Mind-reading or past-life impressions should be widened toward boundless consciousness rather than fixated on specific minds or incarnations.
- Bliss should train a new relation to pleasure, especially non-grasping around arising and passing.
- Light should be inspected for subtle vibration until it breaks up toward Source.
- Entities, angels, allies, ancestors, or avatars should mostly bring clarity and equanimity to the depths rather than deliver messages from them ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S16-S17).

## Boundaries
The intermediate realm is not a proof domain. Powers, entities, spirits, past lives, mind-reading, and out-of-body travel remain subjectively vivid possibilities in Shinzen's frame, not established objective facts.

It is also not a required stage. If future reasoning treats lack of visions, bliss, heat, light, or powers as failure, it contradicts chapter 7's explicit ordinary-path boundary.

The clinical boundary is open. Chapter 7 says illness, trauma, sleep deprivation, drugs, fasting, and chance can contribute to descent into this realm, and it describes waking visions that Shinzen interpreted as nonpsychotic because he functioned well. That is not enough to distinguish meditation phenomena from psychosis, mania, trauma activation, dissociation, neurological issues, substance effects, sleep deprivation, or medical crisis. Route risky cases through [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].

The kriya case sharpens that boundary because movement can be visible,
socially contagious, injurious, frightening, or neurologically relevant. The
available source gives reassurance and practice routing, not criteria for
distinguishing purification-related movement from clinical or medical
conditions ([[Kriyas & Complete Experiences]] S1-S4, S9).

The service boundary is also open. Shinzen says powers may be used to help others after Source contact, but the chapter gives no consent norms, behavioral verification, ethics, community feedback, teacher accountability, or safeguards against grandiosity and charisma.

Chapter 7's direct-Source-contact language should be held with [[Source And Polarities]]. Chapter 10 later sharpens the claim: Source or Zero is not directly experienced as an object at the moment of cancellation; what appears afterward is a conditioned afterglow. Future agents should preserve both the chapter 7 path map and the chapter 10 afterglow boundary.

## Implications
For practice routing, altered phenomena should trigger four questions:

- Is this being met as sensory image, talk, body sensation, Flow, or Gone, or is the content taking over?
- Are concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity increasing, or are fear, craving, status, grandiosity, or confusion increasing?
- Is the practitioner moving through the phenomena toward Source, horizontally into fascination, or back toward avoidance?
- Are clinical, relational, medical, sleep, substance, consent, or teacher-power issues active enough to require support outside meditation?

These checks keep the realm of power usable without letting it become a shortcut around safety or ordinary life.
