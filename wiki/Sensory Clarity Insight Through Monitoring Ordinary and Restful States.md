This short YouTube teaching bridges Shinzen's ordinary/restful sensory taxonomy with no-self practice. Its central move is to make self-arising observable as changing activation in Feel, Image, and Talk, while also making restful states objects for clarity rather than merely pleasant calm.

## Teaching Register
Explanatory practice instruction. Shinzen is not giving a full guided sit; he is showing how a practitioner can use the Basic States taxonomy as an experiment in self-arising, no-self, and Rest practice.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk adds a concrete monitoring experiment to the prior no-self material. Rather than only resolving the self into Feel/Image/Talk once it is already present, the practitioner watches how different activation levels of Feel/Image/Talk correlate with the sense of a perceiving subject and how inactivity in those systems can reveal no-self.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Feel, Image, and Talk can react to Touch, Sight, and Sound; when all three activate strongly in response to an external stimulus, a strong sense of an "I" perceiving an "it" usually appears.
- **S2**: If Touch, Sight, or Sound occurs without a Feel/Image/Talk reaction, there is little or no sense of a perceiving self, and repeated monitoring can produce insight into the mechanism of perceiving I-am-ness.
- **S3**: A large Feel/Image/Talk storm does not have to become a self if clarity keeps the components untangled and equanimity allows them to arise and pass without suppression or fixation.
- **S4**: Feel/Image/Talk also has a proactive mode: when not reacting to the environment, it spins memory, plan, fantasy, problem solving, worry, and confusion.
- **S5**: When Feel/Image/Talk is neither reactive nor proactive and becomes inactive, the practitioner may notice peace, blank, and quiet; noticing that moment can yield no-self insight.
- **S6**: Restful states include physical relaxation, emotional peace, blank mental screen, inner quiet, soft-focus or defocused sight, and silence or defocused hearing.
- **S7**: Focus on Rest can release stress and recharge the system, but if "nothing much is happening," recognizing six simultaneous restful experiences brings clarity into rest and prevents years of pleasant mellowing without insight and purification.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: Shinzen turns no-self into a repeatable contrast experiment: compare outer contact with strong inner reaction against outer contact without inner reaction.
- **S3**: The talk prevents a suppression reading of no-self. The issue is not whether Feel/Image/Talk arises, but whether it is unconscious, coagulated, or fixated.
- **S5**: No-self can be noticed through a fleeting inactive phase of the inner system, not only through philosophical analysis, Flow, or dramatic cessation.
- **S6-S7**: Rest is reclassified from "nothing is happening" into six sensory events, preserving the clarity component inside tranquility practice.

## Practice Handles
- When an outer sight, sound, or touch appears, monitor whether Feel, Image, and Talk react; compare high-reaction moments with low-reaction moments.
- During a strong emotional/cognitive storm, separate Feel, Image, and Talk and add equanimity instead of trying to stop the storm.
- When memory, planning, fantasy, problem solving, worry, or confusion spins without an outer trigger, treat it as proactive Feel/Image/Talk rather than as a failure to meditate.
- When practice seems blank or uneventful, check for six Rest events: physical relaxation, emotional peace, blank mental screen, inner quiet, visual defocus/light, and auditory silence or defocus.
- Use Focus on Ordinary when activity is dominant and Focus on Rest when rest is available, without making either one the only valid meditation.

## Summary
Shinzen begins with ordinary sensory activity: Touch, Sight, Sound, Feel, Image, and Talk. He says Feel/Image/Talk can react to outer contact, and that the sense of being a perceiver varies with how strongly the inner system activates. Strong Feel/Image/Talk reaction to an external stimulus typically generates a strong I-it structure; little or no inner reaction can leave little or no felt perceiver.

The key no-self move is not to eliminate inner activation. Shinzen says even a huge storm of emotional body sensation, visual association, and judgmental chatter does not have to become a self if clarity keeps the components untangled and equanimity prevents suppression and fixation. The self-thing forms when the reactions are unconscious, coagulated, or fixated.

The talk then contrasts ordinary activity with Rest. Feel/Image/Talk can be proactive, spinning memory, plan, fantasy, problem solving, worry, and confusion; it can also become inactive, leaving peace, blank, and quiet. More broadly, each ordinary domain has a restful counterpart: body has physical relaxation and emotional peace, mind has blank and quiet, sight has light or soft focus, and hearing has silence or auditory defocus. Shinzen values Rest for stress release, but warns that rest without clarity can become a pleasant plateau; when nothing much is happening, six restful experiences may be happening at once.

## Weakest Claims
- The neuroscience/default-attentional-system aside is too brief to support scientific claims beyond Shinzen's analogy to proactive inner spinning.
- The claim that noticing fleeting inner-system inactivity yields no-self insight is a practice claim; the transcript does not provide readiness criteria or differential diagnosis for dissociation, shutdown, sleepiness, or depersonalization.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not specify dosage, pacing, label options, or how long to monitor ordinary and restful states.
- It does not give criteria for when high Feel/Image/Talk storms need support, resourcing, or a different practice.
- It does not distinguish restful clarity from sleepiness, freeze, depressive blankness, dissociation, or avoidance.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk values moments with little or no self, but also says strong Feel/Image/Talk activation can be present without becoming self when clarity and equanimity are high. No-self therefore cannot be reduced to inner-system inactivity alone.
- Rest is both an important meditative component and a possible "good place" trap when clarity and growth are missing.
- The Basic States taxonomy makes clean contrasts, while the practice itself often involves mixed ordinary and restful states.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "No self" should not be interpreted as suppressing Feel/Image/Talk reactions or trying to keep the inner system inactive.
- Restful blankness, quiet, and emotional peace should not be treated as proof of insight, completion, or psychological resolution.
- The default-attentional-system reference should not be cited as neuroscience validation of Shinzen's entire sensory grid.
