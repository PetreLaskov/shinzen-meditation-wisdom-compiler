This short Shinzen Young talk names six common ways practitioners get caught in good places: maps, fundamentalist certainty, tranquility, power phenomena, enlightenment, and observer identity. Its durable contribution is a diagnostic overlay: progress can stall not only through obvious failure, but through useful structures that become substitutes for continued practice.

## Teaching Register
Corrective advanced-path warning. Shinzen uses short diagnostic labels and antidotes rather than a formal stage map, with several traps framed as "good places" that can still limit further practice.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk sharpens Gate 6A by making enlightenment safety and practice routing dynamic. The risk is not only fantasy about enlightenment; it is fixation on maps, closed mythic certainty, pleasant calm, altered powers, realization status, or subtle observer identity.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The map trap occurs when so much time and energy go into finding the right map, model, teacher, or situation that little remains for actual practice.
- **S2**: The fundamentalism trap occurs when a mythological structure produces real subjective happiness and objective behavior improvements, but then closes intellectual horizons and blocks the next stage of positive change.
- **S3**: The tranquility trap occurs when calm concentration becomes refreshing and pleasant year after year, but does not develop further; the antidote is to clarify tranquil states by distinguishing flavors of restfulness.
- **S4**: The realms-of-power trap occurs when archetypes, special powers, out-of-body experiences, past lives, or similar phenomena feel like deeper movement toward Source but actually become a sideways diversion.
- **S5**: The antidote to power diversion is to deconstruct spirits or powers as sensory events and apply concentration, clarity, and equanimity to them as with any other event.
- **S6**: The enlightenment trap occurs when genuine, permanent classical enlightenment or no-self makes someone think they know more than they do, perhaps even teaching from that place.
- **S7**: The antidote to enlightenment inflation is courageous feedback from other teachers, teachers vastly senior to oneself, and the environment; Shinzen cites "today's enlightenment is tomorrow's mistake" as a continual-forward-movement rule.
- **S8**: The observer trap is a characteristic mindfulness weakness: subtle image, talk, and emotional body sensations can create the sense of someone meditating or directing the process.
- **S9**: The antidote to the observer trap is that practice becomes second nature; the habit of concentration, clarity, and equanimity eventually enfolds and dissolves the sensory arisings that create the meditator.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S3**: Shinzen treats maps, beliefs, and tranquility as potentially useful but not final. This prevents future agents from equating a better framework, better ideology, or pleasant calm with complete path movement.
- **S4-S5**: The talk gives a compact version of the intermediate-realm plumb-line rule: bizarre or powerful content should become ordinary sensory practice material.
- **S6-S7**: Genuine realization does not cancel developmental incompleteness. Feedback from senior teachers and the environment becomes part of the enlightenment antidote, not a mere social nicety.
- **S8-S9**: The observer problem is not solved by making a stronger watcher. It is solved when CCE momentum becomes automatic enough to include the sensory events constructing the watcher.

## Practice Handles
If a practitioner is spending most of their effort on maps, teachers, models, or ideal conditions, return to actual practice time and test whether the map is serving CCE.

If a belief structure has produced real happiness and behavior gains, preserve gratitude for the gain while checking whether inquiry, learning, and further change have closed down.

If tranquility is stable but stagnant, add sensory clarity inside rest: distinguish visual, auditory, somatic, mental, and emotional flavors of restfulness rather than merely enjoying calm.

If powers, archetypes, or unusual experiences appear, treat them as banal sensory events. Apply concentration, clarity, and equanimity rather than status, fear, or fascination.

If no-self or enlightenment is real but inflating authority, seek senior feedback, peer feedback, environmental feedback, and continued correction.

If mindfulness is strengthening an observing meditator, keep practicing until CCE includes subtle mental images, internal talk, and body emotion that make the meditator seem to stand outside the process.

## Summary
Shinzen first warns that many people lose practice momentum by overinvesting in the right map, model, teacher, or situation. He then names a subtler good-place trap: a mythological system can genuinely improve happiness and behavior, yet close intellectual horizons and prevent later growth.

He next turns to meditative successes. Tranquil concentration can become pleasant, refreshing, and stagnant unless sensory clarity enters the calm. Power phenomena can seem like deepening toward Source while actually moving sideways through archetypes, powers, out-of-body experiences, or past-life material; the antidote is to deconstruct them as ordinary sensory events under CCE.

The final traps concern realization and observer identity. Shinzen says someone may have real, permanent classical enlightenment and still overestimate what they know or teach prematurely. The antidote is ongoing feedback from senior teachers and the environment. In the mindfulness-specific observer trap, subtle image, talk, and emotional body sensations create the sense of someone meditating; with enough practice, the habit of mindfulness itself "meditates you" and enfolds those self-making arisings.

## Source Frame
The source is a short oral trap taxonomy, not a complete map of path development. Shinzen is trying to keep valuable partial successes from hardening into substitutes for continued practice, feedback, and sensory deconstruction.

The talk uses strong phrases such as "permanent classical enlightenment" and "formless Source" inside Shinzen's teaching frame. Those should be preserved as source language while routed through existing no-self, Source, safety, and accountability boundaries.

## Weakest Claims
- The phrase "permanent classical enlightenment" is not defined in this short talk and should not be treated as a complete attainment taxonomy.
- The fundamentalism trap compresses religious, mythological, psychological, and social processes into one warning without giving criteria for when a structure has become closed.
- The observer-trap solution is mechanistically useful inside Shinzen's CCE model, but the transcript does not distinguish healthy automaticity from dissociation, depersonalization, or passive spacing out.

## Important Omissions
- No detailed stop rules for when tranquility, powers, or observer practice have become clinically concerning.
- No ethics policy or teacher-qualification standard beyond feedback from senior teachers and environment.
- No criteria for choosing among maps or teachers besides avoiding overinvestment and remaining practice-oriented.
- No account of how a practitioner recognizes that mindfulness has become automatic enough to include the meditator-making process.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says classical enlightenment can be real and permanent, while also saying the person can still think they know more than they do and need further correction. This supports a component model rather than a single all-completing attainment.
- Fundamentalist structures can produce real happiness and behavioral gains while later blocking growth. The source therefore rejects both cynical dismissal and uncritical loyalty.
- Observer stance is useful enough to be characteristic of mindfulness practice, but it can also become a trap unless the practice eventually enfolds the observer.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Fundamentalism trap" can become contempt for religious forms if separated from Shinzen's admission that mythic structures can produce real positive change.
- "Power realms" can become blanket dismissal of unusual experience if separated from the instruction to deconstruct the content through CCE.
- "Permanent classical enlightenment" can inflate status if separated from feedback, humility, and behavior work.
- "Mindfulness meditates you" can be confused with passivity, dissociation, or lack of responsibility if separated from active CCE training and safety checks.
