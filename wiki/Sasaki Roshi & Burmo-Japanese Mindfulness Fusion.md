## Summary
Shinzen begins by saying Sasaki Roshi reframed early Buddhist impermanence as Expansion and Contraction. He links that to the Visuddhimagga's passing-as-arising stage: after ordinary rising and passing becomes clear, a subtler perception sees passing already present as arising occurs. He doubts Sasaki read the Visuddhimagga, but says Sasaki reached the same territory through decades of Rinzai Zen practice and formulated it differently.

The key contrast is the observer. Shinzen says rising-passing language can imply a meditator over here observing sensory events over there. Expansion-Contraction is three-dimensional and all-encompassing: the observer is included in the arising and passing. When one gives oneself totally to simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, the forces can cancel into Zero or Source and then repolarize, giving rise to self/world and Feel/Image/Talk plus touch/sight/sound.

The final teaching-method move distinguishes Sasaki and Shinzen. Sasaki has students experience the paradigm through koan practice. Shinzen says he takes Sasaki's model and mounts it in Burmese vipassana noting, breaking ordinary experience into progressively finer manageable steps so more people can approach the advanced Expansion-Contraction paradigm. He calls this a Burmo-Japanese fusion created by a Jewish science geek.

## Source Frame

Its weakest evidence is historical and comparative: it does not prove what Sasaki read, what the Visuddhimagga finally means, or whether Source/Zero is a correct model of consciousness.
