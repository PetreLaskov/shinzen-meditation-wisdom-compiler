Total Happiness Aim Structure owns the maps that define Shinzen's practice
goal. It keeps ordinary conditions, deep practice, personal happiness, and
service to others in view at the same time.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen cuts the same aim several ways: appreciate/
  transcend/improve self and world, four self/other and condition-dependent/
  independent quadrants, retreat training quadrants such as appreciate/
  transcend/express/nurture, and five applications of mindfulness.
- **Why this matters**: The maps prevent practice from shrinking into either
  private calm or ordinary self-improvement alone.
- **Key tensions**: The taxonomies are teaching heuristics, not empirical
  happiness equations, and they can be misused if surface needs or ordinary
  action are dismissed.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's stated aim structure; probable as a
  compiled map of his system.

## Three Jobs

The SHF introduction says mindfulness supports three dimensions: appreciate
self and world, transcend self and world, and improve self and world
([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S6).
This is the simplest routing version. It means ordinary life appreciation,
liberating practice, and practical improvement are not rivals inside the
system.

[[Shinzen Young's Welcome to New Viewers]] gives the beginner-facing version:
a deep spiritual path should let a person see beyond or get over self/world
and also improve self/world; their mutual reinforcement is spiritual
maturity ([[Shinzen Young's Welcome to New Viewers]] S2).

[[Appreciate Transcend Improve Our Three Human Jobs]] gives the direct oral
map behind the same taxonomy. Appreciation means sensorially appreciating
outer form and inner reactive/proactive form with CCE rather than suppressing
thought and emotion; transcendence means contact with Source/Zero and the
two movements of Expansion and Contraction; improvement means the ongoing
ordinary-life work of affect, behavior, cognition, support, material service,
and Dharma service ([[Appreciate Transcend Improve Our Three Human Jobs]]
S1-S7).

## Four Quadrants

Basic Mindfulness chapter 11 defines four quadrants: surface happiness for
oneself, deep happiness for oneself, helping others gain surface happiness,
and helping others gain deep happiness ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 -
Life]] S7).

Surface happiness includes ordinary pleasure, comfort, useful answers, and
improved situations. Deep happiness includes fulfillment, reduced suffering,
self-knowledge, empowering Don't Know, and behavior change. The others-side
quadrants include ordinary help and teaching or supporting a path that gives
access to deep happiness ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S7-S9).

[[Chart of Human Happiness]] gives an oral board-chart version of the same
surface/deep split. It adds a useful guidance frame: situations are wanted
because they produce sensory rewards, behavior influences situations on
average, and mindfulness gives leverage over sensory drivers of behavior,
but behavior change may still require therapy, twelve-step, or other
accountability structures ([[Chart of Human Happiness]] S2-S5).

[[Changing the Course of Human History And Happiness]] gives the explicit
periodic-table version. Shinzen names five categories, relief, fulfillment,
wisdom, mastery or skillful action, and service, and says depth means "less
obvious" rather than simply more intense or automatically more important.
He also adds intensity and scope dimensions: happiness can be broad across
types, deep across levels, high in magnitude, and extended from individuals
to groups, humanity, and sentient beings ([[Changing the Course of Human
History And Happiness]] S1-S3).

[[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]] makes the self/other split
explicit in the dedicated oral series. A person with ordinary and
extraordinary happiness for themselves is still not totally happy unless
they contribute to others' happiness ([[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and
Others]] S6-S8).

## Retreat Training Quadrants

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] and [[Four Ways
Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] use "four quadrants" or "four ways"
differently from the self/other and surface/deep happiness map. In the
retreat context, the training themes are appreciate, transcend or release,
express, and nurture: appreciate sensory experience as it is, use experience
to point toward Source-facing transcendence or release, let movement express
spontaneity, and cultivate positive body emotion ([[Shinzen April Daylong
Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] S2, S6, S9-S11; [[Four Ways Forward -
June Shinzen Retreat]] S1, S13).

This is best treated as a practice-organization variant, not a replacement
for the Total Happiness quadrant map. Its value is that one daylong can keep
ordinary sensory appreciation, formless/transcendent interpretation, action
training, and positive reconstruction in the same container, while each
branch still trains CCE toward the five life goals ([[Four Ways Forward -
June Shinzen Retreat]] S13).

## Five Applications

The SHF introduction and [[Why Meditate]] list five applications: reduce
suffering, elevate fulfillment, understand oneself, make positive behavior
changes, and cultivate or discover love and service ([[See Hear Feel
Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S6; [[Why Meditate]]
S1-S5).

The list matters because it is outcome-shaped. Practice is not complete just
because a session felt deep; it should make suffering, fulfillment,
self-knowledge, behavior, and service more workable over time.

## Ordinary And Extraordinary Happiness

[[Total Happiness - 2 of 5 - Ordinary and Extraordinary]] distinguishes
ordinary and extraordinary happiness. Ordinary body happiness seeks pleasant
physical and emotional sensation and avoids unpleasant sensation. Ordinary
mind happiness seeks answers and avoids Don't Know. Extraordinary happiness
shifts the body side toward fulfillment and freedom from suffering and the
mind side toward the deepest self/source question ([[Total Happiness - 2 of 5
- Ordinary and Extraordinary]] S1-S6).

This contrast does not reject ordinary happiness. It prevents ordinary
conditions from being the only basis for well-being.

## Boundaries

The maps should not be used as a value ranking where deep happiness makes
surface happiness expendable. Surface happiness remains a real quadrant.

They also should not be used as a universal empirical model. The taxonomies
are Shinzen's teaching architecture for practice routing and aim-setting.

When the live question involves illness, birth, sleep, sexuality, teacher
pressure, behavior mismatch, or clinical risk, route through [[Complete
Experience Safety Boundary]] and [[Guidance Scope and Accountability
Boundary]] before applying the aim structure.
