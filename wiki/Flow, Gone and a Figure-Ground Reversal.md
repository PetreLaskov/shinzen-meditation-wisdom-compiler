This short Shinzen YouTube talk is an advanced-map instruction on Focus on Flow, Gone, and their combination. Its central contribution is not a new beginner technique but a figure-ground reversal criterion: Flow and Gone can become so dominant that ordinary sensory and self-world distinctions become secondary.

## Teaching Register
Advanced map and practice instruction. Shinzen is explaining what can happen when a practitioner works with Flow, Gone, or both after earlier sensory discriminations have already been developed.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk gives a compact oral bridge between ordinary Focus on Flow, Just Note Gone, and the deeper Source/polarity language. It clarifies that losing specificity can be healthy when it arises from strong practice: sensory modality, subjective/objective, ordinary/restful, size, valence, and complexity distinctions may drop out because Flow or Gone has become the operative ground.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: When noting Flow matures, the practitioner may stop knowing or caring whether the Flow is somatic, visual, auditory, subjective, objective, ordinary, or restful; the discriminations used by other techniques may break down.
- **S2**: Shinzen frames this as a dialectic: practice first makes discriminations in a healthy way, then can destroy those discriminations in a healthy way.
- **S3**: Flow is a great leveler that destroys distinctions, but Gone is an even stronger leveler because it destroys the time and space needed for distinctions.
- **S4**: In advanced Gone practice, the practitioner may no longer know what is disappearing, whether it was large or small, pleasant or unpleasant, simple or complex; vanishing itself dominates.
- **S5**: When Flow and Gone combine as a repeated Flow-Gone rhythm, Shinzen says this can lead to an experience of a matrix of nothingness birthing self and seeing moment by moment.
- **S6**: In that Source-side image, scintillating mist may coagulate into self and world or remain uncoagulated as pure spirit, while effortless spreading and pulling-in continue like an invisible doing.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The key move is developmental: loss of specificity is not recommended as vagueness at the start, but can be desirable after healthy discrimination has done its work.
- **S3-S4**: Flow and Gone are ranked as levelers, preserving their difference while showing why both can dissolve the grid-like distinctions that earlier practice depends on.
- **S5-S6**: The figure-ground reversal gives the advanced Source interpretation: self and world become figures arising within Flow-Gone activity rather than the stable ground from which Flow and Gone are observed.

## Practice Handles
- If Flow becomes globally dominant and modality labels fall away, the instruction is to continue with Flow rather than force See/Hear/Feel specificity back into place.
- If Gone becomes dominant and the vanished object cannot be identified, continue with the vanishing rather than treating the lack of object detail as a failure.
- If Flow and Gone alternate as the whole practice field, use that rhythm as an optional Focus on Flow/Gone object; do not try to manufacture the figure-ground reversal.

## Summary
Shinzen says Focus on Flow may begin with clear knowledge of where the Flow is and what is flowing. With practice, however, descriptors can break down: somatic, visual, auditory, subjective, objective, ordinary, and restful distinctions may no longer matter. He calls this desirable when it happens naturally because the practice dialectic first makes discriminations and then can destroy them.

He then ranks Flow and Gone as levelers. Flow destroys distinctions; Gone destroys time and space, without which distinctions cannot be maintained. Advanced Gone practice may become dominated by vanishing itself rather than by details about the disappearing object.

When Flow and Gone combine, Shinzen says the practitioner may enter a figure-ground reversal: a matrix of nothingness births self and seeing moment by moment. The same scintillating mist may coagulate into self/world or remain uncoagulated as pure spirit, while effortless spreading and pulling-in continue as an invisible doing.

## Source Frame
The talk assumes prior familiarity with Shinzen's sensory distinctions and advanced impermanence vocabulary. It is trying to make a late-stage practice shift intelligible: distinction loss is not confusion when it emerges from sufficiently strong Flow/Gone contact. The Source-side "matrix of nothingness" and "pure spirit" language is Shinzen's contemplative interpretation of that practice field, not independent metaphysical evidence.

## Important Omissions

- No safety or support criteria are supplied for practitioners who become disoriented when self/world distinctions weaken.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Earlier techniques depend on discriminating See, Hear, Feel, In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Gone; this talk says mature Flow/Gone may destroy those discriminations. The tension is developmental rather than contradictory if discrimination is first established and then naturally leveled.
- The Source-side language can sound metaphysical, while the practice instruction remains phenomenological and optional.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Losing distinctions" can be misread as permission for vagueness, spacing out, or bypassing sensory clarity.
- "Matrix of nothingness" and "pure spirit" can be inflated into metaphysical certainty or attainment status.
- The figure-ground reversal can become a target to chase rather than an effect to recognize if Flow/Gone rhythm naturally dominates.
