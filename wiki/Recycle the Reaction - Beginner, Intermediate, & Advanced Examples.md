This short edited Shinzen YouTube talk defines "recycle the reaction" and gives beginner, intermediate, and advanced examples. Its durable value is that it turns practice-produced reactions into workable sensory objects without treating deeper states, fear, alienation, or void flatness as final diagnoses.

## Teaching Register
Practice instruction and troubleshooting. Shinzen speaks in compact examples rather than live coaching: he names a recurring practice effect, identifies the reaction as sensory material, and returns the practitioner to the same attentional skill set.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk is the most direct source-page anchor for [[Recycle The Reaction]]. Earlier pages already used the phrase, but this source supplies a graded ladder: beginner ordinary-state rebound, intermediate fear and relational alienation, and advanced dark-night or void flatness.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Mindfulness applies concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity to sensory experience; the resulting pleasant or unpleasant change is a "reaction," and one available move is to make that reaction the next object of the same practice.
- **S2**: Beginner "20-minute glass ceiling" rebound into ordinary or scattered consciousness should not necessarily be treated as failure or a need to regain depth; the practitioner can focus on the Feel/Image/Talk activity of the rebound.
- **S3**: A common intermediate reaction to spontaneously dropping into a deeper or no-self-like state is fear; Shinzen routes that fear, usually in Feel space, into allowing and direct practice until the system learns the experience is not objectively dangerous.
- **S4**: Aversion to ordinary people after practice is an intermediate reaction, not the endpoint of the path; Shinzen says it should be worked through as Feel/Image/Talk until practice matures toward joy in being with ordinary people and subtly giving through one's presence.
- **S5**: Advanced dark-night or pit-of-the-void flatness can be treated as another reaction to practice: the practitioner watches the freak-out or bleakness around emptiness until the "spring and bounce and vitality" of the void can manifest.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The definition prevents reaction recycling from becoming vague reassurance; it is the specific recursive move of applying CCE to the reaction layer.
- **S2**: The beginner example protects ordinary consciousness from being treated as a failed sit. The reaction itself can be practice material even when the desired depth is gone.
- **S3**: The no-self fear example gives a sensory route before metaphysical interpretation: locate the fear, often as emotional body Feel, and allow it under technique.
- **S4**: The relational example keeps practice accountable to human re-entry. Spiritual alienation from family, friends, or "ordinary people" is itself a reaction to practice, not proof of superiority.
- **S5**: The advanced example links dark-night/void flatness to reaction recycling while preserving the need for safety boundaries because the source does not distinguish practice-only bleakness from clinical depression, DPDR, dissociation, or dysfunction.

## Practice Handles
- When a technique produces a desirable state, apply the technique to craving, excitement, or the wish to keep the state.
- When a technique produces a horrible or frightening state, identify the reaction's sensory composition before deciding whether to continue, switch, stop, or seek support.
- When practice rebounds into ordinary consciousness, work with the current Feel/Image/Talk instead of struggling to recover the earlier depth.
- When a deep state triggers fear, locate the fear in emotional body space and let it happen under CCE if workable.
- When practice creates social alienation or contempt, treat that aversion as Feel/Image/Talk and test maturation by renewed ordinary relational ease.
- When emptiness seems bleak or dehumanizing, recycle the reaction to the bleakness, while routing severe or disabling cases through altered-phenomena and clinical-neighbor safety pages.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by defining mindfulness as concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity brought to sensory experience. Practice can change the experience, and that change can produce pleasant or unpleasant reactions. The "recycle the reaction" axiom says the reaction can itself become the object of the technique that caused it.

He then gives examples across levels. Beginners may drop into a state and then pop back into ordinary scattered consciousness after about twenty minutes; instead of trying to force the previous depth back, they can practice with the Feel/Image/Talk of the rebound. Intermediate practitioners may touch a deeper or no-self-like state and become terrified; Shinzen advises letting the fear happen and applying the technique to the emotional-body Feel until the deeper system learns there is no objective danger. Another intermediate reaction is alienation from family, friends, or non-spiritual people; he frames that as contrary to the path's endpoint and says the aversion should be worked through as Feel/Image/Talk.

For advanced practitioners, Shinzen names the dark night, pit of the void, and flatness where suffering drama has quieted but humanity, edge, or fire seems absent. He treats the bleakness as a reaction to biting into emptiness and says it can be worked through so the vitality of the void appears.

## Source Frame
The talk is trying to normalize practice-produced reactions and give a portable troubleshooting rule. It is strongest as Shinzen's own teaching on what a practitioner can do next when meditation changes experience. It is weaker as a safety protocol: "recycle" does not by itself specify readiness, dosage, teacher competence, grounding, clinical screening, or when ordinary supports override technique.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that the depths of one's being learn there is "nothing objectively dangerous" in a no-self-like experience is practice-useful but under-specified; it does not distinguish healthy fear discharge from panic, trauma activation, dissociation, DPDR, mania/psychosis risk, or unsafe retreat effects.
- The advanced void example assumes eventual vitality after flatness but gives no timeframe, severity criteria, or differential from depression, burnout, derealization, depersonalization, or loss of function.
- The relational example uses compressed language about "ordinary" or "screwed-up" people; the durable claim is about alienation as a reaction, not a license for contemptuous framing.

## Important Omissions
- No criteria for when to stop, switch method, ground, contact a teacher, seek therapy, seek medical help, or reduce practice intensity.
- No detail on whether the examples occur in daily practice, retreat, guided context, spontaneous awakening, or unsupported practice.
- No method-specific instructions beyond applying one's current technique to the reaction.
- No behavior or relationship repair criteria for social alienation after practice.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says to recycle difficult reactions, but the wiki's safety pages preserve the counterweight: some reactions are not best handled by more meditation alone.
- Shinzen frames aversion to ordinary people as intermediate and contrary to the practice endpoint, while other sources celebrate solitude, retreat, and altered states; future routing must distinguish temporary retreat simplification from relational contempt or withdrawal.
- The void-flatness example overlaps with [[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]], [[DPDR and the Pit of the Void]], and [[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]], where similar reports may need positive reconstruction, clinical assessment, or teacher support.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Recycle the reaction" can become forced exposure, state-chasing, or refusal to change method.
- "Nothing objectively dangerous" can become a way to dismiss clinical fear, trauma activation, dissociation, or ordinary risk.
- "Joy in being with ordinary people" can become performative friendliness unless behavior, consent, repair, and humility remain intact.
- "Vitality of the void" can become pressure to interpret bleakness as enlightenment rather than checking depression, DPDR, burnout, or support needs.
