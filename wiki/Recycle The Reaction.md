Recycle the reaction is Shinzen's fourth mindfulness axiom in the Gate 1 assumptions talk. If applying concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity produces pleasant, unpleasant, or strange changes, the reaction to those changes becomes the next sensory material for practice.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Reaction recycling extends CCE one layer outward: the practitioner practices not only with the original object but also with craving, aversion, fear, doubt, fascination, or freshly stirred life activation.
- **Why this matters**: It prevents practice from becoming state addiction, state aversion, or freak-out when sensory experience changes under CCE.
- **Key tensions**: The handle is powerful but not sufficient; ordinary-state rebound, no-self fear, relational alienation, void flatness, or post-retreat aftershock may also require method change, grounding, stopping, competent guidance, therapy, medical care, or other outside support.
- **Source posture**: Established as Shinzen's teaching phrase and now directly supported by a dedicated source talk with beginner, intermediate, and advanced examples.

## Model
[[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] gives the clean formulation. The first three axioms are concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity. The fourth says that when applying those skills changes experience, the practitioner should recycle the reaction, whether the change is pleasant and addictive, unpleasant and suffering-producing, or strange and freak-out-producing ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S5).

This makes reaction recycling recursive without being abstract. The reaction is not solved mainly by interpretation. It is treated as new sensory experience: body feeling, mental image, mental talk, intensity, location, change, rest, Flow, or Gone depending on the active technique and range.

SHF Unit 05 gives a narrower version inside technique doubts. Worries about imagination, spontaneity, doing it right, suppression, or who observes should be recycled as inner See/Hear/Feel activation rather than solved abstractly during the practice period ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Four Okays and Required vs Allowed]] S8). That is reaction recycling at the level of practice self-monitoring.

[[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] gives a
retreat-stream valence case. When pleasure produces craving or discomfort
produces aversion, Shinzen routes the reaction as Image, Talk, and Feel if it
is distinct from the original pleasant or unpleasant event. The same Q&A gives
a cross-valence test: pleasure practice should improve the relationship to
pain, and pain practice should improve appreciation of pleasure
([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] S12).

[[Forcing Spoken Labels]] gives a concrete Gate 1 case. The practice effect is forced spoken labeling; the reaction may be impatience, agitated body sensation, discomfort, or shame about being sent back to a basic-looking technique. Shinzen's move is to note that reaction as Feel when workable, because the previously invisible scatter has become observable sensory material ([[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S1-S4, S7-S8).

The intermediate-realm chapter gives a wider and riskier version. Shinzen describes altered, visionary, blissful, frightening, or empowering phenomena as material to meet with CCE rather than fear, craving, comparison, status-seeking, or message fixation ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S6-S12). There the reaction may be more consequential than the phenomenon itself.

The Gone chapter gives the dark-night version. When Goneness, emptiness, or no-self produces fear, loss, confusion, disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, or hopelessness, Shinzen recommends deconstructing the negative reaction into emotional body sensation, mental image, and mental talk, adding positive reconstruction, and using intensive teacher/practitioner support in extreme cases ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S14).

[[What to Expect and Do After a Mindfulness Retreat]] gives the post-retreat
version. Aftershock means hypersensitivity, vulnerability, disorientation,
and reactive Feel/Image/Talk after retreat; Shinzen says the practitioner
only needs to remember why it is happening and what to do about it. The
"what to do" is reaction recycling: apply the techniques to the reactions
that practice has caused ([[What to Expect and Do After a Mindfulness
Retreat]] S3-S6).

[[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]] gives
the most explicit graded source for this page. At the beginner level, the
reaction may be popping out of a deeper state into ordinary or scattered
consciousness; Shinzen says the practitioner can practice with the
Feel/Image/Talk of that rebound instead of struggling to regain the prior
state ([[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]]
S2). At the intermediate level, the reaction may be fear after a deeper or
no-self-like state, usually presenting in emotional body Feel, or aversion to
ordinary family and friends after practice; both are recycled into CCE rather
than treated as endpoints ([[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate,
& Advanced Examples]] S3-S4). At the advanced level, flatness, loss of
humanity, or bleakness around the void is also framed as a reaction to be
worked through, while the safety differential remains outside this short
talk ([[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced Examples]]
S5).

[[From Suffering to Bliss]] adds a live micro-recycling case inside emotional
catharsis. Once local-global fear and sadness Flow, joy, heat, commentary,
and renewed fear can arise as reactions to the release itself. Shinzen's move
is not to analyze those reactions as stories first, but to let heat be heat,
let joy be present, dissipate fear in the fear if heat produces more fear,
and return from commentary into body Flow ([[From Suffering to Bliss]]
S7-S10).

[[Retroactive Meditation]] is adjacent rather than a replacement for the
named fourth axiom. There the reaction layer is not caused by a meditative
state but by prior intellectual work or ordinary activity. When the activity
stops, the pull of what just happened and what needs to happen next erupts as
present See/Hear/Feel material; Shinzen's route is to start practice
immediately and complete that activation now ([[Retroactive Meditation]]
S1-S6).

## Boundaries
Reaction recycling is not a universal instruction to continue intensity. The assumptions talk itself adds a fifth axiom: keep contact information for a competent guide who can remind the practitioner of the first four axioms ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S6). That guide boundary means forgetting, confusion, or destabilization is not merely personal failure.

The dedicated reaction-recycling talk sharpens both the range and the limit.
It uses no-self fear, relational alienation, and void flatness as examples of
workable reactions, but it does not define when fear, social withdrawal,
loss of humanity, or bleakness should route to teacher support, positive
reconstruction, clinical assessment, grounding, or reduced practice
intensity ([[Recycle the Reaction - Beginner, Intermediate, & Advanced
Examples]] S3-S5).

Forced spoken labels make that boundary practical. The reaction may be exactly the object to recycle, but hard work, discomfort, shame, panic, social exposure, or teacher pressure can also be signs to simplify, stop, or seek support rather than keep forcing ([[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S5-S8).

The handle also does not settle clinical or medical differentials. Fear, pain, confusion, unusual perceptions, dissociation, panic, mania, psychosis risk, trauma activation, sleep disruption, substances, illness, and medication effects may require support outside meditation. [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] holds those unresolved criteria.

Recycling a pleasant reaction is as important as recycling an unpleasant one. Attachment to bliss, clarity, Flow, no-self, or calm can distort method choice and behavior as much as aversion to pain or fear.

Retroactive use adds a task boundary. If a reaction layer comes from work or
life activity, the practice move is not to split attention during the task at
any cost; it is to stop safely, then practice with what the task stirred up
([[Retroactive Meditation]] S1-S5).

## Implications
Practice guidance should ask two questions when meditation produces a reaction:

- What is the original object, practice effect, or just-finished activity?
- What is the sensory composition of the reaction to it?

If the reaction remains workable, it can usually be routed through [[Noting]], range adjustment, equanimity supports, Flow, Gone, Rest, or [[Nurture Positive]]. If it is not workable, the next move is not more heroism. The next move may be simpler focus, grounding, Do Nothing or Noting switching, stopping, teacher contact, therapy, medical care, or ordinary action.
