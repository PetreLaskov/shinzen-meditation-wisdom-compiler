## Why This Talk Matters
The talk adds an important guardrail to Gate 5's taxonomy work. Shinzen's sensory distinctions are not rigid boxes: the same six spaces can be grouped as body/mind/world, subjective/objective, or somatic/visual/auditory when that grouping serves practice.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: One natural grouping is "two plus two plus two equals six": two body spaces, two mind spaces, and two world spaces.
- **S2**: Another natural grouping is "three plus three equals six": Feel/Image/Talk as the subjective triad and Touch/Sight/Sound as the objective triad.
- **S3**: Feel/Image/Talk functions as a system because it reacts together to Touch/Sight/Sound and also proactively spins memory, planning, and fantasy.
- **S4**: Another natural grouping is somatic, visual, and auditory: Touch/Feel, Sight/Image, and Sound/Talk.
- **S5**: Shinzen defines the right way to meditate pragmatically: if the chosen grouping worked, it was the right way in that context.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S4**: The same six sensory spaces can be partitioned by function, subject/object contrast, or modality. This prevents the [[Sensory Grid]] from hardening into one compulsory ontology.
- **S3**: The subjective triad is treated as a real practice system because it reacts and proactively generates; this strengthens the inner-system model without making it the only possible grouping.
- **S5**: "It worked" functions as an outcome-sensitive correctness criterion, but only inside Shinzen's larger CCE and safety frame.

## Practice Handles
- If bottom-line simplicity is needed, group the six spaces into body, mind, and world rather than forcing all six labels.
- If selfing or reactivity is central, use the subjective/objective triad: Feel/Image/Talk reacting to Touch/Sight/Sound.
- If modality training or SHF-style simplicity is central, use the somatic/visual/auditory grouping: Touch/Feel, Sight/Image, and Sound/Talk.
- Treat category choice as a practice variable. Distinctions can be made or dissolved depending on whether they improve clarity, equanimity, and workable contact.

## Summary
Shinzen names several "natural" ways to partition the six sensory spaces. Body/mind/world divides them into three pairs. Subjective/objective divides Feel/Image/Talk from Touch/Sight/Sound and highlights the way the inner system reacts to the outer system or proactively spins memory, planning, and fantasy. Somatic/visual/auditory divides them by modality, pairing Touch with Feel, Sight with Image, and Sound with Talk.

The central teaching is not that one partition replaces the others. Shinzen says the path is about making distinctions and destroying distinctions, and the trick is to do both skillfully. In context, his answer to the student's report is pragmatic: if the chosen grouping worked, that was the right way to meditate.
