## Key Claims
- **S1**: See Hear Feel is introduced as a standard technique in Unified Mindfulness, formerly Basic Mindfulness, and as a form of noting inspired by the Mahasi system.
- **S2**: Shinzen defines mindful awareness broadly as concentration power, sensory clarity, and equanimity working together; any practice that raises the base level of those skills over time counts as mindful awareness practice, including Nurture Positive and Do Nothing.
- **S3**: Labels are optional supports inside noting: they train recognition of a basic level of mindfulness and can be resumed when challenge or distraction requires reestablishing that level.
- **S4**: The opening diagram places See, Hear, and Feel around five theme groups per modality: physical, mental, rest, flow, and spaciousness.
- **S5**: The source's simple answer for why to learn SHF is "so you can be optimally happy in every way possible," where optimal means as happy as possible given conditions that cannot feasibly be changed.
- **S6**: Shinzen presents three happiness dimensions as appreciate, transcend, and improve self and world; four dimensions as surface/deep happiness for oneself and others; and five dimensions as reduced suffering, elevated sensual fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and love/service.
- **S7**: The source claims mindfulness skills directly or indirectly optimize every happiness dimension and that some forms of happiness are essentially inaccessible without mindfulness skills.
- **S8**: SHF is said to affect happiness generically by regular continued practice that raises baseline CCE, and specifically by choosing versions of SHF for situations such as boring tasks, sleep difficulty, or severe emotional distress.
- **S9**: "Focus range" means the set of experience categories intentionally focused on; beginners are expected to explore visual experience, auditory experience, body experience, and any/all experience.
- **S10**: The quiz answers frame practical applications: pleasant experience can be made more fulfilling by noting Feel, unpleasant emotion or pain can be decomposed into See/Hear/Feel components, confusion can be met as sensory chaos, and negative behavior can be interrupted by detecting and equanimizing precursor sensory states.

## Summary
The unit opens by locating See Hear Feel inside Unified Mindfulness. SHF is a noting technique, not mindfulness as a whole; Shinzen uses the broader CCE definition of mindful awareness to say that any practice raising baseline concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity can count as mindful awareness practice. Labels are placed below noting in the method hierarchy: they are practical supports that teach the practitioner what basic mindfulness feels like and can be reintroduced when challenge makes unlabelled noting unstable.

The source then gives the first SHF map. See, Hear, and Feel each cover physical, mental, rest, flow, and spaciousness themes. The unit does not yet explain those themes, but it establishes the old/new transition: readers familiar with pre-2015 paradigms may need the appendix, while new readers can simply learn the new interface.

The main rationale is happiness. Shinzen argues that SHF is worth learning because it supports optimal happiness under conditions that cannot always be changed. He gives three parallel taxonomies: appreciate/transcend/improve self and world; surface and deep happiness for oneself and others; and five applied goals of reduced suffering, sensory fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and love/service. SHF works generically by raising baseline CCE, and specifically by routing attention through focus ranges suited to situations such as boredom, sleeplessness, distress, pain, confusion, and negative behavior impulses.

## Source Frame
This is a teaching preface, not an empirical review. It wants the reader to see SHF as modern, learnable, flexible, and life-relevant before the technical instructions begin. Its most reliable content is definitional and procedural: how Shinzen frames Unified Mindfulness, CCE, labels, noting, happiness aims, focus range, and early use cases.

The broad claims should stay source-attributed. "Modern, evidence-based," the universality of the happiness taxonomy, and the claim that some happiness dimensions are inaccessible without mindfulness skills are asserted here rather than demonstrated. The Lakota sweat lodge story functions as motivation and path rhetoric, not evidence for SHF's effects.

The source also creates a useful bridge between old and new systems. It preserves continuity with Basic Mindfulness through CCE, noting, Nurture Positive, Do Nothing, Focus In, Focus Out, and Rest, while shifting the interface toward the simpler See/Hear/Feel labels whose exact meanings vary by focus range.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that Unified Mindfulness is a modern, evidence-based mindfulness system like MBSR is asserted without citations in this unit.
- The claim that the four-quadrant happiness model can encompass all current and historical happiness models, including Aristotle and positive psychology, is too broad for this source alone.
- The claim that some forms of happiness are essentially inaccessible without mindfulness skills is central to Shinzen's rationale but not evidenced here.
- "Escape into pain" is powerful inside Shinzen's pain-without-suffering frame, but without safety boundaries it can be misread as endurance, medical neglect, or dissociation.
- The quiz answer that objective behaviors are preceded by sensory states is useful for practice routing but too broad as a full behavior-change theory.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The source promises a simplified SHF interface while immediately presenting five theme groups under each of See, Hear, and Feel; simplicity depends on later explanation of how labels change meaning by range.
- The happiness frame includes accepting unchangeable conditions and improving self/world; the tension is productive but can collapse into either passivity or over-control if not routed carefully.
- Labels are called optional, yet proper labeling is said to provide enough CCE for effective noting; this is best read as a training heuristic rather than proof that every labelled session is mindful.
- Condition-independent happiness can support resilience, but it can also be confused with indifference to ordinary needs unless surface happiness and service remain in view.
- The old/new paradigm note preserves continuity with Basic Mindfulness while implying that pre-2015 users may misread the newer SHF label meanings.
