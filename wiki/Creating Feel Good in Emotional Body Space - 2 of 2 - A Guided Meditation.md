## Practice Handles
For a practitioner: settle, choose a brief positive thought, notice whether pleasant body resonance appears, drop the thought, and stay with the resonance for as long or short as it lasts. If it fades, repeat the same or a different thought and return to body Feel.

Add a small smile when helpful. Treat the smile as a mild somatic fortifier, not as performance or emotional display.

When positive Feel is present, let attention spread as broadly as the feeling allows. Notice whether the pleasant object supports a concentrated, in-the-zone state. Later, open the eyes and keep some awareness in positive Feel during simple eye contact or conversation.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by asking the student to stretch, settle, and actively create positive Feel if possible. He immediately qualifies the instruction: sometimes it works and sometimes it does not. The method is to think a thought likely to produce pleasant body feeling, focus on the resonance, and, if it dampens, think the same or a different thought, then drop thought and return to the pleasant body resonance.

He adds a small support: intentionally smile just a little, like a hint of the Buddha's smile, and notice how that can produce or fortify pleasant Feel. After the student reports some success, he asks the student to continue and to let the positive feeling spread as broadly as possible, possibly beyond the body.

The main new explanation is concentration. Shinzen asks the student to taste how the practice develops samadhi: positive Feel is the object, concentration increases the pleasantness, the pleasantness motivates more one-pointedness, and this creates a positive feedback loop that raises concentration power.

Finally, he asks the student to keep the positive emotional body concentration while opening the eyes, making eye contact, and entering a small social interaction. This tests portability: the practice is not only producing a pleasant state but keeping some awareness in that state while relating.

## Phenomenological Distinctions
- **Created resonance versus successful mood control**: the instruction is an experiment, not a guarantee that pleasant Feel will appear.
- **Smile support versus positive thought trigger**: a slight smile can evoke or fortify pleasant Feel through the body without relying only on Image/Talk.
- **Pleasant Feel as object versus pleasure as goal**: the pleasant emotional body sensation becomes the concentration object; pleasure supports concentration but does not replace the object.
- **Private cultivation versus social portability**: the same positive Feel can be held partly online with eyes open and another person present.

## Source Frame

Claims about concentration feedback are strong as practice phenomenology in this teaching, but they are not independent empirical claims about affect or social influence.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen invites active creation while also saying the method may not work. That tension is protective: deliberate effort is permitted, but emotional output is not commanded.
- Pleasantness motivates one-pointedness, but seeking pleasantness can also become grasping if the practitioner forgets the sensory-object frame.
- Eyes-open interaction makes the practice socially relevant, but social portability can be overread as proof of interpersonal impact.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Actively create positive Feel" could become self-blame when no pleasant body resonance appears.
- The smile instruction could be used as emotional masking rather than a small somatic support.
- The concentration feedback loop could become chasing bliss or refusing unpleasant material.
- Holding positive Feel during eye contact could become charisma practice, social manipulation, or avoidance of honest relational feedback.
