The Equanimity Training Ladder gives the concrete "what now?" branch for
noninterference. It treats deliberate support as useful, spontaneous dropping
as especially important, and the inability to be equanimous as possible
practice material rather than failure.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Equanimity can be supported intentionally, recognized when
  it happens spontaneously, and re-entered second-order when it cannot be
  generated directly.
- **Why this matters**: It turns "be equanimous" from a vague demand into a
  set of repair moves.
- **Key tensions**: Deliberate supports can become subtle control, calm
  performance, or avoidance when clarity and safety are weak.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's training cues; limited as a full
  safety or trauma protocol.

## Ladder

1. **Minimum correct support**: In SHF, labels should be paced comfortably,
   correspond to experience, and use an equanimous voice. Shinzen treats that
   as a basic CCE support, not as proof of deep balance ([[See Hear Feel
   Introduction - Four Okays and Required vs Allowed]] S9).

2. **Relax the body**: The Basic Mindfulness introduction says equanimity can
   be cultivated by intentionally relaxing the body while experiences wash
   through ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S11). This is support, not a
   requirement that visible expression stop.

3. **Soften the talk**: The same source recommends replacing judgment with
   appreciation, acceptance, and gentle matter-of-factness ([[Basic
   Mindfulness Introduction]] S11). The short equanimity clip gives an oral
   version: welcome what arises, disregard judging talk when possible, or
   replace it with accepting talk ([[Equanimity Intentional Noticing Dropping
   Deeper and Second-Order Equanimity]] S1-S2).

4. **Use the voice**: SHF says label tone supports equanimity: a low, gentle,
   matter-of-fact tone can help sensory events arise and pass with less
   stickiness ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - CCE and Labeling as Skill
   Support]] S5-S7). The Part 1 labeling talk repeats this as "use the
   voice"; even when expression is intense, the label tone can remain
   equanimous ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2
   Parts]] S8-S9).

5. **Notice spontaneous drops**: Shinzen treats spontaneous equanimity
   recognition as especially important because the felt reward of less
   suffering and more fulfillment trains the deep mind ([[Basic Mindfulness
   Introduction]] S12). [[Equanimity Intentional Noticing Dropping Deeper and
   Second-Order Equanimity]] makes the same point: when equanimity "just sort
   of happens," noticing it creates a positive conditioning loop (S3-S4).

6. **Use second-order equanimity**: If the practitioner cannot relax the body
   or stop judging, the next object is the tension and judging themselves.
   One can have equanimity, or have equanimity with lack of equanimity
   ([[Equanimity Intentional Noticing Dropping Deeper and Second-Order
   Equanimity]] S5).

7. **Use background equanimity when appropriate**: Agitation or physical
   discomfort may remain in the background while concentration and clarity go
   to breath, Rest, positive states, sights, sounds, or another object. This
   counts as mindfulness only when the challenge is permitted to arise and
   there is no agenda to make it disappear ([[Turn Towards, Turn Away Working
   with the Agitation Flavor in Meditation]] S1-S5; [[Using Turn Away and
   Background Equanimity with Sensory Challenges]] S5-S8).

## Feedback Logic

[[The Reptilian Brain, Skinnerian Training & the Experience of God]] gives
the body-level version of the ladder. Formal practice is simplified so the
deep mind can immediately detect that fighting sensation brings suffering,
while noninterference brings fulfillment or reduced suffering (S1-S4). This
is useful as Shinzen's practice-feedback model, not permission to punish the
body or intensify practice.

## Boundaries

Relaxation, accepting talk, loving-kindness, and equanimity voice are allowed
supports, not mandatory performances. If they make the practitioner more
tense, ashamed, dissociated, compliant, or unable to act, load [[Equanimity
Versus Suppression]].

Background equanimity is not denial by default, but it can become denial if
the practitioner refuses to know the challenge, never turns toward it when
capacity allows, neglects ordinary care, or uses practice language to avoid
needed support.

Second-order equanimity is not a substitute for safety. Severe, persistent,
destabilizing, coercive, medical, or clinical non-equanimity routes through
[[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] before more technique.
