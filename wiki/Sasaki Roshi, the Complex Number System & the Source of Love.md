## Summary
Shinzen opens by distinguishing his claim from Sasaki Roshi's. Sasaki, in Shinzen's telling, suggests that the polarity paradigm may describe how everything works: contrasting activities cancel into neutral, and neutral can polarize back into contrasting activities. Shinzen says Sasaki may be right, but his own claim is narrower: the paradigm is useful for analyzing how consciousness works moment by moment.

He then turns to complex numbers. Multiplication can stretch, squeeze, or remain neutral at one; addition can move in opposite directions or remain neutral at zero; rotation can turn right, turn left, or remain at a still point. He uses the dancer as an image of stretch/squeeze, directional movement, and turning, then briefly suggests that complex waves and quantum behavior may hint at something coming from nothing.

The closing move returns the speculative analogy to practice meaning. The whole point of experiencing the pattern is that it resembles the peristalsis of a formless Source: self and world arise from Source and return to Source. Although the Source is impersonal and empty, Shinzen says it is also a source of unconditional love; when contacted, it affects the human part of us and naturally gives rise to the wish to serve.
