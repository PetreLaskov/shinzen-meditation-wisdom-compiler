Gone is the manual's dedicated practice object for sensory passings. It names the instant a sensory event disappears or suddenly diminishes and the practitioner detects that transition.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Gone is not continued absence; it is the noticed moment of vanishing, drop-off, or abrupt diminishing.
- **Why this matters**: It balances the ordinary bias toward arisings, adds a dedicated impermanence practice, gives the grid a cross-cutting event marker, and later becomes a route for repeated self-cessation.
- **Key tensions**: Shinzen frames Gone as relief, tranquility, satisfying nothingness, Source, death preparation, and service, while also warning that intense Goneness, Emptiness, and No-Self can produce fear, loss, confusion, and disorientation.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Basic Mindfulness definition and Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 instruction; Source, Nirvana, service, death-preparation, primordial Feel, and extreme-duress claims remain source-attributed.

## Model
The formal definition is precise: Gone is the moment when all or part of a sensory event drops away or drops off and the practitioner notices it ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S5). Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 gives the mature book definition: Gone means all or part of an event abruptly disappears or diminishes; it need not be permanent, whole-object disappearance, or total disappearance ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S5).

A sudden decrease in intensity counts because something has dropped off. Gradual fading is not itself Gone, but the final transition from subtle presence to nonexistence is Gone ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S5).

Gone requires detection. If attention leaves an object and later discovers it is absent, that does not count as Gone because the moment of vanishing was not noticed ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S5).

Gone is also distinct from [[Way of Tranquility|Rest]]. The ending of mental talk may be noted as Gone. If mental talk then stays absent for a while, that duration is Hear Rest, not Gone. The same distinction applies to physical sound, body sensation, or any other sensory event ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S5).

There is a controlled terminology tension with [[Flow]]. Basic Mindfulness often contrasts Flow as ongoing change with Gone as abrupt passing. [[Dynamic Aspects of the Sensory System]] gives the same distinction as continuous dynamic change versus discontinuous change where something abruptly vanishes ([[Dynamic Aspects of the Sensory System]] S3-S4). Chapter 9 uses a broader change vocabulary in which Flow is any change and Gone is a special case of abrupt Flow, specifically abrupt decrease ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S6). Chapter 10 gives the Source version: Joshu's mu is Gone experienced with great clarity, ordinary vanishings already contain the "no" that transcends duality, and Gone occurs when Expansion and Contraction mutually cease or cancel into Zero ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S2-S4, S12-S13). Future pages should preserve both: the practice menu separates Flow and Gone, while the deep impermanence model can treat Gone as abrupt contractive change.

[[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] restates the same boundary in
oral form: abrupt diminishing counts as vanishing even when the event does not
completely disappear, while all other change belongs to Flow. Its advanced
sequence then links local simultaneous expansion-contraction to a repeated
"both, gone" rhythm ([[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] S1,
S6-S7).

## Practice
Just Note Gone is simple in form: whenever all or part of something drops away or drops off, note Gone. The vanished event may be vague or clear, large or small, simple or complex, long-lasting or fleeting; in some cases the practitioner may not know what vanished. If nothing vanishes for a while, the instruction is to wait until something does. If a reaction to the absence of Gone arises, note Gone when that reaction drops away ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S5).

Gone also appears inside other Noting options. Focus on See, Focus on Hear, Focus on Feel, and Focus on Everything all include Gone whenever the current focus drops away. Focus on Everything includes the 12 grid categories plus Gone, making 13 noteable objects ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S2-S3).

The SHF introduction adds two smaller practice distinctions. First, explicit Gone is optional inside ordinary Noting; the practitioner may explicitly note the moment when all or part of the current object vanishes, or may not bother to label it ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Noting Nutshell and FAQ]] S5). Second, "note 'til gone" is a re-noting option: stay with the same event until all or part of it vanishes, rather than necessarily waiting for total disappearance ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Noting Nutshell and FAQ]] S6).

The Gate 1 Part 1 labeling talk places that option inside the basic Noting rhythm. When an acknowledged event immediately disappears, Shinzen says the missing focus phase is not a failure; the disappearance is insight into vanishings whether acknowledgement caused it or not ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S1-S2). The same talk defines "Repeat until Gone" as locking onto one event and repeating the note until some abrupt subsidence appears, then noting Gone ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S12).

In the mental-chatter FAQ, if the practitioner is using See Hear Feel Everything, distinct bursts of mental talk can be noted as Hear and the end of each burst can be explicitly noted as Gone. If the active focus range excludes mental talk, the recommended move changes: let the talk come and go in the background with equanimity and return to the chosen range, or shift range if that cannot be maintained ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Noting Nutshell and FAQ]] S13).

The chapter says Gone practice develops clarity, intimacy, and familiarity with passings. Concentration then keeps attention on Goneness, and equanimity with new arisings prevents them from blocking detection of their passings. This creates a positive feedback loop: the more passings are noticed, the easier it is to be equanimous with arisings, and the easier it becomes to wait for passings ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S6).

Chapter 9 gives a sharper axiom: total and quick focus plus total and quick equanimity with each arising deepen the clarity and depth of its passing. In Shinzen's metaphor, saying yes quickly and completely to nature's yes makes nature's no easier to experience ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S7). This keeps Gone practice from becoming a hunt for endings; the path to deeper passing is complete contact with arising.

The expansive-contractive Flow talk gives an adjacent heads-up rather than a
doable instruction. If a sensory event is fully affirmed at arising, Shinzen
says one may notice that it is already passing: its spatial volume is expanding
and contracting right behind that, often followed by complete disappearance
([[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] S6). The talk explicitly says
there is nothing one can do to make this perception available.

The same chapter makes Gone ordinary rather than esoteric. Every in-breath and out-breath ends, so breath alone supplies two Gones per cycle; ordinary sounds, lights, thoughts, images, sensations, and partial decreases all qualify when the vanishing is detected ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S8).

[[Abrupt Flow Diminishings, Vanishings and Noting Gone]] gives the clearest
oral confidence rule for this practice. The assumption is not that a
practitioner can detect Gone in every sensory category; the assumption is
that one can find Gone somewhere. Breath endings and mental-talk sentence
endings are enough to practice Noting Gone "absolutely perfect" because the
actual object is the detected abrupt ending, not its dramatic scale
([[Abrupt Flow Diminishings, Vanishings and Noting Gone]] S3-S4).

## Rewards And Interpretive Frame
Shinzen presents Gone as more than a technical label. Micro-endings can become macro-relief when passings are detected continuously enough. Noting Gone may also bring tranquility, a sense of safety, fulfillment, "satisfying nothingness," Source, union, love, and service in the source's own language ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S6-S8; [[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S9-S10).

The abrupt-diminishings talk adds a more compact affective map. Noticing
vanishings in unpleasant experience can bring relief; noticing them in
pleasant and neutral experience can bring a counterintuitive sense of
fulfillment; and the aftereffect of endings can show up as Rest, quiet,
peace, or unexpected richness rather than bleakness ([[Abrupt Flow
Diminishings, Vanishings and Noting Gone]] S5-S6). This supports the existing
reward frame without making any one result mandatory.

The durable model should preserve this claim without overpromoting it. The practice mechanism is the noticed passing of sensory events. The Source, Nirvana, Unborn, shared-womb, and bodhicitta interpretations are Shinzen's contemplative reading of that mechanism, not independently established metaphysics or behavior change evidence.

Chapter 9 adds a useful Source boundary. It says Source is not itself a sensory experience, but can be indirectly contacted through vanishing, while the aftereffect of each vanishing becomes sensorially clearer ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S9). This helps future agents avoid saying that Source is directly perceived as a sense object.

Chapter 10 sharpens that boundary further. In the actual moment of cancellation, no self is present to experience Source or Zero. What appears in the next instant is an after-representation or afterglow made from image, talk, feeling, fulfillment, safety, peace, and love ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S14-S15). Gone can therefore point to Source without making Source another object in the grid.

When Gone becomes frequent, the chapter says Goneness itself may become an object of high concentration. The gaps between Gones shorten until a figure-ground reversal occurs: Gone becomes the abiding ground, while self and world become fleeting figures ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S8). This is the chapter's strongest transformation claim and should be routed with both [[Flow]] and [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].

[[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] adds the cleanest self-inquiry
bridge. Shinzen says self-enquiry looks back from an arising toward where it
came from, while Gone watches the current event vanish because the place A
goes is the place B arises from. That makes Gone the passing-side route to
the same Source-facing boundary that self-enquiry approaches from the
arising side ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S4-S5).

[[The Absolute Witness]] adds the witness-side version of that route.
Shinzen says the absolute witness is not a detached watcher but contentless
cessation, Zero, nirodha, Source, or emptiness, "directly eyeballed" for a
brief instant whenever a phenomenon disappears. The actionable transition is
to detect the Gone of the subtle Feel/Image/Talk that creates the relative
witness ([[The Absolute Witness]] S4-S7).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 gives the fullest Flow/Gone version of this reversal. Subtle vibratory Flow and Gone can alternate until sensory distinctions blur, and with enough Gone the practitioner may no longer know what is disappearing; vanishing itself dominates as the ground ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S11-S12). This is powerful practice language, but it needs the safety boundary below.

[[Flow, Gone and a Figure-Ground Reversal]] confirms that loss of object
detail can be part of mature Gone practice rather than a defect: the
practitioner may no longer know whether what vanished was large or small,
pleasant or unpleasant, simple or complex, because vanishing itself has become
dominant. Shinzen calls Gone the stronger leveler because it removes the
time-space frame required for ordinary distinctions ([[Flow, Gone and a
Figure-Ground Reversal]] S3-S4).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 8 adds a selfhood version of this route. Shinzen says the "real no self" involves experiencing selfhood being loved into and out of existence moment by moment, and closes by asking whether being born many times in a day also means dying many times. His answer links repeated daily self-death to the power of Gone ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S16, S18).

## Safety
Gone practice has an explicit dark-night boundary. Shinzen says intense Goneness, Emptiness, and No-Self can occasionally produce fear, loss, confusion, disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, and hopelessness. His recommended interventions are: accentuate subtle good parts of the void, use [[Noting]] to deconstruct the negative reactions into emotional body sensation, mental image, and mental talk, use [[Nurture Positive]] to reconstruct the self with positive affect, behavior, and cognition, and use [[Flow]] or [[Expansion And Contraction]] to reestablish dynamic vitality. In extreme cases, he says ongoing intensive support from teachers and other practitioners may be needed ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S9; [[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S14).

This is the first Basic Mindfulness chapter in the current compiled sequence to name dark-night-style negative effects and support needs this directly. It strengthens the case that impermanence practice is not automatically safe merely because it is precise.

Chapter 9 adds a second safety-relevant edge through [[Primordial Feel]]. Gone is usually restful, but in some cases it may expose chaotic body emotion that Shinzen interprets as infant-like and purgatorial rather than as the dramatic dark night ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S15-S18). That interpretation should remain source-attributed, especially when trauma, panic, dissociation, depression, developmental history, or clinical support may matter.

Chapter 10 adds [[Dissolution]] as a wider surface-to-Source risk. Bhanga may be blissful vibratory Flow or harsh chaos, fear, disorientation, and apparent loss of center or border. Shinzen recommends equanimity and contact with Expansion-Contraction, but the chapter does not give stop rules, clinical differentials, or support criteria beyond noting competent instructors and individual variation ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S16-S19).

## Boundaries
Gone should not be confused with suppressing, spacing out, or blanking experience. It requires more precise detection, not less.

Gone also should not be used as a generic response to trauma, torture, medical crisis, or unsafe conditions. The chapter uses an extreme-duress example to illustrate microrelief, but it does not provide clinical, emergency, or ethical guidance. Future agents should route such cases through [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] rather than turning Just Note Gone into a universal prescription.

Note 'til gone has a related boundary. It can train continuity with an event through its partial or full vanishing, but if it becomes a hunt for disappearance, it loses the broader Shinzen principle that deeper Gone practice depends on complete contact and equanimity with arisings.

The Part 1 talk's repeat-until-Gone instruction should therefore be treated as an option, not a demand. Shinzen says it may be chosen depending on what is happening and how things are presenting themselves, not that every event should be forced toward vanishing ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S12).

Gone and [[Flow]] are complementary impermanence routes. Flow emphasizes ongoing movement, change, energy, or force. Gone emphasizes abrupt vanishing or drop-off. Chapter 8 connects both through selfhood: each moment of self is described as arising from pure space through simultaneous expansion-contraction and passing through repeated Gone ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S18). Both routes can become fixation or destabilization if isolated from grounding, reconstruction, support, and behavior.

The Both-Gone rhythm should not become an attainment checklist. Shinzen frames
it as vocabulary in case the arising-passing edge presents itself, while also
saying that many people awaken without ever using Expansion-Contraction
language ([[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] S6-S8).

## Implications
The Sensory Grid is now a 12-cell table plus a cross-cutting vanishing marker. Any cell can end; the ending can be noted as Gone. Practice routing can therefore move by column, row, all experience, or the event-status of passing itself.

Gone also clarifies the relationship between impermanence and rest. A vanishing may point to Absolute Rest in Shinzen's frame, but the continued absence after the vanishing is a Rest object. That distinction prevents cessation practice from collapsing into calm-state preference.
