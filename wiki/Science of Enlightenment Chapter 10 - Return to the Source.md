Chapter 10 of Shinzen Young's 2016 *The Science of Enlightenment* is the book's dedicated Source chapter. It translates Zen mu, ordinary Gone, Expansion-Contraction, Zero, dissolution, and service into one advanced practice model while repeatedly cautioning that nature-science extensions are conjectural.

- **Origin**: Shinzen Young, *The Science of Enlightenment: How Meditation Works*, Sounds True.
- **Date**: 2016.
- **Format**: Book chapter converted to Markdown; pages 178-203 in the source markers.
- **Reliability**: Primary source for Shinzen's mature Source/Zero frame, Joshu's mu as Gone, Expansion-Contraction as a route through stable pressure and observer fixation, the afterglow boundary around Source, bhanga/dissolution, and Source-to-service claims. Strong for his internal phenomenological model and practice routing; weaker for comparative mysticism, history of traditions, free-energy speculation, thermodynamics, mathematics, cosmology, and clinical guidance.
- **Scope**: Explains dog Buddha-nature and mu, maps Gone and arising into simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, compares mysticism and science cautiously, applies the polarity model to solid sensations, thought, smile, breath, Zero, afterglow, dissolution, and spontaneous service.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen interprets Joshu's dog Buddha-nature koan as a challenge about transcending duality; both full affirmation of dog activity and Joshu's "no" can answer it when they manifest nonduality rather than egoic position-taking.
- **S2**: Rinzai koan practice can induce a sustained Don't Know that matures into intuitive wisdom, but Shinzen prefers a mindfulness route: Joshu's mu is Gone experienced with great clarity.
- **S3**: Ordinary vanishings already contain the "no" that is neither yes nor no, while each arising can be tracked as spreading activation accompanied by micro-Gones that create collapsing space.
- **S4**: Each sensory or motor event is born from simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, and Gone occurs when those polar forces mutually cease or cancel, carrying the sensory richness into a richer tranquility.
- **S5**: Rich nothingness is path dependent: nihilistic nothingness may be bleak and paralyzing, while mindfulness-based Gone can become tranquility, safety, fulfillment, empowerment, and love.
- **S6**: Shinzen agrees with Bertrand Russell that contemplative experience does not by itself justify claims about objective reality; free-energy, entropy, dialectical, complex-number, and nature analogies are explicitly speculative.
- **S7**: Expansion-Contraction is broader than arising-passing because it can reveal impermanence in apparently stable or solid phenomena as pressure forces rather than visible movement.
- **S8**: Intentionally relaxable tensions should be relaxed, while unrelaxable tensions can be met by giving expansion permission to expand and contraction permission to contract, allowing pressure to transform into "gush and gather."
- **S9**: Expansion-Contraction helps avoid the observer trap because it pulls apart the fixated center and boundary of the observing self; the practitioner becomes Flow rather than a separate watcher.
- **S10**: The sensation-to-Source sequence runs from solid pain, to undulation, to vibration, to simultaneous equal Expansion-Contraction, to cancellation into tranquility, to repolarization; this can shift identification from content to contour or field.
- **S11**: Smile, breath, and thought are ordinary examples of simultaneous Expansion-Contraction; even scattered thought can be reframed as space spreading and gathering when meaning extraction relaxes.
- **S12**: Zero names the mutual cancellation of the polar "two doings"; Shinzen links it to Gone, emptiness, no-self, true self, witness, unborn, nirodha, and the peace of heaven as names for the same rich nothingness in his frame.
- **S13**: Mindfulness reaches Zero through progressive simplification: six sensory elements, then Flow, then Expansion-Contraction, then the One Nothing into which the two doings merge at vanishing.
- **S14**: Source or Zero is not directly experienced at the moment of cancellation because no self is there; what returns is an after-representation or afterglow made of image, talk, feeling, fulfillment, safety, peace, and love.
- **S15**: Enlightened wisdom should not be identified with the unconditioned itself; in this chapter it is a conditioned sensory representation of the formless state immediately preceding form.
- **S16**: Following the senses from surface to Source can be destabilizing and varies by disposition, conditioning, genetics, and chance; Shinzen says commitment and competent instructors matter.
- **S17**: Bhanga or dissolution can appear as chaotic tearing, exploding, crushing, disorientation, and fear, or as blissful bubbly/vibratory Flow; not everyone passes through a dramatic bhanga stage.
- **S18**: In harsh dissolution, Shinzen names two comfort routes: equanimity and contacting the underlying Expansion-Contraction forces until being blown away and crushed can evolve into gentler stretching and squeezing.
- **S19**: The "awkward intermediate zone" is a stage where the old coping mechanism of tightening and turning away is being shed, while open-up-and-turn-toward is not yet strong enough to provide stable safety and fulfillment.
- **S20**: Returning to Source is not only for personal condition-independent happiness; because Source is shared, realizing it makes others appear as close kin and calls the practitioner toward spontaneous service.

## Summary
The chapter begins with Joshu's dog Buddha-nature koan. Shinzen treats the koan as a duality trap rather than a scholastic question: the answer must manifest nonduality, not merely state a doctrine. A full "yes" that becomes the activity of dog and Joshu's "no" that is neither yes nor no are both valid when they come from the same Source. Koan practice reaches this through sustained equanimity with Don't Know; Shinzen's mindfulness translation reaches it through ordinary Gone. Mu is Gone with great clarity.

The middle of the chapter develops a polarity model. Each arising is already passing because activation spreads while micro-Gones collapse space. Each event is molded by simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, and Gone is the point where those polar forces mutually cancel into Zero. Shinzen makes this practical for stable pressure: unrelaxable tensions may still reveal hidden forces of expansion and contraction, and equanimity means letting each force do its thing. This also resolves the observer trap, because Expansion-Contraction removes a fixated center and border rather than leaving a separate observer watching flat arising and passing.

The chapter then expands from sensation to Source. A pain may soften into undulation, vibration, simultaneous equal polar forces, cancellation, tranquility, and repolarization. Smile, breath, and thought become ordinary examples of the same dynamic. The resulting figure-ground reversal shifts identity from mind-body content to the contour or field of impermanence. Shinzen then names Zero as the richest simplification: the six senses reduce to Flow, Flow reduces to Expansion-Contraction, and the two doings reduce to One Nothing at vanishing.

The strongest epistemic boundary is unusually explicit. Shinzen agrees with Russell that mystical experience does not directly establish the nature of objective reality. His free-energy, entropy, thermodynamics, dialectical, complex-number, star, matter/antimatter, and nature analogies are framed as guesses, aesthetic speculation, or not to be taken too seriously as science. The chapter also gives a subtle phenomenological boundary: Zero or Source is never directly experienced in the moment of cancellation because no self is present. What is experienced is the afterglow or after-representation that appears in the next instant.

The last sections add safety and service. Surface-to-Source practice can destabilize. Bhanga can be blissful vibratory dissolution or harsh chaos in which the center, borders, intellect, and body comfort seem torn away. Shinzen recommends equanimity and contacting Expansion-Contraction, but he also notes competent instruction and individual variation. The chapter closes by linking Source to service: because the Source is shared, realizing it should make others appear as close relatives and naturally support care.

## Source Frame
The source's core agenda is to make the most speculative-looking part of Shinzen's system practice-legible. It wants Source, Zero, God language, Buddha-nature, true self, no-self, Gone, and Expansion-Contraction to route through concrete sensory practice rather than float as metaphysics.

The strongest material is internal phenomenology and technique interpretation. The chapter clarifies how Gone, arising, stable pressure, thought, breath, and somatic pain can be observed through Expansion-Contraction and cancellation. It also gives an important epistemic humility marker by explicitly denying that mystical experience alone settles objective-reality claims.

The weakest material is broad analogy. The chapter moves across Zen, Taoism, Patanjali, Buddhist emptiness, Christian mysticism, Jewish practice, Heraclitus, Hegel, Nicholas of Cusa, free energy, thermodynamics, complex numbers, DNA, computers, stars, and matter/antimatter. These are valuable for Shinzen's interpretive range, but not evidence that all those domains instantiate one proven Source mechanism.

The safety frame is mixed. Shinzen directly acknowledges destabilization, individual differences, and competent instructors, but some rhetoric around trusting the forces tearing one apart could be dangerous if detached from clinical, relational, medical, or teacher-competence criteria.

## Weakest Claims
- The chapter's nature, thermodynamics, free-energy, complex-number, DNA, computer, star, and matter/antimatter parallels are explicitly speculative and should not be treated as established science.
- The claim that all names such as true self, no-self, witness, unborn, emptiness, and peace of heaven refer to the same experience is Shinzen's contemplative synthesis, not a demonstrated consensus across traditions.
- The claim that a complete thought contains all meanings in the universe is an advanced Source interpretation, not an ordinary semantic or cognitive claim.
- The claim that harsh dissolution should be trusted as divine tearing can be hazardous if used without support criteria, clinical differential diagnosis, or practitioner consent.
- The spontaneous-service claim is central internally, but the chapter gives no behavioral verification method.

## Important Omissions
- The chapter does not give criteria for distinguishing bhanga from panic, trauma activation, dissociation, depersonalization, derealization, mania, psychosis risk, medical crisis, sleep deprivation, or unsafe retreat conditions.
- It does not define who counts as a competent instructor for dissolution, Source, or no-self destabilization.
- It does not explain when to stop, ground, seek therapy, seek medical care, reduce intensity, or change technique.
- It does not give empirical evidence for free-energy, thermodynamic, mathematical, biological, cosmological, or neuroscience analogies.
- It does not show how spontaneous service is tested against actual conduct, power, consent, repair, or community feedback.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen says Source/Zero is the deepest frame while also saying it is never directly experienced and only represented afterward by conditioned image, talk, and feeling.
- The chapter says the practitioner never needed to train, search, or improve, while also saying it may take considerable training to notice that and that the realization should become an optimal place for improvement.
- It makes Gone rich, loving, fulfilling, and safe, but also says the journey to Source can destabilize and involve fear, disorientation, and apparent tearing apart.
- It agrees with Russell's critique of mystical overreach, then continues to use large nature, science, mathematics, and cosmology analogies.
- The chapter uses universal service language but does not define ethical safeguards for teaching, guiding, or acting from claimed Source realization.
