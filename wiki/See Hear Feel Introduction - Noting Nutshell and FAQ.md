## Key Claims
- **S1**: A period of Noting typically consists of repeated acts of acknowledging and focusing; in practice the two phases may merge into a single act.
- **S2**: Labels are optional aids: labeling can facilitate Noting, Noting can facilitate mindful awareness, and mindful awareness is framed as a key skill for True Happiness.
- **S3**: The five labeling modes are no labels, mental labels, subvocal labels, ordinary spoken labels, and strongly spoken labels; practitioners may shift stronger when spaced out or caught up and weaker when focus stabilizes.
- **S4**: Labels may occur during the acknowledging phase, the focusing phase, or both.
- **S5**: Noting options include emphasis, re-noting, zooming, stance, and whether to explicitly note Gone.
- **S6**: Re-noting can mean double, triple, or quad noting, or "note 'til gone," which stays with the same event until all or part of it vanishes.
- **S7**: Zooming can shrink attention into one area, spread it across the whole event, extend beyond the event to find openness around it, or do local intensity and broad scope simultaneously.
- **S8**: Stance distinguishes active or passive roles in contacting an experience and active or passive roles after contact during the focus phase.
- **S9**: The FAQ normalizes beginner issues including excess self-monitoring, guessed labels, late labels, attention-induced changes, and a strengthened sense of an "I" doing the practice.
- **S10**: Repeatedly noting the same event is not wasted because each note is another moment of focusing and opening to sensory experience.
- **S11**: The source gives pragmatic reasons for Noting: voice tone can support reassurance and equanimity, present-moment focus can reduce overwhelm, sensory breakdown can make experience manageable, and Rest or Flow can become noticed as windows of opportunity.
- **S12**: Mental image and mental talk need not be prevented from co-occurring; they can still be distinguished qualitatively as visual versus auditory and spatially by their usual locations.
- **S13**: Mental chatter guidance depends on context: in See Hear Feel Everything, note talk as Hear, burst endings as Gone, and triggered emotional body sensation as Feel; in a range that excludes talk, let chatter come and go in the background with equanimity and return to the chosen range, or widen the range if that is not workable.

## Summary
The first half of the unit restates Noting as acknowledge-focus practice. Labels are optional, but the source treats them as a practical feedback system with five modes from no labels to strongly spoken labels. The stronger modes are not more advanced; they are recovery supports when attention gets spaced out or caught up. Once attention stabilizes, the practitioner may drop to weaker labeling.

The unit then defines several adjustable Noting options. Emphasis chooses one element of a co-occurrence or includes multiple elements. Re-noting repeats contact with the same event a set number of times, with "note 'til gone" staying until all or part of the event vanishes. Zooming changes the spatial scope of attention, including the "zoom beyond" move of finding openness around an event. Stance names active or passive roles in contacting and focusing. Gone can be explicitly noted or left implicit.

The FAQ is a coaching layer. It normalizes beginner confusion, guesses, late labels, self-consciousness, and repeated objects, and it reframes observation-induced change as valid sensory material. Its most important applied move is context discipline: a request about mental chatter is under-specified until the active technique and focus range are named.

## Source Frame
This is a procedural-and-reassurance unit. It wants practitioners and coaches to see Noting as flexible, adjustable, and forgiving without weakening the central acknowledge-focus discipline.

The strongest claims are definitions and coaching heuristics: the five labeling modes, label escalation, re-noting, zooming, stance, explicit Gone, and the context requirement for answering "what do I do with X?" questions. These directly improve the compiled Noting and guidance model.

The weaker claims are reassurance and outcome claims. The unit says the practitioner's own voice can produce deep safety and self-acceptance, that the mind tends to tire of self-monitoring games, and that lifelong modest practice has a high probability of making incessant chatter more or less fade away. These should remain source-attributed and should not be converted into universal prognosis or clinical advice.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that one's own labeling voice can put one into a deep state of reassurance, safety, and self-acceptance is plausible as a coaching cue but not independently evidenced here.
- The statement that uncomfortable reactions to spoken labels are proof the procedure is correct can overpressure practitioners if treated as a safety rule rather than a narrow practice interpretation.
- The mental-chatter reassurance gives a high-probability lifetime outcome if modest practice is maintained, but the unit gives no population, dosage, attrition, psychiatric, trauma, or coach-support criteria.
- The driving-self and meditating-self metaphor helps reduce self-consciousness, but it should not be used to prove no-self realization or automatic practice competence.
- "Each note is a chance to experience a moment of completeness" is central to the practice frame, but this unit does not establish outcome reliability from repeated notes alone.

## Important Omissions
- The unit does not define the *An Outline of Practice* regimen invoked in the mental-chatter reassurance.
- It does not distinguish ordinary mental talk from rumination, obsessive-compulsive loops, mania, psychosis, trauma intrusions, auditory hallucination, substance effects, or medical causes.
- It does not say when strong labels should be softened, stopped, supervised, or replaced by rest, grounding, therapy, sleep, medical care, or ordinary action.
- It does not define how long to use "note 'til gone," how to avoid hunting for disappearance, or how to balance it with complete contact with arisings.
- It does not give consent, privacy, or social-context boundaries for spoken or strongly spoken labels beyond not disturbing nearby people.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Labels are optional aids, yet the unit strongly recommends escalating labels as soon as the practitioner is spaced out or caught up. This is coherent as a gear model but can confuse practitioners who equate unlabeled practice with purity.
- The source says attention-induced change is "what's really there," but this leaves a boundary between valid observation effects and subtle suppression, control, or performance.
- The mental-chatter example gives two different answers depending on range: include it as Hear when the range includes everything, or let it remain in the background when the range excludes it. The decisive variable is the practice context, not the symptom alone.
- "Note 'til gone" can deepen impermanence detection, but it may also encourage chasing endings unless balanced by the wider Gone instruction to say yes quickly and completely to arisings.
