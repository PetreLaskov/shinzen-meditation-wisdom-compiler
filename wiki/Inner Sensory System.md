The inner sensory system is Shinzen's basic map for analyzing subjective self-experience. Instead of starting with story content, it starts with three sensory components: mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation; the Basic Mindfulness manual turns that map into both [[Way of Thoughts and Emotions]] and [[Nurture Positive]] practice, while *The Science of Enlightenment* chapter 8 makes it the main doorway into [[No-Self And Personality]].

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Self-experience, emotion, no-self practice, and positive cultivation can be tracked or built through changing combinations of See In, Hear In, and Feel In.
- **Why this matters**: It gives sensory clarity and Nurture Positive a concrete inner field and prepares no-self and goodness claims without requiring abstract metaphysics first.
- **Key tensions**: The source presents the three-component model as universal for self-perception, but also admits the eight-state version is a simplifying binary approximation.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's practice map; not established as a complete theory of personhood, social identity, or neuroscience.

## Model
Chapter 4 identifies three basic components of emotional and self-related inner experience ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 4 - Calming and Clarifying]] S6):

- **Mental image / See In**: inner visual scene, memory, fantasy, imagined body, or pictured situation.
- **Mental talk / Hear In**: inner speech, rehearsal, judgment, rationalization, brooding, or internal conversation.
- **Emotional body sensation / Feel In**: body sensations that carry emotional tone, such as sadness in the eyes, anger in the jaw, fear in the belly, or enthusiasm through the body.

[[What Is the Self]] gives this map its shortest public-facing doorway.
Shinzen asks the questioner to notice visual thoughts, auditory thoughts,
physical body sensation, and emotion-related body changes, then says ordinary
self is the moment-by-moment identification with those mind/body events
([[What Is the Self]] S1-S4). The clip is broad: its body side includes both
physicality and emotionality, while this page's technical inner-system core
usually focuses no-self work on emotional body sensation together with Image
and Talk.

[[Peter Marks on the Origins of Body-Image-Talk]] gives the origin boundary
for this primitive layer. Shinzen publicly says he did not invent Body-Image-
Talk and credits Peter Marks as its inventor; Peter describes the original
noticing as a guided-session report in which talk and picture became distinct
enough for Shinzen to recognize their practice significance. Peter also says
Shinzen later rounded the approach out and integrated it into the larger
teaching system ([[Peter Marks on the Origins of Body-Image-Talk]] S1-S3).

The first Zero and One talk preserves why Shinzen sometimes says "Feel" as a
noun rather than "feeling." "Feeling" carries too many ordinary connotations,
so Feel is a technical term for emotional body sensation. The same talk says
the body can be trained into a high-resolution emotional screen in which
flavors such as anger, fear, sadness, embarrassment, impatience, disgust,
interest, joy, love, gratitude, and humor become as distinguishable as hot or
cold ([[Zero and One - Part 1 of 4 Complete Experiences]] S4).

Shinzen then treats each component as either active or restful for a simplified instant-by-instant model. Three binary variables yield eight compositional patterns: image only, talk only, body emotion only, image plus talk, image plus body emotion, talk plus body emotion, all three active, or all three restful ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 4 - Calming and Clarifying]] S7).

The Basic Mindfulness manual operationalizes the same components through See In, Hear In, Feel In, and Focus In. Mental images are noted as See In or See Rest; mental talk as Hear In or Hear Rest; emotional body sensations as Feel In or Feel Rest; and drop-away in any of those streams as [[Gone]] ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 1 - The Way of Thoughts and Emotions]] S7-S11). This makes the inner sensory system not only an explanatory model but a precise focus-option column in the [[Sensory Grid]].

[[Mindfulness and the Categories of Sensory Experience]] adds the reactive/proactive version of the same model. Shinzen says Feel, Image, and Talk can react to Touch, Sight, and Sound, and can also go off on their own as memory, plan, fantasy, and similar inner activity ([[Mindfulness and the Categories of Sensory Experience]] S4). This keeps the inner system from being only a response model; it is also an internally generative self-world simulator.

[[Sensory Clarity Insight Through Monitoring Ordinary and Restful States]]
adds the no-self experiment attached to those modes. Feel/Image/Talk can
react strongly to Touch, Sight, and Sound, producing a strong sense of a
perceiver over against an object; it can spin proactively as memory, plan,
fantasy, problem solving, worry, or confusion; and it can become inactive,
leaving peace, blank, and quiet. Watching those shifts shows how the sense of
subject varies with the inner system's activation rather than standing apart
as a separate thing ([[Sensory Clarity Insight Through Monitoring Ordinary
and Restful States]] S1-S5).

[[Untangling Sensory Experience Leads to Flow, Unifications, and Dynamic
Doing]] gives the same model as Shinzen's contemporary reworking of classical
Buddhist deconstruction. Core subjective self arises through Feel, Image, and
Talk; a broader body-mind self includes physical touch, yielding touch, Feel,
Image, and Talk as the strands to separate. When these are tracked in real
time and kept from tangling, Shinzen says subjective self can be a home rather
than a prison ([[Untangling Sensory Experience Leads to Flow, Unifications,
and Dynamic Doing]] S4-S5).

[[Sensory Clarity - 1 of 2 - No Self As Thing]] adds a historical and
constructive bridge. Shinzen contrasts Feel/Image/Talk practice with the
classical chariot argument, then points to Shingon deity practice as implying
that identity can be assembled through body gesture or sensation, mental
image, mantra or internal talk, and emotional tone. That makes the inner
system both a no-self deconstruction target and a deliberate identity-
construction tool ([[Sensory Clarity - 1 of 2 - No Self As Thing]] S1-S5).

[[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] states that construction tool more
directly. Shinzen says deity yoga replaces ordinary internal talk with mantra,
self-image with deity image, ordinary human feelings with deity feel, and
touch with mudra or other embodied ritual form. Because the practitioner
creates a mythic self from those elements while knowing it is constructed,
the practice reveals the arbitrary constructedness of ordinary self-
identification ([[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] S3-S5).

[[Sensory Clarity - 2 of 2 - No Self As Thing]] adds the follow-through:
after the coarse subjective self resolves into Feel, Image, and Talk, those
same strands can be penetrated until their separateness gives way to
vibrating space or Flow. The inner system is therefore both the deconstructive
field for no-self and the bridge into unification when CCE saturates the
strands ([[Sensory Clarity - 2 of 2 - No Self As Thing]] S3-S7).

Chapter 7 gives the cleanest operational rule for Feel In. The practitioner first asks whether anything emotional is present anywhere in awareness. If yes, the practitioner asks whether part of that emotion appears in the body. A clear yes is Feel In; no or not sure is treated as no Feel In for that moment ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 7 - A Science of Sensory Experience]] S6). This prevents the inner system from requiring theoretical certainty about whether a body sensation is emotional or physical.

Chapter 5 adds finer detail for thought. Conscious thought usually appears as mental talk, mental image, both together, or a temporary absence of both, which Shinzen calls a moment of mental tranquility ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 5 - Insight and Purification]] S4). Mental image and mental talk also have locations: image space, talk space, and their union as mind space. This makes thought spatially tangible, not only meaningful.

[[6 Buddhist Consciousnesses and the 12 Sensory States]] gives this tangibility claim as an explicit teaching move. Shinzen says thinking becomes easier to observe when it is divided into auditory thinking, Talk, and visual thinking, Image; each has qualitative tangibility, and each can be localized in spaces such as the head, ears, front or back of the eyes, ambient space, or body image ([[6 Buddhist Consciousnesses and the 12 Sensory States]] S2).

[[Working with Images and Image Space]] gives the most detailed current
image-space location map. Shinzen distinguishes the body/self image, the
ambient image of where one is, and the central memory-plan-fantasy image
field. Each can be worked with separately, and each can participate in the
construction of who one is, where one is, or what past/future/mythic world is
presenting itself ([[Working with Images and Image Space]] S1-S5).

That talk also broadens no-self work beyond the standard Feel/Image/Talk
triad. The image component may include a subtle visual outline of the body,
face, clothing, or self-presentation that body sensations seem to fill. When
the image and body sensations are clarified and equanimized together, Shinzen
says they can break into integrated Flow, weakening the subtle sense "I am
the body" ([[Working with Images and Image Space]] S1-S2).

[[Discrimination and Unification - Part 4 of 4]] repeats the same practice
logic in the language of tractability. Breaking thought into visual and
auditory components makes thinking tangible and spatial; what can be tracked
can become a source of fulfillment rather than suffering ([[Discrimination
and Unification - Part 4 of 4]] S7-S8).

The chapter also extends the model toward the subconscious. When explicit pictures and words settle, a practitioner may detect subtle stirring in image space and talk space; Shinzen identifies this as subconscious activity and says detection is required before it can unblock into intuition, wisdom, and creativity ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 5 - Insight and Purification]] S4, S5).

The six-consciousnesses talk gives a parallel but more compact depth frame. By becoming intimate with talk space and image space, the practitioner may notice subtle, fleeting, massively parallel undercurrents out of which conscious talk and image arise; Shinzen calls this monitoring the general activation level of the subconscious mind in real time ([[6 Buddhist Consciousnesses and the 12 Sensory States]] S5). That language is useful practice idiolect, but it should remain bounded as Shinzen's contemplative interpretation rather than a clinical model of the unconscious.

[[Taking Temperature of Subconscious Processing]] turns that depth frame into
a pattern-routed practice. After explicit Image, Talk, or emotional Feel has
quieted, the practitioner may notice subtle activation in image space, talk
space, or emotional body space. If the activation is steady, Shinzen says to
cover subjective space evenly and let awareness soak into the whole field;
if it bursts and subsides, notice the burst and the Gone after it
([[Taking Temperature of Subconscious Processing]] S2-S5). The important
boundary is that the content need not be known before the inner system
becomes workable practice material.

[[Watching Subconscious Processing]] adds an earlier and subtler branch. In
Focus In, the field may be neither All Rest nor explicit Image, Talk, or
Feel; Shinzen says the practitioner may only notice a tug toward image space,
talk space, emotional-feeling space, or all three. That tug counts as a cue
of inner-system activation and can be noted without claiming to know its
content ([[Watching Subconscious Processing]] S1-S7).

[[Five Aspects of the Five Ways - 3 of 4]] restates the same depth route
inside a no-self frame. Shinzen says explicit surface images or words may
drop out while the practitioner still detects activity "down there" in image
space or talk space; he calls that subconscious visual or auditory thinking.
The same talk distinguishes those observable undercurrents from deeper habit
patterns, or sankhara, which are contacted indirectly when a subpersonality
or habitual groove surfaces as a sensory pattern ([[Five Aspects of the Five
Ways - 3 of 4]] S4-S6).

[[The True Beauty of Your Soul]] adds the clearest oral boundary for Shinzen's
occasional "soul" language. He says soul is a loose, poetic name for the
deeper subliminal levels of Feel, Image, and Talk, not a theological or
well-defined psychological term. Surface Focus In tracks explicit images,
internal talk, and emotional body sensations; as those play out, subtler,
finer, and broader image-space, talk-space, and emotional-body undercurrents
may show up as rumbling, screen-like snow, or idling circuits. Shinzen also
says this layered system is not literally an infinite fractal and that the
number of layers is unknown ([[The True Beauty of Your Soul]] S1-S4).

The fourth poison-ivy Flow segment gives a live version of the talk-space
probe. Shinzen asks whether, beneath quiet or explicit inner talk, there is an
undercurrent of energy, rumbling, whispering, or subtle vibratory Flow. When
the student does not find much of it, he treats the absence as valid and
returns to body Flow rather than forcing a subtle Hear In object ([[Hear-In to
Mental Talk Space, Feel Flow in Body Space]] S1-S3).

The first difficult-emotion segment gives the same inner system under strong
activation. Shinzen says a large emotional eruption tends to activate all
three spaces at once: image space remembers and relives the situation, talk
space rehearses it, and Feel space carries vivid body flavors such as fear,
grief, anger, shame, impatience, disgust, or interest. The practice value is
that the components are not subliminal; they can be separated, worked with
as parts, and then reintegrated as a less binding whole ([[Turn Towards
Difficult Emotion and Challenging Feel-Image-Talk Eruptions - 1 of 2]]
S2-S7).

The second difficult-emotion segment refines Feel space itself. Shinzen says a
single emotion label can hide multiple affective-somatic flavors, and that
subtle secondary flavors are significant because they can multiply the whole
state when unnoticed. When the flavor is still too large, he divides it by
location and radial sector before returning to the larger Feel/Image/Talk
whole ([[Turn Towards Difficult Emotion and Challenging Feel-Image-Talk
Eruptions - 2 of 2]] S1-S4).

Chapter 8 turns this inner map into the main no-self model. Shinzen distinguishes broad identification with any mind-body state, narrow identification with self-referential image/talk/body emotion, and the midrange practice target of mental image plus mental talk plus body emotion ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S2-S3). He calls that midrange system inner activity: it reacts to outer stimuli, proactively spins memory, plan, and fantasy, interacts among its components, and sometimes becomes inactive ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S4).

The practice importance is that the felt solidity of "I am a separate thing" can be observed as changing with these patterns. The separate-self sense becomes particle-like when image, talk, and body emotion tangle; it becomes wave-like when [[Sensory Clarity]] untangles the components and [[Equanimity]] lets them move without interference ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S7, S10-S11).

Chapter 1 adds a relational and psychological role. Tracking inner components can reduce overwhelm in negative states and help maintain healthy boundaries around impactful others ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 1 - The Way of Thoughts and Emotions]] S2-S4). That means the inner sensory system is not only a no-self map; it is also a practical emotional and interpersonal tool.

The Flow chapter adds an integration role. Mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation are first separated, but at microscopic Flow resolution Shinzen says they can be experienced as the same vibrating impermanence. This integrates personality as a single flow rather than a solid self-thing ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 4 - The Way of Flow]] S11).

The Human Goodness chapter adds a reconstructive role. [[Nurture Positive]] uses the same inner systems as Focus In, but actively creates positive image and talk, attends to positive emotional body sensation, and then holds whichever inner system best carries the chosen positive theme ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S3-S12).

## Boundaries
This is a sensory-composition model, not a full account of selfhood. It does not replace personal history, ethical agency, relationship patterns, culture, memory, or psychological development. Its job is to show how the felt immediacy of self can be observed in present-time sensory events and how positive subjective patterns can be deliberately cultivated.

The short public definition in [[What Is the Self]] uses broader mind/body
language than the technical Focus In grid. That is useful as an entry point,
but future guidance should still distinguish physical sensation from
emotional Feel when method choice or safety depends on the difference.

The eight-state scheme is intentionally simplified. Shinzen says the components actually range along continua from rest to high intensity. Future pages should avoid treating the eight states as a rigid ontology.

The manual also warns by implication against over-literalism. Emotional body sensation can be strong, mild, vague, clear, caused, cause-unknown, or absent; emotion may not always include detectable body sensation ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 1 - The Way of Thoughts and Emotions]] S9-S10).

The Peter Marks dialogue adds a pedagogical non-ontology boundary. Peter says
he continues to teach BIFIT, separating body sensations, feeling, image, and
talk, because many beginners relate differently to physical pain and
emotional pain. He also says Shinzen is probably right technically to
separate touch/body from emotional Feel, so the tension should be read as
interface choice rather than a settled dispute about what experience really
is ([[Peter Marks on the Origins of Body-Image-Talk]] S4-S5).

Image-space practice has its own safety boundary. The three-location map can
clarify self-image, place-image, and memory imagery, but the source does not
distinguish helpful image-space work from obsessive checking, dissociation,
psychosis risk, or trauma-linked flashback processing. Its flashback model
should therefore sharpen sensory discrimination without becoming a clinical
protocol ([[Working with Images and Image Space]] S6).

The chapter also links all-three-restful moments to claims about Nothing, arising, and returning. Chapter 8 now clarifies the Gone side of that territory, but Source and no-self interpretations should still remain source-attributed unless later sources support them directly.

The monitoring talk adds a safety-relevant boundary to inactive inner-system
moments. Peace, blank, and quiet may support no-self insight when noticed
clearly, but the source does not distinguish that from sleepiness, shutdown,
dissociation, depressive blankness, or depersonalization.

Chapter 5's subconscious language should not be overread as a full clinical model. Detecting subtle image-space or talk-space activity is not the same as resolving trauma, attachment patterns, or relational repair. Likewise, Nurture Positive's use of image, talk, and body feeling is not proof that subjective construction has produced external behavior change.

The temperature talk adds the same boundary through science metaphor.
Monitoring a subtle activation field is a practice handle, not evidence that
the practitioner has diagnosed the unconscious, recovered hidden memories, or
measured literal psychological temperature, entropy, viscosity, or scalar
fields ([[Taking Temperature of Subconscious Processing]] S1-S6).

The tug source adds an even stronger nonforcing boundary. A spatial pull can
be a useful cue when it is actually present, but it should not become a
search for hidden content, a diagnosis, or a reason to manufacture subtle
activation when the field is simply restful or unclear ([[Watching
Subconscious Processing]] S5-S7).

The Five Aspects part 3 habit-groove model needs the same boundary.
Subpersonalities and conditioning cones are Shinzen's contemplative teaching
images for surface patterns pointing into depth; they should not be treated
as diagnosis, fixed inner persons, or proof that the practitioner has direct
access to all stored conditioning ([[Five Aspects of the Five Ways - 3 of 4]]
S5-S6).

The soul-language talk adds a parallel boundary. Deep Feel/Image/Talk tracking
should not become a search for a separate soul entity, a required subtle-state
attainment, an infinite excavation project, a trauma protocol, or a settled
neuroscience model. The useful practice move is to notice available subtle
inner activity without forcing it ([[The True Beauty of Your Soul]] S1-S4).

The deity-yoga construction branch adds a grandiosity boundary. Assembling an
alternate archetypal identity from Touch, Feel, Image, and Talk is useful in
Shinzen's frame because it is consciously constructed, not because it proves
possession, external deity contact, special authority, or metaphysical
identity ([[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] S3-S8).

The live talk-space probe adds a nonforcing boundary. Subtle preconscious
activity can be checked, but a practitioner should not manufacture a
vibratory layer or interpret every tune, word fragment, or quiet period as
deep subconscious Flow.

The emotional-eruption talk adds the opposite boundary. Strong activation may
make all three inner components obvious, but intensity is not proof that
turning toward is safe or complete; the source gives no clinical, trauma,
rage-behavior, grief, or self-harm criteria.

The second segment adds a granularity boundary. Finer Feel-space analysis can
increase clarity, but it should not be mistaken for a complete account of
emotion, trauma, relationship, or action. The source gives no criteria for
when radial sweeping should stop, soften, or route to support.

## Implications
For practice, emotional experience becomes workable because it can be divided into smaller sensory streams. For transformation, self becomes less thing-like when its components are observed as waves of image, talk, and body emotion rather than a single owner of experience. The Human Goodness chapter adds that those same components can be reassembled into wholesome patterns rather than only deconstructed.

This page also gives [[Sensory Clarity]] an operational target. To clarify an emotion or self-state is to know which inner components are present, how intense they are, how they interact, and how they change. Chapter 8 adds that no-self practice depends on resolution, sensitivity, and temporal sensitivity: separate the components, detect subtle preconscious activity, and notice the beginnings of self-moments ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S17).
