## Key Claims
- **S1**: Note Everything is presented as open presence structured by knowing whether attention is on visual, auditory, or somatic experience; labels are optional supports, and inner/outer and active/rest distinctions are allowed but not required.
- **S2**: Shinzen distinguishes content from completeness: content is pleasant/unpleasant or answer/no-answer, while completeness depends on concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity, opening the possibility of happiness independent of conditions.
- **S3**: Discussion periods can be treated four ways: continue formal practice, keep the technique in the background, use occasional micro-hits, or deliberately suspend intentional practice.
- **S4**: The first common Note Everything wall is that the technique can feel too effortful, conceptual, or non-meditative; Shinzen normalizes this as an early learning curve and permits choosing another method or alternating with narrower focus.
- **S5**: The second common wall is overwhelm from too much happening at once; Shinzen routes it by narrowing to one modality, re-noting the same event, or using inclusive noting.
- **S6**: Inclusive noting can cover paired or all co-occurring modalities; the None option marks the tug of sensory self/world falling away and is treated as Rest/Gone-like territory, not as an ordinary-label requirement.
- **S7**: A complete experience of the feeling-thinking self is presented as one route to liberation: self becomes less prison-like when mental image, talk, emotional body, and physicality are fully experienced with CCE.
- **S8**: Shinzen's large framing claim is mindfulness as developing attention skills to optimize human happiness; he explicitly holds the paradox that practice both cultivates improvement and reveals a perfection that was already present.
- **S9**: For an individual practitioner, structure includes at least one technique, optional method sequencing or free choice, daily stillness/motion/life cycles, periodic retreat or self-retreat, and occasional competent guide check-ins.
- **S10**: Zooming out, explicit Gone, re-noting, and inclusive noting are adjustable options that can reveal Expansion-Contraction or arising-passing, but the source treats those windows as optional rather than universal.
- **S11**: In Q&A, Shinzen does not treat ordinary object words as automatically adding problematic conceptual overlay; the deeper problem is unconscious or incomplete inner reactivity, not inner reactivity itself.
- **S12**: Craving for pleasant experience and aversion to unpleasant experience can be recycled as Image/Talk/Feel; pleasure practices are tested by whether relationship to pain improves, and pain practices are tested by whether appreciation of pleasure improves.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide could use this source to:

- teach Note Everything as See/Hear/Feel modality tracking before adding subtler distinctions;
- route early effortful or non-meditative reactions by normalizing the learning curve, narrowing the range, alternating methods, or choosing another valid technique;
- reduce overwhelm through modality narrowing, re-noting, or inclusive noting instead of trying to capture every event separately;
- treat All/None, Gone, and Expansion-Contraction as optional windows, not required attainments;
- make discussion, motion, and life-practice modes explicit rather than letting practice fade by default;
- parse craving and aversion as Image/Talk/Feel when they become distinct sensory reactions;
- test pleasure-based and pain-based practice by whether each improves the relationship to the opposite valence.

## Summary
Shinzen opens the daylong with Note Everything: let attention be drawn by the
field, identify whether the current focus is visual, auditory, or somatic, and
briefly focus on it, with See/Hear/Feel labels available but not required. The
deep rationale is complete experience. Ordinary content still varies as
pleasant, unpleasant, answered, or confused, but the degree of CCE changes how
binding that content is.

The middle of the talk is a long troubleshooting clinic for broad practice.
One wall is the feeling that tracking everything is too effortful or too
conceptual; another is the sense that too much is happening at once. Shinzen's
answers are practical: use another method, alternate with narrower focus, work
one modality at a time, re-note the same event, or include co-occurring
modalities as paired or All labels. He also lets the None option, Gone,
Expansion-Contraction, and arising-passing enter as optional windows when they
present themselves.

The final theory-and-Q&A section ties those options back to the whole system.
Mindfulness develops CCE for the broad aim of human happiness, but the term develop
also carries the already-complete side of realization. Practice needs
maintained structures: at least one technique, daily stillness/motion/life
cycles, retreats or self-retreats, and competent input. In Q&A, Shinzen uses
conceptual overlay and craving/aversion questions to clarify that the problem
is not inner reactivity or pleasure itself; the problem is incomplete,
unconscious, or un-equanimous relation to those events.
