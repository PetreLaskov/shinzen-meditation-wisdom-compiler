display of Auto Walk. Its durable value is not the choreography; it locates
Auto Walk inside a body/speech/thought output family and shows how movement
can be tasted as "just happening."

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk makes Auto Move less abstract. It shows that the practice target is
not unusual motion but the detectable automaticity of ordinary movement, and
it places that movement branch inside a larger practice family of body,
speech, thought, and all-three output.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen frames spontaneity training as output practice across bodily motion, speech action, and thought action, with Auto Move/Walk, Auto Speak/Chant, Auto Think, and all-three practice as branches.

## Practice Handles
Use Auto Walk in a safe setting by letting an ordinary walking pattern happen,
tasting the body's known reflexes, and noticing the reduced need for a
controller. The optional visual overlay is to notice expansion ahead and
contraction behind while movement continues.

## Summary
Shinzen introduces a "periodic table" ambition for meditation strategies and
places spontaneity in action as one major contemplative invention. He maps the
three gates of action onto body, speech, and thought, then names his practice
family Auto: Auto Move or Auto Walk, Auto Speak or Auto Chant, Auto Think, and
combined forms.

The demonstration teaches Auto Walk by asking the practitioner to tune into
walking the body already knows how to do. Shinzen presents the felt criterion
as reflex-like, playful, empty, and dynamic: the legs carry the body without
requiring much "you." He then shows his own style, including defocused gaze
and an optional visual Expansion-Contraction perception in which space seems
to carry the walking.
