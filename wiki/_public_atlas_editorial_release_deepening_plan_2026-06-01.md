# Public Atlas Editorial Release Deepening Plan - 2026-06-01

Status: active recommended next work

This is an internal automation plan, not evidence for Shinzen claims. It
supersedes the completed human-delta expansion queue as the next recommended
public-atlas workstream.

## Judgment

The public atlas has crossed the first threshold: it now has enough breadth,
route-tested problem entries, safety surfaces, and source-calibrated pages to
be recognizably useful. The strongest next work is therefore not another
compiler-delta expansion queue.

The next best priority is a **publication-grade editorial transmission pass**:
make the atlas feel like a serious public resource that an intelligent
beginner, advanced practitioner, meditation teacher, or careful Shinzen reader
can repeatedly use and trust.

The deeper remaining delta is not mainly missing nouns. It is the gap between
the compiler's judgment intelligence and the public reader's actual experience
of being oriented, guided, reassured, challenged, and protected while reading.

In practical terms:

- The compiler has strong routing surfaces, safety gates, source tiers,
  behavior/service checks, and high-resolution practice distinctions.
- The atlas now has many good local pages.
- The atlas still needs a stronger cross-page human reading experience:
  guided paths, foundational page depth, compact source credibility, concrete
  examples, page-specific safety, and publication-readiness rules.

## Why This Is The Highest-Value Next Work

The completed expansion plan brought forward the largest known missing public
deltas: Total Happiness aim structure, teaching as service, Auto Output,
discrimination/unification, applied-life boundaries, surface-to-Source path
mapping, and mindfulness/mysticism route clarifications.

A fresh audit on 2026-06-01 found a different pattern:

- Expansion is complete at 69 public content pages.
- The home page is a capable router, but it is still closer to a map index than
  a polished first public reading experience.
- About two dozen public pages still lack compact `Source Trail` sections,
  including important foundation pages such as concentration, equanimity,
  impermanence, Five Ways, effort, focus coverage, zooming, physical senses,
  and several hubs.
- Many pages still lack concrete examples or immediate route surfaces.
- Several important pages are short enough to be readable but not yet strong
  enough to become return-to resources for serious readers.
- Rights, quote policy, citation style, and static-site/publication posture are
  still undecided.

For a careful editor whose priority is the practitioner/reader, the question is
now:

> Does this atlas transmit the living usefulness of the compiler without
> exposing internal machinery, overclaiming, sounding generic, or leaving the
> reader unsure what to do next?

This plan answers that question by making each automation cycle improve one
reader-facing route, cluster, or editorial trust surface.

## North Star

The atlas should become a public Shinzen Practice Atlas that is:

- useful enough for a serious beginner to navigate without getting lost;
- precise enough for an advanced practitioner to recognize Shinzen's actual
  distinctions rather than generic mindfulness;
- safe enough that powerful practice language is not converted into therapy,
  status, metaphysics, medical advice, teacher authority, or self-certifying
  service;
- sourced enough that a careful reader can tell what Shinzen says, what the
  atlas synthesizes, what is editorial inference, and what is not established;
- composed enough to pass a reputable editorial read as a serious page on the
  internet.

The win condition is not more pages. It is higher future reader judgment per
minute spent in the atlas.

## Cycle Method

Each automation cycle should complete one bounded slice:

1. Name the reader job in one sentence.
2. Inspect the current route from `index.md`, `how-to-read-this-site.md`, and
   `glossary.md` when relevant.
3. Load only the active public pages and internal owner pages.
4. Decide the public action:
   - no edit;
   - line-level polish;
   - compact source trail;
   - example or route table;
   - guided-path link;
   - page-specific safety rewrite;
   - rare new page.
5. Edit the smallest page cluster that solves the reader job.
6. Run validation.
7. Update `public-atlas/README.md`, `wiki/_public_atlas_orientation.md`, this
   plan status notes, and `wiki/log.md` only if the state changed.

Do not run a generic polish pass that touches many pages lightly. A cycle
should leave a concrete reader route or page cluster stronger.

## Editorial Quality Gate

Before considering a page improved, check:

- **First screen**: Does the opening say why a human reader should care?
- **Practice handle**: Does the page tell the reader what to notice, choose,
  avoid, or verify?
- **Shinzen fidelity**: Does it preserve the Shinzen-specific distinction
  instead of flattening it into generic mindfulness?
- **Claim tier**: Can the reader tell Shinzen teaching from synthesis,
  inference, speculation, and not-established claims?
- **Safety/service boundary**: Does the page prevent the most likely misuse?
- **Example**: Would one concrete example improve discrimination?
- **Source trail**: Is there enough provenance for trust without audit
  machinery?
- **Next move**: Does `Next Reading` answer the likely next question?
- **Editorial dignity**: Would a reputable editor believe this was written for
  the practitioner/reader rather than for the compiler?
