Chapter 10 of the Basic Mindfulness manual is a design chapter. It argues that the grid can be made beautiful by compressing the sensory taxonomy into simple symbols and a memorized sequence.

## Source Snapshot
- **Path**: `raw/Shinzen Sources/five-ways/10-beauty.md`
- **Origin**: Shinzen Young, Basic Mindfulness manual.
- **Date**: No chapter-specific date in the raw Markdown.
- **Format**: Practice-manual chapter converted to Markdown, with visual diagrams only partially preserved.
- **Reliability**: Primary source for the manual's symbolic layer. Strong for mnemonic intent and symbol meanings; weak for reconstructing the exact diagrams because the raw conversion loses much of the visual layout.
- **Scope**: Defines symbols for the sensory grid, adds a mnemonic for the major categories, explains the symbolic full grid, and sets up the circular Big Picture display developed in later chapters.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The chapter returns to chapter 7's claim that a good scientific theory should be comprehensive, simple, natural, deep, useful, and beautiful, and fills in the previously deferred criterion of beauty.
- **S2**: The symbolic layer represents See, Hear, Feel, Rest, Flow, Expansion-Contraction, subjective activity, objective activity, Gone, and Positive States with simple icons intended to be language-independent.
- **S3**: The mnemonic "See, Hear, Feel; In, Out; Rest, Flow; Good To Go" compresses the grid's major categories, where "Good" points to positive states, "To" points to two Flow flavors, and "Go" abbreviates Gone.
- **S4**: The symbolic full grid includes previously explained options plus two chapter-level clarifications: a three-wave symbol for Focus on Flow and another symbol for Focus on Everything.
- **S5**: The chapter's next move is to display all important sensory categories, including Expansion-Contraction, in a simple circular form.

## Summary
The chapter does not introduce a new practice method. Its function is compression. After the full-grid inventory of chapter 9, chapter 10 tries to make the same system memorable, aesthetically pleasing, and less dependent on words.

The central contribution is a symbolic and mnemonic layer. See, Hear, Feel, In, Out, Rest, Flow, Good, Expansion-Contraction, and Gone are turned into icons and a chant-like memory sequence. The chapter then points toward a circular display that will become the life and Big Picture diagrams in chapters 11 and 12.

Because the raw Markdown preserves text better than image layout, this source page should not be used to reconstruct exact diagram geometry. It is reliable for the claim that the manual treats beauty, symmetry, and language-independent symbols as part of its compression strategy.

## Source Frame
The source wants the reader to see the Basic Mindfulness grid as not only useful but beautiful. "Beauty" here means symmetry, mnemonic power, and compact visual form, not a separate aesthetic meditation technique.

The chapter imports science-language from chapter 7, but its evidence is mostly design coherence. A critic could grant that the icons are useful and still reject the stronger suggestion that aesthetic symmetry supports the grid as a scientific theory.

## Contradictions/Tensions
