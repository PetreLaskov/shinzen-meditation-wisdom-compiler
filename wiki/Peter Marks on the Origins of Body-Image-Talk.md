## Summary
Shinzen introduces Peter Marks as the person who originated Body-Image-Talk and as a major figure in making Shinzen's approach available in Canada. Peter then gives the origin story: during a guided session, Shinzen asked what he was experiencing, Peter reported talk and picture, and Shinzen noticed the significance of picture as a separable part of thinking.

Peter says he did not arrive with a theory; he thought everyone saw pictures that way. Shinzen's contribution, in Peter's account, was to recognize the distinction and round out the system. The early form lacked the later rest categories, and Peter frames Shinzen as responsible for almost all of the mature structure.

The dialogue closes with a live disagreement about interface. Peter says he continues to teach BIFIT: body sensations, feeling, image, and talk. He thinks Shinzen is right in separating touch/body and emotional Feel at the technical level, but for many learners a knee pain and a heart pain are experientially different enough to deserve separate teaching handles. He presents this not as rejection but as an example of making the system one's own.
