Chapter 9 of the Basic Mindfulness manual is a full-chart consolidation. It shows the standard focus options available in the manual and says the three techniques can be learned in any order, need not all be learned, and can cycle together if all three are learned.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The full grid combines four themes of Noting practice: thoughts and emotions, physical senses, rest states, and Flow states.
- **S2**: The grid also includes three modality-wide focus options: Focus on See, Focus on Hear, and Focus on Feel.
- **S3**: The grid includes broad and special focus options: Focus In, Focus Out, Focus on Rest, Focus on Flow in Sense Flow and Expansion-Contraction versions, Focus on Everything, and Just Note Gone.
- **S4**: Human Goodness remains organized through Nurture Positive, with main themes, secondary themes, and modality choices of mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation.
- **S5**: The manual names three techniques - Noting, Do Nothing, and Nurture Positive - and says practitioners may learn them in any order, are not required to learn all three, and can practice them as a cycle if they do learn all three.
- **S6**: The "Spiritual Gym" metaphor frames the practice system as multiple stations, including rest-area and exercise-machine imagery, but the chart does not define that metaphor in detail.

## Summary
The chapter largely consolidates chapters 1-8 into a full grid. The table shows the 12 See/Hear/Feel by In/Out/Rest/Flow cells, the row options added in chapter 8, the column options from the earlier Ways, the all-experience and Gone options, the two Flow focus versions, and the non-Noting techniques Do Nothing and Nurture Positive.

Its small textual addition is technique optionality. Practitioners can learn the techniques in any order and do not need to learn all three. If they do learn all three, they can use them cyclically. The final gym metaphor reinforces the manual's non-linear practice architecture: different techniques function like different stations rather than steps in a single ladder.

## Source Frame
This source is a map rather than an argument. It wants the reader to see the standard Basic Mindfulness focus options as one coherent training environment.

Because it is mostly a chart, it should not be used alone to explain a technique. It routes to the earlier chapters for instruction and to chapter 8 for the full definition of Gone.
