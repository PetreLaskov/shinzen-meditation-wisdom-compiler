## Teaching Register
The talk is an advanced map and service teaching. Shinzen explicitly warns that stage language is dangerous, then still uses "final stage" as a cross-traditional pointer to a lived ordinary-life condition rather than a philosophy or suspended altered state.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk compresses several existing Gate 4-7 strands into one route: Source nonseparation should show up in ordinary sense experience; the way there is sensitivity to arising-passing and Expansion-Contraction locally and globally; and the service outcome is care for a larger identity because self and others share Zero.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says the final stage, if stage language is used, is a sense in ordinary life that self, world, and their Source are one or not fundamentally separate.
- **S2**: He translates that ordinary-life nonseparation through continuous prayer, sahaja samadhi, non-dual awareness, practice of the presence of God, Jewish creation-from-divine-nothing language, Sufi abiding, and Cloud of Unknowing imagery.
- **S3**: He says this is not worthwhile as mere philosophy; it matters as actual sensory experience while looking, moving, dressing, or scratching one's nose.
- **S4**: He gives the practice route as local sensitivity within touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, and Talk, plus global sensitivity to the whole picture, where arising is already passing, and Expansion-Contraction flattens into a line containing creation's richness.
- **S5**: He links shared Zero to service: if one experiences sharing the Zero, one cares for one's larger identity and wants to serve, joining going beyond the world with improving the world.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide could use this talk to ask whether nondual or Source language is touching ordinary sensory events: sight, sound, touch, Feel, Image, Talk, whole-field experience, and daily activity. The practical route is not to hold a nondual belief, but to notice arising-already-passing and the Expansion-Contraction shaping of each sensory moment.

For service routing, the talk asks whether Source contact widens identity into care for others. That care should remain paired with the existing service boundaries: shared-Source feeling does not by itself prove teaching readiness, ethical clarity, or beneficial intervention.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by saying stage talk is dangerous, then describes the final stage as ordinary-life nonseparation of self, world, and Source. He maps this through Christian continuous prayer and presence-of-God language, yogic sahaja samadhi, non-dual awareness, Jewish and Sufi Source language, Cloud of Unknowing imagery, and Native American six- or seven-direction sacred-space imagery.

He insists the point is not philosophy but actual sensory experience. One can look, move, and act in ordinary ways while the separation between ordinary activity and Source is not present. The practice route he names is sensitivity both locally, inside touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, and Talk, and globally, to the whole picture, where each arising is already passing and Expansion-Contraction resolves into a flattened zero-like line.

The talk closes by linking this to service. Shinzen reads a T. S. Eliot line as implying shared access to a zero outside ordinary time-space. If people experience sharing that Zero, they care for their larger identity and want to serve. Going beyond the world and improving the world are therefore intimately linked.
