## Summary
Shinzen presents a chart that summarizes self and world as three dances plus
the still point. The human dance is the three jobs: appreciate, transcend,
and improve self and world. The tools for those jobs are concentration,
sensory clarity, and equanimity, cultivated in visual, auditory, and somatic
experience.

He then turns to the dance of nature. He reads material reality through
Expansion-Contraction patterns, giving Planck-scale, cosmic-density, star,
and intermediate-scale examples. The same contrast pattern is then reflected
in the dance of numbers: addition, real scaling, stretch/squeeze, and
left/right turning in complex-number language. Shinzen compares this to a
dancer moving forward, backward, stretching, pulling in, and twirling.

The closing frame is explicitly Pythagorean. Shinzen says Pythagoras had the
revolutionary idea that the human soul's endeavor, nature, and number form a
single unity, and he presents this chart as a modern attempt to vindicate
that agenda. The still point at the center is Source, supported by the
literary line that without the still point there would be no dance.
