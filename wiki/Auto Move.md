Auto Move is Shinzen's practice for the movement side of the [[Auto Output
Practice Family]]: movement, especially walking, is allowed to happen
automatically while the practitioner detects the "just happening" quality
with concentration, clarity, and equanimity. It translates no-self and
Source-expression into motor practice without certifying impulses or conduct.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Auto Move trains mindful detection of automatic movement rather than unconscious movement; the body is allowed to move while the practitioner knows the automaticity.
- **Why this matters**: It adds an output-gate complement to Shinzen's usual sensory-input emphasis; the broader Auto family includes speech and thought, while this page owns the movement branch in detail.
- **Key tensions**: Spontaneity can be liberating practice material or a pretext for impulsiveness, task-unsafety, teacher authority, or bypassing feedback.
- **Source posture**: Probable from retreat-stream source pages and the Auto-output cluster; exact Zen, Chinese/Japanese, and physiology wording requires spot checks.

## Model
[[Auto Output Practice Family]] is the broader router. Shinzen's Auto shorts
separate output practice into movement, speech, thought, and all-three
branches, while this page keeps the movement branch from becoming overloaded
with Auto Chant, Auto Think, or Auto Everything ([[Auto Walk Demonstrated by
Shinzen Young]] S1-S2; [[Auto Everything]] S1-S4).

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] introduces Auto
Move under the Express quadrant. Shinzen frames walking or body motion as
movement that can be experienced as happening on its own, links it to
Zen/Chan spontaneity, and says it can generalize toward speech and thought
([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] S9). That source
was enough to mark Auto Move as a real practice handle, but not enough by
itself to create a durable owner page.

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] supplies the owner-page logic.
Shinzen contrasts the "bad robot" of unconscious habit with the "good robot":
ordinary automatic walking is already a remarkable coordination, but practice
begins when the automaticity is detected rather than merely lived
unconsciously ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S5). He calls the
simple version Auto Move or Auto Walk and teaches it as a way to tune into
legs, pelvis, and spine moving without a controlling self ([[Four Ways
Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S5-S6).

The CCE structure is explicit. Concentration holds attention on the
automatic quality of the movement; sensory clarity detects that quality,
even if weakly at first; equanimity accepts not knowing exactly where the
body will go or whether the instruction is being done perfectly ([[Four Ways
Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S6). Initial guessing and groping are part
of the training, not an error.

[[Being Confused is Good]] adds a compact upstream frame for spontaneity:
uncoagulated don't-know becomes dynamic spontaneity when expressed through
the body and wisdom when turned in the mind. Auto Move operationalizes only a
bounded motor branch of that larger formula; it does not make every uncertain
impulse into practice ([[Being Confused is Good]] S1-S4).

[[Shaktipat or Energy Transmission in Buddhism]] adds a lineage-transmission
context for the same motor-output branch. Shinzen says Zen training may first
train motor circuits to flow with impermanence through teacher interaction,
body language, and return to hard sitting conditions, with perception later
catching up. This strengthens Auto Move as an output-side Flow practice while
keeping teacher contact under the equals-not-disciples and real-condition
application tests ([[Shaktipat or Energy Transmission in Buddhism]] S5-S7).

The practice sits inside a larger input/output symmetry. Shinzen says one
can disappear into sensory inputs such as touch, sight, and sound, or into
motor outputs such as movement, speech, and eventually thought ([[Four Ways
Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S5). This page owns the movement branch
only.

[[Auto Walk Demonstrated by Shinzen Young]] adds the direct channel
demonstration. Shinzen asks the practitioner to tune into a motion the body
already knows how to do and taste the reflex-like fact that walking can
happen without much thinking ego; his optional visual overlay pairs the walk
with Expansion-Contraction ahead and behind ([[Auto Walk Demonstrated by
Shinzen Young]] S2-S3).

[[Shinzen Young Retreat at the Monastic Academy 05.27.2017]] adds an
adjacent output branch, Auto Speak or Auto Chant. In that stream, Shinzen
lets the speech organs self-organize during Om Mani Padme Hum chanting and
explicitly calls Auto Chant a motor-output analogue of Expansion-Contraction
([[Shinzen Young Retreat at the Monastic Academy 05.27.2017]] S12-S13). The
source is enough to route Auto Chant as adjacent to this page and to
[[Lineage Translation]], but not enough to make this movement-specific owner
page into a full speech/thought procedure.

## Practice Use
Use Auto Move when movement itself is the practice target:

- walk or move in a safe, ordinary environment;
- let the body move in a relatively automatic way, with speed optional;
- detect the fact that movement is happening without a strongly felt
  controller;
- allow uncertainty and groping while keeping light CCE;
- return to another method if movement becomes unsafe, unclear, compulsive,
  performative, or task-inappropriate.

The practice can fit inside [[Practice Cycles]] as formal motion practice, a
retreat walking period, or a bridge from stillness to life. It is not a
substitute for ordinary task attention.

## Boundaries
Auto Move is not unconscious drift. If the practitioner is simply lost in
thought while moving, the automaticity may be occurring, but the practice is
not yet being done mindfully. The practice handle is to detect the
automaticity with CCE.

It is not impulse permission. Spontaneous movement or uncoagulated-don't-know
language does not certify that an urge is wise, safe, ethical, therapeutic,
or Source-authorized. When conduct, relationships, teaching, sexuality,
money, power, harm, or role scope are involved, route through [[Source And
Service Boundary]] and [[Guidance Scope and Accountability Boundary]].

It is not the whole Auto family. Speech, thought, and combined output route
through [[Auto Output Practice Family]], [[Auto Chant or Auto Speak]], [[Auto
Think]], and [[Auto Everything]].

It is not for driving or heavy machinery. The R3 closing instruction limits
driving practice to sensory contact with driving, and the R4 close repeats
the same boundary by saying driving meditation means seeing, hearing, and
feeling the drive, not Source-facing or automatic-output practice ([[Four
Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S14; [[Shinzen Young Retreat at the
Monastic Academy 05.27.2017]] S14).

It is not a complete movement-therapy or clinical protocol. Injury risk,
dissociation, mania-like activation, compulsive movement, trauma activation,
unsafe terrain, falls, social exposure, or inability to stop route through
[[Practice Method Safety Boundary]] and ordinary support before practice
optimization.

## Implications
Auto Move makes Shinzen's no-self and Source-expression language concrete
without requiring metaphysical overclaim. The live question becomes sensory
and practical: can movement be experienced as happening by itself while CCE
and ordinary safety remain intact?

It also explains why retreat streams pair stillness and motion. Movement is
not merely a break from meditation; when bounded properly, it is another
practice venue.
