## Why This Talk Matters
The talk gives the triad a developmental and motivational texture. CCE is not just a definition of mindfulness; each factor can be tasted, grows unevenly, and can occasionally reach enough intensity around a specific phenomenon that the phenomenon is "so fully present that it was absent." The clarity section is especially important because it distinguishes unmixing, resolution, subtle-signal detection, and speed of detection.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity each grow on average over time, but local practice experience includes peaks and valleys.
- **S2**: A specific experience can receive enough concentration, clarity, and equanimity that it completes as it arises, never constellates into a thing, and becomes paradoxically present/absent.
- **S3**: Concentration has an intrinsic "in the zone" taste that Shinzen links to positive psychology's flow-state research.
- **S4**: Equanimity has a taste of knowing that the present openness reduces future suffering and releases past material.
- **S5**: Sensory clarity has an unmixing taste: simultaneous Feel/Image/Talk can arise without tangling, reducing flooding.
- **S6**: Sensory clarity also functions as resolution power, like seeing that white on a CRT screen is really red, green, and blue components.
- **S7**: Sensory clarity detects faint or subliminal sensory events, especially subtle Feel/Image/Talk that can otherwise shape perception and behavior below conscious awareness.
- **S8**: Sensory clarity includes speed of detection: practice should be able to start "on the dot" in the thrust of activity rather than requiring several minutes of settling first.

## Practice Handles
- Track whether the current difficulty is low concentration, low clarity, low equanimity, or some combination.
- Notice brief local moments where an event completes as it arises rather than demanding stable mastery.
- Learn the reward taste of each skill so practice becomes self-reinforcing.
- For emotional storms, test whether Feel, Image, and Talk can all be present without mixing into flooding.
- Treat subtle as significant: faint body emotion, mental image, and mental talk may steer behavior before they become obvious.
- Practice starting immediately in ordinary activity rather than needing a long warm-up before mindfulness becomes available.

## Summary
Shinzen says each member of the mindfulness triad grows on average over months, years, and decades, even though day-to-day experience fluctuates. At a given moment, a particular sensory event may receive a critical mass of concentration, clarity, and equanimity. When that happens, the event can be fully present and yet not fully "there" as a thing; it arises and completes without congealing.

He then describes the taste of each skill. Concentration has the "in the zone" taste and is sensorially fulfilling in itself. Equanimity tastes like the confidence that open contact with this moment will reduce future suffering and release past material. Sensory clarity has an unmixing taste: simultaneous Feel, Image, and Talk remain present but no longer tangle into a flooding effect.

The clarity discussion expands into a four-part practical model. Clarity has resolution power, like seeing red/green/blue components where "white" seemed to be a single thing. It detects faint sensory events that shape perception and behavior below ordinary awareness. It also detects quickly, so mindfulness can start immediately in daily life. Shinzen closes by treating resolution, subtle detection, and speed as dimensions of clarity's growth.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not define how to measure concentration, clarity, or equanimity.
- It does not explain how to cultivate each reward taste when it is absent.
- It does not give safety criteria for emotional storms, flooding, or subtle material becoming more conscious.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says the factors grow on average but fluctuate locally; future routing should avoid judging practice from a single day's clarity or dullness.
- It treats critical mass as possible in specific moments while also using long-term hockey-stick growth; this supports both local completion and long practice arcs.
- It frames subtle subconscious events as sensory and practice-detectable, but does not settle how this maps to clinical or cognitive-science uses of subconscious.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Critical mass" should not become a self-diagnosed attainment label.
- "Subtle is significant" should not become anxious micro-monitoring of every faint event.
- Instant detection should not be used to justify practicing in situations where explicit noting or inward attention would compromise safety.
- The future-suffering reduction taste of equanimity should not be used to bypass present relational, medical, or therapeutic needs.
