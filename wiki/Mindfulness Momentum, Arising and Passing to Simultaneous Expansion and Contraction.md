## Teaching Register
The register is advanced explanation with a transmission warning. Shinzen
first names the teacher's dilemma: explaining possible experiences can
motivate and orient students, but can also seed comparison, craving, and
poisoned practice. He then uses a terse Zen story to undercut the
enlightenment/not-enlightenment distinction before returning to technical
impermanence mapping.

## Why This Talk Matters
The durable value is that it binds the most advanced Expansion-Contraction
language to ordinary skill development and anti-comparison discipline.
Simultaneous Expansion-Contraction is not presented as something to force or
rank oneself by; it becomes available if nature is given a chance to nurture
concentration, clarity, and equanimity over time.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says teachers cannot avoid "bad karma" when describing advanced possibilities, because the same descriptions can motivate and orient practice or create comparison mind where the medicine becomes poison.
- **S2**: He retells a Zen interview in which satori is defined, for anti-comparison purposes, as the disappearance of the distinction between enlightenment and not-enlightenment.
- **S3**: If practitioners give nature a chance to nurture concentration, clarity, and equanimity over weeks, months, and years, that triple skillset may reach a critical mass that makes Expansion-Contraction practice available.
- **S4**: Shinzen reads the Visuddhimagga as first describing awareness dominated by rising and passing, and then a later pattern where the time-space volume of an arising sensory event is already simultaneously collapsing.
- **S5**: Sasaki Roshi's contribution, in Shinzen's reading, is to cast that same arising-and-passing edge as a three-dimensional paradigm in which the observer is not outside the process; Feel/Image/Talk self and Touch/Sight/Sound world arise inside the same molding volume.
- **S6**: The practice culmination is both seeing and manifesting impermanence: in ordinary movement through the world, the practitioner participates in impermanence or Source rather than observing Source from a separate place.
- **S7**: Shinzen says Source is Zero polarizing and neutralizing, while the products of Source are the moment-by-moment sensory self and world; because self and world arise between Expansion and Contraction, Source and its products are not fundamentally separable.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The opening warning is part of the instruction, not a preface. Advanced maps should be disclosed with comparison-risk discipline and with the enlightenment/not-enlightenment distinction already undercut.
- **S3**: "Mindfulness momentum" keeps the talk developmental. The advanced pattern is not a status badge; it is made plausible by long-term CCE maturation.
- **S4-S6**: The central technical move repeats the previous paradigm distinction but adds participation: the point is not only to see simultaneous Expansion-Contraction but to move, look, and act as impermanence.
- **S7**: The Source claim should be preserved as Shinzen's contemplative interpretation of sensory self/world, not promoted into independent metaphysics.

## Practice Handles
- Treat advanced-map descriptions as orientation, not comparison fuel.
- When comparison mind appears around enlightenment, notice the extra distinction being made between enlightened and not-enlightened and do not feed it as the practice goal.
- Build the conditions first: sustained concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity over time are the route by which Expansion-Contraction may become available.
- When arising-passing becomes salient, test whether the sensory event's time-space volume is also collapsing as it arises.
- Include the apparent observer in the field: Feel/Image/Talk self and Touch/Sight/Sound world are both shaped by the same Expansion-Contraction volume.
- In ordinary looking, moving, and interacting, test the shift from observing impermanence to riding or manifesting impermanence.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by naming a transmission dilemma. Teachers either describe
possible advanced experiences and how to work with them, or they do not. If
they describe them, they help by giving motivation and orientation, but they
also harm by creating comparison mind, expectation, and poisoned medicine. He
then tells a Zen story in which a roshi answers the question "what is satori"
by pointing to the disappearance of the distinction between enlightenment and
not-enlightenment. Shinzen uses the story to loosen the very comparison that
advanced maps can trigger.

He then gives the developmental route. If a practitioner gives nature a
chance to nurture concentration, clarity, and equanimity over the long haul,
that triple skillset may reach critical mass. If the practitioner then wants
to work with Expansion-Contraction in the senses, the option may be available.
He links this to the Visuddhimagga's arising-and-passing map: first awareness
is dominated by things welling up and subsiding; later, as soon as a sensory
event occupies time-space as arising, that same time-space volume is already
collapsing into simultaneous Expansion-Contraction and neutralization into
Zero.

The talk closes by contrasting observation with participation. Shinzen says
Sasaki Roshi's three-dimensional paradigm does not leave an observer over
here watching birth and death of space over there. The observer, parsed as
Feel/Image/Talk, and the world, parsed as Touch/Sight/Sound, arise inside the
same spherical volume. Vipassana may begin by observing impermanence and then
learning to ride it; Zen may force riding through lifestyle and koans before
one sees it. In the end, both seeing and manifesting impermanence matter. The
Source is Zero polarizing and neutralizing, while the products of Source are
the momentary sensory self and world that arise between Expansion and
Contraction.
