This short YouTube teaching is a Gate 6A teacher-student accountability source. Shinzen says the main issue to watch is dependence on the teacher, whether induced by the teacher or generated by the student, because liberation training should make practitioners independent and able to apply the teaching in real conditions.

## Teaching Register
The register is informal teacher-student discernment and transmission commentary. Shinzen starts from the question of whether teacher or student issues are blocking progress, then reframes the shared risk as dependence on the teacher and illustrates a training format that combines direct transmission with immediate return to self-reliant practice.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk adds the missing complement to feedback-loop ethics. The previous ethics source says teachers need feedback; this source says students need independence. Together they prevent teacher-student relationships from becoming either uncorrectable teacher centrality or dependent student devotion.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: The main obstacle to look for, whether caused by teacher behavior or student tendency, is developing dependence on the teacher.
- **S2**: The spiritual path aims at happiness independent of conditions, so competent liberation teachers do not want dependent students; they want colleagues, peers, and independent people.
- **S3**: Shinzen's own stated response to student gratitude is to say, in effect, forget the person and take the principles and skill set onward.
- **S4**: Teaching is better understood as an activity rather than a person: individual teachers are reflections or appendages of a larger teaching activity that should grow and improve over time like science.
- **S5**: Rinzai-style sanzen or dokusan can involve an intense non-linear encounter with the teacher's emptiness, but the student must then return to the meditation hall and apply it under heat, cold, bugs, fatigue, confusion, pressure, and ordinary difficulty.
- **S6**: A healthy transmission format gives the benefit of contact with the teacher while making it hard to develop dependency, because the student must internalize and apply what was received through their own effort.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The source makes dependency the shared teacher-student failure mode rather than assigning causality only to manipulative teachers or needy students.
- **S2**: Shinzen ties anti-dependency directly to condition-independent happiness, so independence is not merely social preference but part of liberation's aim.
- **S4**: "Teaching as activity" deflates teacher-person centrality without denying that transmission can occur through particular teachers.
- **S5-S6**: The Rinzai example preserves a strong transmission claim while making the transfer incomplete until the student applies it in difficult ordinary conditions.

## Practice Handles
For a practitioner, guide, or community, this talk suggests:

- Watch for increasing dependence on the teacher, regardless of whether the teacher encourages it.
- Treat gratitude as a reason to practice, apply, and transmit principles rather than to center the teacher-person.
- Evaluate whether guidance produces more independence, confidence, and peer-like agency over time.
- After a powerful teacher encounter, test the "hit" in ordinary challenge instead of turning it into reverence or story.
- Treat teaching systems as improvable activities rather than final personal possessions of a charismatic teacher.

## Summary
Shinzen says the main issue to watch in teacher-student problems is dependence on the teacher. Teachers may foster dependence through mixed altruistic and selfish motives, and students may develop dependence even when the teacher has no such issue. In either case, dependence obstructs the path because the "name of the game" is happiness independent of conditions.

He defines the competent liberation teacher by the result they want: not students as dependents, but colleagues, peers, and free people. When students ask how to repay him, Shinzen says his impulse is to tell them to forget him, take the principles and skill set, and run with them. He then depersonalizes teaching itself: there is one teacher in the universe, not a person but an activity, with individual teachers as reflections or appendages of that activity.

The Rinzai Zen example shows why this is not a denial of transmission. Shinzen describes sanzen or dokusan as an intense encounter with the master's dynamic emptiness, something like an unadvertised shaktipat, but says the important next step is that the student returns to the meditation hall and must apply what was received amid discomfort, fatigue, confusion, and pressure. The training works, when it works, because it gives both contact and self-reliant application, producing peers rather than dependent devotees.

## Source Frame
The talk is a teacher-student discernment teaching, not a general sociology of spiritual communities. Shinzen wants to preserve the possibility of real transmission while blocking teacher-person dependence. The strongest contribution is the independence criterion: teacher contact should be judged by whether it helps the student internalize the skill and stand on their own. The weakest institutional side is that dependency is named as the main issue without a full account of coercion, abuse, financial dependence, trauma bonding, or organizational power.

## Weakest Claims
- The Rinzai "transfer" or "energy of emptiness" description is Shinzen's interpretive and comparative framing; the transcript does not independently establish what is transferred or how.
- Teaching as a single universe-wide activity is mythological and archetypal language, useful for deflating teacher centrality but not a literal claim the wiki should promote.
- Dependency is named as the main issue, but some teacher-student harms may be better explained by coercion, misconduct, institutional incentives, trauma dynamics, or clinical vulnerability.

## Important Omissions
- No criteria for distinguishing healthy reliance on a teacher from harmful dependence.
- No institutional safeguards for teacher abuse, coercion, financial dependence, retaliation, secrecy, or exploitative devotion.
- No guidance for students who cannot safely "run with it" because they need clinical, community, legal, or practical support.
- No account of how to measure whether students are becoming peers, colleagues, or independent practitioners.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen strongly values teacher transmission while also saying the teacher-person should be forgotten; the balance depends on whether contact becomes student-owned practice capacity.
- The Rinzai example praises intense teacher contact but assumes the training structure throws students back on their own resources; outside that structure, the same intensity could increase dependence.
- "Forget the teacher" supports independence, but it should not be used to erase legitimate gratitude, lineage obligations, consent, misconduct reporting, or accountability.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Forget the teacher" could be misused by teachers or communities to avoid accountability, repair, or crediting real support.
- "Transmission" language could be used to inflate teacher mystique unless paired with the anti-dependency and application-under-challenge tests.
- "Happiness independent of conditions" could become indifference to ordinary relational needs unless balanced with support, feedback, and surface well-being.
