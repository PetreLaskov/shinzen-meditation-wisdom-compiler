## Why This Talk Matters
The talk clarifies why Shinzen sometimes links Source, polarity, mathematics, and future science. The durable value is not proof that mathematics models enlightenment. It is a posture: Pythagoras had an overly narrow number system, no experimental science, and no systematic concentration technology; Shinzen thinks the modern world may now have all three ingredients and therefore may be able to model both "outer dance" and "inner dance."

## Summary
Shinzen begins with Pythagoras as a civilizational ancestor who suspected a link among the material world, the inner world, and number. He says the ancient project was limited by three deficits: a too-small number system, lack of experimental method, and lack of systematic concentration technology for exploring the inner world.

He then argues that the modern situation is different. Mathematics now has a broad enough number system, the West developed the experimental method for outer nature, and the West has discovered concentration methods from the East. In his view, this creates the possibility of mathematically modeling both outer and inner experience, including the contrasts involved in Expansion and Contraction.

The talk closes with a cautious world-historical hope. Shinzen imagines that the human condition could change rapidly, globally, and dramatically for the better in the next century if this agenda matures. He frames practitioners on this path as having a small role in that possible transformation.
