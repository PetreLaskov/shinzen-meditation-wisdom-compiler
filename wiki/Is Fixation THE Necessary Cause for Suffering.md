## Summary
Shinzen begins from a conversation about necessary causes. He says a deeper layer of necessary cause may sit underneath an already recognized cause. Sasaki Roshi, in Shinzen's recollection, used fixation language for the necessary cause of suffering, presenting liberation as unlearning the habit of fixation. Shinzen finds this interesting because "fixation" differs in connotation from "craving" and suggests a more global property of how the nervous system processes events.

He then develops analogies from physical systems: viscosity, resistance, friction, Reynolds number, laminar versus turbulent flow. He repeatedly keeps the claim hypothetical. The point is not that suffering has already been modeled as turbulence, but that fixation language opens the imagination toward measurable parameters rather than only subjective descriptions.

The concrete research example is attentional blink. Shinzen describes it as an objective task measure in which holding on to one event makes the next event harder to detect, and says meditators show less of it. He does not equate attentional blink with Sasaki's fixation, but treats it as a possible clue. The final speculation is that deep craving or addiction research might someday uncover a physiological necessary condition under craving, making an intervention possible. He explicitly says he does not know what the intervention would be.
