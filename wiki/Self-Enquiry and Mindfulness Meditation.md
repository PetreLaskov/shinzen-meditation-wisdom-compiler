This edited YouTube transcript is a short Q&A-style teaching on how self-enquiry relates to mindfulness practice. Shinzen frames the two as apparently different methods that become symmetrical at depth: self-enquiry looks back toward the origin of an arising, while mindfulness of Gone watches the passing that points to the same Source.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk gives Gate 6B a precise bridge between self-enquiry and Shinzen's own system. It prevents self-enquiry from being treated as either alien to mindfulness or simply identical with ordinary observing: the shared deep target is attention turning toward Source, but mindfulness can approach that target by noticing vanishings rather than repeatedly asking where arisings come from.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Self-enquiry asks questions such as "Who am I?", "What am I?", "Who's experiencing this?", or "Where did this thought come from?", all as ways of turning awareness back on itself.
- **S2**: Mindfulness may begin as "I observe objects," but with time the habit of concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity can meditate itself, reducing the felt meditator and deliberate effort.
- **S3**: Mindfulness also develops sensitivity to impermanence, especially the passing side Shinzen labels Gone.
- **S4**: Each noticed vanishing briefly adverts attention toward Source because where something goes when it vanishes is where the next thing comes from when it arises.
- **S5**: Self-enquiry and mindfulness/Gone are symmetrical strategies: self-enquiry looks back from an arising toward its origin, while Gone watches an event vanish into the same place from which the next event arises.
- **S6**: Shinzen says self-enquiry is powerful and works well for some people, but not necessarily for everyone.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: Shinzen defines self-enquiry by its live attentional gesture, not by sectarian affiliation: turn consciousness back on itself.
- **S2**: The automaticity claim connects the talk to the observer-trap material: mature mindfulness is not necessarily an "I" doing observation.
- **S3-S5**: The symmetry is the main durable contribution. It makes Gone a self-inquiry-adjacent route without collapsing the two methods into one instruction.
- **S6**: The fit boundary keeps self-enquiry from becoming a universal superior method.

## Practice Handles
- If a practitioner resonates with self-enquiry, the core move is to ask where the current experience, thought, or experiencer comes from.
- If that inquiry feels abstract, forced, or unworkable, Shinzen's parallel route is to notice the passing of the current sensory event and let its Gone point attention toward Source.
- When mindfulness feels like an observer over here watching objects over there, treat that as an initial phase, not the final model; CCE can become self-sustaining enough that "the habit of meditating begins to meditate itself."
- In Gone practice, do not hunt for mystical events. Ordinary endings of talk, images, sounds, and sensations are enough to train the passing-side route.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by defining self-enquiry as a cross-traditional method: ask who or what is experiencing, thinking, or aware, and thereby turn awareness back on itself. He says this appears very different from mindfulness, which initially looks like a meditator observing objects.

He then identifies two changes that occur as mindfulness matures. First, concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity build momentum until the practice seems to meditate itself, with less sense of an "I" making the observation happen. Second, the practitioner becomes increasingly sensitive to impermanence, including vanishings. Shinzen names that passing-side sensitivity Gone and emphasizes that ordinary mental talk, images, sounds, and other events already vanish all the time.

The talk's central comparison is that self-enquiry and Gone approach Source from opposite sides of the same event cycle. Self-enquiry asks where A came from after A arises. Gone watches A disappear, because the place where A vanishes is also where B arises. For Shinzen, this makes a deep mindfulness practice "more or less" correspond to self-enquiry, but through the passing side rather than through repeated origin-questioning.

- Shinzen says self-enquiry and mindfulness seem very different, then shows a deep symmetry; future agents should preserve both the surface method difference and the deeper Source-cycle correspondence.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not use "turn awareness back on itself" to encourage endless cognitive self-questioning when it increases agitation or self-fixation.
- Do not treat every Gone as proof of direct Source realization.
- Do not imply that mature mindfulness requires a dramatic loss of agency; the automaticity claim depends on concentration, clarity, and equanimity actually functioning.
