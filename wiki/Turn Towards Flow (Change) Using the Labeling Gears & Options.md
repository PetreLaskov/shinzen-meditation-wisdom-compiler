## Practice Handles
- Choose the Flow label when the physical discomfort is presenting as popping, bubbling, scintillation, waves, pulses, or other change.
- Use spoken labels when a guide needs feedback, when attention is climbing a hill, or when spacing out needs strong quality control.
- Listen for label pace and tone as evidence about concentration and equanimity, not just compliance with instructions.
- Use a gentle, matter-of-fact, impersonal label voice to cue equanimity toward the sensation.
- Shift from spoken labels to mental labels only if the same equanimity tone and pacing can be internalized.
- Drop labels only when direct awareness has momentum; reinstate mental or spoken labels when spacing out returns.
- When discomfort becomes neutral or pleasant Flow, concentrate on that Flow as massage or nurture without pretending all unpleasantness has disappeared.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that voice tone can induce equanimity is a useful Shinzen practice heuristic, not an independently established psychological mechanism.
- The shift from uncomfortable itch to neutral or pleasant Flow is a live case report; it should not be generalized into a guarantee that pain or itch will become pleasant.
- The massage, nurture, comfort, and energize language could be overpromoted if detached from the student's actual report of neutral or pleasant Flow.

## Important Omissions

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen treats dropped labels as cruise control when momentum is strong, but he also says labels should be reinstated quickly when spacing out returns; no-label practice is therefore conditional, not a status marker.
- The talk turns pulsing into Flow when the student can interpret it that way, but not every new body sensation should be folded into practice without ordinary medical or safety judgment.
- Focusing on neutral or pleasant Flow can reduce the sense of a big discomfort, but it could also be misused to deny ongoing unpleasantness if CCE and ordinary responsiveness are not preserved.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not use "Flow" to relabel all pain as positive energy.
- Do not drop labels because no-label practice seems more advanced if direct awareness is losing clarity.
- Do not treat the guide's ability to hear label pace and tone as a substitute for consent, context, or support criteria.
- Do not make pleasantness the required outcome of focus on change.
