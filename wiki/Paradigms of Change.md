## Teaching Register
The register is advanced explanation and lineage translation. Shinzen moves
from early Buddhist doctrine, to Burmese body-sweeping practice, to Sasaki
Roshi's Zen polarity idiom, then returns the comparison to a concrete
practice consequence: whether the practitioner remains a flat observer of
arising and passing or becomes part of spherical Expansion-Contraction.

## Why This Talk Matters
The durable value is the paradigm distinction. The talk says Flow,
impermanence, and Expansion-Contraction are not interchangeable labels at the
same level. U Ba Khin-style activated impermanence gives a positive,
purifying, vibratory face of anicca; Sasaki Roshi's Expansion-Contraction
frame then reorganizes change as simultaneous three-dimensional force, which
can undermine the watcher standing apart from change.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says impermanence has changed through Buddhist history: early Buddhist anicca emphasizes the suffering of holding to what passes, while U Ba Khin-style body sweeping develops a positive, purifying, vibratory face of impermanence.
- **S2**: Body sweeping can reveal pleasant subtle vibratory or bubbly Flow, but can also reveal solid, intense, horrific experience; the instruction in both cases is not to attach or avert, but to continue observing.
- **S3**: Shinzen attributes to Sasaki Roshi a further innovation: diverse Flow flavors, including vibration and inward/outward pressure, can be interpreted as two fundamental patterns, Expansion and Contraction.
- **S4**: Solid pain can be analyzed as outward and inward pressure; if the outward pressure is allowed to push and the inward pressure is allowed to press, the apparent solidity can reveal Expansion-Contraction and empty space.
- **S5**: Shinzen reads the Visuddhimagga's udayabbaya stage as passing occurring as soon as arising occurs, but says Sasaki Roshi's "simultaneous Expansion and Contraction" creates a subtler paradigm shift than flat arising-and-passing observation.
- **S6**: The three-dimensional or spherical frame matters because "flat Zen" leaves an observer over here watching rising and passing over there, while simultaneous Expansion-Contraction yanks the observer into the process and dissolves the observer fixation.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1-S2**: The talk preserves a historical-practice sequence without making history the point: impermanence can be sorrowful conditionality, activated purification, or simultaneous polarity depending on the practice frame.
- **S3-S4**: The concrete pressure example matters for routing apparently solid pain or tension into Expansion-Contraction without pretending it is already pleasant Flow.
- **S5-S6**: The observer critique is the central delta. Shinzen is not only renaming arising and passing; he is emphasizing a spatial, participatory, non-flat way of knowing change.

## Practice Handles
- When experience seems vibratory, bubbly, or pleasant, observe without attaching to the pleasant activated-impermanence flavor.
- When experience seems solid, intense, or horrific, look for inward and outward pressure forces rather than assuming the event lacks Flow.
- In a pressure event, let the part that wants to push out push out and the part that wants to press in press in; the handle is permission plus clarity, not muscular control.
- When arising-and-passing language leaves a subtle watcher, test the same event as three-dimensional Expansion-Contraction of the whole field, including the sense of observer and surroundings.
- Treat Sasaki Roshi's binary lists as polarity pointers, not as literal metaphysical, moral, or gendered classifications.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by saying Focus on Change descends from anicca, but the notion
of impermanence itself has changed. In early Buddhism, impermanence is linked
to suffering because passing conditions cannot safely carry all happiness. In
the U Ba Khin body-sweeping lineage, detailed somatic scanning can reveal a
fine vibratory or bubbly Flow that feels positive and purifying, though the
same practice can also reveal intense solidity and horror. Shinzen compares
this activated impermanence to religious language such as Holy Spirit working
within, while preserving the practical instruction not to attach or avert.

He then presents Sasaki Roshi's Expansion-Contraction vocabulary as another
innovation. Many Flow flavors, including vibration and pressure, can be read
as two fundamental patterns: expansion and contraction. Even a rock-solid
pain may contain outward and inward pressure banging together. If the
practitioner lets each force do what it is already doing, the event may reveal
interdigitating Expansion-Contraction and empty space rather than mere solid
pain.

The talk closes by comparing this with udayabbaya, the stage where passing is
seen as soon as arising occurs. Shinzen says "simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction" is a subtle but significant shift because it becomes
three-dimensional and spherical. Instead of an observer here watching rising
and passing there, the observer and surroundings are drawn into the same
field of creation and annihilation by the flux and reflux of Source.
