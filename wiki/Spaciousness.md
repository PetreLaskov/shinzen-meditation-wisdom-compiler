Spaciousness is the Space theme in Shinzen's later SHF grid. It names openness around or thinness within visual, auditory, and somatic experience, and it is kept as its own category so spacious experience can be practiced whether it is stable, flowing, or polarizing into Expansion-Contraction.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Space/Spaciousness is a sensory practice dimension: openness around or thinness within what is seen, heard, or felt can be noted as See Space, Hear Space, or Feel Space.
- **Why this matters**: It prevents future routing from forcing spaciousness into Rest, Flow, jhana, Tibetan spacious awareness, or Sasaki-style Source before the live phenomenology supports that move.
- **Key tensions**: Shinzen links Space to high realization language and multiple traditions, but gives limited procedure and no clinical or destabilization criteria.
- **Source posture**: Strong for Shinzen's taxonomy and oral design rationale; only probable as a cross-tradition synthesis.

## Source Anchors
- [[See Hear Feel Introduction - Five Themes Space and Depth Boundary]] anchors the formal SHF definition: Space means openness around and/or thinness within sensory experience, with See Space, Hear Space, and Feel Space as the grid cells.
- [[How Shinzen Uses the Term Spaciousness]] anchors the rationale: Shinzen made Space independent so it can integrate formless absorption, Tibetan spacious-awareness language, and Sasaki Roshi's formulation without requiring Flow or Expansion-Contraction.
- [[Enlightenment Simultaneous Expansion and Contraction Sahej Samadhi Non-Dual Awareness]] anchors the advanced daily-life branch: vastness all around plus thinness throughout all inner and outer See/Hear/Feel is simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, and Shinzen equates that lived pattern with sahaja samadhi and true nondual awareness.
- [[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] anchors a body-based Feel Space procedure: rest can become subtle fulfillment, openness can be found around the body, thinness can be found through the body, and both can be practiced together without immediately turning Space into a realization claim.
- [[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] anchors a broader Zoom Beyond procedure: openness is explored beyond body, auditory, and visual sensory boundaries, while dropouts and endings are routed as contraction/Gone rather than proof of attainment.
- [[Shinzen Young Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic Academy - July 22, 2017]] anchors the most detailed retreat-stream Space procedure, under quality-C caution: created somatic relaxation as thinness, openness around the felt body, representations versus direct focus range, and optional visual/auditory Space extensions.

## Model
The SHF introduction adds Space to the older Basic Mindfulness grid. The later table has three modalities, See/Hear/Feel, and five themes: In, Out, Rest, Flow, and Space ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Five Themes Space and Depth Boundary]] S1). Space is short for Spaciousness, defined as openness around and/or thinness within what one sees, hears, or feels ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Five Themes Space and Depth Boundary]] S2).

As a focus family, Focus on Space can float among visual, auditory, and somatic spaciousness. The same unit gives a strong effect slogan: Focus on Space shows "the perfection that was always there" ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Five Themes Space and Depth Boundary]] S5). That phrase should remain source-attributed because the unit gives little operational detail beyond the definition.

The later dialogue clarifies why Space is independent. Har-Prakash asks whether spaciousness is just Expansion-Contraction by another name, since openness resembles Expansion and thinness resembles Contraction. Shinzen answers that there is overlap, but not one-to-one correspondence ([[How Shinzen Uses the Term Spaciousness]] S1-S4).

Shinzen says the term also points practitioners toward the formless absorptions, Tibetan spacious-awareness language, and Sasaki Roshi's space/polarity formulation ([[How Shinzen Uses the Term Spaciousness]] S2-S3). The important routing boundary is that classical formless absorption descriptions do not necessarily mention energy, Flow, or impermanence; Space therefore needs to be available as a stable, non-dynamic dimension ([[How Shinzen Uses the Term Spaciousness]] S4).

When Flow is present within spaciousness, the branch changes. Shinzen frames Flow inside a deep state as insight absorption. If Expansion-Contraction Flow is present, the vastness moves outward, thinness becomes the basis for effortless contraction, and the experience approaches Sasaki Roshi's Source formulation ([[How Shinzen Uses the Term Spaciousness]] S5).

The short sahaja-samadhi clip gives the most compressed mature version of
that branch. Shinzen says spatial expansion usually thins out at the same
time; the resulting flavor is vastness all around and thinness throughout.
When that vastness and thinness pervade everything seen, heard, and felt
inside and outside while walking around in life, he says he would largely
define the person as enlightened and names the pattern sahaja samadhi and
true nondual awareness ([[Enlightenment Simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction Sahej Samadhi Non-Dual Awareness]] S1-S4). This strengthens the
Space-to-Expansion-Contraction route but does not make every spacious state
an enlightenment marker.

## Practice Routing
Use Spaciousness when the salient feature is openness, thinness, or surrounding/within-space quality rather than ordinary active content, restfulness, or movement.

Keep three branches distinct:

- **Stable Space**: openness or thinness is present without obvious movement; stay with Space rather than forcing Flow.
- **Space with Flow**: openness or thinness is dynamic, vibrating, shifting, or impermanent; route through [[Flow]] while preserving the spacious field.
- **Space with Expansion-Contraction**: spaciousness moves outward, contracts inward, or does both; route through [[Expansion And Contraction]] and Source/polarity pages when the phenomenology is strong enough.
- **Pervasive vast-thin Space in life**: vastness all around plus thinness
  throughout all inner and outer domains while functioning in ordinary life is
  an advanced nondual branch; route through [[Source And Polarities]],
  [[Non-Dual Awareness]], [[Operational Enlightenment]], and safety/behavior
  boundaries before using attainment language.

The April daylong gives a more ordinary body-entry route. Practitioners first
selectively attend to physical relaxation, emotional peace, or absence of
sensation as Feel Rest, then explore the end of body sensation and the
beginning of openness to the right and left, front and back, above and below.
The main practice is to sense openness around the whole body, thinness or
"not much there" through rested body experience, or both at once
([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] S10). Because
the transcript is quality `B`, exact technical terms in that section should
be spot-checked before quotation.

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] gives the expanded retreat
version. Shinzen first has practitioners cover the whole body, then Zoom
Beyond the body boundary into surrounding openness, then apply the same
spatial move to auditory space and visual space. He explicitly says this is
an experience of openness, not a physics claim, and treats loss of detail as
detection clarity rather than failure when the spaciousness itself is being
known ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S12).

[[Shinzen Young Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic Academy - July 22, 2017]]
gives the most procedural retreat-stream version, but the transcript is
quality `C` and should not be used for exact phrase claims without spot
checks. The broad handle is still durable: practitioners create or find
somatic thinness by relaxing into posture and out-breath, then find openness
around the felt body by spreading attention beyond the body boundary. Mental
images of space, body, or flow may guide attention, but the intended focus
range remains the direct sensory spaciousness unless the guide explicitly
includes image or talk ([[Shinzen Young Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic
Academy - July 22, 2017]] S5-S10).

For guidance, Space should not be used as a vague synonym for "relax," "dissociate," "space out," or "be less identified." The relevant question is sensory: what openness or thinness is actually available in sight, sound, body sensation, image, talk, or emotional body?

## Boundaries
Spaciousness is not simply Rest. Rest attenuates activity into visual blankness, silence/quiet, relaxation, or peace; Space emphasizes openness around or thinness within experience. They can overlap, but the practice handle differs.

Spaciousness is not simply Flow. Shinzen explicitly keeps Space independent because Flow and impermanence may be absent. When Flow is present, it is an additional feature, not the definition of Space.

Spaciousness is not simply Expansion-Contraction. Openness and thinness can resemble Expansion and Contraction, and may become those force patterns, but the category exists so those dynamics are optional rather than required.

Spaciousness is not proof of formless jhana, Tibetan realization, Sasaki-lineage Source, sahaja samadhi, or stable nondual awareness. The dialogue and short sahaja-samadhi clip support Shinzen's translation intent and advanced routing vocabulary, not doctrinal equivalence or realization certification.

The safety boundary remains underdeveloped. The sources do not distinguish spaciousness from dissociation, depersonalization, shutdown, dullness, visual defocus, or emotional numbing. High-intensity, formless, cessation-adjacent, or destabilizing Space reports route through [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].
