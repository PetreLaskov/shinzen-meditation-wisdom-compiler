## Teaching Register
This is procedural instruction with an advanced-practice caution. Shinzen teaches a concrete Noting option, but repeatedly frames it as optional, subtle, and non-default.

## Why This Talk Matters
Part 1 made Noting options a Gate 1 troubleshooting layer through label mode, voice tone, pacing, repetition, and repeat-until-Gone. Part 2 completes the spatial option: when an event persists long enough to focus on, a practitioner can leave attention unforced, narrow to a small region, widen to the whole event, or combine local intensity with broad body awareness.

## Key Claims / Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: A sensory event that persists for the focus phase is extended in time and space; body sensation is easiest to understand, but all sensory categories can have spatial extent, even when that extent is vague or not well defined.
- **S2**: The default for attention inside the spatial scope of the acknowledged event is not to control attention.
- **S3**: Zooming in intentionally focuses on a small part of the phenomenon, often a weaker perimeter region around pain or intense emotion, so it may be easier to open completely to that part than to the whole event.
- **S4**: Zooming out spreads attention broadly over the whole sensation or as much of it as can be detected; Shinzen says this can sometimes dissipate intensity by giving it more volume.
- **S5**: Zooming in and out at the same time typically means focusing on the most intense local point while spreading awareness as broadly as possible through the body.
- **S6**: Shinzen claims much suffering is not in local intensity but in resistance to subtle spread through the body, including spread below the threshold of surface awareness.
- **S7**: Simultaneous local-global zooming is said to "grease the rails" for spread to occur without resistance, even when the spread is not consciously detectable.
- **S8**: With time, Shinzen says this option can produce a rhythm of splash, ripple through the body, radiation beyond the body, and release, interpreted as a taste of purification in which a quantum of potential suffering is released.
- **S9**: The option can initially feel overwhelming or like loss of control because the mind is no longer occupying its usual ordering role; Shinzen reassures that another, more primordial ordering principle can become apparent.
- **S10**: Shinzen explicitly says the practitioner never has to use this option; it is subtle and complex, while the default remains not controlling attention.

## Practice Handles
- First check whether the event persisted after acknowledgement. If it vanished immediately, this talk is not the relevant branch; the earlier branch is Gone.
- When the event persists, start from the default of not controlling spatial attention.
- Use zooming in when the whole event is too much but a small, weaker, local part can be opened to with more CCE.
- Use zooming out when broad spatial holding may reduce pressure or integrate the whole field.
- Use simultaneous zooming in/out only when it seems appropriate: localize the most intense point while spreading awareness as widely as possible through the body.
- Treat vague boundaries as workable. The spatial extent of a sensation need not be cleanly mapped.
- Watch for overwhelm or loss-of-control impressions as a known risk of local-global zooming, not as proof that the option must be continued.
- Do not make zooming a requirement. Shinzen says never using it is fine.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by locating zooming inside the focus phase of Noting. After an event has been acknowledged, it may persist long enough to focus on. If it does, it has temporal duration and some spatial extent. Body sensations make the point easiest: an emotional or painful body sensation may have a central intensity, weaker border regions, subtle pings elsewhere, or no clearly detectable spread. The boundaries may be vague, but the event still has spatial scope.

The default is not to control attention within that spatial scope. Zooming is an option, not the essence of Noting. Zooming in narrows attention to a small part of the event, often a less intense perimeter region that is easier to allow. Zooming out spreads attention over the whole event or as widely as the event can be detected, which Shinzen compares to reducing pressure by increasing volume.

The most subtle option is zooming in and out at the same time. In body practice, the practitioner intentionally contacts the most intense local point while spreading awareness through the whole body. Shinzen says local sensations often have global spread, including spread below awareness, and that resistance to this subtle spread accounts for much suffering. Local-global zooming is presented as a way to let spread occur without resistance, eventually taking on a splash-ripple-radiate-release rhythm and the taste of purification.

## Source Frame
The source is a practice-mechanics lesson embedded in Shinzen's purification frame. Its strongest evidence is procedural: the definitions of zooming in, zooming out, and simultaneous local-global zooming, plus the non-default status of control.

The more speculative layer is the causal and energetic explanation. Shinzen says suffering often comes from resistance to subtle spread below awareness, compares zoomed release to a raindrop's energy dissipating into the universe, and interprets the resulting flavor as a quantum of suffering potential released forever. Those claims matter for his teaching system but should remain source-attributed.

## Weakest Claims
- The claim that most suffering is resistance to subtle spread below awareness is central to Shinzen's explanation but not independently evidenced here.
- The raindrop, conservation-of-energy, entropy, and infrared-radiation analogy is evocative teaching imagery, not evidence that psychological purification literally follows physical energy dissipation.
- The claim that a quantum of potential suffering is released forever should remain a source-attributed purification claim, not a verified clinical or empirical mechanism.
- The reassurance that an overwhelm or loss-of-control impression is actually entry into a primordial ordering principle is a contemplative teaching frame, not a safety guarantee.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not give criteria for when to choose zooming in, zooming out, simultaneous zooming, no spatial control, switching methods, stopping, or seeking support.
- It does not define safe duration, dosage, posture, or setting for local-global zooming with intense pain or emotion.
- It does not distinguish ordinary body sensation from trauma activation, panic, medical pain, dissociation, mania, psychosis risk, neurological symptoms, or unsafe somatic intensity.
- It does not explain how a guide should evaluate reports of overwhelm, loss of control, shaking, or perceived purification.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Spatial control is useful enough to teach in detail, but the default is explicitly non-control; zooming should therefore be read as an option, not as a standard that replaces simple Noting.
- Zooming in can reduce intensity by choosing a smaller contact point, while simultaneous zooming deliberately contacts the most intense point and broadens through the body; those are different regulation strategies and should not be collapsed.
- The source says local-global zooming can help overwhelm mature into purification, but also says it may initially feel overwhelming or like loss of control; the safety line is not operationalized.
- The purification analogy points toward irreversible release, while the talk provides no behavior, support, or long-term evidence that such release has occurred.

## Easily Misused Claims
- Do not make zooming, especially simultaneous zooming, a requirement for correct Noting.
- Do not use local-global zooming as a universal prescription for trauma, panic, medical pain, or destabilizing body sensations.
- Do not treat shaking or overwhelm as proof of purification.
- Do not use the raindrop analogy as a scientific explanation of suffering release.
- Do not override the default of non-control when simple contact is working.
