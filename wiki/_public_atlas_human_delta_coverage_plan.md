# Public Atlas Human Delta Coverage Plan

Created: 2026-05-31

Status: complete as of 2026-06-01

This is the governing implementation plan for converting the strongest
remaining compiler-to-atlas deltas into public, human-usable atlas material.
It is not evidence for Shinzen claims. Use the internal compiler as the
back-office judgment system, but optimize the public artifact for readers who
need orientation, practice discrimination, safety boundaries, and next moves.

## Objective

Extensively cover the highest-value internal compiler deltas in the public
atlas without turning the atlas into the compiler.

The internal `wiki/` is optimized for future agent reasoning: source posture,
uncertainty, claim routing, contradiction preservation, and durable memory.
The public `public-atlas/` should be optimized for human use: clear routes,
plain openings, concrete practice examples, decision surfaces, safety
boundaries, and trustworthy source posture.

The automation should therefore treat each compiler delta as raw product
intelligence. Its job is to ask:

1. What human reader problem does this internal distinction solve?
2. Should it become a new public page, a section, a table, a route update, a
   glossary entry, or only a source-trail note?
3. What must a reader be able to notice, choose, avoid, or verify after
   reading it?
4. What safety, behavior, consent, support, or claim-tier boundary must travel
   with it?
5. What links make the reader's next move obvious?

## North Star

The win condition is not maximum source coverage. It is a public atlas that a
serious practitioner, Shinzen student, meditation teacher, or careful
cross-tradition reader can actually use.

Optimize for:

- reader routes from live questions, not internal taxonomies;
- concrete practice discrimination over abstract completeness;
- compact source posture over audit machinery;
- examples, tables, and route tests where they improve choices;
- safety and service boundaries as first-class public content;
- links that answer the likely next question;
- page openings that reveal the human use of the page in the first screen.

Avoid:

- copying internal frontmatter fields, claim IDs, model deltas, or source-page
  audit sections;
- making pages because an internal concept exists;
- publishing dense compiler synthesis without a reader job;
- hiding uncertainty, safety, or service boundaries for smoother prose;
- letting Total Happiness, Source, no-self, or service language become
  authority claims.

## Phase Method

For each priority row:

1. Name the reader job in one sentence before editing.
2. Inspect the current public coverage.
3. Load the internal owner page and high-value neighbors.
4. Decide the public form:
   - new page;
   - substantial section;
   - compact table;
   - glossary entry;
   - index route;
   - next-reading link;
   - source-trail clarification.
5. Draft the public material in ordinary Markdown.
6. Add route links only where they improve a reader's next move.
7. Add examples where the distinction otherwise remains abstract.
8. Add safety, scope, consent, service, or claim-tier boundaries where the
   topic could be misused.
9. Run reader-route tests.
10. Validate and update handoff surfaces.

One automation cycle may complete a small phase or one bounded slice of a
large phase. If the work is too large, leave a precise status note in this
plan and in `wiki/log.md`.

## Human Page Design Rules

A good public page or section should answer four questions quickly:

1. Why would a human reader care?
2. What should they notice in experience, conduct, or study?
3. What should they not infer?
4. Where should they go next?

Use this default public shape unless a better reader structure is earned:

1. Plain opening claim.
2. Why this matters.
3. Core map, table, or distinction.
4. How it shows up in practice or life.
5. Common confusions.
6. Safety, conduct, or scope boundary.
7. Source posture.
8. Source trail.
9. Next reading.

Keep source trails short. A reader should get trust and orientation, not a
source audit.

## Reader Route Tests

After each phase, test from `public-atlas/index.md`,
`public-atlas/glossary.md`, and the most relevant hub page.

Use these standing reader questions:

1. "What is Shinzen's system for?"
2. "Which practice should I try next?"
3. "Is ordinary happiness part of the path?"
4. "How do behavior change and service fit meditation?"
5. "Should I teach, trust this teacher, or interpret practice influence as
   service?"
6. "What if spontaneous movement, speech, or thought shows up?"
7. "Is analysis the opposite of unity?"
8. "How does practice relate to illness, sleep, sexuality, birth, substances,
   retreat aftereffects, or public suffering?"
9. "Does Source contact prove love, ethics, science, or metaphysics?"
10. "Where are the limits of what this atlas can responsibly say?"

Expected result:

- Each completed delta is reachable within one or two clicks from a plausible
  reader start.
- Advanced or risky topics route through safety, behavior, support, and
  claim-tier pages.
- The atlas gives a next move without pretending to be a teacher, therapist,
  doctor, or authority over the reader's life.

## Public Delta Standards

Each completed delta should leave behind:

- a clear public artifact or explicitly documented decision not to create one;
- updated `Next Reading` links where useful;
- index or glossary routing if the delta changes first-pass navigation;
- concrete examples when needed for practice discrimination;
- safety/scope language when the topic touches intensity, authority, clinical
  risk, conduct, service, or metaphysical overreach;
- a compact source posture section or source trail;
- validation results in `wiki/log.md`.
