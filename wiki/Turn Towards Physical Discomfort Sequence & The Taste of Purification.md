## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide can use the talk as a turn-toward checklist:

- Name the physical challenge's components before choosing an object.
- Start with the least intense component, not necessarily the worst sensation.
- Check Feel/Image/Talk reactions early, because aversion, unwillingness, and freak-out develop there.
- Then look for global spread in body space.
- Use zooming in for smaller parts and zooming out for the whole spread.
- Work gradually toward the local intensity after momentum has developed.
- If the field starts showing waviness, bubbliness, or Flow, include touch and reactions together rather than forcing them back into separate targets.
- Treat purification taste as a motivating signal inside Shinzen's frame, not as proof that continuing through pain is medically or psychologically safe.

## Summary
Shinzen begins by defining "it" in physical discomfort: local uncomfortable touch, possibly global spread, and possibly Feel/Image/Talk reactions. He generalizes the principle to any sensory challenge but immediately corrects a common interpretation. Turning toward does not mean one must go straight to the most horrible, intense, solidified part. His preferred starting point is the weakest link, the least intense component.

For him, the least intense component is often the early Feel/Image/Talk reaction, not the physical touch. He emphasizes that freak-out, unwillingness, and refusal to continue practice occur in Feel/Image/Talk space, so he wants momentum there before entering the strongest touch. He then looks for subtle global spread, uses zooming in and zooming out, and finally works with the strongest local intensities. By that time, the discomfort may have begun to break into Flow.

At the Flow stage, Shinzen describes touch plus Feel/Image/Talk integrating into one body-mind field of energy. If this works, the practitioner tastes purification: the present moment feels productive, as though future suffering is being reduced and past pain is being worked out. He treats that taste as a major practice watershed because mindfulness becomes immediately rewarding. The talk closes by distinguishing the relatively easier complete experience of uncomfortable touch from the harder complete experience of uncomfortable Feel.
