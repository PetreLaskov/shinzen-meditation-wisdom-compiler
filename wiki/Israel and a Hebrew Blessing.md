## Summary
Shinzen explains that the chant he used after loving-kindness was Hebrew, a peace blessing often associated with Oseh Shalom. He first disambiguates "Israel": for him in this context it does not primarily mean the modern state, the land, or the Jewish people, but anyone struggling to climb the ladder toward Source. He extends that to all humanity.

He then explains, roughly, that Semitic languages build words from consonantal roots. The root behind shalom and shalem lets him connect peace with completion. His main teaching move is direct: peace comes when something is completed, and this is how complete sensory experience works.

The final move translates the blessing into Shinzen's Source vocabulary. Completed sensory experience is said to move from particle to wave, return to Source, and reveal true rest or rich nothingness. He compares that to Nirvana as ultimate rest, cessation, and total fulfillment. The result is a Jewish blessing used as a loving-kindness closing and as a compact expression of completion, Source, and universal service.
