## Key Claims
- **S1**: The daily rhythm recommended in the source includes formal stillness most days, with a recommended minimum of 10 minutes; formal motion most days, also with a recommended minimum of 10 minutes; and life practice most days through at least 3-6 micro-hits plus background practice with no specified minimum.
- **S2**: The season-to-season rhythm includes at least one week-long residential retreat or equivalent each year and at least one yearly check-in with a competent coach; the source treats monthly participation in the Home Practice Program for a year as equivalent to a week-long residential program for many people.
- **S3**: Optional accelerators are duration training, trigger practice, and motion challenge sequences.
- **S4**: Formal practice may be organized by mostly using one focus method, following a set sequence of methods, or freely shifting among methods.
- **S5**: Focus-method choices can be motivated by interest, opportunity, and necessity; should not be driven by craving, aversion, or unconsciousness; and need not be made into a big deal because every option develops concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity.
- **S6**: The free-shifting example moves from Focus on Everything to Focus on Rest, then to a customized Focus In using strongly spoken labels, inclusive emphasis, zooming out, and explicit Gone during rage, and finally to Nurture Positive in Feel Space using Flow to radiate lovingkindness.
- **S7**: The appendix says the pre-2015 system, formerly Basic Mindfulness, organized noting in a complex grid; the newer system is essentially similar but integrates the bulk of noting techniques into the simpler See Hear Feel approach, with the grid still present, somewhat extended, and hidden inside the simplifying paradigm.
- **S8**: Flexible labeling is presented as the breakthrough innovation: compared with the previous system, it covers more territory while requiring fewer words, though practitioners may need practice dropping specifiers such as In, Out, and Rest.
- **S9**: Old bisyllabic terms such as See In, Hear Out, and Feel Rest still function as convenient shorthand and may still be used as labels; the old system is described as upwardly compatible with the new one.
- **S10**: The current formulation adds a standardized starting point, the Spaciousness theme, simplified Nurture Positive, optional rather than automatic explicit Gone, and unchanged Do Nothing.
- **S11**: Spaciousness is defined through contacting space around each modality as a form of expansion and/or contacting thinness within each modality as a form of contraction.
- **S12**: Nurture Positive is simplified from six themes to four themes Shinzen says he has found most useful: Affect, Behavior, Cognition, and Ideal, summarized as ABCD with D standing for Ideal or Vajrayana-style deity yoga.

## Summary
The unit first summarizes the practice ecology Shinzen attributes to *An Outline of Practice*. Daily practice should include stillness, motion, and life practice; yearly practice should include retreat or equivalent and a competent coach check-in; and accelerators such as duration training, trigger practice, and motion challenge sequences can be used as needed.

It then gives a practical rule for formal sessions. A practitioner may stay with one focus method, cycle through a sequence, or shift freely according to what is happening. The source's seven method-choice principles are deliberately light: choose by interest, opportunity, or necessity; avoid choice driven by craving, aversion, or unconsciousness; and do not inflate choices because every option trains the same core CCE skills.

The appendix is the transition document for old and new terminology. The newer Unified Mindfulness version is not a rejection of Basic Mindfulness. It keeps the grid, extends it, and hides it under flexible See/Hear/Feel labels. Flexible labeling is the main compression mechanism. The old terms remain available as shorthand and optional labels, Nurture Positive is simplified, Gone becomes an option rather than an automatic feature of noting, and Do Nothing remains unchanged.

## Source Frame
This is a closing organization-and-transition unit. It wants the reader to leave with a practice rhythm, a low-friction approach to choosing methods, and confidence that the new SHF interface is compatible with the older Basic Mindfulness system.

The strongest claims are internal and procedural: the daily/yearly rhythm, three formal-practice organization styles, seven choice principles, flexible labeling as the simplifying mechanism, old terminology as valid shorthand, and the specific list of new-system changes.

The weaker claims concern adequacy and safety. The source invokes *An Outline of Practice* without supplying its full definitions, says a year of monthly Home Practice Program participation counts as week-long retreat equivalent, names a competent coach without defining competence, and treats every focus option as developing the same core skills without specifying method-specific risks or contraindications.

## Weakest Claims
- The Home Practice Program equivalence to a week-long residential retreat is asserted as practical guidance, not demonstrated with criteria for intensity, support, setting, risk, or outcomes.
- "Competent coach" is load-bearing but undefined.
- The claim that every option develops the same core skills is useful for reducing choice anxiety, but it can understate method-specific effects and risks such as Gone destabilization, Do Nothing spaciness, positivity bypass, or turn-toward flooding.
- "Whatever works" is consistent with Shinzen's pragmatic style, but without outcome and safety criteria it can become too permissive.
- The example of radiating lovingkindness to a loud neighbor and all creation through Nurture Positive in Feel Space is a practice illustration, not evidence that the interpersonal effect occurs.

## Important Omissions
- The source does not include the full *An Outline of Practice* definitions it tells readers to consult.
- It does not define coach competence, retreat contraindications, Home Practice Program requirements, or how to adapt the yearly rhythm for high-risk practitioners.
- It names duration training, trigger practice, and motion challenge sequences without explaining dosage, sequencing, supervision, or stopping criteria.
- It does not provide diagnostics for choosing one method, a sequence, or free shifting, beyond the seven broad principles.
- It does not explain how to decide when the newer compact labels are too ambiguous and the older bisyllabic terms should be restored.
- It reduces Nurture Positive themes but does not give full updated instructions for the simplified technique.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The new SHF interface is simpler because it uses fewer words, but it remains dependent on a hidden and extended grid; simplicity therefore requires implicit structural knowledge.
- Method choice is framed as not a big deal, yet the same unit names retreats, coaching, trigger practice, duration training, and challenge sequences that can materially affect safety and outcomes.
- Old bisyllabic terminology is no longer standard, but it remains valid shorthand and acceptable labeling. Future agents should preserve this as upward compatibility rather than treating old and new systems as rivals.
- Explicit Gone moves from standard feature to optional feature, which reduces beginner load but can also obscure one of Shinzen's major impermanence routes if not taught later.
- Every option trains CCE, but options also emphasize different sensory territories and failure modes; the sameness claim should not erase routing distinctions.
