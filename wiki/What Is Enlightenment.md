## Teaching Register
This is a concise Q&A definition. Shinzen begins with ordinary phenomenology, asks the listener to verify thoughts and body sensations, then asks them to imagine the same contents without immediate identity capture. The register is explanatory and evocative rather than technical instruction.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk opens Gate 6A with a usable operational handle: enlightenment is not framed as the deletion of thought, body sensation, or human functioning, but as freedom from compulsory identification with them. That handle can route later map, ethics, and downside talks without treating enlightenment as perfection, permanent blankness, or metaphysical proof.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Ordinary experience includes thoughts and body sensations, and there is a tendency to identify each as "me."
- **S2**: Enlightenment can be imagined as thoughts and body sensations continuing while identity is no longer immediately trapped by them.
- **S3**: After enlightenment, identity becomes elastic: mind and body remain a comfortable home rather than a prison, and identity can move beyond them.
- **S4**: Shinzen calls this liberation because the practitioner is set free from being locked in the mind-body location.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The definition starts from trackable inner sensory material rather than doctrine, making enlightenment continuous with [[No-Self And Personality]] and the inner-system model.
- **S2**: The key move is non-capture, not content suppression: thoughts and body sensations can persist without owning identity.
- **S3**: The "home, not prison" image preserves ordinary embodiment while opening larger-identity language.
- **S4**: The still-point and universe language should be preserved as Shinzen's evocative public explanation, but calibrated as metaphorical in this source.

## Practice Handles
A practitioner or guide can use this talk to ask: are thought and body sensation present, and is identity being automatically claimed by them? The practice direction is not to remove the contents, but to clarify and allow them enough that identity is less compulsively located in them.

The talk also gives a safety-sensitive check on enlightenment language: if a claim of liberation implies contempt for the body, denial of thought, or inability to inhabit ordinary life, it departs from this source's "comfortable home" image.

## Summary
Shinzen answers "what is enlightenment?" by pointing to ordinary thoughts and body sensations. He says people usually identify with those events as "me." He then asks the listener to imagine that thoughts and body sensations continue, but identity no longer becomes trapped by them.

He describes post-enlightenment identity as elastic. The mind and body remain available as a home, but not as a locked location. Identity can move into other minds and bodies, merge with them, embrace the universe, or rest at a still point beyond time and space. He explicitly marks the Big Bang phrase as metaphorical, then concludes that this freedom is why enlightenment is called liberation.

## Source Frame

The strongest source-backed claim is about identity non-capture; the cosmic and still-point images are oral teaching language rather than independently evidenced metaphysics.
