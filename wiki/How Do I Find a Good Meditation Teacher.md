This short Q&A-style source answers how to find a good meditation teacher by reframing "teacher" as levels of teaching activity. Its durable contribution is the fallback rule: if a practitioner cannot find a master-level teacher they can relate to, step down through professional, paraprofessional, descriptive, and subtle teaching rather than treating all teaching roles as equivalent.

## Teaching Register
The register is practical Q&A. Shinzen is not giving a credentialing checklist; he is giving a simple map of teaching activity so a seeker can choose the best available relationship without inflating every practitioner into an explicit guide.

## Why This Talk Matters
The talk converts the broader teaching-as-service ladder into a teacher-finding heuristic. It preserves the value of practice-changed presence and coherent description, but it also makes explicit teaching, professional teaching, and enlightenment-edge support progressively more competence-sensitive.

## Key Claims
- **S1**: Shinzen says he thinks less in terms of the noun "teacher" and more in terms of the activity of teaching, which he broadly classifies as subtle, descriptive, and explicit teaching.
- **S2**: Subtle teaching happens automatically when practice changes a practitioner's presence, body language, breathing, "vibe," and behavior; every practitioner therefore teaches subtly, but this is not the same as giving guidance.
- **S3**: Descriptive teaching happens when a practitioner can coherently describe what they do in words an average person can understand; Shinzen's local phrase is that what is coherent is compelling.
- **S4**: Explicit teaching means giving techniques, answering questions, offering detailed guidance, and monitoring progress; Shinzen says explicit teachers may include paraprofessional facilitators and full-time professional teachers.
- **S5**: Among full-time teachers, Shinzen names a special class able to handle classical enlightenment and occasional associated pathologies; his practical recommendation is to find such a teacher if possible and relatable, otherwise step down to professional, paraprofessional, descriptive, or subtle teaching.

## Load-Bearing Teaching Moves
- **S1**: The anti-status move continues: teaching is an activity with levels, not a sacred identity possessed by a person.
- **S2**: Subtle teaching is valuable but easy to misuse; practice-changed presence and behavior may inspire others but should not be treated as authorization to guide.
- **S3**: Coherent description becomes a legitimate middle level between silent example and formal instruction.
- **S4**: Explicit teaching carries responsibility for technique, questions, progress monitoring, and role competence.
- **S5**: The source adds a seeker-facing fallback rule: do not make master-level support mandatory if unavailable, but do prefer competence appropriate to the practice edge.

## Practice Handles
For a practitioner seeking a teacher:

- Ask what level of teaching is actually being sought: subtle example, coherent description, explicit instruction, professional guidance, or enlightenment-edge support.
- Prefer a teacher competent with classical enlightenment and related edge cases when those issues are live and when rapport is possible.
- If that is not available, use the highest available lower level without pretending it supplies the higher one.
- Treat behavior improvement and coherent explanation as useful signs, but keep guidance, monitoring, and pathologies within competence boundaries.

## Summary
Shinzen opens by replacing the noun "teacher" with the verb or activity "teaching." He names three broad levels: subtle teaching, descriptive teaching, and explicit teaching. Subtle teaching happens as practice changes how a person carries themselves, breathes, looks, behaves, and affects others; in this sense every practitioner teaches through presence and behavior.

Descriptive teaching begins when a practitioner can coherently describe their own practice in ordinary language. The person is not proselytizing or formally instructing; they are explaining what they do with discomfort, emotional challenges, formal practice, and its effects. Shinzen says coherent description is compelling because it makes the practice intelligible.

Explicit teaching begins when a person gives techniques, answers questions, gives detailed guidance, and monitors progress. Shinzen then stratifies explicit teachers into paraprofessional facilitators, full-time professional teachers, and a special class of full-time teachers who can handle classical enlightenment and occasional associated pathologies. The practical recommendation is to find a teacher in that highest class if one can relate to them, and otherwise step down through professional, paraprofessional, descriptive, or subtle teaching.

## Source Frame
The source is a selection heuristic and teaching-level map, not a complete teacher-evaluation system. Shinzen explicitly values all levels of teaching, but the ladder should be read as increasing responsibility and competence. The talk's pathologies language is brief and should be routed through the existing DPDR, dark-night, clinical, and guidance-scope boundaries rather than expanded into a meditation-only protocol.

## Weakest Claims
- Subtle changes in body language, odor, pheromones, breathing, eyes, or "vibe" are not independently verified by this source and should not become attainment or competence claims.
- The master-level category is useful as a competence aspiration but underdefined: the talk does not say how to verify ability with classical enlightenment or associated pathologies.
- Shinzen's own transition into explicit teaching without formal training is autobiographical context, not a general recommendation that training is optional.

## Important Omissions
- No concrete ethics standards, consent rules, privacy safeguards, supervision requirements, lineage authorization, clinical referral thresholds, or grievance processes.
- No method for verifying claims that a teacher can handle classical enlightenment, DPDR-like distress, dark-night distress, psychosis/mania-like instability, or other pathology-adjacent states.
- No discussion of cost, accessibility, power imbalance, community reputation, misconduct history, adverse student outcomes, or cultural/lineage fit.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- The talk says every practitioner teaches subtly, but also implies that explicit guidance and enlightenment-edge support require much higher competence.
- Shinzen says explicit teaching usually needs training but then says his own explicit teaching began experimentally without training; this should remain autobiography, not a standard.
- The teacher-selection advice values relatability as well as competence, creating a real tradeoff between rare expertise and workable relationship.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "Everyone is a subtle teacher" could be inflated into authority, aura claims, or pressure to teach before there is competence.
- The master category could become status-seeking unless paired with behavior, student-results, feedback, clinical referral, and anti-dependency checks.
- Coherent description could become covert proselytizing or premature instruction if the speaker starts monitoring another person's practice without role clarity.
- "Associated pathologies" should not be treated as a promise that a meditation teacher can replace clinical support.
