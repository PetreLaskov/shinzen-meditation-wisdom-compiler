## Summary
Shinzen begins by defining teaching as fostering extraordinary happiness in others. Because practice changes a person, every practitioner is already teaching at the subtle level: others may sense where the practitioner is coming from, first those who know them and eventually others at a subtler level.

He then names an explanatory or descriptive level. When someone asks what the practitioner does, a coherent description can itself transmit something useful: the path becomes clear enough that another person may find it desirable without being sold on it. Explicit teaching is stronger: offering technique, concepts, life application, questions, encouragement, sequential lessons, groups, or retreats.

The final section stratifies explicit teachers. Paraprofessionals facilitate; professionals make teaching their work; a special subclass can deal competently with classical enlightenment, sudden enlightenment experiences, and rare enlightenment/DPDR mixtures. Shinzen then distinguishes a Buddha-like master as one who discovers something new and important about enlightenment and makes it available in a way that changes human history, possibly as a team effort. The series closes by adding support for teaching to the Total Happiness map.
