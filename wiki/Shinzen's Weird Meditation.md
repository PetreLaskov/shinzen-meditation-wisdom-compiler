- **S2**: Holding color/form as paper-thin patches created an urgent need, compared to needing to breathe, to turn those patches into people, room, and objects.

## Summary
Shinzen reports that, while guiding others, he was doing his own visual practice rather than following the guidance he was giving. He narrowed visual aperture enough to see vivid color patches while suspending the usual move that turns those patches into people, room, and objects. The patches appeared bright, paper-thin, and close, and they created a bodily urgent need to objectify them.

He then analyzes that urge. One layer was subtle inner processing: beneath organized surface Image, Talk, and Feel, there were multi-scale, tangled associational spreads trying to make sense of the scene. The deeper driver was fear. He describes a hot, paralytic, incandescent terror cutting through the system; because years of practice let it move as Flow, the fear also had a blissful, pleasurable quality.

The talk widens into lineage and analogy. Shinzen recalls Sasaki Roshi telling him to reach the point where he no longer needed to make an object out of self or world, and says he could see in Roshi's body language that such freedom did not block enjoyment or effective action. Later, the chapel became every church, synagogue, mosque, and related sacred space in a tangible way, which Shinzen interprets as inner and outer visual worlds unfixating and merging. He speculates that color/form may abbreviate a vast relational network, comparing this loosely to dependent arising, entropy, and category theory.
