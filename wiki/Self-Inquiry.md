Self-inquiry is Shinzen's bridge term for practices that ask who or what is experiencing, thinking, or aware. In his translation, its deep symmetry with mindfulness is that self-inquiry looks back from an arising toward its origin, while Gone practice watches a passing return to the same Source-facing boundary.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen treats self-inquiry as a powerful arising-side route to Source/no-self territory, but not as a universal best method.
- **Why this matters**: It prevents future agents from isolating self-inquiry from mindfulness or flattening it into ordinary discursive questioning; the practice issue is whether awareness is turned back on itself in a way that strengthens concentration, clarity, and equanimity.
- **Key tensions**: The Source equivalence is Shinzen's contemplative interpretation, not established metaphysics, and self-inquiry can become self-referential rumination if it lacks CCE, guidance, or fit.
- **Source posture**: Probable as Shinzen's own method-translation frame; limited as comparative religion and as safety guidance.

## Model
In the short Gate 6B talk, Shinzen defines self-enquiry by questions such as "Who am I?", "What am I?", "Who's experiencing this?", "Who's thinking this thought?", and "Where did the thought come from?" The common gesture is to turn consciousness or awareness back on itself ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S1).

The talk then distinguishes surface method from deep symmetry. Mindfulness begins as a meditator observing experience, but with time the momentum of concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity can reduce the felt doer, so the habit of meditating begins to meditate itself ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S2). At the same time, mindfulness becomes sensitive to impermanence, especially vanishings or [[Gone]] ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S3).

The symmetry is event-cycle based. Self-inquiry starts after A has arisen and asks where A came from. Gone starts after A has arisen and watches A disappear, because where A vanishes is where B arises. Shinzen therefore says mindfulness has something that corresponds to self-enquiry, but the model is watching where things go when they pass away rather than repeatedly looking back to where they came from ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S4-S5).

The book no-self chapter gives the broader owner-page frame. Shinzen says self-enquiry may recursively track I-am-ness behind I-am-ness until transfinite I-am-ness is reached, while analytic vipassana may break mind-body experience into components until it is seen as expanding-contracting space. He interprets both as routes into territory some traditions call true self and others call no-self ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S6).

The Basic Mindfulness Gone chapter gives an earlier manual bridge. Just Note Gone and self-inquiry are presented as two ways to approach what is behind or prior to self-arising, with Gone using ordinary passings rather than a direct "Who am I?" style question ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S10).

[[The Absolute Witness]] refines the danger and the target. If inquiry or
watching leaves a detached observer over here watching phenomena over there,
Shinzen calls that a distance-creating equanimity metaphor, not the absolute
witness. The absolute side appears when the subtle Feel/Image/Talk that
creates the witness itself vanishes into contentless cessation or Zero
([[The Absolute Witness]] S2-S7).

## Boundaries
Self-inquiry is not automatically better than mindfulness. Shinzen explicitly says it works very well for some people but not necessarily for everyone ([[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] S6). Method choice should therefore route through fit, current CCE, support, and life effects rather than tradition prestige.

It is also not mere thinking about the self. In Shinzen's frame, the question should turn awareness back on itself; if it instead produces abstract analysis, anxiety, or self-fixation, another method such as sensory Noting, Gone, Rest, Do Nothing, grounding, or support may be the better route.

The Source equivalence should remain bounded. "Same place" means Shinzen's Source-facing contemplative interpretation, while [[Source And Polarities]] preserves the rule that Source is not directly perceived as a sensory object and is known through afterglow or representation.

## Implications
For practice routing, self-inquiry and Gone can be treated as complementary doors:

- Use self-inquiry when the question "Who or what is experiencing?" naturally gathers attention, clarity, and non-grasping.
- Use Gone when the arising-side question becomes too abstract, but ordinary sensory endings are available.
- In either case, the route is only Shinzen-compatible when it increases concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity rather than tightening the observer.
