## Practice Handles
- Treat self-as-thing as a perceptual unity to investigate, not as an enemy to destroy or a doctrine to affirm.
- When "I am" feels solid, increase resolution: look for emotional body sensation, mental image, and internal talk that are falling together.
- Ask what happens to the conventional self-appearance when attention is close and what happens when attention relaxes, without forcing a simple exists/does-not-exist answer.
- After separating the strands, let concentration, clarity, and equanimity soak into each strand so Feel, Image, and Talk can reveal movement, vibration, space, or Flow.
- Keep science and physics parallels as analogies unless direct evidence is available.

## Summary
Shinzen begins with a CRT display analogy. What looks like one white area on a
screen can resolve into red, green, and blue components when magnified. The
magnifying glass does not change what is there; it increases resolution, the
ability to separate perceptual data that ordinarily fall together.

He then applies the analogy to Buddhist no-self. Classical materials say the
perception of self, body, or cow can disappear when a skilled analysis
reveals components. Shinzen's contemporary version is narrower and more
practice-operational: in the coarse subjective self of thought and emotion,
the perception "I am a thing" may disappear while Feel, Image, and Talk arise
as sensory strands that are not me.

The talk then adds two boundaries. First, no-self is a perceptual research
project, not a demand to believe in aggregates or metaphysics. Second, the
status of the resolved appearance is subtle: like white on a CRT, it appears
when not inspected and disappears under close resolution. Shinzen speculates
that future science might investigate this perceptual shift, but he carefully
marks the claim as analogy. Finally, he extends vipassana from separating
strands to penetrating strands: Feel, Image, and Talk can begin to present as
vibrating space or Flow, at which point their distinctness gives way to empty
impermanent nature.

## Weakest Claims
- The butcher/cow metaphor is reported through a short oral summary and not checked against the relevant Buddhist text here.
- The analogy between CRT color resolution and self-resolution is pedagogically useful but not established as a literal perceptual or neural equivalence.
- The future-science prediction is speculative even though Shinzen frames it cautiously.
- The "vibrating space" and electromagnetic-energy parallel is explicitly only an interesting parallel, not evidence that inner sensory Flow is physically the same phenomenon.

## Important Omissions
- The talk does not give criteria for when no-self inquiry is destabilizing, dissociative, or clinically risky.
- It does not specify a step-by-step protocol for moving from Feel/Image/Talk discrimination into vibrating Flow.
- It limits the claim to the coarse subjective self of thought and emotion, leaving social identity, behavior, memory, trauma, and relationship selfhood outside the immediate proof field.
- It does not explain how to verify the claimed perceptual shift beyond first-person practice.

## Contradictions/Tensions
- Shinzen says the resolved self-appearance both exists and does not exist in a "sort of" way; this is a productive tension, not a defect to smooth out.
- The talk relies on scientific analogy while warning that the analogy may not carry very far.
- Discrimination reveals Feel/Image/Talk as not-self, yet deeper penetration dissolves the distinction among the strands into vibrating Flow; both moves need to be preserved.

## Easily Misused Claims
- "The perception I am goes away" should not be used to deny functional identity, responsibility, memory, relationship, or clinical symptoms.
- The CRT metaphor should not be turned into proof that self is unreal in every sense.
- "Vibrating space" should not encourage energy-chasing or physics overclaim.
- Future-science language should not be treated as existing empirical validation.
