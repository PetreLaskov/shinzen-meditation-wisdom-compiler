## Summary
Shinzen begins by explaining how his Dharma name is written and pronounced. He says the same written characters are pronounced differently across East Asian languages, and that in his Japanese reading they are pronounced "Shinzen." He then explains that the name is not the same character as the "Zen" of Zen Buddhism. In his rendering, the characters mean true or authentic and good or goodness.

The autobiographical core is his 1970 ordination. Just before making him a Buddhist monk, his teacher gave him the name, asked him to keep it for life, and asked whether he was willing to accept it. Shinzen says he felt he could not live up to a name meaning truth and goodness. His teacher's response kept the issue practical: never mind living up to it; was he willing to take it?

Shinzen then unfolds several layers of meaning. The name links him to his teacher and to the founder of the Mount Koya temple, implying inherited responsibility to carry a lineage back to the United States. It also names what he calls the twin pillars of the universal spiritual path: truth or authenticity as wisdom and transcendence of self/world reification, and goodness as personal character, sila, and bodhisattva service.
