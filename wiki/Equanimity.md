Equanimity is Shinzen's term for noninterference with sensory experience. It
is the third possibility between stuffing experience down and identifying
with it: sensations can arise, spread, contract, and pass without the person
adding push, pull, freeze, or subtle ownership.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Equanimity allows sensory experience to complete itself
  without suppression, identification, state-chasing, forced positivity, or
  heroic endurance.
- **Why this matters**: It is the skill that keeps concentration from
  becoming clench, clarity from becoming struggle, and purification from
  becoming pain-seeking or catharsis-chasing.
- **Key tensions**: Equanimity can be confused with numbness, compliance,
  passivity, dissociation, calm performance, or indifference to ordinary
  repair.
- **Source posture**: Strong for Shinzen's definition and training handles;
  limited for clinical, medical, relational, or high-intensity safety
  criteria.

## Hub Model

[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 4 - Calming and Clarifying]] defines
equanimity as letting go of craving and aversion around each sensory
experience and allowing experience to expand and contract without
interference (S8). [[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] gives the key boundary:
suppression stuffs or tightens around a state, identification fixates on it,
and equanimity is the balanced third possibility of non-self interference
(S10).

The beginner-facing phrase is "not fighting with yourself." Shinzen frames
this as an ability, not as a permanent rule that inner struggle must never
occur ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S4). [[Basic
Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] sharpens what equanimity is not:
it is not indifference, passivity, suppression, blankness, lack of thought or
sensation, or behavioral inhibition (S8).

[[What Is Equanimity]] adds the concise public-facing boundary. Equanimity
means radical noninterference with Touch/Sight/Sound/Feel/Image/Talk, not
hands-off passivity toward world events. Shinzen says one can be proactive or
forceful with circumstances while training sensory circuits not to grind
against themselves, like an oiled engine ([[What Is Equanimity]] S1-S4).

[[Equanimity Training Ladder]] owns the practical repair sequence. The
manual teaches body relaxation, replacing judgment with appreciation,
acceptance, and matter-of-factness, and especially noticing spontaneous drops
into equanimity ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S11-S12). The short
equanimity clip adds the fallback: when relaxation and accepting talk cannot
be controlled, the practitioner can have equanimity with tension and judging
themselves ([[Equanimity Intentional Noticing Dropping Deeper and
Second-Order Equanimity]] S5).

[[Equanimity And Purification Taste]] owns the transformation mechanism.
[[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 5 - Insight and Purification]] places
equanimity inside the CCE-plus-time theorem for insight and purification
(S9-S14). The Equanimity and Purification talks make the anti-ascetic
correction explicit: equanimity, not pain level, is the purifier, and pleasant
absorptions can purify through equanimity too ([[Equanimity and the Taste of
Purification - Part 1 of 2]] S2-S4; [[Equanimity and the Taste of
Purification - Part 2 of 2]] S1-S3).
The public Q&A gives the beginner version of the same valence rule: pain can
still hurt without becoming a problem, and pleasure can satisfy rather than
leave hunger when equanimity is present ([[What Is Equanimity]] S7).

[[Equanimity Versus Suppression]] owns the safety differential. If
"equanimity" makes action less accurate, less caring, less willing to repair,
or less able to seek medical, clinical, ethical, relational, or social
support, the word is no longer enough. The question becomes function,
feeling access, responsiveness, consent, and support.

[[Shinzen Young Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic Academy - July 22, 2017]]
adds a quality-C retreat confirmation of the key boundary: equanimity is a
relationship to sensory experience, not to objective circumstances. In that
stream, other body, thought, emotional, sight, or sound activity may be
permitted in the background while spaciousness is foregrounded, but this is a
method choice rather than a rule to stop acting on conditions that need
ordinary action ([[Shinzen Young Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic Academy -
July 22, 2017]] S2, S8).

[[Mindfulness with Sickness]] adds a selected-source illness case for the same
skill. Shinzen distinguishes foreground equanimity, when the practitioner
turns toward the challenge, from background equanimity, when sickness-related
local/global physical discomfort, Image, Talk, or body emotion is permitted
while attention stabilizes elsewhere. The talk treats these as related but
partly independent skills, and it keeps the background version bounded by
rest and ordinary care ([[Mindfulness with Sickness]] S2, S6-S8).

[[Is Fixation THE Necessary Cause for Suffering]] adds the Sasaki Roshi
anti-fixation bridge. Shinzen treats fixation as related to craving but with
a different teaching resonance: experience is held, gripped, or carried into
the next moment in a way that may be measurable, for example through
attentional blink. This confirms noninterference as untraining inappropriate
holding, while leaving the neuroscience and intervention claims speculative
([[Is Fixation THE Necessary Cause for Suffering]] S1-S8).

## Boundaries

Equanimity is not an instruction to endure unsafe conditions. Physical pain,
illness, injury, sleep disruption, trauma activation, dissociation, panic,
mania, psychosis risk, self-harm risk, coercive teacher pressure, or major
behavior mismatch should route through [[Complete Experience Safety
Boundary]] before technique is optimized.

Equanimity is also not state preference. It applies to unpleasant, pleasant,
neutral, active, restful, self-arising, no-self, ordinary, and unusual
experience. Preferring calm, Flow, no-self, power phenomena, or bliss over
ordinary experience is itself a loss of equanimity.

Finally, equanimity does not self-certify service or conduct. Reduced inner
friction should eventually support clearer behavior and repair, but behavior
still needs feedback, accountability, ordinary care, and sometimes outside
support.
