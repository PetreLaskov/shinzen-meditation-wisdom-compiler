The Source and service boundary owns the return-to-life side of Shinzen's
Source language. It preserves his claim that shared Source can become love,
kinship, healthy merging, and service while blocking the stronger and less
supported move that Source contact certifies behavior, teaching authority, or
ethical reliability.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: In Shinzen's frame, realizing shared Source may make others
  feel like kin, support healthy merging, and call a practitioner toward
  service; this remains a practice interpretation, not a conduct guarantee.
- **Why this matters**: Source language can either prevent emptiness from
  becoming cold or become a way to bypass repair, consent, feedback, and
  role competence.
- **Key tensions**: Spontaneous service rhetoric is strong, but the sources
  do not provide behavioral verification, power-safety, clinical, legal,
  organizational, or teaching-qualification criteria.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's service-facing interpretation;
  incomplete as an accountability protocol.

## Model

Science of Enlightenment chapter 10 gives the core Source-to-service claim.
After the afterglow boundary and bhanga cautions, Shinzen says shared Source
realization can make others appear as close kin and call the practitioner
toward spontaneous service ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return
to the Source]] S14-S20). This is a real part of his model, but it comes
after strict limits on direct Source knowledge and destabilization.

[[The Final Stage and Service]] gives the ordinary-life version. Shinzen says
the final stage, if stage language is used at all, is ordinary-life
nonseparation of self, world, and Source rather than suspended altered state
or philosophy. He routes it through touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, Talk,
and whole-field arising-already-passing, then links shared Zero to serving
one's larger identity ([[The Final Stage and Service]] S1-S5).

[[After Enlightenment Love Deeply And Act Effectively]] gives the formation
version of the same boundary. Shinzen says the simplified motivation for
practice is optimal service, then names four conditions that made service
natural for him: early practice misery as compassion training, explicit
Mahayana service orientation, role models who became available to the full
human spectrum, and Source contact that softened fundamental alienation into
larger-identity love ([[After Enlightenment Love Deeply And Act Effectively]]
S1, S6-S12). This strengthens the service side of the model while keeping it
developmental and behavior-facing rather than merely private Source feeling.

[[How to Do Healthy Merging]] gives the interpersonal-contact version. Shinzen
says healthy merging should not make the other person feel invaded; it should
register as unconditional positive regard, require some control and ordinary
human presentation, and leave the practitioner bigger, cleaner, or more
elastic rather than disempowered or identified with harmful dynamics ([[How to
Do Healthy Merging]] S1-S7). This makes Source-adjacent love a relational
skill problem, not merely a private state.

[[Be The Master of Every Situation]] gives the Zen host/master version.
Shinzen separates the phrase from toughness or domination: one mode is
inner and outer arising together from shared Source, and a more radical mode
is outer contact with no inner reactivity, where identity lives as the
formerly other for the duration of contact ([[Be The Master of Every
Situation]] S2-S5). This strengthens the Source-to-service model while
keeping it phenomenological rather than a conduct or intimacy certification.

[[Touching the Heart]] gives the moment-by-moment service sequence. Shinzen
links literal contact with sensory consciousness in the absolute now to
emotional love, then says ordinary judgment, planning, correction, or moving
on may still occur after that first contact and love ([[Touching the Heart]]
S1-S4). This sharpens the boundary: Source-love can precede action without
replacing discernment, consent, role clarity, or ordinary protection.

[[What Is Love at the Deepest Level]] gives the conflict-facing version of
the same sequence. Shinzen says the practice is to perceive the other, enemy,
or problem as loved into existence by Source before ordinary reaction, while
judgment, opposition, confrontation, and action remain available ([[What Is
Love at the Deepest Level]] S4-S6). This is a stronger service handle than a
general love slogan because it gives the sensory entry point and the
accountability limit together.

[[Where Does Love Come In]] adds the multi-source map that keeps Source-love
from becoming the only explanation. Shinzen names natural human love,
purification that uncovers love covered by poison and pain, compassion
learned through one's own misery, relative Focus Out merging into I-Thou
contact, and absolute merging in shared Source as distinct channels through
which love and concern can arise ([[Where Does Love Come In]] S2-S8). This
strengthens the boundary because love can be cultivated, uncovered, learned,
and sensed relationally without making any single Source feeling a conduct
guarantee.

[[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] gives a more detailed larger-
identity sequence. When subjective Feel/Image/Talk and objective sensory
events both present as simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, Shinzen says the
inside/outside split may be annihilated into Source activity. Regular contact
with shared Source then makes one respond to the needs of a larger identity
through affect, behavior, coherent description, and explicit teaching
([[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] S3, S5).

[[How to Guide Someone through the Death Process using Mindfulness]] gives an
end-of-life service case. Shinzen frames death coaching as a reverse-midwife
role that helps nature's transition when the person wants that support, but
his practical sequence begins with survival-versus-dying orientation and
advance consent for possible one-way guidance ([[How to Guide Someone through
the Death Process using Mindfulness]] S2, S4-S5). This is Source-adjacent
service under role humility, not authority over medical care, family systems,
religion, legal decisions, or what happens after death.

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] adds an action
training version under the Express quadrant. Shinzen frames body movement,
speech, and eventually thinking as motor outputs that can arise from and
return to the same Source that sensory experience arises from and returns to;
his concrete retreat handle is Auto Move, especially "just walk" or body
motion happening by itself ([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant
Training]] S2, S9). This belongs here as a Source-expression claim, not as a
conduct-certification claim.

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] gives Auto Move its clearer
owner-page form. Movement can be practiced as a "good robot" or
"just-happening" output when the automaticity is detected with CCE, but the
same retreat closes by restricting driving and heavy machinery to sensory
driving practice ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S5-S6, S14).
That pairing is the boundary: Source-expression may train action, but it
does not override task safety.

[[The Big Picture as I See It]] gives the key paradox. Shinzen says the human
being has pleasant and unpleasant feelings and good and bad behaviors, while
Source has neither feeling nor good/bad; contact with the impersonal Source
is nevertheless said to nurture human fulfillment and refine behavior over
time. He frames this as a tendency, not an inevitable result ([[The Big
Picture as I See It]] S4-S7).

[[How a Positive Human Being Emerges from the Source]] gives the concise Q&A
version of that paradox. Shinzen says positive cultivation is not inside
Both-Gone and that Source is not intrinsically love or hate; love and
compassion appear because the impersonal touches a human being, and the
surface should return as approachable, admirable, helpful, and ordinary
rather than as crazy-wisdom specialness ([[How a Positive Human Being
Emerges from the Source]] S1-S6).

[[From Surface to Source and the Gold Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] gives
the strongest verification rule in the cluster: getting over self/world
through complete sensory experience and improving self/world must reinforce
each other. Source language that weakens ordinary repair, behavior, service,
or support fails that maturity criterion ([[From Surface to Source and the
Gold Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] S6-S7).

## Boundaries

Source contact should not be used to self-certify service. A felt calling,
afterglow, nondual insight, love, or larger-identity experience still needs
ordinary checks: consent, role clarity, competence, feedback, repair,
support, and sometimes professional or institutional referral.

Deep-love language should not be used to flatten conflict. Seeing an opponent
or problem as Source-loved does not settle what action is effective, what
harm is occurring, what protection is needed, or which ordinary expertise
applies ([[What Is Love at the Deepest Level]] S1, S5-S6).

Healthy merging should not be used to self-certify intimacy. Shinzen's test
is partly interpersonal: the other person should not feel invaded or weirded
out, and the practitioner should be able to present the depth in ordinary
human terms ([[How to Do Healthy Merging]] S1-S4).

Host/master language should not be used to self-certify intimacy either.
Identity moving into the formerly other is a contemplative contact report,
not permission to override consent, claim literal access to another person's
interiority, or ignore ordinary safety and relational feedback ([[Be The
Master of Every Situation]] S3-S6).

The same boundary applies to Source-expression and Auto Move. Spontaneous
movement, speech, thought, or creativity may be meaningful practice material,
but it does not certify truth, skill, safety, ethical reliability, or
permission to override ordinary constraints.

It should not erase the human self. Shinzen's service route includes return,
reconstruction, positive Feel/Image/Talk, personality, affect, behavior, and
ordinary tasks. The impersonal side is not an excuse to become cold,
unavailable, above feedback, or indifferent to harm.

It should not make Source itself positive content. The positive return is a
human-surface effect: love, compassion, and helpfulness should show in the
person, but they do not prove that Source is an emotion, ethical law, or
conduct guarantee ([[How a Positive Human Being Emerges from the Source]]
S1-S6).

It should not inflate service pressure. The improve-supports-transcend branch
can include volunteering, repair, diet, exercise, reduced conflict, or
ordinary support, but it does not imply every stuck practice is ethical
failure or that service should override rest, clinical care, consent, or
practical limits.

It should not romanticize extreme practice cultures. Shinzen uses severe
old-school role models to illustrate service resolve, but he also says he
does not expect modern practitioners to practice that way and wants to find a
smarter route to similar effects ([[After Enlightenment Love Deeply And Act
Effectively]] S10). Ordeal, intensity, and vows are not service proof.

End-of-life Source language needs the same boundary. A guide may query Rest,
Flow, or Gone and may personally hold a Source interpretation, but that does
not authorize premature dying-orientation, unconsented one-way guidance,
care-plan pressure, medical or palliative advice, or claims about
consciousness after death ([[How to Guide Someone through the Death Process
using Mindfulness]] S4-S11).
