This is the Gate 1 synthesis for practice entry and method choice. It depends on the compiled source pages named below and is now complete for the planned Gate 1 sequence, though still provisional as a practice-selection model until later live-guidance and safety gates are compiled.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen's entry model asks why a person is practicing, what CCE capacity is being trained, how reactions are handled, what path or method works now, which intellectual model, teacher style, sensory object, and system rationale fit, whether the broad menu should simplify to one workable technique or broaden into backhoe-like field clearing, whether attention should float, scan, or cover broadly, how a formal session or activity should be organized, which Noting or positive-focus options preserve clarity, whether effort should bear down or ease up, whether broad awareness carries clarity momentum, and whether Rest/Do Nothing is clarifying or drifting.
- **Why this matters**: Method choice can be routed by aim, skill deficit, current state, reactions, support, fit, and coverage strategy rather than by tradition prestige, teacher rhetoric, or state-chasing.
- **Key tensions**: "Whatever works" needs outcome, safety, and behavior criteria; CCE assumptions are useful but not a proof; guide fallback requires real competence; broad coverage can integrate or strain; one-technique simplicity, workout sequencing, and algorithmic branching each solve one problem while creating another; forced labels, repeat-until-Gone, equanimity voice, zooming, and effort can help or overpressure; no-goal practice can free control or become aversion to systematic practice; whole-field ease can carry clarity momentum or become vague globality; Rest can clarify subtle experience or become shutdown/blankness.
- **Source posture**: Probable as a Gate 1 synthesis from the compiled entry, path-fit, focus-method, session-setup, Noting-options, forced-label, zooming, effort-regulation, Rest, SHF, and Do Nothing sources; incomplete as a full practice-selection model.

## Landscape
- **Entry aims** ([[Why Meditate]], [[Total Happiness]]) - practice is justified by reduced suffering, elevated fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and love/service rather than by meditative state depth alone ([[Why Meditate]] S1-S5).
- **CCE mechanism** ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]], [[Mindfulness Skill Triad]]) - mindfulness practice matters because raised baseline concentration, clarity, and equanimity are said to support complete sensory experience and sensory happiness independent of conditions ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]] S3-S5).
- **Operating assumptions** ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]], [[Recycle The Reaction]]) - concentration, clarity, and equanimity are practical assumptions to implement; pleasant, unpleasant, or strange reactions to practice effects become further practice material, with competent guidance as fallback ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S1-S6).
- **Path fit** ([[The Best Path]], [[How Do I Know Which Kind of Meditation Is Best for Me]], [[Shinzen's Teaching Method]]) - the best path is person-and-time-specific; beginner choice can check intellectual fit, teacher style, sensory focus object, and system consistency; strengths and weaknesses are tendencies rather than absolutes; and Shinzen's own method has costs around complexity, vocabulary, procedural specificity, and secular framing ([[The Best Path]] S1, S5-S7; [[How Do I Know Which Kind of Meditation Is Best for Me]] S1-S6).
- **Local method choice** ([[See Hear Feel]], [[Practice Guidance Toolkit]], [[Do Nothing]]) - focus-method choices can be motivated by interest, opportunity, and necessity rather than craving, aversion, or unconsciousness; Noting and Do Nothing can be switched by the racy/spacey diagnostic, and pleasant-interest wandering can sometimes be converted into conscious Focus on Positive ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S5; [[Do Nothing Meditation]] S7; [[The Happy Wanderer]] S1-S5).
- **Initial overwhelm and one-technique simplification** ([[Working Smarter]]) - the many-option menu is exposure to possibilities, not a demand to learn the whole system; when vocabulary or the Five Ways overwhelms, the immediate route can be gradual acclimation plus one good technique that works ([[Working Smarter]] S1-S6).
- **One deep route versus broad clearing** ([[Digging Holes]]) - one focus
  route can be taken deeply, but broad work need not be dismissed as "many
  shallow holes" when it functions as backhoe-like field clearing; use the
  strategy that is fun, interesting, and productive while preserving outcome
  and safety checks ([[Digging Holes]] S4-S7).
- **Coverage strategy** ([[Focus Coverage Strategies]]) - attention can free-float, scan systematically, or hold even coverage; the right strategy depends on whether the live need is natural contact, detail, integration, or divide-and-conquer simplification ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S1-S11).
- **Broad Note Everything routing** ([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]]) - broad See/Hear/Feel practice can be continued, narrowed, alternated with another method, slowed through re-noting, or widened through inclusive noting depending on whether the live wall is effort/agitation or too much happening at once ([[Appreciate the Senses Transcend the Self Express the Source]] S4-S6).
- **Retreat workout routing** ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]], [[Auto Move]]) - a daylong can sequence Note Everything, Auto Move, Feel Good, and Space/Expansion-Contraction, while live method choice is guided by interest, opportunity, and necessity rather than craving, aversion, or unconsciousness ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] S1, S5, S7-S8, S13).
- **Session setup and activity carryover** ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]], [[Organizing Your Practice]], [[A Psycho-Spiritual Workout Routine]], [[Practice Cycles]], [[Can Ordinary Experience Count as Meditation]]) - formal practice can reduce choice by staying with one technique, sequence the Five Ways as a psycho-spiritual workout, or branch dynamically when interest, opportunity, or necessity changes; the broader practice cycle also asks whether the method is being trained in stillness, motion, and life; ordinary art or sport counts only when it raises baseline daily-life focus rather than merely producing in-the-zone absorption ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S1-S7; [[Organizing Your Practice]] S1-S11; [[A Psycho-Spiritual Workout Routine]] S1-S5; [[Can Ordinary Experience Count as Meditation]] S1-S6).
- **Ritual/activity practice** ([[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]], [[Lineage Translation]]) - even a traditional chant can be treated as a formal meditation period by choosing sound, body, Rest, silence, Flow, Focus on Positive, or labels when spacing out requires support ([[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]] S5-S8).
- **Positive-content branching** ([[The Focus on Positive Theme]], [[Nurture Positive]]) - unusually positive Image, Talk, or Feel can be deconstructed, held formally as Focus on Positive, or occasionally pursued through ordinary thinking and note-taking when it functions as an insight window ([[The Focus on Positive Theme]] S1-S5).
- **Wandering-mind branching** ([[The Happy Wanderer]], [[Nurture Positive]], [[Do Nothing]]) - when wandering mind has a clear pleasant-interest driver, it can become conscious positive-focus practice or alternate with Do Nothing rather than being treated only as distraction ([[The Happy Wanderer]] S1-S5).
- **Noting apparatus** ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]], [[Forcing Spoken Labels]], [[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]], [[Noting]]) - inside Noting, method choice can be as small as no-label versus labeled tracking, label strength, forced spoken labels, equanimity voice, pacing, repeated notes, repeat-until-Gone, or spatial-scope zooming ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S3-S12; [[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S1-S8; [[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S2-S5).
- **Effort polarity** ([[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]], [[Effort Regulation]], [[Do Nothing]]) - systematic methods such as Noting let practice bear down, Do Nothing lets practice ease up, and each approach is tested by the practitioner's freedom to do the other ([[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]] S2-S8).
- **Parts-whole momentum** ([[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]], [[Focus Coverage Strategies]]) - effortful work with parts and no-effort practice can combine so whole-field awareness retains clarity with minimum effort ([[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]] S2-S7).
- **Single-technique sufficiency** ([[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]], [[Practice Guidance Toolkit]]) - when Do Nothing is the only workable method for ADD or attention difficulty, Shinzen permits it in theory but requires occasional competent input and treats later ability to do organized techniques as a sign that it is working ([[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] S4-S6).
- **Rest and advanced Do Nothing** ([[Focus on Rest - Standard (Relative Rest) and Advanced (Do Nothing)]], [[Focus on Rest]]) - "nothing is happening" can reveal tangible rest events, cessation-like vanishing, or a need for clearer routing; standard Focus on Rest clarifies rest flavors, while advanced Do Nothing releases deliberate meditation without becoming ordinary mind wandering ([[Focus on Rest - Standard (Relative Rest) and Advanced (Do Nothing)]] S1-S9).
- **Jhana/shamatha translation** ([[Jhanas and Focus on Rest]], [[Lineage Translation]]) - Focus on Rest is Shinzen's partial modern reworking of early Buddhist absorption practice into six tangible sensory flavors and a pleasant-rest feedback loop that must still be clarified toward impermanence and emptiness ([[Jhanas and Focus on Rest]] S1-S9).
- **Empowering contrasts** ([[A Mindfulness Path Arising Between Empowering Contrasts]]) - method choice matures by working complementary sides such as label/no-label, bearing down/easing up, discrimination/unification, and self/world deconstruction/reconstruction rather than fixating one side.

## Through-Line
Gate 1 starts with aim before method. [[Why Meditate]] gives the outcome rubric: reduced suffering, elevated fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and service. That makes method choice accountable to life effects rather than only to calm, insight language, tradition identity, or teacher preference.

The beginner FAQ adds the mechanism underneath the aim. Shinzen says mindfulness raises baseline CCE, and that raised CCE can make body sensation and thinking complete enough to support sensory happiness independent of conditions ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]] S3-S6). The assumptions talk then lowers the epistemic pressure: these are reasonable assumptions to implement, not proofs that every technique or intensity level is always beneficial ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S1).

The practice contract has two immediate safeguards. First, reactions to practice effects are not outside the path; craving, aversion, fear, doubt, or fascination can become practice material through [[Recycle The Reaction]] when workable ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S5). Second, a competent guide is part of the beginner/intermediate ecology when the practitioner forgets the first four assumptions ([[Five Basic Assumptions in Mindfulness Practice]] S6).

[[The Best Path]] widens method choice beyond Shinzen's own system. A practitioner may need a different path at a different time; traditions can be significant in combination; and comparison should name strengths and weaknesses rather than claim final superiority ([[The Best Path]] S1-S5). This gives future Gate 1 ingestion a crucial posture: even if a later talk praises a method strongly, the claim should still be read through fit, timing, costs, and alternatives.

[[How Do I Know Which Kind of Meditation Is Best for Me]] makes that posture
beginner-operational. Shinzen tells a beginner to look at which tradition
appeals intellectually, which teacher style and personality fit, what sensory
object the method uses to train concentration, and whether the system is
designed for stable repetition or algorithmic variation. The final safeguard
is explainability: the teacher should be willing and able to say why the
student is being asked to practice in that way ([[How Do I Know Which Kind of
Meditation Is Best for Me]] S1-S6).

[[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] brings the model back from outer path choice to local attentional design. A practitioner choosing Focus In, Focus Out, Focus on Rest, Focus on Flow, Focus on Positive, body scanning, or another method is also choosing how attention covers the field: free-floating, systematic inventory, or even coverage. Shinzen gives no hierarchy; he gives tradeoffs. Free-floating is natural and easy, inventory builds coverage and detail, even coverage integrates and trains expansive concentration, and broad practice should be simplified when it strains or loses contact ([[Focus Methods in Mindfulness Advantages and Disadvantages]] S5-S11).

[[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] adds the next Gate 1 layer: once a method menu exists, the session itself needs an organizing style. One-technique practice reduces choice load and may be enough if pursued seriously. A workout sequence deliberately touches multiple psycho-spiritual muscle groups, with Focus on Positive functioning as a reconstructive close after deconstruction. Looping and branching gives the most responsiveness, especially in interactive guidance, but Shinzen explicitly names its complexity cost ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S1-S7).

[[Working Smarter]] adds the beginner-overwhelm version of that same menu
logic. Shinzen says the range of approaches is meant to expose people to
possibilities so they can find what works; they do not have to learn the
whole system, and one good technique is enough if they want to stay with it
([[Working Smarter]] S5-S6). That makes method simplification an entry move,
not a consolation prize after the full architecture is mastered.

[[Digging Holes]] adds the method-strategy version of the same issue.
Shinzen validates one deep hole for some people, but rejects the pejorative
assumption that broad practice is necessarily many shallow holes. A broad
strategy can instead clear the field level by level like a backhoe, while the
live criterion remains what is fun, interesting, and productive ([[Digging
Holes]] S4-S7). This does not replace outcome, safety, or behavior checks;
it prevents method breadth from being treated as shallow by definition.

[[Organizing Your Practice]] places that same triad inside a larger
implementation question. A method is not only chosen for the formal sit; it
is also trained through formal stillness, formal motion, practice-in-life
micro-hits, trigger practice, motion challenges, retreat or equivalent, and
periodic teacher contact. This strengthens the method-choice test: a method
that works only in closed-eye stillness may still need graded carryover work
before it can be expected to function in ordinary life ([[Organizing Your
Practice]] S1-S11).

[[Can Ordinary Experience Count as Meditation]] applies the same test to
activities that are not presented as formal meditation. Ballet, piano,
martial arts, sport, running, or singing can be meditation in principle, but
Shinzen says the decisive criterion is carryover: the activity must raise the
person's baseline focus in daily life. Being in the zone during performance
does not by itself make the activity a sufficient meditation path, and
coaching or formal practice is usually needed to make the transfer reliable
([[Can Ordinary Experience Count as Meditation]] S1-S6).

[[A Psycho-Spiritual Workout Routine]] gives the most compressed version of
the workout branch: ordinary sensory experience, Rest, Flow and vanishing,
Source or Zero language, and then positive Feel/Image/Talk, joy, compassion,
and service. This sharpens the sequence's logic while also showing why it
needs boundaries: Rest, Flow, Gone, Source, and positive reconstruction
should be used when available and workable, not forced as a session-completion
checklist ([[A Psycho-Spiritual Workout Routine]] S1-S5).

[[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]] adds
that the same method-choice logic can govern a ritual activity. Shinzen says
chanting should be treated as a formal meditation period and asks
practitioners to decide what they will focus on: pure sound, physical breath
and touch, Rest, background silence, chant-induced Flow, deity visualization
and compassion, or labels if spacing out becomes strong ([[Om Mani Padme Hum
Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]] S5-S8). This matters
because the formal container need not be silent sitting; the same CCE-routing
question can organize voice, group rhythm, and inherited ritual.

[[The Focus on Positive Theme]] adds a smaller branch inside a session. If
unusually positive Feel, Image, or Talk arises, the practitioner can keep
deconstructing it as ordinary sensory material, shift into formal Focus on
Positive by holding or repeating it, or occasionally stop formal practice to
think and write when an insight window seems open. The last route is
permitted but bounded: the practitioner should know formal meditation is
suspended, and meditation should not become only discursive thought ([[The
Focus on Positive Theme]] S5).

[[The Happy Wanderer]] adds the same logic to one common distraction. When
wandering mind is powered by detectable pleasant interest, the practitioner
may convert it from unconscious positive focus into conscious Focus on
Positive, then stay, play, or return to the original technique. When Do
Nothing is being blocked by wandering mind, Shinzen validates alternating Do
Nothing with this positive-focus conversion ([[The Happy Wanderer]] S1-S5).

[[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] moves from session-scale choice to option-scale choice inside Noting. A practitioner can try direct no-label tracking when clarity and momentum are present, add mental or spoken labels when tracking collapses, strengthen labels when attention is going "up a hill," use an equanimous label voice, adjust pace, average rapid events, repeat the same note, or repeat until Gone ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S3-S12). The guidance posture is important: if practice is difficult, first ask which technique and which options have been tried before inventing special exercises ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, 1 of 2 Parts]] S4-S5).

[[Forcing Spoken Labels]] explains one reason the strong-label option may be chosen. If attention is being scattered by an invisible force, forcing labels may turn that force into tangible impatience or agitated Feel, so the practitioner has a sensory object with location, intensity, and flavor. In that case the method choice is not "labels versus no labels" as status; it is whether first gear is needed for a steep hill ([[Forcing Spoken Labels]] S1-S4, S7-S8).

[[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] adds the spatial-scope branch. If an event persists into the focus phase, the default is not to control spatial attention, but the practitioner may zoom in to a smaller workable region, zoom out to include the whole event, or simultaneously contact the most intense point while spreading awareness through the body ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S1-S5). This is especially relevant when intensity is local but suffering seems tied to resistance to subtle body-wide spread; it is also explicitly optional and can initially feel overwhelming or like loss of control ([[How to do Labeling and Noting During Meditation, Part 2 of 2, Zooming]] S6-S10).

[[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]] adds the effort-regulation layer that sits across Noting and Do Nothing. Shinzen says there is no magic formula for when to bear down or ease up, so his system gives both effortful techniques and a clear non-effort practice. The practical rule is the familiar racy/spacey switch, but the deeper diagnostic is cross-testing: effort is maturing if the practitioner can drop effort, and non-effort is maturing if the practitioner can still use precise systematic focus with high CCE ([[Bear Down or Ease Up in Meditation]] S2-S8).

[[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]] adds how that balance can look when it matures. Shinzen validates a report of global awareness that covers vast experience with little effort by saying it carries momentum from effortful local work with individual parts, combined with the minimum-effort capacity trained by Do Nothing. The resulting mode is "half like Do Nothing" while retaining the crispness of bear-down penetration ([[Parts & Wholes, Efforting & Do-Nothing A Certain Momentum]] S3-S7).

[[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] adds a special case for attention-difficulty routing. If Do Nothing is the only practice that works for a person with ADD, Shinzen says it can be sufficient in theory, but the choice should be checked by someone competent at least once or twice a year. The model is not "stay unable to use structure forever"; a sign that Do Nothing is working is later improvement enough to implement systematic or organized techniques ([[A.D.D. & the Do Nothing Technique]] S4-S6).

[[Focus on Rest - Standard (Relative Rest) and Advanced (Do Nothing)]]
completes Gate 1 by clarifying the Rest side of method choice. If a session
feels like "nothing is happening," Shinzen does not treat that report as
automatically useless. It may indicate a cessation-like event, or it may hide
six rest events that can be made tangible: physical relaxation, defocused
visual and auditory experience, emotional neutrality, blank mental screen,
and inner quiet. Standard Focus on Rest trains those flavors as objects;
advanced Focus on Rest is Do Nothing, provided it is not reduced to
thumb-twiddling or ordinary mind wandering ([[Focus on Rest - Standard
(Relative Rest) and Advanced (Do Nothing)]] S1-S7).

[[Jhanas and Focus on Rest]] gives the Gate 8 lineage-translation version of
the same method-choice branch. When a practitioner asks whether Rest is
related to shamatha, jhana, or absorption, Shinzen's answer is that Focus on
Rest reworks some early Buddhist absorption principles into a procedure with
six sensory rest flavors: Relaxation, Light, Silence, Peace, Blank, and
Quiet. The routing point is not tradition prestige but whether rest can be
made tangible, noted, used for concentration feedback, and eventually
clarified into impermanence and emptiness ([[Jhanas and Focus on Rest]]
S1-S9).

That talk also gives Rest a transformation route rather than only a calming
route. Absorption thins self and world, then rest factors can be penetrated
into [[Flow]] and [[Gone]]; dry vipassana works more directly with active
sensory experience. The method-choice implication is that Rest is appropriate
when it preserves or develops clarity, not when it merely produces blankness
or preference for quiet states ([[Focus on Rest - Standard (Relative Rest) and
Advanced (Do Nothing)]] S3-S5).

[[A Mindfulness Path Arising Between Empowering Contrasts]] makes the
method-choice principle explicit. Shinzen says the path cannot be fixated
because it arises between contrasts, and the label/no-label example gives a
small diagnostic: drop labels when labeling gets racy, but label again when
dropping labels gets spacey. The harder guidance warning is that practitioners
often hear the side their ego pattern wants, so simply naming both sides does
not guarantee balance ([[A Mindfulness Path Arising Between Empowering
Contrasts]] S3-S6).

## Provisional Routing
For now, a future guide or agent should ask:

- What is the person's current entry aim: suffering, fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior, service, or some combination?
- Which part of CCE seems most relevant: stable focus, sensory discrimination, noninterference, or balance among them?
- Is the reaction to practice itself now the material, and is that reaction workable enough to recycle?
- Is guidance, method simplification, grounding, switching, stopping, or outside support needed?
- Is the chosen method motivated by interest, opportunity, or necessity, or by craving, aversion, unconsciousness, identity, or status?
- What are the likely strengths and weaknesses of this path or method for this person now?
- Which intellectual model, teacher style, and sensory object seem workable
  enough to try?
- Is object-switching supported by the system's internal consistency, or is
  the system asking for stable repetition?
- Can the teacher explain why this method or object is being assigned?
- Is the person overwhelmed by the system itself, and would one good
  technique plus gradual vocabulary exposure be enough for now?
- Is the current strategy really one deep hole, broad field clearing, or
  unexamined method switching?
- Should this formal session stay with one technique, run a sequence, or branch dynamically?
- Does the chosen method have a carryover plan: stillness, motion,
  micro-hits, trigger practice, motion challenges, retreat or equivalent, and
  feedback?
- If the chosen container is an art, sport, running, singing, work, or
  another ordinary activity, is it raising baseline daily-life concentration,
  or only producing absorption while the activity is underway?
- If the session is a retreat workout, is the active branch Note Everything,
  Auto Move, Feel Good, Space/Expansion-Contraction, or deliberate rest from
  formal practice?
- If the practice container is chanting or another formal activity, what is the chosen focus route: sound, body, Rest, silence, Flow, Focus on Positive, or labels for spacing out?
- Should attention free-float, scan systematically, or hold broad coverage?
- If the range is Everything and practice feels too effortful or overwhelming,
  should the person narrow to one modality, re-note, use inclusive noting,
  alternate with a simpler method, or choose another technique for now?
- If the method is Noting, are labels needed now, and at what strength?
- Are forced spoken labels needed to make invisible scatter observable, or would they create unworkable strain, shame, or overpressure?
- Is label pace creating contact, frenzy, or spaciness?
- Is the overall practice becoming racy and goal-driven enough to ease up, or spacey and no-goal-avoidant enough to bear down?
- Can the current approach pass the cross-test: effort can release into Do Nothing, and non-effort can organize into systematic Noting?
- If Do Nothing is the only workable technique, is there competent periodic input, and is later capacity for systematic techniques increasing?
- If nothing seems to be happening, are tangible rest events available, is a
  vanishing being detected, is Do Nothing appropriate, or is the practice
  becoming blank, sleepy, avoidant, or shutdown-like?
- If Rest is being pursued as jhana or shamatha, is the practitioner using
  Shinzen's sensory Focus on Rest procedure rather than assuming full
  classical absorption-stage training?
- Is standard Focus on Rest clarifying rest flavors, or would active
  sensory practice, Noting, guidance, or support restore better contact?
- If awareness is broad and low-effort, does it still carry clarity from parts work, or has it become vague globality?
- If the event persists into a focus phase, should spatial attention be left alone, zoomed in, zoomed out, or held local-and-global?
- Would repeating the note or repeat-until-Gone clarify the event, or would it become disappearance-chasing?
- Is broad coverage supporting integration, or is it straining and losing contact enough to divide-and-conquer?
- Is method-switching optimizing effort, or is it becoming choice-confusion, avoidance, novelty seeking, or over-control?
- If positive Image, Talk, or Feel arises, should it be deconstructed, held formally as Focus on Positive, or pursued briefly as ordinary discursive insight work?
- If wandering mind is frustrating, is there a clear pleasant-interest flavor that can be made conscious as Focus on Positive, or is the wandering better routed to labels, grounding, rest, support, or ordinary task attention?
- What life or behavior signal would show that the path is actually working?
