Nurture Positive is the constructive technique in Basic Mindfulness. It intentionally uses inner sensory systems to cultivate wholesome patterns rather than only observe what is already arising; Shinzen explicitly does not posit a parallel technique that nurtures negative content. When the positive carrier is body emotion, live guidance begins by finding already-present pleasant Feel without an agenda before creating it.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Nurture Positive follows a choose-check-choose-hold algorithm over positive themes and inner sensory systems, with the older manual using six ABCISO themes and the later SHF appendix simplifying them to ABCD.
- **Why this matters**: It gives Shinzen's system an explicit reconstruction technique alongside Noting and Do Nothing, and chapter 8 uses it as one intervention for emptiness/no-self distress.
- **Key tensions**: Deliberate positive construction can support behavior and service, but it can also bypass pain or substitute imagery for action if not tested.
- **Source posture**: Strong as manual instruction; outcome and interpersonal-radiation claims remain source-attributed.

## Model
Nurture Positive uses mental image, mental talk, and emotional body sensation, the same elements used by Focus In. The difference is that Focus In observes what is present, while Nurture Positive actively creates positive images and talk that may evoke pleasant emotional body sensations ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S3-S4).

[[How a Positive Human Being Emerges from the Source]] adds a Source-side
boundary to the same constructive route. When asked where cultivating or
identifying positive exists within Both-Gone, Shinzen says it does not:
Both-Gone is no positive and no negative. Positive love and compassion belong
to the human return from impersonal Source, not to Source as an emotional
content ([[How a Positive Human Being Emerges from the Source]] S1-S6).

The practice then uses concentration to spread the positive feeling through the whole body and radiate it beyond the body to people and objects around the practitioner ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S5). The radiating-service claim belongs to Shinzen's source frame; the durable mechanism is constructive training of image, talk, and body affect.

[[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human Positivity]] gives the same
move a vivid oral form. Shinzen identifies lovingkindness as a traditional
version of Focus on Positive Feel, then says advanced practice can radiate
positive energy beyond the body "in all six directions" like a high-wattage
broadcast of human positivity ([[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human
Positivity]] S3-S4). The phrase is useful as idiolect; the interpersonal
effect remains source-attributed rather than verified.

[[Mindfulness Strategies when Interacting with Others]] gives a live
conversation version of the same positive-Feel direction. Shinzen says the
ordinary social smile can be used as a body basis for whole-body
unconditional positive regard while listening to someone, but only when the
conversation leaves enough attention for the person and the response
([[Mindfulness Strategies when Interacting with Others]] S1-S5).

[[Nurturing the Positive Creating Holding and Radiating Positive Subjective States]]
gives the shortest oral definition of the technique: intentionally create,
hold, and radiate positive states in Feel, Image, and Talk. It also gives a
guidance-relevant difficulty gradient. Positive Talk is usually easiest
because one can choose and repeat a phrase; Shinzen compares that operation
to mantra practice and to cognitive therapy when the phrase is a rational or
appropriate antidote to irrational or inappropriate cognition. Positive
Image is somewhat harder but can represent behavior change, performance,
desired outcomes, spiritual symbols, or pleasant associations. Positive Feel
is harder for many people, but Shinzen says it can be systematically learned
as pleasant interest, joy, love, gratitude, compassion, and related tones
([[Nurturing the Positive Creating Holding and Radiating Positive Subjective States]] S1-S5).

[[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]] adds
the ritual form. Shinzen says traditional chanting can visualize the
associated deity, listen to the external chant and internal mantra, and
access emotions such as joy and compassion. He explicitly calls this a kind
of Focus on Positive ([[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and Some Mindful Strategies
When Chanting]] S7). The durable point is not doctrinal belief in a deity,
but the constructive use of Image, Talk/sound, and Feel inside a chant
container. [[Leonard Cohen's Love Itself - Part 2 of 2]] contains duplicate
chant content under misleading metadata, so it is better used as a
transcript-quality caution.

[[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] gives the stronger identity version.
Shinzen says deity yoga replaces internal talk with mantra, self-image with
deity image, ordinary human feelings with deity feel, and touch with mudra or
other embodied ritual form. He explicitly says this belongs inside Focus on
Positive as he defines it, because the practitioner is systematically working
with the elements that create self-identification ([[The Secret of Archetypal
Deity Yoga]] S3-S7).

[[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 1 of 2 - Introduction]]
gives the concrete creation procedure for positive Feel when finding it is
not enough. The practitioner briefly makes a positive Image or Talk likely to
evoke happiness, love, joy, or a similar tone, then drops that thought and
focuses on the pleasant body resonance. If the resonance fades, the same or a
different thought can be used again, like striking an emotional-body bell
just long enough to hear its resonance ([[Creating Feel Good in Emotional
Body Space - 1 of 2 - Introduction]] S1-S6).

[[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 2 of 2 - A Guided Meditation]] gives the guided sequence. Shinzen begins with "if possible,"
uses a small Buddha-like smile as an optional somatic support, asks the
student to spread positive Feel broadly, names the pleasant Feel as a samadhi
object that can create a concentration-pleasure feedback loop, and then opens
the eyes while keeping some awareness in the positive emotional body
([[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 2 of 2 - A Guided Meditation]] S1-S6).

[[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] gives a longer
retreat-container version. After body-location noting, Auto Move, Rest, and
Space work, Shinzen asks practitioners to activate positive body emotion
through available body pleasure, rest, flow, positive Image, positive Talk,
directly turning on the emotional body, or at minimum a physical smile. If
nonpositive or negative material appears, it is given background equanimity
rather than fought, while the foreground stays with positive Feel
([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]] S11).

[[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]] repeats the body-Feel branch in
a broader four-way workout. Shinzen treats Feel Good as the body-centered
part of lovingkindness and explicitly says it develops concentration,
sensory clarity, and equanimity: the practitioner holds positive Feel, learns
the emotional body by activating it, and lets nonpositive material arise in
the background without making it the foreground object ([[Four Ways Forward -
June Shinzen Retreat]] S7). It also adds an outdoor motion variant: pleasant
See Out, Hear Out, and Feel Out while walking can trigger positive Feel that
is then held and radiated ([[Four Ways Forward - June Shinzen Retreat]]
S11).

## Choose-Check-Choose-Hold
All forms of Nurture Positive follow the same pattern ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S10-S12).

First, choose a specific positive theme. The six main categories are Positive Affect, Positive Behavior, Positive Cognition, Positive Ideals, Positive Situations, and Other Positives.

Second, check the theme in two common systems. Repeat a word or phrase that represents the theme while tuning into related pleasant emotional body sensation. Then create a mental image that represents the theme while focusing on that image and any related pleasant body sensation.

Third, choose the subjective system that seems most natural for this theme at this time:

- Image only.
- Talk only.
- Emotional Body only.
- Image and Talk.
- Image and Emotional Body.
- Talk and Emotional Body.
- Whole subjective system: Image, Talk, and Emotional Body together.

Fourth, hold the theme in the chosen system for a set period of time.

## Theme Space
The chapter's ABCISO mnemonic separates content from sensory location. Positive Affect includes joy, friendliness, enthusiasm, compassion, love, gratitude, and forgiveness. Positive Behavior includes eliminating negative behaviors, initiating desired behaviors, and strengthening current positive behaviors. Positive Cognition includes creating rational, productive thought patterns and replacing inappropriate ones. Positive Ideals, Positive Situations, and Other Positives cover symbolic, imagined, outcome, and uncategorized goodness themes ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S7-S9).

This is important because Nurture Positive is not one emotion practice. It is a general constructive interface: what to cultivate can vary, and where to cultivate it can vary.

The short Nurturing the Positive talk adds a practical ordering cue without
making it mandatory: Talk may be the most accessible entry, Image may be
doable with more effort, and Feel may need systematic training. This helps a
guide avoid forcing positive Feel when positive Talk or Image is the more
available carrier ([[Nurturing the Positive Creating Holding and Radiating Positive Subjective States]] S3-S5).

[[The Focus on Positive Theme]] adds that positive content need not be
manufactured from scratch. Positive Image, Talk, or Feel may arise
spontaneously while doing ordinary observation practice. At that point
Shinzen gives three valid choices: deconstruct it matter-of-factly as
Feel/Image/Talk, hold or repeat it formally as Focus on Positive, or
occasionally suspend formal practice to think and write in the ordinary way
when the content seems to open an insight window ([[The Focus on Positive
Theme]] S1-S5). This makes Nurture Positive a choice point, not a command to
privilege every pleasant or spiritually meaningful event.

[[The Happy Wanderer]] adds a narrower spontaneous branch for wandering mind.
If the practitioner can detect the pleasant-interest flavor driving the
wandering, the event may be reframed as unconscious Focus on Positive and
made conscious. The practitioner can then stay with that positive focus, play
with it, or return to the earlier technique ([[The Happy Wanderer]] S1-S3).
This does not make all mind wandering useful; the handle depends on conscious
contact with the positive driver.

[[Practicing Noting Mix 2]] adds a small social-positive variant. Shinzen
reports that when he suggests Focus on Positive to a group and therefore does
it himself, trying to have positive social emotion with actual people
immediately reveals subtle fear and sadness. The practice move is not to
explain the sadness before proceeding, but to detect and "quantize" it as
workable sensory material ([[Practicing Noting Mix 2]] S2-S4).

[[Hold Positive Feel]] gives a testimonial example under acute family stress.
The practitioner first holds naturally available love Feel while sitting by
her unconscious brother, then uses positive memory Image when the direct body
love fades, and finally uses loving Talk when exhaustion and negative Talk
arise. The durable technique point is flexible carrier switching inside one
positive direction; the brother's later medical recovery is not evidence that
positive radiation caused physical healing ([[Hold Positive Feel]] S1-S5).

[[Simultaneous Zooming In and Out During Positive and Restful States]] adds a
small local-global option. Shinzen says simultaneous zooming, often used with
pain or emotional freakout, can also be applied to positive states, where it
may become intensely pleasant. The durable handle is to let already available
positive Feel become broader or more immersive without treating "bliss city"
as a guaranteed result ([[Simultaneous Zooming In and Out During Positive and
Restful States]] S1).

[[Finding Feel Good in Emotional Body Space]] adds a live body-Feel branch. Shinzen first asks the student to look for already-present pleasant emotional body sensation such as interest, enthusiasm, joy, smiliness, humor, or pleasant excitement. If it is present, the instruction is to cover it broadly with awareness; if it evaporates, that is acceptable. If the pleasant Feel settles into calmness, he classifies the calm as emotional relaxation or Rest rather than positive Feel ([[Finding Feel Good in Emotional Body Space]] S1-S5).

The creating-positive-Feel branch continues that live guidance. When positive
Feel is not already present or will not return, thought can deliberately
trigger it; once detected, attention spreads as broadly as the pleasant Feel
is actually present, ideally whole-body but without forcing. Shinzen presents
whole-body and beyond-body radiation as an advanced development, not the
entry requirement ([[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 1 of 2 -
Introduction]] S2-S8; [[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 2 of 2 - A Guided Meditation]] S1-S4).

Chapter 12 places those choices inside guidance. The "what" options remain A-B-C-I-S-O: Affect, Behavior, Cognition, Ideal, Situations, and Other positives. The "where" options are one inner space, two inner spaces, or all three: image, talk, and emotional body sensation ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S13). This makes Nurture Positive teachable as a set of adjustable parameters.

SHF Unit 08 records a later simplification. The current formulation reduces the six old themes to the four Shinzen says he has found most useful: Affect, Behavior, Cognition, and Ideal. The source suggests ABCD as the newer acronym, with D standing for Ideal or Vajrayana-style deity yoga ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S12). Future agents should preserve both interfaces: ABCISO is the older Basic Mindfulness manual and guidance-map version, while ABCD is the later Unified Mindfulness simplification.

## Difference From Noting
Nurture Positive is not [[Noting]]. Noting acknowledges and focuses on sensory events, with labels as optional support. Nurture Positive intentionally creates content and holds it in a chosen inner system. The Basic Mindfulness introduction already named Nurture Positive as the technique used by the Way of Human Goodness, while four other Ways use Noting ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S8; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S3-S4).

[[Evoking and Working Through Challenging Material]] adds the negative-side
contrast. Shinzen says Focus on Positive intentionally holds and radiates
positive content, but there is no corresponding practice that holds or
nurtures negative content. When negative material is intentionally evoked, it
is only struck like a body-mind bell so the remaining resonance can be
deconstructed through Focus In ([[Evoking and Working Through Challenging
Material]] S2-S5).

The two techniques can complement each other. If positive construction evokes resistance, grief, shame, numbness, or aversion, those reactions may become Noting objects. If Noting or [[See Hear Feel]] deconstructs self so thoroughly that warmth, aspiration, or behavior are underdeveloped, Nurture Positive becomes a balancing route.

The outtake source makes that complement concrete in a social field: the
attempt to create positive emotion toward actual people may itself bring fear
or sadness to the surface, at which point the immediate Shinzen move is
sensory clarification rather than positive-state forcing ([[Practicing Noting
Mix 2]] S3-S4).

The Focus on Positive Theme adds an even smaller-scale switch. A positive
event can stay inside deconstructive Noting, become a formal positive
cultivation object, or briefly move outside formal meditation into ordinary
discursive insight work. The third option should remain explicit:
discursive thinking may be appropriate on occasion, but a session that is
only discursive thought is no longer meditation ([[The Focus on Positive
Theme]] S5).

Chapter 8 makes that balancing role explicit in a safety context. When intense Goneness, Emptiness, or No-Self produces fear, loss, confusion, or disorientation, Shinzen recommends reconstructing the self with positive content regarding affect, behavior, and cognition using Nurture Positive ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S9). Basic Mindfulness chapter 9 then confirms Nurture Positive as one of the three techniques that may be learned separately or cycled with Noting and Do Nothing ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 9 - The Full Grid]] S5).

The session-setup talk gives a simpler sequencing image. Shinzen compares ending a deconstructive workout with Focus on Positive to entering a jacuzzi after physical exercise: a pleasant support that helps the reconstructive direction after self/world deconstruction ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S4-S5). The metaphor strengthens Nurture Positive as a closing or balancing move, not as a replacement for contact, repair, or behavior.

Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 repeats the same safety logic in stronger dark-night language. When Goneness, emptiness, and no-self produce disorientation, terror, paralysis, aversion, or hopelessness, one intervention is to affirm positive emotions, behaviors, and cognitions in a sustained way, gradually reconstructing a new habitual self through lovingkindness-related practice ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S14).

[[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] gives the same
reconstructive move a sharper clinical-neighbor context. When meditation
practice begins moving toward pit-of-the-void distress, Shinzen says one
branch is to use concentration skills to create positive Feel/Image/Talk
again and again, like physical therapy for atrophied capacity, until the
person can build a new and better functional self ([[Enlightenment DPDR and
Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] S6).

[[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]] repeats that branch for the rare
case Shinzen judges to be fully practice-based dark night or pit-of-the-void
distress. It also adds the limit: when depression, anxiety, or clinical
factors may be involved, Focus on Positive is not a substitute for competent
mental-health evaluation. If medication is used and side effects appear,
those effects can be met as touch, Feel, Image, and Talk, while medication
decisions remain outside the meditation teacher's scope ([[Classic Dark Night
or Clinical Issues]] S1-S7).

[[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] gives the most compressed formulation:
emptiness is only half the picture. When advanced students lose humanity,
edge, or dynamic motivation after emptiness, Shinzen says to balance the void
by intentionally holding, manifesting, and radiating positive Feel/Image/Talk
as a consistent practice, thereby creating a new and better conditioned self
from emptiness ([[Advanced FAQs Regarding Emptiness]] S1-S5).

[[How We Evolve and Integrate]] adds a behavior-output version of the same
branch. When emptiness produces flatline, disinclination to act, or loss of
old bounce, Shinzen pairs Focus In with Nurture Positive: deconstruct the
freak-out or resistance to action as inner See/Hear/Feel, then build an
"admirable self" through rational thought, positive behavior, positive human
traits, and habitual good feeling toward people ([[How We Evolve and
Integrate]] S9-S12).

## Boundaries
Nurture Positive should not be reduced to forced optimism. The chapter includes Positive Behavior, which means the practice should be answerable to external action and feedback, not only pleasant inner states.

It should not be turned into Source proof. Positive Feel/Image/Talk can be a
reconstructive human practice and may express the return from Source, but
Both-Gone itself is not positive content and positive affect does not verify
realization or conduct ([[How a Positive Human Being Emerges from the
Source]] S1-S6).

The ABCD simplification should not erase the older Situation and Other categories when reading Basic Mindfulness sources. It is a later interface choice, not proof that those older categories were mistaken or unusable.

It also should not erase negative experience. The manual's broader system includes direct tracking of thoughts, emotions, physical sensations, rest, and Flow. Constructive cultivation becomes bypass when it replaces necessary contact, repair, grief, anger, medical response, therapy, or boundary setting.

The Feel Good guidance sharpens the boundary on the pleasant side. Pleasant
emotional sensation should not be forced merely because the practice is
positive; absence, calm, or emotional relaxation are valid reports and may
route to Rest or another positive-practice branch ([[Finding Feel Good in
Emotional Body Space]] S3-S5). The evocation contrast sharpens the same
boundary in the other direction: negative experience may be contacted or
occasionally evoked for deconstructive practice, but it is not intentionally
held, amplified, or radiated as a cultivation object.

The trigger branch adds a second boundary. Brief Image/Talk can be used to
evoke body Feel, but the focus is not discursive positive thinking; the
thought is dropped once the emotional body resonates. Frequent retriggering
is allowed, but it should still return to pure body Feel rather than become a
thinking session ([[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space - 1 of 2 -
Introduction]] S3-S6).

The guided meditation adds a third boundary. A slight smile, pleasant
concentration feedback, and eyes-open social contact can support the
practice, but none of them should become forced emotional display, bliss
chasing, or charisma training ([[Creating Feel Good in Emotional Body Space -
2 of 2 - A Guided Meditation]] S1-S6).

The conversation source adds an interpersonal boundary to body-based
lovingkindness. Positive regard during interaction is practice only when it
increases contact and preserves appropriate response; it is not a license to
space out, perform warmth, override boundaries, or treat spontaneous speech
as automatically wise ([[Mindfulness Strategies when Interacting with
Others]] S1-S7).

The April daylong adds the same boundary in a retreat sequence. Background
equanimity with nonpositive material means the negative is allowed to
process; it is not a fight, but it is also not permission to suppress,
deny, or replace needed grief, fear, anger, medical care, therapy, rest, or
boundary setting ([[Shinzen April Daylong Retreat - Four Quadrant Training]]
S11).

The wandering-mind branch adds another boundary. Pleasant interest in a
thought-stream may be a doorway into Focus on Positive, but if the event is
compulsive fantasy, avoidance, task-unsafe drift, dissociation, or
mania-like acceleration, the positive frame is not enough to make it
workable ([[The Happy Wanderer]] S2-S5).

The social-positive branch adds a related boundary. Fear, sadness, shame, or
self-consciousness during positive social emotion may be valid Noting
material, but the clip does not authorize public exposure pressure, group
vulnerability mandates, or ignoring relational or clinical support needs
([[Practicing Noting Mix 2]] S3-S4).

The chapter does not give clinical or safety criteria for mania, compulsive positivity, depression, trauma, or shame around inability to feel positive. Those route to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].

The CBT analogy in the Nurturing the Positive talk sharpens the same boundary
rather than relaxing it. Repeating rational or appropriate Talk may be a
useful sensory operation, but this brief teaching is not a psychotherapy
protocol and should not replace clinical assessment, ordinary support, or
external accountability when those are needed ([[Nurturing the Positive Creating Holding and Radiating Positive Subjective States]] S3).

The deity-visualization chant source sharpens the devotional boundary.
Visualizing Avalokiteshvara and evoking joy or compassion can be a positive
Image/Talk/Feel practice, but it should not be treated as required belief,
identity inflation, initiation-complete deity yoga, or a reason to override
consent and bodily limits during group chant ([[Om Mani Padme Hum Meaning and
Some Mindful Strategies When Chanting]] S4, S7).

The deity-yoga source makes that boundary explicit. "Becoming" an archetype
is practice-useful only when it is held as deliberate construction, not
insanity, possession, special authority, or loss of ordinary discrimination
([[The Secret of Archetypal Deity Yoga]] S3-S5). Its permission to use
Western archetypes should not erase religious commitments, cultural context,
initiation requirements, or clinical safeguards.

The bedside-crisis testimonial sharpens the medical boundary. Holding and
radiating love may transform the caregiver's relationship to fear, grief, and
love, but it should not be treated as a medical intervention, a reason to
neglect rest or support, or evidence that positive states heal another
person's body ([[Hold Positive Feel]] S4-S5).

The DPDR talk does not change that boundary. Positive reconstruction in
void distress is not a substitute for clinical assessment, referral,
ordinary functioning, or emergency support when those are needed
([[Enlightenment DPDR and Falling Into the Pit of the Void]] S4-S6).
The dark-night/clinical talk strengthens the same boundary: if the distress
is not clearly practice-only, Shinzen's recommendation is professional
assessment and openness to standard treatment, not more positivity practice
alone ([[Classic Dark Night or Clinical Issues]] S5-S7).

## Implications
For practice routing, Nurture Positive is useful when the practitioner needs to strengthen wholesome motivation, affective warmth, behavioral intention, rational cognition, or service orientation.

For Shinzen-system interpretation, it proves that Basic Mindfulness is not purely deconstructive. The system explicitly trains the human personality as well as its dissolution.
