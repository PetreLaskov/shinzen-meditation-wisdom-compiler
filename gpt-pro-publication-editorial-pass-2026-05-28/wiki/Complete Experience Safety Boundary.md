Complete Experience Safety Boundary is the stable entry point for safety questions around Shinzen's system. It now routes the former all-in-one frontier into focused pages so future agents can load the relevant risk surface without reloading every unresolved clinical, ethical, phenomenological, and guidance question at once.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen's complete-experience model is useful only when practice increases sensory differentiation, noninterference, embodiment, support, ethical responsiveness, and service.
- **Why this matters**: The same vocabulary can be misused to spiritualize endurance, dissociation, medical neglect, emotional flooding, no-self fixation, Flow chasing, altered-phenomena status, Source overclaim, teacher authority, or positivity bypass.
- **Key tensions**: Compiled sources give strong partial criteria but not full stop rules, trauma/clinical differentials, medical triage, teacher-competence standards, or behavior-verification protocols.
- **Source posture**: Open question informed by compiled primary sources; the hub is a map, while the child pages carry the working criteria and evidence needs.

Gate 9 begins the medical-adjacent layer. [[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 1
of 3]] says health-crisis meditation can aim at objective healing,
suffering reduction, or both, but Shinzen's default teaching is the
suffering-reduction branch because it does not depend on proving a disease-
course effect ([[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 1 of 3]] S3-S8). Part 2
adds the practice fork: the illness-related "sensory challenge" includes
physical symptoms, emotional body distress, negative Image, and negative
Talk; focus-away can be healthy when the challenge is permitted in the
background, but this does not make positivity, Rest, music, or ten minutes
daily into medical treatment ([[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 2 of 3]]
S5-S9). Part 3 adds the focus-on branch, where direct contact becomes
healthy only through trained separation of physical body, emotional body,
visual thought, and auditory thought plus equanimity toward each strand
([[Mindfulness, Cancer & Healing - 3 of 3]] S1-S9). Cancer, serious illness,
injury, treatment decisions, symptom escalation, and cure claims therefore
stay under ordinary medical care and role clarity before any practice
optimization.

[[How to Guide Someone through the Death Process using Mindfulness]] adds the
end-of-life edge to the same medical-adjacent layer. Shinzen's usable safety
sequence is: do not treat the person as already dying when survival or
recovery orientation is still live; ask permission before offering guidance;
and secure advance consent before speaking one-way when weakness, medication,
or dying removes feedback ([[How to Guide Someone through the Death Process
using Mindfulness]] S4-S5). Rest, Flow, and Gone can be queried as practice
doors, but the source supplies no hospice, palliative, medical, capacity,
family, religious, legal, or grief-care criteria ([[How to Guide Someone
through the Death Process using Mindfulness]] S6-S9).

[[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 1 of 2]] extends the same medical-
adjacent caution to childbirth. Shinzen's durable instruction is sensory and
rhythmic: a trained practitioner may use contraction peaks as Touch expansion
while Image, Talk, and emotional selfing contract into Rest, then relax during
valleys ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 1 of 2]] S1-S9). The source
does not supply obstetric triage, pain-relief rules, trauma screening,
clinician communication, or fetal/maternal safety criteria, so birth guidance
must route through ordinary qualified birth care before meditation
optimization.

[[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]] adds the postpartum and
parenting edge. Shinzen frames the baby as a Roshi-like source of dynamic
nothingness and treats raw infant-like Feel as a compassion handle, but the
source gives no pediatric, postpartum, sleep, family, attachment, trauma, or
clinical criteria ([[A Mindful Birth and Zen Parenting, Part 2 of 2]]
S4-S9). Parenting practice therefore routes through responsive care and
ordinary support before Source, monastery, or primordial-Feel language.

[[Sleep Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] adds the sleep edge. Shinzen
normalizes some practice-linked sleep disturbance and gives a simple router:
keep the body lying still so it gets rest, keep a continuous technique going
so consciousness rests, and reframe the goal from a good night's sleep to a
good night's rest ([[Sleep Interruption & A Good Night's Rest]] S1-S8). The
source does not give sleep-medicine, psychiatric, neurological, medication,
substance, pain, trauma, or retreat-management criteria, so severe or
function-impairing sleep disruption remains an ordinary qualified-support
case before meditation optimization.

[[Do You Think Sex is Dirty]] adds the sexuality edge. Shinzen distinguishes
sex itself from craving and unconsciousness that obscure pleasure, then says
lovemaking can become pleasure meditation when CCE carries into intense
pleasant Touch and the practitioner does not tighten around pleasure's
arising or grasp at its passing ([[Do You Think Sex is Dirty]] S1-S9). The
source gives no consent, ethics, trauma, compulsion, power-dynamics,
relationship, sexual-health, or clinical criteria, so sexuality guidance must
route through ordinary protection, mutuality, and qualified support before
complete-experience language is used.

[[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways Mindfulness Meditation]] adds the dream edge.
Shinzen says lucid dreams can use the same practice labels as waking life:
dream sight as sight, dream contact as Touch, dream self-talk as Talk, and
dream melting or permeability as Flow ([[Lucid Dreaming and Five Ways
Mindfulness Meditation]] S2-S8). The source gives no induction, dosage,
nightmare, trauma, parasomnia, sleep-disruption, dissociation, or clinical
criteria, so dream-practice guidance must protect ordinary sleep, waking
functioning, and altered-state boundaries before treating dream lucidity as
practice mileage.

[[Tea, Coffee and Meditation]] adds the caffeine/substance edge. Shinzen says
a stimulant is not automatically a problem if it does not make the
practitioner jangled, because meditation has a clarity-alertness component as
well as a tranquility component ([[Tea, Coffee and Meditation]] S2-S4). The
source gives no dose, sleep, anxiety, mania, medication, cardiovascular,
pregnancy, addiction, or medical criteria, so stimulant questions route
through ordinary health, sleep, recovery, medication, and functioning checks
before being treated as practice optimization.

## Compact Criteria
The child pages should preserve five cross-cutting tests:

- **Clarity test**: experience should become more distinguishable and trackable, not more vague, blank, or fused.
- **Equanimity test**: practice should reduce craving, aversion, and interference without becoming suppression, self-punishment, or numbness.
- **Embodiment test**: practice should preserve bodily safety, posture responsiveness, sleep/rest needs, medical care, and ordinary action.
- **Behavior test**: feelings should motivate and direct rather than drive and distort; claimed insight should improve repair, commitments, and service.
- **Support test**: difficulty that exceeds the current practice frame should route to competent guidance, therapy, medical care, recovery support, legal protection, peer feedback, or stopping.

Gate 3 adds one checkpoint rule to all five tests: intensity is never the
criterion by itself. Complete experience, purification taste, Strong
Determination, kriyas, retreat aftershock, and bhanga should be interpreted
through CCE, non-fighting learning, embodiment, behavior, and support before
being treated as transformation evidence.

[[How We Evolve and Integrate]] adds a behavior-output form of the same rule.
Complete sensory experience should not stop at inner relief: Shinzen says the
output side must shift from behavior driven by suffering and frustration to
dynamic behavior motivated by pain and fulfillment, and that no-self action
must learn from mistakes rather than justify mistreating people ([[How We
Evolve and Integrate]] S2-S5, S13).

[[How Meditation Can Bring an Enlightened Perspective to the 6 O'Clock News]] adds the public-suffering version of the behavior test. News-triggered
rage, terror, grief, shame, and helplessness can be valid Focus In material
when the result is emotion that motivates and directs action rather than
drives and distorts it; the same source explicitly says meditation is not the
whole picture of effective action ([[How Meditation Can Bring an Enlightened Perspective to the 6 O'Clock News]] S1-S8).

[[The Reptilian Brain, Skinnerian Training & the Experience of God]] adds a
feedback-loop version of the same rule. Formal practice may train the body-
level mind through the immediate contrast between fighting sensation and
noninterference, but "reward and punishment" is only an analogy for felt
learning. It must not become self-punishment, coercive retreat pressure,
biological reductionism, or a reason to ignore destabilization signals
([[The Reptilian Brain, Skinnerian Training & the Experience of God]]
S2-S5).

[[Strengthening a Weak Ego Structure Through Mindfulness]] adds a self-
boundary edge to the clarity and support tests. Shinzen says the same
Feel/Image/Talk method can be oriented toward ego deconstruction or ego
strengthening, depending on whether ego is the problem or healthy ego
function is needed. If a person loses self-contact around another through
sight, sound, or touch, the safer Shinzen-internal move may be to keep
noting Feel, Image, and Talk so psychological boundaries remain available,
not to deepen merger or no-self language
([[Strengthening a Weak Ego Structure Through Mindfulness]] S1-S5). The source is too short to supply
clinical triage, so diagnosis, trauma, dissociation, impairment, or relational
safety still route through ordinary qualified support.

## Open Evidence
- The focused child pages now carry speculative routing matrices, but the
  wiki still lacks source-independent or professional-grade thresholds for
  pain, Strong Determination, kriyas, bhanga, dark-night reactions, no-self
  distress, trauma activation, DPDR, depression, anxiety,
  medication/substance effects, mania/psychosis risk, medical pain, and
  retreat aftershock.
- It lacks teacher-competence and referral criteria for live coaching, crisis routing, altered phenomena, Source-side destabilization, and claimed power/service interventions.
- It lacks criteria for coordinating Shinzen's micro-scale sensory practice with psychotherapy's macro-scale personality, behavior, history, diagnosis, and treatment work.
- It lacks criteria for medical-adjacent practice: when meditation is aimed at suffering reduction, when objective-healing imagery is being used, when focus-away is supportive versus suppressive, how to coordinate with medical care, and how to avoid cure promises or patient-blame causation claims.
- It lacks death-process guidance criteria: how to coordinate survival-versus-dying orientation, consent capacity, one-way guidance, hospice or palliative care, medication, delirium, family systems, religion, privacy, legal decisions, and grief support before Rest, Flow, Gone, or Source language is used.
- It lacks childbirth- and parenting-specific criteria: how contraction-rhythm practice coordinates with obstetric care, pain relief, consent, trauma history, support people, fetal/maternal monitoring, medical complications, postpartum mental health, pediatric needs, sleep, and family support.
- It lacks sleep-specific criteria: how to distinguish transient practice-linked wakefulness from chronic insomnia, sleep deprivation, pain-related sleep disruption, medication/substance effects, mania/psychosis-like activation, trauma flooding, neurological or medical sleep disorders, and unsafe daytime impairment.
- It lacks dream-practice criteria: how to distinguish optional lucid-dream practice from sleep disruption, lucid-control chasing, nightmare or trauma activation, parasomnias, dissociation, mania/psychosis-like activation, medication/substance effects, and impaired daytime functioning.
- It lacks caffeine/substance criteria: how to distinguish alert-clarity support from jangling, dependence, withdrawal, sleep loss, anxiety-like activation, mania/psychosis-like activation, medication interaction, medical risk, recovery concerns, and impaired ordinary function.
- It lacks sexuality-specific criteria: how to distinguish pleasure meditation from indulgence, dissociation, performance pressure, coercion, trauma reenactment, compulsive sexual behavior, relational avoidance, teacher overreach, sexual-health neglect, or clinical distress.
- It lacks behavior-verification standards for total happiness, Nurture Positive, purification, trickle-down change, Source-based care, and teacher accountability.
- It lacks criteria for distinguishing Shinzen's identity-elasticity definition of enlightenment from dissociation, depersonalization, disembodiment, inflation, or claims of ethical completion.
- It lacks criteria for distinguishing healthy no-self or relational openness from weak-ego self-loss, interpersonal boundary collapse, dissociation, trauma-related disorientation, personality-structure concerns, or other clinical-adjacent self-function issues.
- It still lacks source-backed dosage standards for stillness, motion, life
  practice, retreat, Do Nothing as a sole method, strong labels, zooming,
  challenge sequences, and high-intensity formal practice beyond the current
  green/yellow/red routing heuristics.
- It lacks criteria for live emotional-catharsis guidance: when prolonged
  fear/sadness, physiological heat, annihilation fear, subtle Image/Talk
  trigger tracking, local-global Flow, and eyes-open transfer are workable
  Shinzen practice versus reasons to simplify, stop, ground, add support, or
  seek therapy, medical care, or emergency help ([[From Suffering to Bliss]]
  S1-S10).
- It lacks criteria for when formal simplification is helpful body-level feedback training versus unsafe retreat intensification, isolation, coercion, dissociation, or endurance identity.
- It lacks safety, consent, privacy, escalation, clinical, regulatory,
  behavior-verification, and access criteria for Shinzen's late-life hopes
  around online retreats, AI or virtual-Shinzen guidance, and ultrasonic
  neuromodulation as equanimity support ([[A Life of Practice and Service
  Shinzen Young at 80]] S4-S8).
