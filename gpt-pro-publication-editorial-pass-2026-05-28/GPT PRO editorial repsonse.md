# Editorial Memo: Public Branch Recommendation

**My judgment: do not publish the wiki. Publish a curated “Shinzen Practice Atlas” built from it.**

The internal wiki is doing a very different job from what a public reader needs. Its own index says it is a “compiled working model,” not a transcript archive, biography, clinical guide, or general Buddhism encyclopedia, and it routes by practice handle, phenomenological distinction, technique choice, safety boundary, source tier, and service implication. That is a superb architecture for an agent memory layer, but it is not a finished human publication. 

The public object should be a **human-facing atlas of Shinzen Young’s practice system**: part field guide, part reference work, part curated hypertext book, with a visible safety-and-source posture. It should help serious readers understand how the system fits together, choose what to read next, distinguish Shinzen’s claims from editorial synthesis, and avoid misusing powerful practice language as therapy, status, metaphysics, or authority.

A note on scope: I reviewed the required files that were available in the packet and sampled across practice, transformation, teaching, service, and safety pages. The standalone `Complete Experience Safety Boundary.md` file was not present in the mounted file set I could inspect directly, so the safety assessment below relies on the available index/catalog/log references to that hub, the neighboring `Guidance Scope and Accountability Boundary.md`, and the extensive safety sections inside `Complete Experience.md`.

---

## 1. What should the human-facing branch be?

It should be **a curated practitioner’s atlas of Shinzen’s system**, not a public mirror of the current wiki.

The central public promise should be:

> This site maps Shinzen Young’s meditation system for serious human readers: the core skills, sensory vocabulary, practice routes, transformation claims, safety boundaries, and source posture.

That object is not exactly a curriculum, because Shinzen’s system is modular and path-fit rather than simply linear. It is not merely a reference work, because readers need editorial sequencing and judgment. It is not a conventional wiki, because the material is too safety-sensitive, source-tiered, and jargon-laden to let every page sit at the same public altitude.

The current “Current Model” page already names the strongest editorial through-line: Shinzen’s system is best modeled as **sensory completion with interface evolution**. Concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity train experience toward “complete experience”; the older Sensory Grid and newer See Hear Feel interface route practice; and the deeper material around insight, purification, no-self, impermanence, Source, behavior, and service must remain map-humble and safety-aware. 

That is the public branch’s organizing thesis. The public branch should make that thesis intelligible to humans.

I would give it a working title such as:

**The Shinzen Practice Atlas**
*A field guide to CCE, See/Hear/Feel, complete experience, Flow, Gone, no-self, service, and safety in Shinzen Young’s system.*

The atlas should have two reading modes: a **guided path** for newcomers to the system, and a **reference map** for practitioners, teachers, researchers, and editors who arrive with a term or problem in mind.

## 2. Who is the best reader, and who is not?

The best reader is not “the general public.” The best reader is a serious, motivated reader who can tolerate precision.

Best readers:

* A Shinzen or Unified Mindfulness practitioner trying to understand the architecture behind the techniques.
* A meditation teacher, coach, or advanced practitioner who needs source-calibrated distinctions.
* A researcher, writer, or translator trying to understand Shinzen’s idiolect without flattening it into generic Buddhism or generic mindfulness.
* An experienced meditator from another tradition trying to map terms like noting, equanimity, no-self, Flow, Gone, Source, and purification into Shinzen’s vocabulary.
* A careful beginner who wants a map before going deep, but not someone seeking a quick wellness app experience.

Not the best reader:

* Someone in acute psychiatric, medical, relational, or spiritual crisis who needs direct qualified support.
* Someone looking for individualized practice diagnosis from a website.
* Someone seeking proof that Shinzen’s deepest claims are scientifically, metaphysically, or clinically established.
* Someone looking for a simple “10-minute mindfulness” introduction.
* Someone seeking attainment certification, teacher authority, or spiritual status.

The public branch should be welcoming, but it should not pretend to be universally accessible. The current model itself says the system is strongest as Shinzen’s own practice, teaching, and transformation architecture, while speculative when used as metaphysics, empirical science, clinical guidance, power ontology, or universal comparative religion.

## 3. What should the first public navigation experience feel like?

It should feel like entering a **map room**, not a database.

The current internal index is already a first-load router for agents: it tells the agent what to load next by task, when to go to guidance, when to go to safety, and when to descend to source pages.  For humans, that structure should become a designed landing experience with three visible doors:

**Door 1: Understand the system.**
Start with CCE, See/Hear/Feel, sensory experience, complete experience, and why Shinzen’s system is not just “pay attention.”

**Door 2: Choose a practice route.**
Noting, Do Nothing, Focus on Rest, Nurture Positive, Turn Toward/Turn Away, Flow, Gone, and daily-life practice.

**Door 3: Read the boundaries.**
What this site is not; when to get support; completion versus bypass; intensity; DPDR/void distress; teacher/accountability; behavior and service tests; what is Shinzen’s claim versus editorial inference.

The first screen should not list 345 pages. It should say, plainly:

> This is a map of Shinzen Young’s practice system. It is not a substitute for a teacher, therapist, clinician, or emergency support. It distinguishes Shinzen’s stated teachings, compiled synthesis, editorial inference, and speculation.

The public branch should preserve the internal wiki’s “load next by task” intelligence, but convert it into human categories: **Start Here**, **Practice Routes**, **Phenomenology Atlas**, **Transformation Claims**, **Safety and Scope**, **Sources and Claim Tiers**.

## 4. What current material is strongest as public writing?

The strongest public material is where the wiki has distilled Shinzen’s system into durable distinctions rather than merely routing future agents.

The most public-ready core is:

**`Mindfulness Skill Triad.md`**
This should become one of the first public pages. It defines mindful awareness as concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity working together, and it explains why mindfulness is not just focus, calm, or bare awareness.  Its boundary section is also valuable because it explicitly says the triad is not a measurement model and does not supply dosage, clinical, trauma, disability, or worsening criteria. 

**`Basic Mindfulness Practice Architecture.md`, `See Hear Feel.md`, and `Sensory Grid.md`**
These should become the “interface” section. The public needs to understand the relationship between the older Basic Mindfulness grid and the later See Hear Feel compression. This is one of the most valuable editorial distinctions in the corpus.

**`Noting.md`, `Do Nothing.md`, `Focus on Rest.md`, `Nurture Positive.md`**
These are natural public “technique cards,” but they should be rewritten in a human page shape: what it is, why it matters, how it is commonly misunderstood, when to switch, and where safety enters. `Nurture Positive.md` is especially useful because it preserves Shinzen’s constructive practice route while warning that positive construction can support behavior and service or become bypass if not tested. 

**`Complete Experience.md` and `Insight and Purification.md`**
These are the transformation heart of the project. `Science of Enlightenment Chapter 5 - Insight and Purification.md` gives the source-level formula: concentration + sensory clarity + equanimity + time = insight + purification.  But this material needs public rewriting because it is high-leverage and high-risk. The same source page flags weak evidence, missing criteria, and the danger of overstating “science” language. 

**`Flow.md`, `Gone.md`, `Impermanence Flow Gone And Source.md`**
This cluster is valuable as an advanced phenomenology atlas. It should not be the entry point. It should be available once the reader understands CCE, sensory categories, and the safety posture.

**`No-Self And Personality.md`**
This is public-worthy because it protects against a common failure mode: reading no-self as personality erasure, nihilism, or aversion to self-experience. It should be edited carefully and probably paired with DPDR/void safety.

**`Total Happiness.md` and `Total Happiness Behavior And Service Test.md`**
These pages are essential because they keep the system from becoming private-state spirituality. The public branch needs a visible claim that practice must return to behavior, service, repair, and ordinary life.

**`Lineage Translation.md` and `Shinzen’s Teaching Method.md`**
These are valuable public essays. `Shinzen’s Teaching Method.md` correctly names precision, idiolect, live feedback, lineage translation, self-critique, path-fit humility, and anti-guru accountability as part of the teaching style.  This could become one of the more distinctive public pieces, because it explains why Shinzen’s language is both powerful and burdensome.

A lot of the current wiki should not be public in its present form.

**Definitely internal-only:**

* `_operations.md`
* `_templates.md`
* `_post_ingest_knowledge_health_plan.md`
* `_page_catalog.md`
* `log.md`
* raw ingest plans

* frontmatter fields like `load_when`, `best_linked_pages`, `importance`, `confidence`
* source-page integration notes and model-delta machinery
* agent-facing “use contract” language

These are valuable editorial back-office materials, not public content. The post-ingest plan itself says the next constraint is durable compiled knowledge health: cheaper first loads, progressive disclosure, better routing, and less redundant prose, not making the wiki “prettier.”  That is an internal production strategy, not a public reading experience.

**Do not publish `Current Model.md` as-is.**
It is an excellent editorial brief and whole-system compression, but it reads like a strategic routing document. Turn it into public essays, section architecture, and a short “editor’s model” page. Do not ask the public to parse the whole internal synthesis.

**Do not publish `Practice Guidance Toolkit.md` as-is.**
It is explicitly “agent-facing,” and its job is to help a future agent decide what to load next and what provisional guidance branch fits a live practice report.  It should be mined for a public page called something like “Choosing a Practice Route,” but the public page must not look like a website can safely guide live practice problems.

**Do not publish raw source pages as the main public experience.**
Source pages are excellent audit artifacts. They preserve provenance, weak claims, omissions, tensions, and integration notes. But most public readers should not land there first. Selected source notes can be public in a “Sources and Claim Tiers” section.

**Delay or heavily rewrite high-risk applied pages.**
Material involving severe pain, Strong Determination, sexuality, dying, illness, substances, retreat aftershock, DPDR, dark night, altered phenomena, powers, Source, and teacher conduct must be rewritten under an explicit safety/scope frame. `Complete Experience.md` repeatedly warns that complete experience should not be inferred from intensity, endurance, decomposition, dramatic phenomena, erotic charge, or willingness alone.

## 7. Tone, page shape, citation posture, and safety posture

### Tone

The tone should be:

* precise but not bureaucratic;
* serious but not solemn;
* Shinzen-literate but not cultic;
* practice-facing but not prescriptive beyond scope;
* calm about mystical language without either inflating or sneering at it.

Use phrases like:

* “In Shinzen’s system…”
* “The compiled synthesis reads this as…”
* “This is source-attributed, not independently established here.”
* “This page is not clinical guidance.”
* “This distinction matters in practice because…”

Do not use a promotional wellness tone. Do not use a guru-transmission tone. Do not use a purely academic tone that kills the practice handles.

### Page shape

A good public page should usually have:

1. **Plain opening claim**
2. **Why this matters**
3. **The core distinction**
4. **How it shows up in practice**
5. **Common confusions**
6. **Safety and scope**
7. **Source posture**
8. **Related pages**

The post-ingest plan’s “hub and child” theory is good, but it should be humanized. It recommends that hub pages be compact decision surfaces with key points, decision maps, safety/source posture, and decision-relevant links, while child pages own one durable subjob.  That is exactly right for the public branch, but the tables should be used sparingly and written for readers, not agents.

### Citation posture

The public branch needs explicit claim tiers:

* **Shinzen says**: directly attributed to Shinzen source material.
* **Compiled synthesis**: the editor’s best compression of multiple Shinzen sources.
* **Editorial inference**: reasoned but not directly stated.
* **Speculative extension**: useful possibility, not established.
* **Not established here**: beyond the source base or outside the site’s scope.

This matters because the current model explicitly says the system is strongest as Shinzen’s own practice architecture and weaker when used as metaphysics, empirical science, clinical guidance, power ontology, or universal comparative religion. 

### Safety posture

Every technique and transformation page should carry the same public baseline:

> Meditation language does not replace medical care, therapy, emergency support, consent, ordinary ethics, relationship repair, or qualified guidance.

The available safety materials strongly support this. `Guidance Scope and Accountability Boundary.md` says practice depth and good intention do not remove the need for feedback channels, referral, behavior verification, consent, anti-dependency, dialogue, and outside support.  Its decision matrix explicitly blocks meditation-teacher authority in medical, medication, obstetric, sleep, sexual-health, legal, and relationship domains.

## 8. First concrete editorial moves

1. **Create a separate public branch.**
   Do not compromise the internal memory system. Treat it as the editorial back office.

2. **Write a new public home page from scratch.**
   Do not adapt `index.md` linearly. The home page should have: what this is, who it is for, how to read it, where to start, and safety/source limits.

3. **Create a public “How to read this site” page.**
   Explain Shinzen claim versus synthesis versus inference versus speculation. Explain that the site is not a clinical or individualized guidance tool.

4. **Write 10 spine pages before publishing any broad archive:**

   * What Shinzen’s System Is
   * Mindfulness as CCE
   * See/Hear/Feel and the Sensory Grid
   * The Main Practice Routes
   * Complete Experience
   * Insight and Purification
   * Flow and Gone
   * No-Self Without Erasing Personality
   * Total Happiness and the Behavior/Service Test
   * Safety, Scope, and Accountability

5. **Recast `Current Model.md` into an editorial outline.**
   Use it to drive structure, but do not publish it as a reader-facing page.

6. **Rewrite `Practice Guidance Toolkit.md` as “Choosing a Practice Route.”**
   Remove agent-language. Keep method-choice intelligence. Add a strong boundary that the page does not diagnose live reports.

7. **Split public `Complete Experience` into at least three pages:**

   * Complete Experience: the core idea
   * Signs and Non-Signs of Completion
   * Completion Versus Bypass and Intensity

   This is necessary because the current page contains both the central beauty of the system and a long list of high-stakes misuse boundaries. It says the claim is not proven for every person, condition, or pain intensity; it also says complete experience is not liking pain, suppressing emotion, dissociating, becoming indifferent, or using insight language to avoid repair. 

8. **Create a glossary early.**
   Terms like CCE, Feel, Image, Talk, Flow, Gone, Rest, Space, Source, complete experience, purification, and Total Happiness must be discoverable without each page becoming bloated.

9. **Publish only a curated first release.**
   I would launch with 25–40 pages, not 345. The first release should privilege orientation, core practice routes, safety, and a few source notes.

10. **Keep source notes optional and clean.**
    A reader should be able to click through to source posture, weak claims, and omissions, but should not have to read source audit pages to understand the system.

## 10. Risks if mishandled

The biggest risk is **publishing the agent memory layer as if it were a human book**. That would make the project look more authoritative, more confusing, and less safe than it is.

Specific risks:

**1. Turning practice maps into clinical advice.**
The source base repeatedly admits missing criteria around trauma, medical issues, psychiatric risk, dosage, referral, and stop rules. `What Is Mindfulness.md` says the public article does not provide clinical protocols for trauma, DP/DR, panic, mania, psychosis risk, severe depression, medical pain, substances, sleep deprivation, or medication interactions. 

**2. Letting “complete experience” become endurance ideology.**
Complete experience is one of the system’s most powerful ideas and one of its easiest to misuse. The page itself warns against inferring completion from intensity, endurance, strong activation, erotic charge, kriyas, visionary material, or dramatic polarizations. 

**3. Letting Source, Zero, Flow, Gone, and no-self become status claims.**
The current model says no-self, witness, void, Gone, dissolution, and Source language should route through operational and safety boundaries before being treated as liberation evidence.  Public pages must preserve that humility.

**4. Flattening Shinzen’s idiolect into generic mindfulness.**
The value here is not “meditation helps.” It is the exactness of concentration, sensory clarity, equanimity, sensory decomposition, Feel/Image/Talk, Flow, Gone, Rest, See/Hear/Feel, complete experience, and Total Happiness. If those are flattened, the public branch loses its reason to exist.

**5. Over-preserving internal structure.**
The opposite risk is publishing too much of the internal apparatus: source deltas, route tables, frontmatter, audit logs, and decision matrices. That would make a human reader feel like they have walked into a compiler.

**6. Self-certifying service and ethics.**
A major strength of the wiki is that it keeps behavior and service visible. A major risk is letting “Source,” “love,” “service,” or “realization” certify conduct. The guidance/accountability page explicitly says feedback, consent, referral, behavior verification, anti-dependency, and outside support remain necessary. 

**7. Overclaiming science.**
The Chapter 5 source page flags the claim that Shinzen’s theory deserves the name science as rhetoric unless later evidence adds measurement and falsifiability.  A public branch should be science-literate, not science-branded.

**8. Publishing before source rights and quotation policy are settled.**
The public branch should cite and paraphrase carefully. It should not expose long raw-source material unless rights and quotation limits are clear.

---

## Bottom-line recommendation

Build the public branch, but only as a **curated, source-calibrated atlas**.

Do not launch a public clone. Do not launch the full index. Do not launch the source archive first. Do not start with advanced Source/no-self/Flow/Gone material. Start with the public spine: CCE, sensory routing, practice routes, complete experience, safety, and life/service verification.

The internal wiki’s highest value is that it has already done the hard interpretive work: it knows what distinctions matter, where safety lives, what claims are source-attributed, and what remains unresolved. The public branch should not expose that machinery. It should turn the machinery into a readable map.
