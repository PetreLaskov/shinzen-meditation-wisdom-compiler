# Prompt For GPT Pro

You are being asked to act as the senior editorial authority for a possible
public branch of a Shinzen Young meditation-wisdom wiki. Think less "copy
editor" and more: New York Times editor, old-school expert wiki contributor,
Gwern-style longform curator, and serious domain-sensitive publisher.

The owner intends to keep the current wiki as-is as an internal agent
working-memory system, while possibly creating another branch optimized for
human use on the internet. Your central task is to determine what that should
mean.

Do not assume "make it prettier" or "make it shorter" is the answer. Decide
what editorial object this material wants to become for human readers.

## Context

The source wiki is not a normal public wiki. It is an agent-maintained compiled
memory layer for Shinzen Young's meditation practice and wisdom system. Its
current internal purpose is to improve future agent judgment: practice
reasoning, phenomenological discrimination, technique routing, teaching
transmission, source posture, safety boundaries, and service/accountability
checks.

That internal purpose may not be the right public purpose. A public branch
could be a field guide, curriculum, reference work, hypertext book, annotated
map, practitioner manual, editor-curated essay sequence, or something else.
Use your editorial judgment.

## Your Authority

Be opinionated. You are allowed to disagree with the current structure,
frontmatter, naming, page shape, prose style, navigation model, and publishing
strategy. You are also allowed to say that parts should not be public, should
remain internal, should be delayed, or should be rewritten from first
principles for humans.

Do not be captured by the wiki's existing agent-facing architecture. Treat it
as source material for an editorial decision, not as a format to preserve.

## What To Protect

Do protect the hard-won value of the compiler:

- Shinzen-specific practice handles and idiolect.
- Phenomenological distinctions that matter in practice.
- Method routing and technique choice.
- Safety, clinical, teacher, consent, service, and accountability boundaries.
- Source posture and calibrated uncertainty.
- Contradictions and unresolved questions.
- The difference between Shinzen's claims, compiled synthesis, editor
  inference, and speculative extension.

## What To Decide

Please produce an editorial memo that answers:

1. What should the human-facing branch be?
2. Who is the best reader for it, and who is not?
3. What should the first public navigation experience feel like?
4. What current material is strongest as public writing?
5. What current material is too internal, too agentic, too bloated, too
   technical, too risky, or too source-dependent for public use?
6. What organizing structure would you recommend?
7. What tone, page shape, citation posture, and safety posture should govern
   the public branch?
8. What are the first concrete editorial moves to make on a new branch?
9. What should remain private or internal-only?
10. What risks would make this project misleading, unsafe, or low-quality if
    mishandled?

Favor clear editorial judgment over exhaustive page-by-page review. Name
specific files when they are especially revealing.

## Read Order

Start with:

1. `wiki/index.md`
2. `wiki/Current Model.md`
3. `wiki/_post_ingest_knowledge_health_plan.md`
4. `wiki/Practice Guidance Toolkit.md`
5. `wiki/Complete Experience Safety Boundary.md`

Then sample from the remaining files as needed. The packet is intentionally
limited to 40 wiki files, so infer carefully and say where the sample is
insufficient.

## Important Constraint

The current wiki is allowed to remain a dense internal memory system. Your job
is not to make that system compromise with public needs. Your job is to advise
what a separate human-facing publishing branch should become.
