The Practice Guidance Toolkit is a routing page, not a source digest. It helps a future agent decide what to load next and what provisional guidance branch fits a live practice report.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen-style guidance is branch-and-loop: understand the person's sensory and objective world, choose a provisional strategy, tune the method, ask what happened, and update the branch.
- **Why this matters**: The same symptom can route differently depending on active focus range, capacity, venue, safety signals, and whether the issue is inside the chosen method or calls for a different method.
- **Key tensions**: The sources supply rich practice routing but not full clinical, medical, trauma, consent, teacher-competence, privacy, or emergency protocols.
- **Source posture**: Strong as a compiled map of Shinzen's guidance moves; incomplete as a safety or professional-scope standard.

## Agent Use Contract
Use this page to decide where to route, not to answer high-risk questions from memory. When a concrete branch matters, load the linked owner or source page and cite it.

Every guidance answer should preserve four checks:

- **Context**: What technique, focus range, venue, and life situation are active?
- **Branch**: Is the next move contact, support, Flow/change, Rest/positive, Do Nothing, method simplification, or outside help?
- **Quality**: Does the branch increase concentration, sensory clarity, equanimity, ordinary responsiveness, or behavior change?
- **Loop**: What report, behavior commitment, or follow-up would confirm or revise the branch?

## Minimum Inputs
| Input | Why It Routes |
| --- | --- |
| Practice aim | Reduced suffering, fulfillment, self-understanding, behavior change, and service are different success surfaces ([[Why Meditate]] S1-S5). |
| Current method and range | The same event is handled differently in Focus on Everything, Focus Out, Focus on Rest, Do Nothing, or Nurture Positive ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Noting Nutshell and FAQ]] S13). |
| Live report after the last instruction | Shinzen's oral coaching loop depends on giving a technique, checking what happened, and modifying or redirecting from there ([[What Are Your Specialties as a Teacher]] S4). |
| CCE capacity | Low contact, low discrimination, or low equanimity imply different tuning moves inside the same method. |
| Venue | Stillness, motion, and life practice have different load levels and task-safety constraints ([[Practice Cycles]]). |
| Risk and support | Pain, trauma activation, dissociation, medical signals, teacher pressure, or behavior harm can override meditation optimization. |
| Follow-up signal | Guidance is incomplete unless there is a behavior, support, or next-report check ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S10-S13). |

## Fast Routing Algorithm
1. **Scope gate first**: if the report involves emergency risk, medical danger, self-harm, psychosis/mania-like instability, abuse, coercion, severe dissociation, or ordinary protection needs, route to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] and ordinary qualified support before technique optimization.
2. **Classify the live problem**: path choice, session setup, focus coverage, effort regulation, Noting option, Do Nothing/Rest issue, sensory challenge, emotional challenge, positive reconstruction, life transfer, teacher/accountability issue, or crisis-scale Wall/Window.
3. **Choose the branch family**: turn toward, turn away with background equanimity, focus on Flow/change, stabilize in Rest or positive material, release effort through Do Nothing, simplify the method, add guidance/support, or shift to behavior/accountability work.
4. **Tune the controls**: range, coverage strategy, label strength, label pace, zooming, emphasis, re-noting, effort level, venue load, sequence, or follow-up.
5. **Close the loop**: ask what changed in sensory contact, equanimity, behavior, support, and life functioning. If the report worsens or becomes ambiguous, downgrade confidence and load the safety frontier.

## Branch Families
**Turn toward** brings CCE directly to the challenge. Load [[Turn Toward and Turn Away]] first, then a physical, emotional, agitation, evocation, or Flow source as needed.

**Turn away** stabilizes attention elsewhere while the challenge is genuinely allowed in the background. The core criteria are permission, no disappearance agenda, and some willingness to turn toward later ([[Using Turn Away and Background Equanimity with Sensory Challenges]] S5-S8).

**Focus on change** uses Flow or vanishing when change is actually detectable. It is a third branch, not a status marker; when the Flow branch is absent, drop it rather than manufacture it ([[Turn Towards Flow (Change) Using the Labeling Gears & Options]] S1-S9; [[Hear-In to Mental Talk Space, Feel Flow in Body Space]] S1-S5).

When Flow is broad, pleasant, and stable, the branch can widen rather than
intensify: spoken or optional Flow labels, zoom-out coverage through
body-mind-world, eyes-open sight or light if it naturally appears, and
carefully paced eye-contact transfer in a consented live-coaching setting
([[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 3 of 3]] S2-S6).

**Rest or positive support** stabilizes with rest factors, positive Feel/Image/Talk, or Nurture Positive. This is useful support when it preserves CCE and ordinary responsiveness, but it can become shutdown or positivity bypass.

**Do Nothing** releases the choosing and controlling function when choice-confusion or racy technique is active; it routes back to Noting when it becomes spacey ([[Do Nothing Meditation]] S3, S7).

**Outside support and accountability** enter when practice is not changing behavior, when the issue is clinical/medical/relational, or when teacher authority itself becomes part of the distortion ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S6-S10).

## Safety Gates
Do not continue algorithmic meditation routing as the only response when these are active:

- Medical red flags, injury risk, faintness, severe pain, neurological symptoms, or medication/substance concerns.
- Self-harm, harm to others, abuse, violence, coercion, emergency instability, or ordinary protection needs.
- Severe dissociation, panic, mania/psychosis-like states, trauma flooding, or inability to function.
- Teacher pressure, shame, privacy exposure, group contagion, retaliation risk, or lack of consent.
- Practice language replacing therapy, medical care, legal protection, behavior repair, or accountability.
- Repeated worsening without clear CCE gain, behavior improvement, or competent support.

When any gate is active, load [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] and answer with lower confidence. The guidance branch may still matter, but it is no longer the whole response.
