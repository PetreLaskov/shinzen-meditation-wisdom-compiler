Total Happiness is Shinzen's aim structure for why mindfulness matters. The Basic Mindfulness manual gives a four-quadrant version, the See Hear Feel introduction adds three-job and five-application versions, and the beginner FAQ talk names CCE-based complete sensory experience as the centerpiece of sensory happiness independent of conditions.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Total Happiness can be framed as three jobs, four quadrants, or five applications, but each version asks mindfulness to optimize surface and deep well-being for oneself and others through trainable CCE.
- **Why this matters**: It makes practice accountable to life and service, not only to concentration, calm, insight language, or private state changes.
- **Key tensions**: The model preserves ordinary goals while claiming condition-independent happiness; strong formulas and universal taxonomies should be read as practice heuristics rather than empirical equations.
- **Source posture**: Primary manual evidence for Shinzen's aim structure; probable as an interpretation of his system, not established as a universal happiness theory.

## Model
The See Hear Feel introduction gives the broadest explicit rationale: learn SHF to be optimally happy in every way possible, where "optimal" means as happy as possible given conditions that cannot feasibly be changed ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S5). Shinzen then gives three parallel taxonomies:

- **Three dimensions**: appreciate self and world, transcend self and world, and improve self and world.
- **Four dimensions**: gain condition-dependent happiness for oneself, gain condition-independent happiness for oneself, help others gain condition-dependent happiness, and help others gain condition-independent happiness.
- **Five dimensions**: reduce perceived suffering, elevate sensual fulfillment, understand oneself at all levels, make positive behavior changes, and cultivate or discover love and service ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S6).

The source says these are different ways of cutting up the same territory and claims that mindfulness skills directly or indirectly optimize every branch ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S7). That claim is load-bearing for Shinzen's rationale, but it remains his teaching frame rather than an established universal taxonomy.

[[Total Happiness - 1 of 5 - May Happiness Be]] opens the dedicated YouTube
series by making the phrase itself technical. Shinzen treats the Buddhist
"may happiness be" blessing as universal rather than merely personal and
uses it to defend happiness as a serious contemplative aim, then immediately
analyzes ordinary happiness-seeking as getting wanted conditions and avoiding
unwanted ones ([[Total Happiness - 1 of 5 - May Happiness Be]] S2-S4). The
philological details should stay source-attributed because the transcript
has auto-caption artifacts, but the practice move is durable: Total Happiness
means more than feeling cheerful, and it does not erase ordinary conditions.

The same talk gives a key sensory bridge. Objective conditions are known only
through sensory experience, including body sensation, emotional sensation,
mental image, and internal talk; thought is something one does, but it can
also be treated as visible/audible sensory material. Shinzen's oral handle is
that what is tangible is trackable, and what is trackable is tractable
([[Total Happiness - 1 of 5 - May Happiness Be]] S5-S6). This lets Total
Happiness route through [[Sensory Clarity]] without reducing material
conditions to "just thoughts."

[[Total Happiness - 2 of 5 - Ordinary and Extraordinary]] gives the dedicated
series' ordinary/extraordinary contrast. Ordinary body happiness means getting
pleasant physical and emotional sensations and avoiding unpleasant ones;
ordinary mind happiness means getting answers and avoiding Don't Know,
including muddle and confusion. Extraordinary happiness shifts the body side
from pleasure and discomfort management to fulfillment and freedom from
suffering, and shifts the mind side from ordinary answer-getting to the
question "what am I?" and direct Source-knowing ([[Total Happiness - 2 of 5 -
Ordinary and Extraordinary]] S1-S6).

The short YouTube talk [[Why Meditate]] gives the public-entry version of the five-dimension taxonomy. It repeats the same outcomes and adds a useful sequencing claim: love and service are described as the consequence of reduced suffering, elevated fulfillment, self-understanding, and behavior change, not only as a fifth item on a list ([[Why Meditate]] S1-S5).

The next beginner FAQ, [[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]], gives the mechanism underneath the taxonomy. Shinzen frames practice as personal conditional and unconditional happiness plus contribution to others' conditional and unconditional happiness, then says mindfulness raises baseline concentration, clarity, and equanimity and thereby raises baseline human happiness. He calls sensory happiness independent of conditions the centerpiece and links it to complete sensory experience of body and mind at a threshold of CCE intensity ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]] S2-S5).

Chapter 11 defines the four quadrants of total happiness in the Basic Mindfulness manual ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S7):

- **Surface happiness for oneself**: get pleasure, avoid discomfort, get mundane answers, avoid ordinary Don't Know, get desired situations, and avoid undesired situations.
- **Deep happiness for oneself**: use mindful awareness to increase fulfillment, decrease suffering, know who one is at the deepest level, let Don't Know become empowering, and change behavior.
- **Surface happiness for others**: help others get pleasure, avoid discomfort, get useful answers, and improve situations.
- **Deep happiness for others**: teach a path such as mindfulness or something with similar effects, or support those who teach such a path.

The manual does not reject surface happiness. It includes body pleasure, comfort, ordinary knowledge, and life situations. The distinctively contemplative move is that surface happiness is not enough to exhaust the aim.

## Deep Happiness
The deep-happiness formulas are the chapter's most compressed claims:

- fulfillment equals pleasure multiplied by mindful awareness,
- suffering equals discomfort divided by mindful awareness,
- Don't Know multiplied by mindful awareness becomes a new kind of knowing ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S8).

These formulas should be read as pedagogical equations. They express Shinzen's claim that [[Mindfulness Skill Triad|mindful awareness]] changes the relationship to pleasure, pain, and uncertainty. They do not prove a measured quantity of fulfillment or suffering.

Part 2 of the Total Happiness series restates the first two formulas in oral,
practice-routing form. Perceived suffering equals body discomfort intensity
divided by mindfulness: when concentration, clarity, and equanimity increase,
the discomfort may remain while suffering decreases, sometimes effectively to
zero in Shinzen's framing. Fulfillment equals pleasure multiplied by
mindfulness: small pleasure with high mindfulness may satisfy deeply, while
large pleasure without mindfulness may not satisfy ([[Total Happiness - 2 of
5 - Ordinary and Extraordinary]] S3-S5).

The same source makes the Don't Know branch more explicit. Ordinary mind
happiness seeks this-or-that answers and fears muddle, but extraordinary
mind happiness is oriented toward the question "what am I?" Shinzen calls
directly knowing Source salvation and wisdom, which should be kept as
source-attributed contemplative language rather than proof of a metaphysical
claim ([[Total Happiness - 2 of 5 - Ordinary and Extraordinary]] S2, S6).

[[Total Happiness - 3 of 5 - Don't Know Mind]] supplies the practice bridge
for that mind-side branch. Shinzen treats Don't Know as a disciplined
suspension of the need to know, not mere vagueness: equanimity with muddle
and confusion can become the basis for a new kind of knowing. He links this
to Greek skeptical suspension, Christian learned ignorance, and Zen Don't
Know Mind, but those historical parallels should stay source-attributed
because the transcript is auto-captioned and the practice handle matters
more than the philology ([[Total Happiness - 3 of 5 - Don't Know Mind]]
S2-S4).

The same talk extends the condition-independent claim to senility and
Alzheimer's through Shinzen's anecdotal reports of advanced practitioners
whose confusion manifested differently because of practice. This sharpens
the model but also raises the safety boundary: severe confusion and cognitive
impairment remain ordinary care, medical, and support issues, even if they
can also be parsed as touch, Feel, Image, and Talk experience under CCE
([[Total Happiness - 3 of 5 - Don't Know Mind]] S5-S6).

[[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]] adds the objective-behavior
side of extraordinary happiness. Shinzen says healthy transcendence of mind
and body must eventually appear in behavior; character, ethics, positive
behavior change, and best-effort performance are objective dimensions of
condition-independent happiness, even when outcomes fail. He also gives a
support-humility boundary: mindfulness skills can potentiate behavior change
because behavior is driven by touch, Feel, Image, and Talk, but they may not
be sufficient by themselves, and he reports needing psychiatric support for a
behavior issue ([[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]] S1-S5).

[[Purification and Fulfilment Four Formulas]] supplies the YouTube
micro-version of the body side. It separates mindful awareness into the
resistance/equanimity pivot: pleasure multiplied by resistance becomes
frustration, pleasure multiplied by equanimity becomes fulfillment,
discomfort multiplied by resistance becomes suffering, and discomfort
multiplied by equanimity becomes purification ([[Purification and Fulfilment
Four Formulas]] S1, S4-S7).

The body side of deep happiness links directly to [[Complete Experience]]. Pleasure with mindful awareness becomes more fulfilling; discomfort with mindful awareness becomes less binding as suffering. The mind side links to self-knowledge and uncertainty: mindful awareness functions as a microscope for "who am I at the deepest level," and not-knowing can become empowering rather than merely confusing ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S8).

The beginner FAQ makes this less abstract by naming body sensations and the thinking process as the fields where CCE crosses a threshold. When they become complete enough, Shinzen says ordinary body-mind becomes extraordinary and shifts from a prison into a home one can abide in but leave anytime ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]] S5-S6). This is a teaching analogy for freedom in relation to body-mind, not a standalone criterion for attainment.

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and Death,
Nirvana]] adds a Source-side gloss on the same condition-independent branch.
Shinzen defines nirvana as both cessation and fulfillment: the fire of
suffering is quenched, and thirst is quenched as happiness independent of
conditions. He explicitly keeps both meanings of *sukhi hotu*: may good
things happen, and may happiness not depend on good things happening
([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and Death,
Nirvana]] S6).

The objective side is behavior. Chapter 11 says behaviors are controlled by thoughts and body sensations, and mindful awareness gives a handle on those thoughts and sensations, helping desired behavior changes ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S8). This links Total Happiness to [[Suffering Distortion Cycle]] rather than leaving it as private well-being.

[[The Trickle-Down Paradigm of Transformation]] gives a retreat-aftereffect
version of the same behavior claim. Shinzen's marijuana example frames
behavior change as sometimes arriving without conscious decision, struggle,
or explicit self-improvement reasoning; the proposed test is whether
perception and behavior later change in significant, positive, and durable
ways ([[The Trickle-Down Paradigm of Transformation]] S4-S7).

[[What to Expect and Do After a Mindfulness Retreat]] gives the same test in
plain retreat-aftercare language. Shinzen says he does not evaluate whether
practice is working by asking about formal-practice experiences, afterglow,
aftershock, Flow, or special states; he asks whether the person is more
fulfilled, suffering less, and behaving better in daily life ([[What to
Expect and Do After a Mindfulness Retreat]] S7).

## Service
The final two quadrants make service structural. Helping others with surface happiness includes ordinary care: reducing discomfort, helping with knowledge, and improving situations. Helping others with deep happiness means teaching a path or supporting those who teach one ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S9).

The Basic Mindfulness introduction already anticipates this by saying a practice works if it reduces suffering, increases fulfillment, deepens self-knowledge, changes behavior, or cultivates love and service ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S5). Chapter 11 turns that outcome list into a final aim structure. [[Why Meditate]] then makes the sequence more explicit: service is the way the other four dimensions sum up as contribution to others and to the world ([[Why Meditate]] S5).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 10 gives a Source-side version of service. Returning to Source is not only for personal condition-independent happiness; because Source is shared, Shinzen says realizing it makes others appear as close kin and calls the practitioner toward spontaneous service ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S20). This strengthens the model's service axis, but it remains an internal contemplative claim unless behavior is verified.

The retreat-aftercare talk adds a post-retreat service version. Shinzen says
leaving retreat moves practitioners from mostly doing practice for themselves
toward service, including subtle effects of positive Feel, tranquility,
energy, and Flow in ordinary contact, describing the practice to others, and
for some people explicitly teaching it ([[What to Expect and Do After a
Mindfulness Retreat]] S10).

[[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] adds a four-level transmission
ladder to the service model. Shared Source contact may teach subtly through
affect, through visible behavior change over time, through a coherent
description of what one does in meditation, and finally through explicit
prescription of a technique with report-back. The talk treats coherent
description as real teaching without equating it with taking on the role of
meditation teacher ([[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] S5).

[[After Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The Point]] gives Gate 6A's
after-realization version of the service axis. Shinzen says the need to find
ultimate purpose itself must be worked through; as that need loosens, purpose
arises spontaneously, and the word he gives for the point is love ([[After
Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The Point]] S6). This supports the Total
Happiness claim that love and service are not ornamental add-ons to
realization, while leaving open how love is verified in conduct.

[[Classical Enlightenment Healing the World and Screw-ups]] gives a
large-scale version of the same service hope. Shinzen says millions of
stream-enterers would, over centuries, heal the world, and speculates that
millions of arhats would transform it much faster ([[Classical Enlightenment
Healing the World and Screw-ups]] S1-S6). This strengthens the system's
world-healing aim only as a scale-and-time claim, not as evidence that one
realized person, teacher, or community will reliably improve the world.

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell, Integration and Three
Tastes of Freedom]] adds a kinship image to the same Source-to-service
sequence. If self and world are experienced as coming from and returning to
the same formless womb, Shinzen says every human, animal, plant, and thing
can appear as literal kin; the egocentric view then becomes cosmocentric
concern as a call to service ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven,
Hell, Integration and Three Tastes of Freedom]] S7-S8).

The balanced-enlightenment talk makes that last boundary explicit. Shinzen says emptied ego, loving contact with each arising, and unswerving service can still coexist with bad judgment and harmful consequences ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S1). Total Happiness therefore cannot treat service feeling or deep realization as self-validating; behavior needs feedback, correction, and sometimes outside support.

[[Teacher's or Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] adds a
teacher-service boundary to condition-independent happiness. Shinzen says the
path aims at happiness independent of conditions, so a competent liberation
teacher should not create dependence on the teacher; the teacher should help
students become colleagues, peers, and independent practitioners who carry
the principles and skill set forward ([[Teacher's or Student's Issues
Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S1-S4). Teaching a path is therefore service
only when it transfers agency rather than capturing it.

[[Enlightenment and the Ten Zen Ox Herding Pictures]] gives the retreat-ending
marketplace version of the same service axis. Shinzen says practitioners may
return from retreat with gifts: the effects of practice in ordinary presence,
a coherent description if asked, and for some people eventual teaching when
students appear. He frames this as bodhicitta, the final cause of the path,
while preserving the boundary that service should not be imposed as a trip on
others ([[Enlightenment and the Ten Zen Ox Herding Pictures]] S4-S5).

[[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] gives the compact series version of
that final service picture. Shinzen says practice may begin from curiosity,
desperation, relief, or self-discovery, but its final cause is entering the
marketplace with hand outstretched: ordinary availability, practice effects
communicated through presence, coherent description when asked, and sometimes
teaching when students appear ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] S1-S5).

[[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 1 of 3]] gives the front-end happiness version of
the same path map. Searching for the ox means people know they want something
but mistake the target: they seek happiness dependent on conditions while
really wanting happiness independent of conditions ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics -
Part 1 of 3]] S2).

[[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]] makes the self/other split
explicit in the dedicated series. A person with ordinary and extraordinary
happiness for themselves is still not totally happy unless they contribute to
others' happiness. On the ordinary side, Shinzen names charity,
volunteering, hospice, helping professions, service-oriented work, family
care, and raising children as ways of helping others obtain conditions,
pleasure, and knowledge. On the extraordinary side, he introduces the
Source-knowing rationale: knowing where one comes from means recognizing
that everyone comes from the same place, so others' happiness becomes
automatically relevant ([[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]]
S6-S8).

[[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] completes the
dedicated series by defining teaching as fostering extraordinary happiness in
others. Shinzen makes teaching broad enough to include subtle influence
through practice-changed presence, coherent description when asked, explicit
technique and concept instruction, professional teaching, special competence
with classical enlightenment and rare enlightenment/DPDR mixtures, rare
Buddha-like path discovery, and support for teachers ([[Total Happiness - 5
of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S1-S5).

This source sharpens the service branch. Teaching a path is not only formal
authority; it may begin as presence or description. But it becomes more
competence-sensitive as it moves toward explicit instruction,
enlightenment-edge support, path innovation, and institutional support
([[Teaching A Path]]).

[[Bodhicitta, and the Bodhisattva Ideal]] adds a named service polarity after
the dedicated Total Happiness series. Shinzen defines bodhicitta as the clear
co-presence of "we've got to save the world" and "it's really not there":
compassion, service, and love are held together with emptiness rather than
ranked against liberating wisdom. The guided meditation then asks the human
self to return from nothingness and energy as kinder, more fulfilled, and
rejoicing in service ([[Bodhicitta, and the Bodhisattva Ideal]] S1-S5).

[[The Final Stage and Service]] gives the final-stage Source-side version of
the same service axis. Shinzen says ordinary life can become continuous
self/world/Source nonseparation and that if people experience sharing Zero,
they care for their larger identity and want to serve. This makes going
beyond the world and improving the world intimate rather than rival projects
([[The Final Stage and Service]] S1-S5).

[[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human Positivity]] adds a small
Human Goodness service image to the same axis. Shinzen identifies
lovingkindness as Focus on Positive Feel and uses a refugee-camp chaplaincy
anecdote to suggest that trained positive affect can become visible,
magnetizing, and radiative beyond the body ([[Becoming a High-Wattage
Broadcaster of Human Positivity]] S2-S4). This supports the subtle-service
branch while keeping the interpersonal-effect claim anecdotal.

[[From Surface to Source and the Gold Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] gives
the most compact maturity criterion for the same boundary. Shinzen names two
path principles: get over self and world by completely experiencing self and
world, and improve self and world. Their mutual reinforcement, not either
side alone, is the gold standard for spiritual maturity ([[From Surface to
Source and the Gold Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] S6-S7).

## Boundaries
Total Happiness should not be used to dismiss ordinary needs. Because surface happiness remains inside the model, food, safety, relationship, knowledge, medicine, and practical problem-solving are not replaced by deep happiness.

The SHF phrase "given conditions that cannot feasibly be changed" is an important boundary. It keeps condition-independent happiness from becoming an argument against changing conditions when change is possible. Shinzen's own three-job model includes improve self and world, so acceptance and action must stay paired ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Source Frame and Happiness Rationale]] S5-S6).

The same boundary applies to the YouTube phrase "sensory happiness independent of conditions." It should route to [[Complete Experience]], not to passivity, medical neglect, dissociation, or refusal to improve workable conditions ([[Beginner FAQs - Why Are We Doing This Why Meditate]] S4-S5).

The surface-to-Source gold standard sharpens that boundary: transcendence of
self/world is not mature when it weakens self/world improvement, and
self/world improvement is not the full contemplative aim when it never
loosens self/world fixation ([[From Surface to Source and the Gold Standard
for Spiritual Maturity]] S6-S7).

The same boundary applies to the nirvana language. Shinzen's gloss of
happiness independent of conditions explicitly coexists with wishing that
good conditions occur; it should not become a reason to dismiss conditional
suffering or avoid improving changeable circumstances ([[Expansion and
Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and Death, Nirvana]] S6).

Part 2 adds a practical branch criterion without fully operationalizing it.
Shinzen says to remove uncomfortable touch or Feel when that is appropriate
or possible, but to bring mindfulness to discomfort when it cannot or should
not be removed. The source does not specify medical, safety, trauma,
relationship, or practical-action criteria for deciding which branch is live,
so difficult cases route to [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] rather
than to endurance or passivity ([[Total Happiness - 2 of 5 - Ordinary and
Extraordinary]] S3-S4).

Part 3 adds the parallel mind-side boundary. Don't Know Mind should not be
used to dismiss facts, avoid decisions, refuse ordinary learning, romanticize
cognitive impairment, or discourage medical care. Confusion that is severe,
new, disabling, unsafe, dissociative, intoxication-related, neurological, or
clinically concerning needs ordinary support while any practice framing stays
secondary ([[Total Happiness - 3 of 5 - Don't Know Mind]] S1-S6).

Part 4 adds the behavior-support boundary. "I did my best" should not become
a way to avoid feedback, repair, skill development, or accountability; and
practice-supported behavior change should not be treated as sufficient when
psychiatric, therapeutic, coaching, medical, legal, community, or other
external support is needed. Shinzen's own psychiatrist example makes outside
support compatible with serious practice rather than evidence against it
([[Total Happiness - 4 of 5 - Self and Others]] S2-S4).

The model also needs behavior verification. A practitioner can feel more fulfilled or less reactive while behavior remains unchanged. Chapter 11 says mindfulness helps behavior by giving a handle on thoughts and body sensations, but it does not define how to verify durable behavior change.

Teaching a path is not automatically beneficial. The dedicated Total
Happiness source distinguishes subtle, descriptive, explicit, professional,
master-level, and Buddha-like teaching, but it does not define consent,
competence, power dynamics, trauma boundaries, supervision, or harms from
poor teaching. Those issues route to [[Teaching A Path]], [[Practice
Guidance Toolkit]], and [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]] ([[Total
Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S1-S5).

Source-based service carries the same boundary. A person may sincerely feel afterglow, kinship, or love and still need feedback, consent, repair, competence, and power-safety checks before teaching or intervening in another person's life. The YouTube entry-rationale claim that service follows from the other four dimensions should be treated as Shinzen's practice ideal, not as proof that internal improvement automatically produces beneficial contribution ([[Why Meditate]] S5).

The final-stage service talk strengthens that Source rationale but not the
verification standard. Continuous ordinary-life nonseparation and shared-Zero
care remain Shinzen's contemplative claims; they do not establish consent,
role clarity, service competence, or beneficial consequences by themselves
([[The Final Stage and Service]] S1-S5).

The high-wattage positivity talk has the same boundary on the Human Goodness
side. Radiating positive Feel may be a service practice, but perceived warmth,
charisma, or magnetism does not establish maturity, consent, skill, or
beneficial consequences ([[Becoming a High-Wattage Broadcaster of Human
Positivity]] S2-S4).

The after-enlightenment love claim has the same boundary. Naming love as the
point does not prove behavior, ethics, or service competence; the same talk
also says bad habits and screw-ups remain after realization and must be
worked through over time ([[After Enlightenment What Is Left What Is The
Point]] S2, S5-S6).

The mass-enlightenment healing claim is likewise not an immediate behavior
guarantee. Shinzen explicitly says he is not claiming world healing right
away and acknowledges that people who have seen no-self can still have major
public screw-ups ([[Classical Enlightenment Healing the World and Screw-ups]]
S3, S7-S8).

The formless-womb kinship image strengthens the felt rationale for service
but not the verification standard. Cosmocentric concern still needs ordinary
ethical limits, role clarity, consent, feedback, and behavior checks
([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell, Integration and Three
Tastes of Freedom]] S7-S8).

The marketplace ox-herding image strengthens the same service rationale but
not the verification standard. Subtle presence, coherent description, and
eventual teaching can be gifts; they still need consent, competence,
feedback, role clarity, and anti-dependency safeguards ([[Enlightenment and
the Ten Zen Ox Herding Pictures]] S5; [[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]]
S3-S5).

Teacher-based service carries an additional boundary. A person can be highly developed in some contemplative components and still need peer accountability, student feedback, therapy, 12-step support, or other structures when behavior does not change ([[Towards a Balanced Enlightenment]] S4, S6-S10).

Teacher-based service also has an anti-dependency boundary. Guidance,
gratitude, and transmission should not make the teacher-person a new
condition for happiness; the teacher-dependency source makes independent
application of principles and skills the success test ([[Teacher's or
Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S1-S6).

Finally, "deep happiness" is not the same as indifference to outcomes. The manual's equanimity frame says noninterference should free energy for response, not weaken care.

## Implications
Practice advice should ask which quadrant is active. Sometimes the right move is ordinary surface help. Sometimes it is sensory practice for one's own suffering. Sometimes it is behavior repair. Sometimes it is supporting another person's access to a path.

This also prevents a private-enlightenment reading of Basic Mindfulness. If practice does not support surface and deep well-being for oneself and others, it has not fulfilled the manual's stated aim.
