Basic Mindfulness is the practice-manual layer of Shinzen's system. It turns mindful awareness into a modular training environment: choose a skill emphasis, choose a sensory route, choose a technique, apply it in stillness, motion, and life, then evaluate whether practice reduces suffering, increases fulfillment, deepens self-knowledge, improves behavior, or supports love and service. The later See Hear Feel interface simplifies that architecture without replacing its core grid, skills, or techniques.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: The manual is organized by CCE, the Five Ways, the Sensory Grid, expanded focus options, three techniques, practice cycles, guidance, and total happiness; later SHF hides much of that grid under flexible labels.
- **Why this matters**: It gives future agents a router rather than a technique list; practice can move by cell, column, row, all experience, SHF range, Gone, non-effort, positive reconstruction, life venue, Wall/Window, or service aim.
- **Key tensions**: The architecture is elegant and large; chapters 8-12 preserve optionality and guidance, but selection, clinical boundaries, teacher competence, and behavior verification remain under-specified.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's own practice architecture; outcome, Source, death-preparation, science, and interpersonal-radiation claims remain source-attributed.

## Architecture
The manual's first invariant is the [[Mindfulness Skill Triad]]. Mindful awareness is concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity working together ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S2-S3). This is the training target underneath every focus option.

The second invariant is modularity. The introduction says practitioners do not need to try every option if one or two work ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S1). Chapter 9 confirms the same principle for techniques: [[Noting]], [[Do Nothing]], and [[Nurture Positive]] may be learned in any order, need not all be learned, and can be practiced as a cycle if all three are learned ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 9 - The Full Grid]] S5). The session-setup talk gives this modularity a practical format: use one technique, make a Five Ways workout sequence, or branch dynamically through the method menu ([[Three Ways to Set Up Your Basic Mindfulness Session]] S1-S7).

The third invariant is sensory grammar. Chapter 7 makes the [[Sensory Grid]] explicit as See/Hear/Feel crossed with In/Out/Rest/Flow, using descriptive language for discussion and short labels for practice ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 7 - A Science of Sensory Experience]] S3, S8). Chapter 8 then extends the grid beyond columns to rows, all-experience noting, and [[Gone]] ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S2-S5).

The fourth invariant is application. Chapter 10 makes the grid memorable through symbols and a mnemonic sequence ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 10 - Beauty]] S2-S5). Chapter 11 links sensory experience to actions, situations, practice cycles, fun, and [[Total Happiness]] ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S1-S8). Chapter 12 turns the full system into a Big Picture guidance map with Walls, Windows, turn-toward and turn-away strategies, behavior commitments, and stillness/motion/life venues ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S9-S13).

The fifth invariant is interface evolution. SHF Unit 08 says the pre-2015 system, formerly Basic Mindfulness, used a complex grid of noting techniques, while the newer Unified Mindfulness system integrates most noting techniques into the simpler [[See Hear Feel]] approach. The grid remains present, somewhat extended, and hidden under the simplifying paradigm of flexible labeling ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S7-S8).

## Route Map
The first five Ways remain the manual's major thematic routes:

- [[Way of Thoughts and Emotions]]: inner image, talk, and emotional body sensation; "Untangle & Be Free"; know oneself as a sensory system ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 1 - The Way of Thoughts and Emotions]] S7-S12; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 6 - The Five Ways in a Nutshell]] S1-S2).
- [[Way of Physical Senses]]: physical sight, sound, and body sensation; "Anchor & Merge"; know oneself as Another ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 2 - The Way of the Physical Senses]] S4-S12; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 6 - The Five Ways in a Nutshell]] S1-S2).
- [[Way of Tranquility]]: visual, auditory, and somatic rest plus [[Do Nothing]]; "Refresh & Release"; know oneself as Serenity ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 3 - The Way of Tranquility]] S2-S10; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 6 - The Five Ways in a Nutshell]] S1-S2).
- [[Way of Flow]]: movement, change, energy, force, and [[Expansion And Contraction]]; "Dissolve & Digest"; know oneself as Spirit Energy ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 4 - The Way of Flow]] S1-S7; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 6 - The Five Ways in a Nutshell]] S1-S2).
- [[Way of Human Goodness]]: [[Nurture Positive]] through positive image, talk, body feeling, behavior, cognition, ideals, situations, and service; "Love & Serve"; know oneself as the best one can be ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S7-S12; [[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 6 - The Five Ways in a Nutshell]] S1-S2).

The expanded grid adds five more Noting options: Focus on See, Focus on Hear, Focus on Feel, Focus on Everything, and Just Note Gone ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S2-S5). These are not new philosophies. They are different widths and orientations for applying the same grid.

The later SHF introduction turns four of those expanded options into the standard beginner interface: Focus on Everything, Focus on See, Focus on Hear, and Focus on Feel. The suggested optional sequence starts broad, scrolls through the three modality rows, and returns to broad: Everything, See, Hear, Feel, Everything ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Starting Focus Ranges]] S2-S3). This does not erase the older Five Ways route; it shows a later Unified Mindfulness entry point that begins with range width and modality rather than chapter-by-chapter themes.

Unit 08 then confirms the compatibility rule. Old terms such as See In, Hear Out, and Feel Rest remain useful shorthand and may still be used as labels, even though they are no longer the standard compact interface. The old system is upwardly compatible with the new one ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S9).

## Techniques
[[Noting]] acknowledges a sensory event and focuses on it, with labels as optional supports ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S8-S9). It can drill into cells, float through columns, float through rows, note all experience, or specialize in Gone.

[[Do Nothing]] drops noticed voluntary intentions to control attention. It belongs to the manual's rest/release logic but is not merely a rest-state technique ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 3 - The Way of Tranquility]] S8-S10).

[[Nurture Positive]] intentionally creates and holds wholesome inner image, talk, and body-feeling patterns around positive themes ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 5 - The Way of Human Goodness]] S10-S12). It gives the system a constructive technique alongside deconstructive observation and effort-dropping.

The current Unified Mindfulness formulation keeps these technique boundaries but changes some interface defaults: Nurture Positive is simplified from six themes to four, explicit Gone becomes optional rather than automatic, and Do Nothing remains unchanged ([[See Hear Feel Introduction - Practice Organization and System Transition]] S10-S12).

Chapter 9's cycle claim is important but not diagnostic. The source says the three techniques can cycle; it does not specify when cycling is better than staying with one practice ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 9 - The Full Grid]] S5).

## Outcome Logic
The manual's pragmatic test remains decisive: in a reasonable time frame, practice should deliver at least one of reduced suffering, increased fulfillment, deeper self-knowledge, positive behavior change, or love and service ([[Basic Mindfulness Introduction]] S5).

The expanded grid strengthens that outcome logic rather than replacing it. Chapter 7 says sensory classification is useful because it can reduce suffering, increase fulfillment through Rest and Flow, and enable behavior change by deconstructing negative urges and reinforcing positive intentions ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 7 - A Science of Sensory Experience]] S4).

The final chapters make the outcome logic explicit as [[Total Happiness]]. Surface happiness for oneself and others remains part of the aim, while deep happiness depends on mindful awareness changing pleasure, discomfort, Don't Know, self-knowledge, behavior, and service ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S7-S9). This means practice maturity is not only a session-state question; it is also an action, support, and service question.

## Boundaries
Basic Mindfulness should not be flattened into Noting. Noting is central, but the full architecture also includes Do Nothing and Nurture Positive, and chapter 9 explicitly allows technique optionality and cycling.

It also should not be flattened into the older grid or the newer SHF triangle alone. The old grid is precise and still valid; SHF is more compact and easier to start; both are interface choices inside a larger CCE architecture.

The architecture should also not be treated as a complete safety map. Chapter 8 introduces a major new safety marker: intense Goneness, Emptiness, and No-Self may produce fear, loss, confusion, and disorientation, and may require Noting, Nurture Positive, Flow, and intensive support ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 8 - Five More Ways]] S9). That belongs in [[Complete Experience Safety Boundary]].

Finally, the grid is pragmatic rather than ontological. Chapter 7 argues for a science-like sensory classification but admits that some conventions are artificial, especially smell/taste placement and Feel In/Feel Out ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 7 - A Science of Sensory Experience]] S5-S7). Chapters 11-12 add Source, historical, scientific, and guidance frames, but those should be treated as Shinzen's interpretive architecture unless independently supported.

## Implications
Future practice reasoning should route through five questions:

- Which skill is most needed: concentration, clarity, equanimity, or balance?
- Should this session reduce choice to one technique, sequence multiple Ways, or branch responsively?
- Which scope fits now: one cell, one column, one row, an SHF starter range, all experience, Gone, Do Nothing, or Nurture Positive?
- Which theme is live: inner activity, physical senses, rest, Flow, goodness, vanishing, or everything?
- Which risk is active: avoidance, over-effort, spaciness, racy noting, Flow fixation, emptiness/dark-night distress, positivity bypass, medical need, trauma activation, or behavior not changing?
- What evidence would show the practice is working in life?
- What support, teacher feedback, behavior commitment, or ordinary action is needed now?

For advanced practitioners, the manual's value is not the novelty of every label. Its value is that object, scope, effort, construction, deconstruction, and safety can be adjusted without leaving one CCE-based system.
