Source and Polarities is Shinzen's deep-view frame. It interprets experience as arising from a zero-like Source that polarizes into Expansion and Contraction, returns through Gone, and is known afterward as a sensory afterglow, while leaving science and cosmology parallels uncertain.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen uses Source, Zero, Expansion, Contraction, and Gone as a contemplative polarity model for experience, practice, life, nonduality, and service.
- **Why this matters**: The frame helps interpret advanced Flow, Gone, Do Nothing, bhanga, and Big Picture material without laundering it into established physics or metaphysics.
- **Key tensions**: The practice-level polarity labels are clear, but direct-Source, science, nature, mathematics, and spontaneous-service claims require careful posture.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's own interpretive frame; speculative as an account of objective reality beyond practice phenomenology.

## Model
The local root is [[Expansion And Contraction]]. Chapter 4 defines Expansion and Contraction as deep Flow labels: increasing, spreading, scattering, decreasing, squeezing, gripping, and simultaneous Both. At a deeper level, Shinzen says one may notice pure space spreading and collapsing, and may experience each moment as Nothingness polarizing into simultaneous Expansion-Contraction and returning to Nothingness when those forces cancel ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 4 - The Way of Flow]] S6-S7).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 3 supplies the first book-level cross-tradition Source setup. Shinzen reads Jewish bittul ha-yesh and creation from nothing, Sufi fana into Allah, Taoist yin-yang polarity and internal alchemy, and Raja yoga's seedless samadhi as parallel or roughly corresponding ways of pointing from high concentration into Source, Nothingness, unitive, or polarity territory ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 3 - Mysticism in World Culture]] S4, S6-S7). This is a translation strategy, not proof that the traditions have identical metaphysics.

Chapter 11 makes the same frame part of life architecture. It names the "Activity of the Source" as one source or zero with two sides, Expansion and Contraction. One zero polarizes into effortless spread and collapse, and all experience arises in the gap this creates ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 11 - Life]] S10).

Chapter 12 then expands the frame. The Basic View includes Source as Zero, Expansion, and Contraction; the Deep View describes polar forces molding practice and human life; later sections extend polarity analogies into nature, mathematics, and contemplative-science questions ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S3, S14).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 6 adds the strongest local Source-wave claim so far. At the deepest level, Shinzen frames [[Impermanence]] as the activity of Source and says liberation is the freedom to move between ordinary functional solidity and Source-wave experience rather than uninterrupted altered-state absorption ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 6 - The Many Faces of Impermanence]] S19).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 7 supplies the surface/intermediate/Source map. Surface consciousness experiences separate self, solid objects, rigid space, and linear time; Source contact is described as nonseparate self/world, nonsolid objects, elastic or collapsing space, and cyclic arising and returning. The [[Intermediate Realm]] lies between them as blockage and power, which explains why altered phenomena are neither the surface nor Source itself ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S1-S4).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 8 adds the no-self version. Shinzen says self-enquiry may track the I-am-ness behind I-am-ness until transfinite I-am-ness is reached, while analytic vipassana may penetrate mind-body components until they are seen as expanding-contracting space ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S6). The chapter's deepest no-self claim describes each moment of self as born from pure space through simultaneous expansion-contraction and dying through [[Gone]] ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S18).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 9 gives the strongest local Gone-to-Source account. It says ordinary Gones point to the Uncreated or Source, but also clarifies that Source is not itself a sensory experience; it is indirectly contacted through vanishing, while the aftereffect of vanishing becomes more sensorially defined ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S9). This is an important posture marker: Shinzen is not simply adding a new subtle object to the sensory grid.

[[Abrupt Flow Diminishings, Vanishings and Noting Gone]] gives the oral
practice version of the same boundary. Repeated ordinary Gones are said to
advert attention toward "some thing that's actually not a thing," contacted
through the senses but not experienced as a sensory experience; the sensory
side is the aftereffect becoming clearer in the circuits ([[Abrupt Flow
Diminishings, Vanishings and Noting Gone]] S4-S5). This strengthens Gone as
a Source route while preserving the not-a-sense-object boundary.

[[Self-Enquiry and Mindfulness Meditation]] adds the method-comparison
version. Shinzen says self-enquiry turns awareness back toward the origin of
an arising, while mindfulness can approach the same Source-facing boundary by
watching the current event vanish. The claim sharpens the event-cycle model:
where A goes in Gone is where B comes from in arising ([[Self-Enquiry and
Mindfulness Meditation]] S4-S5).

[[The Absolute Witness]] adds the witness translation. Shinzen says a
detached relative witness is a useful equanimity metaphor but still
constructed by subtle Feel/Image/Talk. The absolute witness is not a watcher
behind experience; it is contentless consciousness, Zero, nirodha, Source,
emptiness, or cessation, exposed at the disappearance of phenomena and at the
disappearance of observer-making Feel/Image/Talk ([[The Absolute Witness]]
S2-S7).

[[A Deeper Freedom Experiences of Selflessness]] adds the self-rearising
version of the same Zero boundary. Feel/Image/Talk can first be untangled,
then known as waves, then stop into Zero, but the deepest freedom named in
the talk is their re-arising from Zero as unblocked personality that is never
fundamentally separate from nothingness ([[A Deeper Freedom Experiences of
Selflessness]] S1-S5). This confirms that Source language should include the
return-to-life phase, not only cessation.

[[Humility to the Vanishing Point No Self Around the World]] adds the
comparative-language version of that return-to-life phase. Shinzen glosses
Jewish *bittul ha-yesh* as annihilation of self/world somethingness followed
by continuous arising from divine nothing, pairs Sufi *fana* with *baqa* as
stabilization with Source/God, and pairs Christian *kenosis* with *theosis*
as goodness, compassion, and service after emptying ([[Humility to the
Vanishing Point No Self Around the World]] S1-S5). This strengthens the
claim that Source rhetoric should be read as emptying plus re-arising, not
as blank cessation alone.

[[Discrimination and Unification - Part 2 of 4]] repeats the same Source-facing
Gone claim in a compact way: the instant of vanishing turns attention toward
the place outside place and time where the next instant of experience is born.
The talk is useful because it immediately returns to ordinary Flow taxonomy,
so the Source claim stays tied to practice discrimination rather than becoming
a standalone metaphysical assertion ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part
2 of 4]] S1-S2).

[[Flow, Gone and a Figure-Ground Reversal]] adds the combined Flow-Gone image.
When Flow and Gone become the whole rhythm, Shinzen says a matrix of
nothingness can appear to birth self and seeing moment by moment. The same
scintillating mist may coagulate into self/world or remain uncoagulated as
pure spirit, with effortless spreading and pulling-in continuing as invisible
doing ([[Flow, Gone and a Figure-Ground Reversal]] S5-S6).

[[No Place to Stand]] adds the no-center teaching version. Shinzen says he
does not want the practitioner to secure a center; he wants the center to
have the practitioner. This belongs to the Source/polarity page only as a
practice metaphor for decentering and figure-ground reversal, not as proof
that Source, center, or place is a directly perceived metaphysical object
([[No Place to Stand]] S4-S7).

[[Enlightenment and the Ten Zen Ox Herding Pictures]] adds a classical
path-map expression of the same Source-facing return. Riding the ox
backwards means surrendering to impermanence and the dynamic wave of
"nothing whatsoeverness" without needing orientation, while the final three
pictures are read as the substance, appearance, and ultimate use of
enlightenment: no substance, ordinary things, and marketplace service
([[Enlightenment and the Ten Zen Ox Herding Pictures]] S3-S5).

[[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 2 of 3]] gives the compact series version of
that middle and late map. Riding backward is surrender to the Flow of
impermanence, "falling up into existence" moment by moment, and letting go of
the need to be oriented or have answers. The same source pairs empty
substance with the ordinary world's appearances, making no-substance
inseparable from mountains, blossoms, and things as form ([[Zen Ox-Herding
Pics - Part 2 of 3]] S2-S5).

[[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] completes the Source-to-service side of
the same map. Shinzen says the final call to service follows from tangibly
knowing that one's Source is everyone's Source, but this belongs here as a
Source-facing contemplative interpretation rather than as a verified conduct
guarantee ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] S2-S5).

[[Tri-Modal Rest and Flow Thinning Out into Nirvana]] adds the tranquility-side
route into the same Source territory. Shinzen says somatic, visual, and
auditory Rest that all begin to Flow thin and melt self/world; he then places
that intermediate zone between ordinary solidity and the absolute Rest of
nirvana ([[Tri-Modal Rest and Flow Thinning Out into Nirvana]] S1-S4).

The second Discrimination and Unification talk adds a bounded spaciousness
translation. Shinzen links Expansion-Contraction flavors to space or
time-space inflating and collapsing, then cautions that spaciousness should
not mean a pre-existing solid stage called space. Complete experience is rich
and transparent enough to present as space-ness ([[Discrimination and
Unification - Part 2 of 4]] S4-S6).

The third Discrimination and Unification talk adds a self/world version of
the same spaciousness route. If space is noticed growing with expansion and
collapsing with contraction, Shinzen says practice can focus on the spacious
nature of consciousness; from there, the "I" and the "it" may be understood
as one arabesque of flowing spacious energy rather than two fixed objects
([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 3 of 4]] S7-S8).

Chapter 9 then adds the [[Primordial Feel]] version of Source. Gone points to Zero as the most primordial, nonpersonal groundless ground; the next human level is chaotic infant body emotion. At the deepest polarity edge, Zero splits into Expansion and Contraction, the gap becomes pure space, and that space vibrates into pure heat of Feel before resolving back through the polarities into Gone ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S15-S18).

Science of Enlightenment chapter 10 is now the strongest Source page in the compiled sequence. It translates Joshu's mu into Gone experienced with great clarity, says each sensory or motor event is born from simultaneous Expansion-Contraction, and identifies Gone as the mutual cancellation of those polar forces into Zero ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S2-S4). This makes Source a direct extension of the impermanence model rather than an unrelated metaphysical add-on.

The first Zero and One talk adds the series-level entry. Shinzen names Zero
and One as two major contemplative path paradigms and links Sanskrit
shunya/shunyata to both zero and emptiness. He then frames overwhelming
experience as large polarization from Source and purification as the return
or neutralization that follows a complete experience of that polarization
([[Zero and One - Part 1 of 4 Complete Experiences]] S1-S3). This confirms
that the Zero/One language is not separate from the polarity model; it is one
of Shinzen's ways of teaching it.

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 1 Kenotic Christianity and Shuniya]]
returns to that Zero/One setup from a comparative angle. Shinzen links
Sanskrit shunya or shunyata, Greek kenos, Christian kenosis, and theosis to
emptying the somethingness of self so direct participation in spiritual
Source can occur. In the same talk, the still point is approached not only by
seeking stillness but by surrendering to movement and all Flow ([[Expansion
and Contraction - Part 1 Kenotic Christianity and Shuniya]] S3-S4).

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 2 Zen Metaphors and Three Tastes]]
connects that Source polarity frame to momentary selfhood. Shinzen says the
"child" born between father and mother can mean the self of this moment:
Zero comes apart and vibrates into the Feel/Image/Talk self and the
Touch/Sight/Sound scene. The same talk compresses the sensory result into
three tastes: positive, negative, and Zero, with Zero pervading both
polarities and surrounding the whole field ([[Expansion and Contraction -
Part 2 Zen Metaphors and Three Tastes]] S3-S6).

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and Death,
Nirvana]] adds the living-nirvana version. Shinzen says simultaneous
Expansion-Contraction is surrounded by Zero, so contrast need not imply
separate birth. Surrendering to life and death means giving oneself to the
yes/no and push-out/pull-in forces of experience until the small personal
fraction merges with the totality and re-emerges from Zero through ordinary
activity ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and
Death, Nirvana]] S2-S5).

The same talk also sharpens the nirvana side of the Source frame. Shinzen
defines nirvana as both cessation and complete fulfillment: the fire of
suffering is quenched, and thirst is quenched as happiness independent of
conditions. Living nirvana means not losing track of the Source-polarity
cycle while looking, talking, planning, eating, lovemaking, and arguing
([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to Life and Death,
Nirvana]] S5-S6).

[[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell, Integration and Three
Tastes of Freedom]] extends the same frame into integration and service.
Shinzen says ordinary tastes disappear into liberation yet can be parsed as
life, death, and Zero; integrated body, mind, and world arise from Zero
without fully separating from Source; and shared formless-womb origin makes
all beings appear as kin ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell,
Integration and Three Tastes of Freedom]] S5-S8).

The fourth Zero and One talk supplies the series-level completion. After
describing head-heart, body-mind, inside-outside, and self-world integration
through Flow, Shinzen says a deeper contrast remains: creation and formless
Source, or in Western language creation and Creator. He calls their
integration "one with zero" and "living nirvana" ([[Zero and One - Part 4 of
4 Complete Experiences, Unifications & Integrations]] S5-S6). This confirms
that Zero/One language is both a practice-integration map and an advanced
Source interpretation.

The same chapter adds the afterglow boundary. In the actual cancellation of Expansion-Contraction there is no self to know Source, so Source or Zero is not directly experienced. The next instant supplies an after-representation composed of image, talk, feeling, fulfillment, safety, peace, and love. Enlightened wisdom is therefore a conditioned sensory representation of the unconditioned, not the unconditioned itself ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S14-S15).

Chapter 10 also links Source to [[Dissolution]] and service. Surface-to-Source practice can destabilize, and bhanga may appear as blissful vibratory Flow or harsh chaos ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S16-S19). The chapter closes by saying shared Source realization makes others appear as close kin and calls the practitioner toward spontaneous service ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S20).

[[From Surface to Source and the Gold Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] gives
the compact oral ladder for this page. Ordinary or restful untangling weakens
self's thingness; deeper untangling sees the strands as Flow; watching the
vibrations disappear routes to Gone and then Source; and the mature test is
that getting over self/world through complete sensory experience reinforces
improving self/world ([[From Surface to Source and the Gold Standard for
Spiritual Maturity]] S1-S7).

[[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] gives a more detailed oral
sequence for that Source and service link. When self-side Feel/Image/Talk and
object-side sight, sound, or touch both present as simultaneous
Expansion-Contraction, Shinzen says their waves cannot be held apart and the
inside/outside split is momentarily annihilated into Source activity. The
same talk says regular contact with shared Source makes one respond to the
needs of a larger identity through affect, behavior, coherent description,
and explicit teaching ([[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] S3, S5).

[[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] gives a shorter Gate 4 route
from ordinary Flow to Source-side language. Local sensory events can be seen
as creating spatial volume while already passing, producing bi-flow and then
Gone; globally, Shinzen describes everything as born between expanding,
contracting, spiraling Flow, with vanishings giving tranquility. The same talk
warns that this is optional vocabulary, not a required awakening map
([[The Theme of Expansive and Contractive Flow]] S6-S8).

[[Paradigms of Change]] adds a Source-side reason for preferring the
three-dimensional polarity idiom in some advanced contexts. Shinzen says
flat arising-and-passing can leave an observer watching events, while
spherical simultaneous Expansion-Contraction draws observer and surroundings
into the same process of being stated into existence and annihilated by the
flux and reflux of Source ([[Paradigms of Change]] S5-S6).

[[Mindfulness Momentum, Arising and Passing to Simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction]] adds a products-of-Source formulation. Shinzen defines Source
as the activity of Zero that polarizes and neutralizes, while the products of
Source are the moment-by-moment sensory self and world: Feel/Image/Talk on
the subjective side and Touch/Sight/Sound on the objective side. Because both
arise between Expansion and Contraction, the source and its products are not
fundamentally separable ([[Mindfulness Momentum, Arising and Passing to
Simultaneous Expansion and Contraction]] S5-S7).

[[Non-Dual Awareness]] gives the Gate 6B name for this Source/products
nonseparation when it becomes habitual in daily life. Shinzen reserves his
preferred use of non-dual awareness for repeated Zero-polarization-Zero
cycling: self and world regenerate in the cleft, but if their origin is
recognized, they are not fundamentally severed from Zero and ordinary life is
not split from Source ([[Non-Dual Awareness]] S3-S4).

[[The Final Stage and Service]] gives the same Source/products relation as a
final-stage service teaching. Shinzen says the final stage, if stage language
is used at all, is ordinary-life nonseparation of self, world, and Source,
not a philosophy or suspended altered state. He routes it through local
touch, sight, sound, Feel, Image, and Talk and global whole-field sensitivity
to arising-already-passing and Expansion-Contraction, then links shared Zero
to service toward one's larger identity ([[The Final Stage and Service]]
S1-S5).

[[Zen, Vipassana, and Becoming Impermanence]] gives a related
emptiness-to-form translation in compressed oral form. Shinzen says the Zen
style attends not only to form as emptiness but to emptiness becoming form
moment by moment, with nothing repolarizing into energy that one can ride.
This supports the Source/polarity frame, but its qi and Kabbalah language
should remain source-attributed translation rather than independent
metaphysical proof ([[Zen, Vipassana, and Becoming Impermanence]] S1-S3).

[[Dissolution (Bhanga), and T.S. Eliot]] adds a compact Christian translation
example. Shinzen reads Eliot's purgatorial imagery as a mostly harsh
bhanga-like expression and says the Holy Spirit, in that passage, functions
as the Christian term for the activity of impermanence that dissolves the
somethingness of self so nothing remains between the practitioner and the
beatific vision, which he glosses as direct experience of Source
([[Dissolution (Bhanga), and T.S. Eliot]] S6-S7). This strengthens the
translation map while preserving source posture: it is Shinzen's teaching
bridge, not proof of doctrinal equivalence.

[[Experiences of the Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 2 of 3]] adds an
interactive Source-side idiom. Shinzen frames the journey as surface to
Source and says some practitioners dissolve into the Flow and Gone of Source,
while the student later describes a figure-ground reversal in which the self
that expected to gain consciousness, power, clarity, and success is erased
and the always-present qualities become clear ([[Experiences of the
Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 2 of 3]] S1, S7).

## Practice Polarities
The most practical part of this frame is not cosmology. It is the set of tensions that shape practice:

- active stance versus passive stance,
- bearing down versus easing up,
- working with parts versus working with the whole,
- making distinctions versus destroying distinctions,
- appreciating the personal self versus going beyond the self through impersonal Source,
- improving the self versus going beyond the self through impersonal Source ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S14).

These pairs help interpret why Basic Mindfulness contains contrasting techniques. [[Noting]] makes distinctions; [[Do Nothing]] drops control. [[Nurture Positive]] improves the person; Flow, Gone, and Source language go beyond ordinary personhood. A mature practice may need both sides rather than privileging only one.

[[Impermanence Flow Gone And Source]] now serves as the Gate 4 synthesis for
this page's practical route. It should be loaded before this page when the
question is mainly how ordinary change, Flow, Gone, Expansion-Contraction,
Source afterglow, and service fit together; this page remains the deeper owner
for Zero, Source, polarity, comparative translation, and speculative science
boundaries.

## Life And Nature Analogies
Chapter 12 also maps Expansion and Contraction onto human life: subjective activation can feel like inward tug, physical senses can feel like outward tug, undesired events can be interpreted impersonally as space contracted, and desired events as space expanded ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S14).

The natural-world and mathematics sections are much broader. The chapter points to galaxies, stars, black holes, planets, animal anatomy, cells, proteins, atoms, and number systems as examples or analogies of expansion-contraction structures. It also says the natural world can be looked at through nothingness and polarity, then asks whether that resembles contemplative formulations ([[Basic Mindfulness Chapter 12 - The Big Picture]] S14).

Chapter 10 repeats and sharpens that caution. Shinzen explicitly agrees with Russell that mystical experience does not settle objective reality claims, then marks free-energy, entropy, thermodynamics, dialectics, complex numbers, and nature analogies as conjectural or playful speculation ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S6). This keeps the page from treating analogy as evidence.

[[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] repeats the same caution inside a
more expansive analogy set. Stars, supernovae, galaxies, atoms, cosmology, and
quantum-scale examples illustrate the polarity frame, but Shinzen explicitly
allows that he may be projecting from his own contemplative experience and
returns the point of practice to deep happiness rather than scientific
validation ([[Born Between Expansion and Contraction]] S4).

[[The Three-Dimensional Shape of Simultaneous Expansion and Contraction]]
adds a neuroscience-flavored version of the same boundary. Shinzen says the
corona radiata and broad sensory-input/motor-output loops visually resemble
the contemplative fountain of gushing and gathering, but he explicitly says
he does not know whether the anatomy has anything to do with what he is
describing ([[The Three-Dimensional Shape of Simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction]] S3).

[[Fulfilling the Pythagorean Agenda]] gives the mathematics-and-science
version of this aspiration. Shinzen reads Pythagoras as having wanted to link
outer nature, inner thought/emotion/spirit, and number, but lacking three
infrastructures: a broad enough number system, experimental method, and
systematic high-concentration technology. He then says the modern world may
now have all three, so the "outer dance" and "inner dance" might someday be
modeled mathematically, including Expansion-Contraction contrasts
([[Fulfilling the Pythagorean Agenda]] S1-S4). The source is useful because
it names the hope; it does not supply the model.

## Boundaries
Source and Polarities is not established physics, biology, mathematics, or theology. It is safe to say Shinzen uses those fields analogically and speculatively; it is not safe to say the manual proves that consciousness and nature share a single polarity mechanism.

The Zero and One talk's nature language belongs under the same caution. It
speaks as if creating and wiping away distinction may be a fundamental
activity of nature, but this functions in the talk as contemplative framing
for practice completion rather than evidence about objective nature ([[Zero
and One - Part 1 of 4 Complete Experiences]] S3).

The Eliot talk's Christian language belongs under the same caution. Holy
Spirit, impermanence, beatific vision, and Source are linked by Shinzen as a
comparative contemplative reading, not as a settled theological or
metaphysical identity ([[Dissolution (Bhanga), and T.S. Eliot]] S7).

The Kenotic Christianity talk belongs under the same caution. Shunya,
kenosis, theosis, and Source function as Shinzen's oral translation bridge;
they should not be promoted into philological proof or doctrinal identity
without independent evidence ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 1 Kenotic
Christianity and Shuniya]] S3).

The Zen metaphors talk belongs under the same caution. Father/mother,
God/devil, success/failure, praise/blame, and similar pairs function as
Sasaki Roshi's polarity poetry in Shinzen's retelling; they should not be
promoted into moral hierarchy, gender ranking, theology, or cultural
essentialism ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 2 Zen Metaphors and Three
Tastes]] S1-S2).

The surrender talk belongs under the same caution. Its Neoplatonic,
corona-radiata, Pali, and Sasaki Roshi references function as Shinzen's oral
translation bridges. The corona-radiata claim is explicitly speculative, and
the torn-apart-by-nature language should not be promoted into a safety-free
practice prescription ([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 3 Surrendering to
Life and Death, Nirvana]] S1, S7).

The heaven/hell integration talk belongs under the same caution. Shamanic,
Greek, Sanskrit, Buddhist, and God/devil language functions as Shinzen's oral
Source/polarity translation, not as independent anthropology, philology,
theology, or proof that Source contact automatically produces ethical service
([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell, Integration and Three
Tastes of Freedom]] S3-S8).

The fourth Zero and One talk's "living nirvana" language belongs under the
same caution. It is Shinzen's Source-side interpretation of completed
integration, not independent evidence that creation and Creator are
metaphysically unified or that a practitioner has permanent behavioral
realization ([[Zero and One - Part 4 of 4 Complete Experiences, Unifications
& Integrations]] S6).

The tri-modal Rest talk's nirvana and self/world vaporization language belongs
under the same caution. It confirms Shinzen's Rest-to-Flow path-map rhetoric,
but the degraded transcript does not support textual proof, attainment
certification, or claims about death and heaven ([[Tri-Modal Rest and Flow
Thinning Out into Nirvana]] S2-S5).

The Discrimination and Unification spaciousness language belongs under the
same caution. "Space is laughing" and "space is crying" are Shinzen's Sasaki
Roshi translation of complete experience as space-ness, not a license to turn
space into an object, attainment marker, or dissociative self-erasure
([[Discrimination and Unification - Part 2 of 4]] S6).

The third talk's cosmic-consciousness and I-Thou language belongs under the
same caution. It is a practice interpretation of self/world waves adding
together, not evidence that ordinary boundaries, consent, behavior, and
accountability have been transcended ([[Discrimination and Unification - Part
3 of 4]] S7-S8).

It is also not a directly perceived object. Chapter 10's strongest phenomenological boundary is that Zero or Source is represented only afterward as afterglow because no self is present at the cancellation itself ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S14-S15). Future agents should avoid saying Shinzen literally experiences Source as another sensory datum.

The non-dual awareness talk should be held inside the same boundary. Its
"born but not really born" and "umbilical cord" language is a Source-side
practice interpretation of ordinary arising and return, not independent
evidence that Source has been objectively perceived or metaphysically proven
([[Non-Dual Awareness]] S3-S4).

The absolute-witness talk belongs under the same boundary. Its "contentless
consciousness" language is Shinzen's term for cessation/Zero, not evidence
that an ultimate observer-substance has been directly perceived as an object
or located behind experience ([[The Absolute Witness]] S5-S7).

The deeper-freedom selflessness talk belongs under the same boundary. Its
personality-from-nothingness language is Shinzen's contemplative
interpretation of no-self re-arising, not independent evidence that
personality has a metaphysical origin that can be objectively established
([[A Deeper Freedom Experiences of Selflessness]] S4-S5).

The humility-to-vanishing talk belongs under the same boundary. Its Sufi,
Jewish, Christian, Buddhist, East Asian, and Taoist parallels are Shinzen's
oral translation bridge, not independent evidence that those traditions share
one metaphysical doctrine or that divine-nothing language has been
objectively established ([[Humility to the Vanishing Point No Self Around the
World]] S1-S5).

The ox-herding talk belongs under the same boundary. Its Zen imagery and
reported oral tradition are Shinzen's path-map translation; they do not by
themselves prove a final metaphysics of consciousness or a historically
audited account of the last three pictures ([[Enlightenment and the Ten Zen
Ox Herding Pictures]] S1-S5).

The second ox-herding part belongs under the same boundary. "No informed
consent to enlightenment," "complete enlightenment," and "falling up into
existence" are Shinzen's oral path-map idioms; they should not override
ordinary consent, safety, or the afterglow-bound caution around Source
language ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 2 of 3]] S2-S5).

The third ox-herding part belongs under the same boundary. Shared-Source
service and "your pores will teach" language should not be used to certify
teaching readiness, ordinary conduct, or beneficial influence without
feedback and role clarity ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] S2-S5).

The final-stage service talk belongs under the same boundary. Continuous
prayer, practice of the presence of God, sahaja samadhi, non-dual awareness,
Sufi baqa, Jewish divine-nothing language, Cloud of Unknowing imagery, and
Native American direction-song imagery are Shinzen's oral translation bridges
for ordinary-life Source nonseparation, not independent comparative proof;
shared-Zero care also does not verify service competence by itself ([[The
Final Stage and Service]] S1-S5).

Chapter 3 uses stronger perennial and seedless-samadhi language before the later afterglow boundary has been stated. It should be read as Shinzen's broad [[Mysticism As Concentration|mysticism]] frame unless a question specifically asks for chapter 3's comparative rhetoric.

Chapter 7 uses stronger direct-contact language for Source. That should be read as a path-map claim unless the task specifically concerns chapter 7's rhetoric; chapter 10 supplies the later, more precise afterglow boundary and should govern direct-object claims ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 7 - The Realm of Power]] S2-S3; [[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S14-S15).

Chapter 8's true-self/no-self equivalence belongs under the same caution. It is Shinzen's interpretation of overlapping contemplative territory, not proof that all traditions mean the same thing by true self, no-self, Source, or transfinite I-am-ness ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 8 - The Real No Self]] S6).

Chapter 6 is also explicit that ordinary self and world still arise for liberated people. The claimed difference is interpretive and attentional freedom, not the permanent disappearance of ordinary functioning ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 6 - The Many Faces of Impermanence]] S19).

The frame can also be destabilizing if used prematurely. Gone and Source language can evoke fear, loss, confusion, and disorientation in some practitioners, according to the manual's Gone chapter ([[Gone]]). Flow and Expansion-Contraction can help restore vitality, but support may still be needed.

The second dissolution dialogue adds a disclosure version of this caution.
Shinzen says teaching about dissolution gives practitioners a map if it
happens, but can also scare them, make them crave it, or make them compare
their progress against dramatic Source-side phenomena ([[Experiences of the
Dissolution (Bhanga) Process - 2 of 3]] S2).

Chapter 9 widens the same boundary. Goneness, emptiness, and no-self can produce terror, paralysis, aversion, and hopelessness, and primordial Feel can involve intense body-emotion agitation ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 9 - The Power of Gone]] S13-S18). Source language should therefore not be used to encourage unsupported collapse, clinical neglect, or romanticized regression.

Finally, Source should not be used to bypass personal repair. Chapter 12's own practice polarities include both appreciating/improving the self and going beyond the self. Treating Source as superior to human behavior would misread the manual's life and service architecture.

Chapter 10 intensifies the same point from the service side: Source realization should make care easier and more natural, but the chapter does not give behavioral verification, power-safety, consent, or repair criteria for action that claims to come from Source ([[Science of Enlightenment Chapter 10 - Return to the Source]] S20).

The fourth Expansion-Contraction talk strengthens the kinship rationale for
that service claim, but also preserves the same missing criteria. Seeing all
beings as sharing the formless womb can support cosmocentric concern; it does
not by itself verify competence, consent, repair, or beneficial action
([[Expansion and Contraction - Part 4 Heaven, Hell, Integration and Three
Tastes of Freedom]] S7-S8).

The surface-to-Source talk adds the clearest bypass guardrail in this cluster:
getting over self/world and improving self/world must reinforce each other.
Source language that weakens ordinary repair, behavior, service, or support
fails that stated maturity criterion ([[From Surface to Source and the Gold
Standard for Spiritual Maturity]] S6-S7).

The expansive-contractive Flow theme adds an overuse caution. Shinzen says the
vocabulary is only a theme to explore and that many people become enlightened
without it, so Source/polarity language should not be treated as mandatory
terminology or a sign of superior realization ([[The Theme of Expansive and
Contractive Flow]] S8).

The paradigms talk adds a precision caution. It can sound as if
Expansion-Contraction simply replaces arising-and-passing, but Shinzen's
actual point is subtler: the spherical frame matters when it includes the
observer in the process. That should not be generalized into a claim that
ordinary anicca or activated impermanence practice is obsolete
([[Paradigms of Change]] S1-S6).

The mindfulness-momentum talk adds an attainment-comparison caution.
Describing Source/polarity experiences can orient practice, but it can also
turn into comparison mind; the talk itself undercuts the enlightenment versus
not-enlightenment distinction before presenting the advanced map
([[Mindfulness Momentum, Arising and Passing to Simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction]] S1-S3).

The three-dimensional shape talk adds a science-analogy caution. The
corona-radiata resemblance is a visual analogy framed by Shinzen as
uncertain, not evidence that Source/polarity practice has been anatomically
validated ([[The Three-Dimensional Shape of Simultaneous Expansion and
Contraction]] S3).

The Pythagorean-agenda talk adds the same caution for mathematics and
civilizational progress. Its claim is that a future conjunction of number,
experimental science, and concentration technology could model inner and
outer experience and perhaps improve the human condition; it does not show
that such modeling has been achieved or that global transformation follows
automatically ([[Fulfilling the Pythagorean Agenda]] S3-S7).

## Implications
When future sources mention Source, Nothingness, Zero, polarity, or cosmology, route here before promoting claims. Ask whether the claim is:

- a practice instruction,
- a phenomenological report,
- a symbolic mnemonic,
- an analogy,
- a metaphysical claim,
- a scientific speculation,
- or a safety-relevant state.

That classification keeps the deep frame useful without making it overconfident.
