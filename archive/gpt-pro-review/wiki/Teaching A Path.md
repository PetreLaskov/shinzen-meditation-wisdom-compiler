Teaching a path is Shinzen's service category for fostering others' extraordinary happiness. It is broader than the formal role of meditation teacher, but it becomes more competence-sensitive as it moves from subtle presence to explicit instruction and enlightenment-edge support.

## Key Points
- **Core claim**: Shinzen places teaching inside Total Happiness as deep service to others, with levels of teaching that range from practice-influenced presence to coherent description, explicit guidance, professional mastery, rare path discovery, and support for teaching.
- **Why this matters**: Future agents should not collapse service into private kindness or collapse teaching into status; Shinzen treats teaching as an activity with effects, roles, and escalating responsibility.
- **Key tensions**: Everyone may teach subtly by practicing, but explicit teaching and sudden-awakening or DPDR-adjacent support require competence, safeguards, and humility.
- **Source posture**: Strong as Shinzen's stated teaching ladder in the Total Happiness series; probable as a compiled service router; incomplete as a credentialing, clinical, or ethics framework.

## Model
The Total Happiness teaching branch begins with Shinzen's definition: teaching is the activity of fostering extraordinary happiness in others ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S1). This makes teaching part of deep service rather than an optional professional identity.

Shinzen gives three broad levels. **Subtle teaching** happens when practice changes a person enough that others sense the difference. It may be noticed first by close contacts and later more generally, but it remains influence through presence rather than instruction ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S1).

**Descriptive or explanatory teaching** happens when someone asks what the practitioner does and the practitioner can give a coherent description. Shinzen treats clear description as a real service because it can make the practice intelligible and desirable without persuasion ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S2).

**Explicit teaching** begins when the person offers technique, concepts, life application, questions, encouragement, repeated lessons, a group, or retreats. This is where role clarity matters most: a facilitator, a full-time professional teacher, and a teacher competent with sudden enlightenment or DPDR-adjacent edge cases are not the same role ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S2-S3).

Shinzen's rarest category modifies Buddha/arhat language into a path-discovery criterion. A Buddha-like master discovers something new and important about enlightenment and makes it available in a way that produces widespread positive historical change; he speculates that such discovery might come from a team rather than one person ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S4). This should be read as a service-and-innovation frame, not as a license for self-certified grandiosity.

Supporting teaching is also part of the model. A person can foster others' extraordinary happiness by supporting competent teachers and their efforts rather than personally teaching ([[Total Happiness - 5 of 5 - The Activity of Teaching]] S5).

The ox-herding sequence supplies an adjacent service frame: mature practice returns to the marketplace through ordinary presence, practice effects communicated through life, coherent description when asked, and eventual teaching for some people when students appear ([[Zen Ox-Herding Pics - Part 3 of 3]] S1-S5). The teacher-qualification and anti-dependency sources add the main safeguards: behavior and student results matter more than liberation signs, and competent teaching should produce independent peers rather than dependence ([[Meditation Teacher's Qualifications and Liberation Experiences]] S4-S5; [[Teacher's or Student's Issues Impeding Spiritual Progress]] S1-S4).

## Boundaries
Subtle teaching is not credentialing. Being changed by practice may affect others, but it does not prove competence, consent, role clarity, or benefit.

Coherent description is not the same as explicit instruction. It may be service, but taking responsibility for another person's practice introduces safety, privacy, follow-up, and referral questions.

Explicit teaching requires differentiated competence. A person may facilitate well without being able to handle sudden realization, no-self destabilization, DPDR-like distress, teacher projection, or behavior-harm patterns.

Path discovery should remain a high bar. Shinzen's Buddha-like category requires new insight made available in a way that produces widespread positive historical change; it should not validate every novel map, method, charisma claim, or technology promise.

Supporting teachers requires evaluation. Material, social, or institutional support is service only when the supported teaching is sufficiently competent, accountable, and beneficial.

## Implications
Questions about "should I teach?" should first ask which level is active: subtle influence, description, facilitation, explicit instruction, professional teaching, enlightenment-edge support, innovation, or support for another teacher.

The safest default is to let practice teach subtly, describe accurately when asked, and escalate only with training, feedback, supervision, clear scope, and ordinary behavior accountability.

In Shinzen's frame, teaching a path is part of Total Happiness because it helps others access condition-independent happiness. In safety terms, that same importance raises the burden of competence.
